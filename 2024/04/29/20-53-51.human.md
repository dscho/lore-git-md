This is a review response to the new `git url-parse` builtin series, focusing on URL parsing edge cases and naming concerns.

Torsten Bögershausen raises several thoughtful points about the proposed URL parsing functionality. He acknowledges the value of having a dedicated URL parser but questions whether it will integrate with Git's existing URL handling in `connect.c` and `fetch`, or remain a standalone verification tool. His experience with URL parsing quirks (particularly around IPv6 addresses and SCP-style syntax) leads him to emphasize comprehensive test coverage for edge cases.

The review identifies two specific areas for potential improvement:
1. Naming of the `enum protocol` (suggesting `git_scheme` to better match RFC 1738 terminology and avoid future naming conflicts)
2. Test case redundancy (questioning whether both `file:///` and `file://` need separate testing)

Bögershausen's comments reflect practical experience with Git's URL handling complexities, particularly noting undocumented but widely-used syntax patterns that have become de facto standards. His feedback suggests the series would benefit from:
- Explicit IPv6 test cases
- Clarification about whether the parser aims to match Git's legacy behavior exactly
- Potential renaming of the protocol enum for better standards alignment

The review is substantive, engaging with both technical implementation details and broader design questions about the parser's intended role in the Git ecosystem. It advances the discussion by identifying specific edge cases and terminology concerns that weren't addressed in the initial series.
