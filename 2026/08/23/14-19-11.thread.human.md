This thread is an RFC proposing a new `stash.ref` configuration knob to make Git's stash stack per-worktree, addressing the problem of silent interference between worktrees (or AI coding agents) sharing a repository. The idea, introduced by Vladimir Sitnikov, would allow users to configure a per-worktree stash ref (e.g., `refs/worktree/stash`) while preserving backward compatibility by defaulting to the current global `refs/stash`.

The discussion is still in its early stages, with no substantive reviews or decisions yet. The only feedback so far comes from Kristoffer Haugsbakk, who questions the necessity of the change. While he acknowledges the problem Vladimir describes, he argues that the current behavior—though not elegant—can be useful for humans (e.g., moving changes between worktrees) and that automated agents (like AI tools) could avoid the issue entirely by not using the stash. He suggests that the bar for adding new configuration to porcelain commands is high and that the proposal may not meet it, given that the harm is primarily to automated workflows rather than human users. Kristoffer also dismisses concerns about `gc` and `fsck` reachability, noting that no ref namespace is inherently special.

The thread is now at a crossroads: the technical feasibility of the change is not in dispute, but its justification is. The discussion may shift toward whether the problem is severe enough to warrant a new config knob or if alternative mitigations (e.g., agent-side restrictions) would suffice. Key open questions remain, including whether `stash.ref` values should be restricted to valid ref names, whether `git stash list` should gain a `--all` flag, and how reachability of per-worktree stashes would be handled. No other participants have weighed in yet.
