This is the latest reply in Karthik Nayak’s three-patch series optimizing reftable stack reloads during transactions. The series targets a performance bottleneck in the reftable backend where excessive `fstat()` calls during bulk reference creation were causing measurable slowdowns.

In this email, Karthik acknowledges Jeff King’s independent benchmark results showing a consistent 25% speedup across ref counts from 10,000 to 50,000—far higher than the 1–2% improvement he originally reported. The discrepancy stems from differences in test methodology: Karthik’s benchmarks used `refs/heads/*` (which shortcuts reflog creation), while Peff’s used `refs/tags/*` (which triggers a stack reload for each ref). This explains why Peff’s measurements show a much larger impact, as the optimization directly addresses the reload overhead that his test exposed. Karthik plans to update the commit message to reflect this nuance.

The discussion remains focused on validating the patch’s real-world benefit. No new technical concerns are raised, and the series continues to enjoy broad support. The only remaining open question is an implementation detail in patch 3—whether to embed lock-checking logic in `stack_uptodate()` or expose it to callers—but this is a minor point of encapsulation rather than a functional disagreement. The series looks well-crafted, thoroughly tested, and clearly motivated by profiling data.
