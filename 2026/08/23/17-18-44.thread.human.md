Michael Montalbo’s RFC series proposes `git organize`, a new command to systematically restructure Git’s source tree by moving files into subsystem-specific directories (e.g., `odb/`, `refs/`, `pack/`) based on rules in a `.gitorganize` file. The series, now under initial review, spans 14 patches: the first six establish the tool’s infrastructure and configuration, while the remaining eight demonstrate its use by mechanically moving the ODB, refs, pack, diff, revision, index, setup, and transport subsystems to their own directories. All moves update references to maintain build integrity, but the series has sparked debate about directory layout and coding conventions.

Oswald Buddenhagen raised two substantive concerns in his review of the ODB move (patch 7): first, that subsystems should be nested under `lib/` (e.g., `lib/odb/`) to align with Patrick Steinhardt’s `libgit.a` effort, and second, that the patch’s use of `#include "odb/object-file.h"` violates Git’s include style conventions. Junio C Hamano has now pressed Oswald for clarity on the latter point, asking what the "right" include style should be—whether the issue is the use of quotes versus angle brackets, alphabetical ordering, or another convention. This exchange highlights a tension between the series’ mechanical goals and the opportunity to address technical debt, particularly around inconsistent include practices.

The thread remains focused on design questions rather than implementation or testing. Key open issues include whether the tool’s layout rules should accommodate nested directories, how aggressively to standardize includes, and whether the demonstrated moves (e.g., `transport/`) are complete or risk breaking external tools like Git for Windows. No merge status has been reported, and the series is still in the early review phase.
