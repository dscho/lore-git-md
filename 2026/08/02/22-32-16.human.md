This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about a performance patch for `git index-pack` that speeds up promisor pack link recording by eliminating a serialization bottleneck. The patch was originally submitted as a direct contribution but is now being reframed as an RFC due to a policy concern about AI assistance in its development.

Arijit Banerjee acknowledges brian m. carlson’s procedural objection—namely, that the patch’s commit message discloses AI assistance in identifying the bottleneck and implementing the solution, which may violate Git’s `SubmittingPatches` guidelines. Arijit does not contest the policy but asks whether the patch’s clear performance benefits (a 15–20% speed-up for `--filter=blob:none` clones) might justify an exception. He proposes treating the submission as an RFC to solicit maintainer guidance on whether the technical result is worth preserving despite the policy issue.

In this reply, brian reiterates the policy stance: while using AI tools for *analysis* (e.g., identifying performance bottlenecks) is acceptable, generating *substantial code* with AI is not. They suggest that the patch would need to be rewritten entirely by hand without AI assistance to comply with project guidelines. The response remains focused on the procedural question and does not engage with the patch’s technical merits, performance measurements, or test coverage. It also does not address whether an exception might be considered for a well-tested, high-impact optimization.

The thread is now at a crossroads: the technical implementation is unchanged and appears sound, but its acceptance hinges on a policy decision. No maintainer or PLC member has yet weighed in on whether the performance gains warrant an exception, leaving the patch in limbo. For readers tracking this thread, the key question is whether Junio or the PLC will provide guidance—or if the author will need to reimplement the solution manually to proceed.
