This is a substantive review exchange in the GSoC series extending `git cat-file --batch-command` to support `%(objecttype)` in remote-object-info queries. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Pablo Sabater’s proposal to refactor the remote-object-info path to bypass `struct object_info` entirely, endorsing the plan and clarifying design details.

Peff agrees the refactoring should happen now rather than being deferred. He elaborates on the current code’s pain points: semi-opaque structs like `object_info_args`, unclear ownership of field requests, and the protocol’s awkward handling of space-separated values in arbitrary order. His feedback reinforces the need for a lightweight, purpose-built structure (`object_info_results`) to hold batch results, with boolean flags (`wants_size`, `wants_type`) to declare requested metadata and arrays to store the results. He suggests pushing the string-ification of protocol options down to `send_object_info_request()` so the rest of the code can consistently use the boolean flags, and confirms Pablo’s two-patch refactoring plan (first restructure the size-only logic, then add type support) is the right approach.

The exchange leaves no open questions—Peff’s endorsement is explicit and actionable. Key files affected are `fetch-object-info.c` and `builtin/cat-file.c`, where the new `object_info_results` struct will replace the current `object_info`-based parsing logic. The series remains under review, with no merge status implied. The architectural refactoring is now a confirmed part of the series, not a post-GSoC cleanup.
