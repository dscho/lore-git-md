This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the RFC v2 series introducing `git repack --drop-filtered` for partial clones. The series, now complete at seven patches, proposes a new option to safely reclaim disk space by removing locally cached promisor blobs exceeding a user-specified size threshold, such as `--filter=blob:limit=10M`.

Junio’s latest reply explores a potential refinement to the feature’s design. He suggests that the "cull anything refetchable" approach could be made smarter by incorporating object age into the drop criteria. Specifically, he proposes avoiding the removal of recently fetched objects—such as a large blob downloaded just 20 minutes ago—even if they meet the size threshold. This would reduce unnecessary network traffic and improve user experience by preserving objects likely to be reused soon. The idea is framed as a natural extension of the existing logic, leveraging the promisor infrastructure’s ability to refetch objects on demand.

The proposal is speculative ("perhaps") and does not critique the current implementation or question its correctness. Instead, it opens a new design dimension for the feature, one that could be addressed in a future iteration or follow-up patch. Junio does not demand changes to the current series, nor does he express a preference for whether the safety guards (merge/rebase/cherry-pick checks and index validation) should be retained or removed. His focus here is purely on the long-term potential of the feature.

For readers tracking the series, this exchange signals that the core functionality is technically sound but still open to refinement. The age-based optimization is a forward-looking suggestion rather than a blocker for the current RFC. The thread remains in the design phase, with no maintainer decision on merging or integration branch placement yet. If the list adopts this idea, it would likely be implemented as an additional filter criterion (e.g., `--drop-filtered=blob:limit=10M,age=1h`) rather than a replacement for the existing size-based logic.
