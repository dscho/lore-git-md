This is a maintainer reply in the ongoing thread about the promisor pack performance patch. Junio C Hamano responds to brian m. carlson's policy objection by citing the Linux kernel's guidance on AI-assisted contributions, which allows them provided the human submitter reviews the code, ensures licensing compliance, and certifies the DCO with their sign-off. Junio notes that while Git is not bound by the kernel's policy, there is broad community consensus to proceed cautiously with AI-assisted contributions, particularly regarding DCO attestation.

The key uncertainty Junio raises is enforcement: it is difficult to verify whether a submitter has genuinely reviewed and understood AI-generated code, and Git currently lacks mechanisms to detect violations of this expectation. This reply does not resolve the policy question or endorse the patch, but it clarifies that the project's stance is not an outright ban on AI assistance—only a cautious, case-by-case approach with heightened scrutiny. The thread remains stalled on the procedural question of whether the performance benefit justifies an exception, with no maintainer guidance yet on how to proceed.
