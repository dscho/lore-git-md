This thread began as a performance optimization for `git index-pack` in partial clones, proposing to eliminate a serialization bottleneck by replacing a per-object mutex with thread-local data structures. The patch, authored by Arijit Banerjee, promised a 15–26% speed-up for `--filter=blob:none` clones but was quickly repositioned as an RFC after maintainers raised concerns about the project’s policy on AI-assisted contributions. What started as a technical discussion has since stalled entirely on procedural grounds.

The core policy question—whether Git should accept non-trivial patches with disclosed AI assistance—has been decisively answered in the negative. brian m. carlson’s latest reply rules out such contributions for the foreseeable future, citing legal exposure, code quality concerns, project reputation, and ethical considerations. This supersedes Junio C Hamano’s earlier, more nuanced stance (a case-by-case approach with heightened scrutiny) and leaves no room for the patch’s performance benefits to be weighed as an exception. Arijit’s speculative proposals for governance changes (e.g., a karma system or experimental release train) were dismissed as unproductive, and no maintainer has proposed an alternative path forward.

The thread has attracted only surface-level engagement since the policy objection was raised. Collin Funk’s recent reply questions the practicality of legal risks in sanctioned countries but does not advance the discussion. Meanwhile, the technical merits of the patch—its correctness, safety, and the 15–26% speed-up—remain unaddressed. The patch itself touches `builtin/index-pack.c` and adds new test coverage, but the conversation has not returned to these details. With no procedural opening and no maintainer willing to consider an exception, the thread appears to have reached an impasse.
