This is a **design discussion** about the `hn/history-squash` topic, focusing on user experience and message-handling behavior. Instead, he suggests a **context-sensitive default**: the editor would open by default *unless* the commits being squashed are primarily `fixup!` or `amend!` messages, since those already signal the user’s intent. This approach aims to reduce friction for common cases while preserving flexibility. He also reiterates his concern about reusing rebase’s squash template, arguing it’s poorly suited for multi-target squashing and creates a fragmented editing experience.

His context-sensitive default is a thoughtful middle ground between Harald’s "always edit" and Matt Hunter’s `--no-edit` default, but it introduces new complexity that will need careful validation.
