This thread is about a small but long-standing bug in Git’s bash completion: `git diff` would suggest different sets of paths depending on how it was invoked, especially with `git -C` or when no revision was given. Junio sent a three-patch series (v3) that makes the behavior consistent—always suggesting tracked paths first, just like `git status` and `git add` do, with untracked paths as a fallback. The series also handles edge cases like `--no-index` explicitly, so nothing is left to Bash’s built-in fallback.

The patches themselves are straightforward: a small refactor of the completion script, a helper for tracked paths, and another for untracked paths. Tests were expanded to cover every scenario, including `git -C` and `--no-index`. Reviewers (notably SZEDER Gábor and D. Ben Knoble) gave it a clean bill of health, with only minor stylistic feedback on the test layout. Junio has now posted a v4 that tidies up the test organization—no logic changes, just removing a redundant test case. The series is queued on `seen` and ready to graduate to `next` and then `master` once the v4 is merged.
