This is a substantive review reply in the ongoing RFC thread about the promisor pack performance patch. Brian m. carlson expands on the policy objection to AI-assisted contributions, shifting from a procedural concern (DCO compliance) to a broader set of risks: legal exposure across jurisdictions, code quality and review burden, project reputation, environmental impact, and ethical considerations around uncredited use of open-source code.

The reply is a firm reaffirmation of the project's cautious stance, explicitly ruling out acceptance of nontrivial LLM-generated patches in the foreseeable future. It dismisses the author's earlier speculative governance ideas (experimental release trains) as unproductive and unlikely to be well received, while leaving open the possibility of personal forks. The tone is direct but not personal; the arguments are framed as shared project concerns rather than individual objections.

Key technical details from the original patch remain unchanged and unaddressed: the thread is now entirely about policy, not implementation. The performance benefit (15–26% speed-up for partial clones) is acknowledged only implicitly through the author's continued advocacy, but the review does not engage with it as a potential exception case. No new technical or procedural path forward is proposed; the thread remains stalled on the policy impasse.
