This is the latest exchange in the long-running `git history squash` feature effort. Phillip Wood responds to Harald Nordgren’s question about how to integrate his seven fixup patches into the series.

Phillip suggests a measured approach: first review his patches to understand the reasoning behind the stricter validation, ref-handling, and structural improvements, then address the remaining gaps (notably, printing branch names in error messages and adapting the ref-handling logic to support message editing). He also proposes a more incremental patch structure for the next revision, breaking the 1500-line final patch into five smaller, logically distinct steps (skeleton command, rev-list validation, branch checks, commit creation, and message editing). This would make the series easier to review and allow clearer explanations of each design decision.

Phillip gently chides Harald for posting v11 before this discussion concluded, noting that the mailing list is asynchronous and that frequent, uncoordinated updates can overwhelm reviewers. The thread remains open on the integration path: whether to treat Phillip’s branch as a full replacement or as incremental fixups, and how to reconcile it with v11’s default message-editing behavior. The series is technically sound but still needs this coordination before it can be queued.
