This thread is about adding bash completion for the `git history` command, covering its four subcommands (`drop`, `fixup`, `reword`, `split`) along with their options and revision arguments. Vincent Mailhol authored the patch, which has drawn substantive review from Patrick Steinhardt and surface-level endorsement from D. Ben Knoble. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, noted some boilerplate duplication in the completion script but did not request changes, suggesting instead a future refactoring into a shared helper.

The patch is under review and considered a clear improvement, though two minor refinements were identified: handling pathspec completion for the `split` subcommand (switching from revisions to file paths after the first argument) and a trivial rebase to include the upcoming `squash` subcommand once it lands. Both are considered non-blocking. In a recent follow-up, Vincent responded to Junio’s boilerplate suggestion, arguing that the current per-subcommand structure offers better maintainability for future extensions, such as the `split` subcommand’s pathspec handling. He sketched how the boilerplate acts as a flexible skeleton for such cases but stopped short of proposing a concrete refactoring, leaving the trade-off between duplication and extensibility as an open discussion point. The patch itself remains ready to advance once the `squash` subcommand graduates from `seen`.
