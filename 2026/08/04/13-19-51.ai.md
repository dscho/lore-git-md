Phillip Wood responds to Junio C Hamano’s critique of his proposed context-sensitive default for editor invocation during squash operations, ultimately conceding that a simpler, always-open-editor approach may be preferable.

While he still believes the dynamic approach could reduce friction for common cases (e.g., squashing `amend!` commits), he now agrees that an always-open-editor default is easier to explain and aligns better with Git’s existing conventions. This shift simplifies the design debate, removing the need to handle edge cases like multiple `amend!` commits or ambiguous `fixup!` behavior, which Junio had flagged as problematic.

The email also touches on the broader theme of design trade-offs in Git commands. Phillip’s initial proposal aimed to balance usability (reducing unnecessary editor invocations) with predictability, but Junio’s feedback highlighted the risks of subtle, context-dependent behavior. By conceding to the simpler approach, Phillip prioritizes clarity and consistency over nuanced UX optimizations—a recurring tension in Git’s design philosophy. This resolution may accelerate the fixups for `hn/history-squash`, as the message-handling behavior is now less contentious.
