This is Junio C Hamano, the Git maintainer, replying to Caleb White’s enthusiastic but surface-level review of Domen Kožar’s `post-worktree-*` hook series. Junio pushes back hard on the necessity of the feature itself, arguing that the proposed hooks do not meet any of the five criteria that justify adding new hooks to Git.

Junio’s core position is that lifecycle automation for worktrees can already be achieved by wrapping the `git worktree` command in a custom script (his example shows a simple shell wrapper that runs pre- and post-actions around `git worktree add` and `git worktree remove`). Since users would have to write the hook logic anyway, he sees no compelling reason to bake it into Git itself. He explicitly states that the series does not fall into any of the five categories where hooks are truly necessary—most notably, the hooks cannot veto the operation, do not operate on data generated mid-command, and do not require remote or locked execution.

The email is a clear signal that the series faces an uphill battle: Junio is not convinced the problem is worth solving inside Git, and his tone suggests he is unlikely to merge the patches unless the discussion shifts to demonstrate a broader need or a technical justification that aligns with his hook-adding policy. Domen’s next move will likely need to address this fundamental objection rather than focusing on implementation details.
