This is Junio’s reply to the fourth patch in Pablo Sabater’s nine-patch GSoC series (v4), which refactors `fetch_object_info()` to use a dedicated `struct fetch_object_info_results` instead of `struct object_info`. The patch is part of the effort to extend `git cat-file --batch-command` with `%(objecttype)` support for remote-object-info queries.

Junio’s feedback is surface-level and advisory. He notes that the patch removes about 30 lines of code, primarily by eliminating the intermediate `struct protocol_placeholder_entry` and its associated `remote_atom_map[]`. This is a mechanical cleanup that aligns with the new design, which avoids string-based lookups in favor of explicit flags (`wants_size`, `wants_type`) and dedicated arrays for each attribute. Junio’s only question is why the `remote_atom_map[]` already included the "type" entry, even though the `%(objecttype)` support is not introduced until later in the series (patches 6/9 and 7/9). This is a curiosity rather than a technical concern, and it does not block the patch’s readiness. The question is likely motivated by the fact that earlier iterations of the series introduced the `ask_type` flag prematurely (in patch 4/9), which was later fixed in v4 by moving the flag to patch 7/9. The presence of "type" in `remote_atom_map[]` is harmless and does not affect behavior, as the server’s allow-list filters out unsupported capabilities.

The patch itself remains uncontroversial and ready for integration. It is a purely mechanical refactoring that prepares the codebase for the `%(objecttype)` feature, and Junio’s feedback does not raise any technical issues or suggest changes. The series as a whole is feature-complete, with all prior review feedback addressed and the architectural design finalized. The remaining patches (5/9 through 9/9) are similarly uncontroversial and ready for review.
