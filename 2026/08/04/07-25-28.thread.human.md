This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-patch series refactoring Git’s object database (ODB) streaming API to unify the previously separate read and write paths into a single, direction-agnostic `odb_stream` structure. The goal is purely structural—no behavior changes are intended—and it’s part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort to enable pluggable backends.

The series is now complete and under review, with all patches posted as v1. Justin Tobler has reviewed most of the series, offering substantive feedback on the core logic (e.g., the removal of the `is_finished` field and the addition of an object-type field to support non-blob streaming) and raising naming consistency questions for the new wrapper structures. His reviews were positive but noted a few unresolved edge cases, such as the lifecycle management of the unified stream and potential performance regressions from added indirection.

The most significant development is Junio’s recent review of patch 3/7, where he identified a critical correctness bug in the in-memory ODB backend. The patch updates the object type passed to the backend but fails to update the type used for computing the object name, which could lead to silent corruption or hash collisions when streaming non-blob objects. This issue is a clear blocker and must be fixed before the series can proceed. No other reviews have been posted, and the series remains in the review phase with no indication of merge status beyond targeting `next` or `master` in the near term.
