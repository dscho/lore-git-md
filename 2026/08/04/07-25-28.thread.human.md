This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-patch series refactoring Git’s object database (ODB) streaming API to unify the previously separate `odb_write_stream` and `odb_read_stream` into a single `odb_stream` structure. The goal is purely structural—no behavior changes—but it’s a key step in the broader ODB abstraction effort to enable pluggable backends. The series is now complete and under review, with all patches posted in their first version.

Justin Tobler has been the most active reviewer so far. His latest feedback, on the third patch, is substantive: he confirms the addition of an `object_type` field to `odb_write_stream` and the mechanical updates to callers are a logical step toward unification, though he notes a now-outdated comment in `odb/transaction.h` that still assumes blob-only streaming. Earlier, he reviewed the second patch in detail, endorsing the removal of the `is_finished` field and the loop restructuring in `builtin/unpack-objects.c` as a clean alignment with the read stream’s design. His reviews have been positive and technically grounded, focusing on core logic and edge cases, but the remaining four patches (including the critical unification in Patch 5/7 and the renames in Patch 7/7) have yet to attract feedback.

The series touches 37 files and introduces new symbols like `struct odb_stream`, `struct fd_stream` (renamed from `read_object_fd_data`), and standardized function names (`odb_stream_from_object()`, `odb_stream_from_fd()`). No on-disk format changes or new CLI options are involved, but reviewers may still focus on edge cases like error handling in the unified API, wrapper structure lifecycle management, and potential performance regressions in stream-heavy code paths. The series is based on Junio’s "10th batch" for the upcoming release and is targeting `next` or `master` in the near term.
