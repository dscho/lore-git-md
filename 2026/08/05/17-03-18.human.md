This is a **maintainer policy update** about Git’s integration pipeline. Junio responds to Phillip Wood’s feedback on his earlier proposal to pause new additions to the `seen` branch until they receive substantive review. Instead of a moratorium, Junio outlines a revised approach that keeps `seen` open for new patches but imposes stricter time limits and review requirements to prevent stagnation.

The new policy has three key conditions: patches in `seen` must receive *some* feedback to avoid being dropped, will be automatically discarded after four weeks without positive engagement, and Junio will no longer feel obligated to review them himself. This strikes a balance between Phillip’s concern (that `seen` makes it easier for reviewers to test patches) and Junio’s goal (to avoid promoting under-reviewed patches to `next`). The change is pragmatic—it preserves the low-friction staging area reviewers rely on while introducing gentle pressure on contributors to actively seek feedback.

The update is light on technical details but has clear procedural implications. Contributors can still expect their patches to land in `seen` without prior review, but they’ll need to monitor the "What's cooking" reports and solicit feedback to avoid automatic removal. Reviewers, meanwhile, retain the convenience of testing patches directly from `seen` but may see fewer "orphaned" topics lingering indefinitely. The policy is now settled unless further discussion surfaces, though its effectiveness will depend on how consistently the four-week rule is enforced.
