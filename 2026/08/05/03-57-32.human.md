This is a two-patch series from Taylor Blau targeting the `git maintenance` command, specifically improving how geometric repacking handles promisor packs. The series addresses a gap where the maintenance task’s decisions about when to run and what repack mode to use were based only on ordinary packs, ignoring promisor packs even after geometric repacking learned to handle them separately.

The first patch adjusts the repack-mode logic so that the choice between geometric repacking and the all-into-one fallback considers both ordinary and promisor pack progressions. The fallback now only triggers if neither progression would keep a pack above its split threshold, avoiding unnecessary rewrites of large promisor packs. The second patch extends the `--auto` condition to also consider `geometry.promisor_split`, ensuring the task runs when a promisor rollup is possible even if ordinary packs don’t meet the threshold.

The motivation is clear: partial clones and all-promisor repositories were being underserved by the existing logic, leading to either over-aggressive repacking or missed opportunities to optimize. The tests demonstrate the fix by creating three promisor packs where only the two smaller ones should roll up, verifying that the task now behaves as intended. The changes are small (73 insertions, 3 deletions) and focused on the decision logic in `builtin/gc.c` and its tests.

This looks like a well-scoped, practical improvement. The commit message is thorough, the tests are targeted, and the problem being solved is real for users of partial clones. If you care about repository maintenance or promisor pack behavior, this series is worth reviewing.
