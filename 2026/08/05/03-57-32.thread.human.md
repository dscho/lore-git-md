This thread is a two-patch bugfix series from Taylor Blau addressing a regression in `git maintenance`'s geometric repack task, where promisor packs were mishandled after gaining their own geometric progression support earlier this year. The series ensures the maintenance task properly evaluates both ordinary and promisor pack progressions before deciding whether to fall back to an all-into-one repack, and adjusts the `--auto` trigger to account for promisor pack geometry.

The discussion has focused on a potential edge case raised by Patrick Steinhardt: whether the current logic might under-trigger all-into-one repacks when promisor and non-promisor packs remain "perfectly out of phase," risking unreachable objects not being pruned. After further discussion, Patrick now accepts Taylor's argument that this scenario is unlikely in practice, and the current behavior is a strict improvement over the regression. Both patches remain under review, with patch 2 ("adjust `--auto` trigger") uncontested and patch 1 ("evaluate both progressions") now cleared of the edge case concern.

The series touches `builtin/gc.c` and adds test coverage in `t/t7900-maintenance.sh`, with minor adjustments to `t/t5331-pack-objects-stdin.sh`. The key technical changes involve the repack mode decision logic now checking `geometry.promisor_split` alongside `geometry.split`, and the auto-condition in `geometric_repack_auto_condition()` updated to include promisor pack geometry. The thread is now aligned on the trade-off between geometric efficiency and garbage collection guarantees, with no further revisions planned unless real-world usage reveals issues.
