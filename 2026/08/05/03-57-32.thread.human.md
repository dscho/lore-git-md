This thread is a bugfix series from Taylor Blau addressing a regression in `git maintenance`'s geometric repack task, where promisor packs were not being handled correctly after gaining their own geometric progression support earlier this year. The two-patch series ensures the maintenance task properly evaluates both ordinary and promisor pack progressions before deciding whether to fall back to an all-into-one repack, and adjusts the `--auto` trigger to consider promisor pack geometry.

The series is under review, with Patrick Steinhardt providing substantive feedback. While he acknowledges the problem and accepts the proposed fix, he raised a concern about a potential edge case in the first patch: the current logic might under-trigger all-into-one repacks when the combined set of ordinary and promisor packs is large enough to warrant a single-pack rollup, but neither progression alone crosses the threshold. This could leave unreachable objects unpruned. Taylor defended the current approach, arguing the edge case is already handled by existing logic, but Patrick’s follow-up elaborates that the issue might be more fundamental—since it’s unlikely both progressions will simultaneously meet the threshold, all-into-one repacks could be prevented entirely. He suggests a more nuanced approach, like triggering a full repack when objects exceed a certain percentage of the repository size, to balance geometric efficiency with garbage collection.

The discussion remains open, with no code changes proposed yet. The outcome could refine the patch or confirm the current behavior as acceptable. The series touches `builtin/gc.c` and adds test coverage in `t/t7900-maintenance.sh`, with the fix targeting the interaction between `geometry.promisor_split`, `geometry.split`, and the `geometric_repack_auto_condition()` function.
