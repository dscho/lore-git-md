This is a **maintainer policy update** about Git’s integration pipeline, specifically the `seen` branch. Junio responds to Phillip Wood’s feedback on his earlier proposal to pause new additions to `seen` until they receive substantive review. The outcome is a compromise: Junio will continue queuing patches in `seen` but with stricter conditions to prevent stagnation and reduce the risk of under-reviewed topics advancing to `next`.

The new policy imposes three rules for patches in `seen`:
1. **No automatic promotion**: Topics will not graduate to `next` without at least one substantive review or acknowledgment from the community.
2. **Automatic expiration**: Topics will be dropped after four weeks if they receive no positive feedback (e.g., no `Reviewed-by`, `Acked-by`, or even a "this looks reasonable" comment).
3. **No maintainer obligation**: Junio explicitly disclaims responsibility for reviewing every patch himself, even if no one else does.

This addresses Phillip’s concern that `seen` serves as a useful staging area for reviewers (eliminating manual patch application) while also mitigating Junio’s worry about prematurely promoting under-scrutinized patches. The four-week expiration period creates a natural cadence for contributors to solicit feedback, and the "no obligation" clause clarifies that the burden of review lies with the community, not the maintainer.

The policy is now active, though its long-term impact on review velocity and patch quality remains to be seen. Contributors should note that simply sending a patch is no longer sufficient to ensure it stays in `seen`; they must actively engage with reviewers or risk having their work dropped. The thread leaves open whether this will improve review depth or merely shift the bottleneck from `next` to `seen`. No technical changes or patch specifics are discussed.
