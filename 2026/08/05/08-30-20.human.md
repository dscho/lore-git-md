This is a performance-focused refactoring patch series that revives a previously reverted `writev(3p)` wrapper to reduce system call overhead in Git’s low-level I/O operations. The series targets pktline transmission in `sideband.c` and cat-blob responses in `fast-import.c`, aiming for measurable speed-ups. Patrick Steinhardt reintroduces the wrapper after addressing platform compatibility issues (NonStop, CMake) that caused its prior removal in Git 2.54, but the API design and maintainer appetite remain unresolved.

The discussion has converged on the naming dispute: Junio C Hamano and Johannes Sixt favor renaming the wrapper from `git_writev()` to `git_write_gather()` (or similar) to avoid implying POSIX semantics the emulation cannot guarantee. Junio proposes a two-level abstraction where Git uses a `write_gather()` API that platforms can implement either via `writev(2)` or as a series of `write(2)` calls, explicitly documenting which guarantees are *not* required. Patrick acknowledges the precedent of Git’s current POSIX-centric compatibility wrappers but expresses hesitation about diverging from this pattern without a broader discussion on I/O abstraction. He is open to the rename but questions its value without rethinking Git’s overall approach to platform-specific I/O.

The series is technically ready for a respin, but Patrick is at an offsite, so no immediate update is expected. The integer-overflow concern raised earlier has been resolved with an explicit check, and the platform fixes are incorporated. What remains unclear is whether the performance gains justify the complexity—benchmarks are referenced but not provided—and whether maintainers consider the effort worthwhile. The lack of a range-diff to the reverted version also complicates review. If Patrick proceeds, the next version will likely adopt the `write_gather()` name, but the broader architectural questions may linger.
