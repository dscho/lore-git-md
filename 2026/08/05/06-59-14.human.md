This is a review of Tim Wiederhake’s feature patch adding user-configurable custom commands to `gitk`’s context menus. The reviewer is Johannes Sixt, the current maintainer of `gitk`.

The review is substantive but focused on process and motivation rather than code correctness. Sixt’s primary concern is the patch’s lack of a clear rationale: the commit message describes *what* the feature does but not *why* it is useful or what problem it solves. Without that context, he says, it is impossible to assess whether the design is sound or whether the implementation matches the intended purpose.

Sixt also flags the AI-assisted authorship. He acknowledges the transparency of the `Assisted-by` trailer but insists that Git’s AI contribution rules must be followed. More pointedly, he states he will not review the code until he is confident the human author has personally verified and understood every line produced by the AI. This is a gatekeeping stance: the patch will not advance until the author demonstrates hands-on ownership of the implementation.

No technical feedback on the code itself is offered yet. The ball is back in Wiederhake’s court to either expand the commit message with a compelling use case or to clarify the human review process. Until then, the patch remains stalled.
