This is a follow-up in the ongoing bugfix discussion about `git-replay` and `git-history` silently dropping Git notes during history rewriting.

D. Ben Knoble replies to Patrick Steinhardt’s acknowledgment of the issue, confirming that both commands currently lose notes on intermediate commits (only the tip commit’s notes can be manually recovered). The exchange briefly speculates about whether a similar issue might have been discussed recently—possibly related to `git rebase`—but neither participant provides concrete references. The thread reaffirms that for `git history` (a user-facing command), the omission is an unimplemented feature rather than a design choice, while `git replay`’s status remains less clear due to its plumbing nature. Patrick’s earlier GitLab issue remains the only tracking mechanism, and no patches or design proposals have emerged yet. The tone suggests the issue is recognized but not urgent, with community contributions welcomed. Key unresolved questions—such as whether all rewritten commits or only the tip should preserve notes, and how this compares to other history-rewriting commands—remain unaddressed.
