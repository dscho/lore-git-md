This is the fourth patch in Justin Tobler’s seven-patch v2 series that refactors `git receive-pack` to use the ODB transaction system for pluggable packfile writes. The series is part of Patrick Steinhardt’s ongoing ODB abstraction effort, and this patch focuses on eliminating global state in the `unpack()` function.

The patch introduces `struct unpack_opts`, which bundles eight previously global variables (`fsck_msg_types`, `shallow_file`, `max_input_size`, `fsck_objects`, `reject_thin`, `err_fd`, `quiet`, and the ternary logic for `fsck_objects`) into a single structure passed explicitly to `unpack()`. This is a mechanical but necessary step to prepare for the later introduction of `odb_transaction_write_pack()`, which will move packfile processing behind a generic ODB backend interface. The change is confined to `builtin/receive-pack.c` and does not alter observable behavior.

The patch is clean and well-motivated: the commit message clearly explains why the global state must be lifted (to enable backend-agnostic packfile processing), and the implementation is straightforward. The ternary initialization of `fsck_objects` and the late assignment of `err_fd` in `unpack_with_sideband()` are noted trade-offs for the larger goal of removing global state, and Patrick Steinhardt has already accepted these as necessary. No test failures or edge cases are introduced, and the patch relies on existing test coverage.

This is a solid refactoring step that directly supports the series’ goal of making `receive-pack` backend-agnostic. The patch is unlikely to be controversial, but reviewers may focus on the ergonomics of the new `struct unpack_opts` and whether the explicit passing of options feels natural in the call chain. The next patches in the series will complete the transition to the ODB transaction interface.
