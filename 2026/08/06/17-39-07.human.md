This is the fourth patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s six-patch series making Git’s on-disk object database (ODB) structures pluggable, part of the larger ODB abstraction effort. The patch has now reached a final state after incorporating feedback from Toon Claes and Junio C Hamano. Junio’s reply confirms the series is nearly complete, with only a small reroll expected to address minor docstring and formatting nits.

The patch abandons the earlier `APPLY_REPOSITORY_FORMAT_SKIP_ODB_CREATION` flag in favor of Toon Claes’s alternative design, which moves `odb_new()` out of `apply_repository_format()` and makes ODB creation an explicit step at every call site. This change is purely mechanical, touching only the initialization sequence in `repository.c` and `setup.c`, and ensures ODB initialization and on-disk creation are handled together. The patch introduces a new helper function, `create_object_database()`, which replaces `create_object_directory()` and will be used in the next patch to invoke the `create_on_disk` callback in ODB backends.

Junio’s reply is brief but significant: he marks the topic as "Expecting a (hopefully small and final) reroll," signaling that the series is now effectively approved and ready for merging once the last cosmetic issues are addressed. The remaining feedback from Toon Claes—about a misleading docstring in `setup.h` and a minor formatting inconsistency in `init_db()`—is acknowledged by Patrick and will be fixed in the final reroll. Neither issue is substantive, and the patch remains uncontroversial.

This patch is a key enabler for the series’ goal of decoupling ODB structure creation from repository setup, allowing future backends to implement custom on-disk layouts. The first patch in the series, a correctness fix with regression test coverage, is already marked "will merge to 'next'" in Junio’s "What's cooking" report, and the rest of the series is now poised to follow. For readers tracking the ODB abstraction effort, this is a clear signal that the infrastructure work is nearing completion.
