This thread is about adding Bash completion for the new `git history` command, now in its second iteration. Vincent Mailhol is the author, and the series has drawn feedback from Patrick Steinhardt and D. Ben Knoble.

The goal is to provide shell completion for `git history` and its subcommands, including `--empty`, `--update-refs`, and `split` pathspecs. The key challenge has been ensuring the completion logic correctly handles the command’s requirement for exactly one revision argument before any options or pathspecs. After feedback on the first version, Vincent revised the series to address revision handling, `--` termination, and test coverage. A compromise was reached on how to handle future exceptions to the "revision-first" pattern, though the maintainability of the helper function `__git_history_has_revision` remains a concern due to its hardcoded options.

The latest feedback from D. Ben Knoble is stylistic, noting that the placement of the `split` subcommand’s pathspec completion logic diverges from the pattern used in other Git completion functions. While the current implementation is functionally correct and well-tested, Knoble suggests aligning it with the broader codebase’s idiom for consistency. No technical objections remain, and the series is now close to readiness, though a third version is likely to incorporate this final feedback. The unresolved question is whether to simplify the helper function further by ignoring the undocumented `--option <value>` syntax, a change Knoble supports for consistency with Git’s documented conventions.
