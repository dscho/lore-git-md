This thread is about adding Bash completion for the new `git history` command, now in its second version. Vincent Mailhol is the author, and the series aims to provide shell completion for subcommands like `--empty`, `--update-refs`, and `split` pathspecs, using Git’s existing parse-options infrastructure.

The latest round of feedback from D. Ben Knoble focuses on a design choice in the second patch: whether to support both `--empty=<value>` (the documented form) and `--empty <value>` (an undocumented but accepted form). Knoble argues for consistency with Git’s `gitcli(7)` man page, which recommends `--option=value`, and points out that the completion codebase itself rarely handles the unstuck form. His review aligns with the thread’s earlier discussion about simplifying the completion helper to ignore `--option <value>` entirely, reducing complexity and maintenance burden.

The series is under review, with no maintainer decision yet. The key open question is whether to proceed with the simplified approach (option 2) or retain the current implementation (option 1). Patrick Steinhardt previously raised concerns about the helper’s maintainability, especially as new options (like commit signing or notes handling) are added. Vincent Mailhol and Knoble now favor the simplified approach, but Steinhardt hasn’t yet responded to these latest arguments. The series is likely headed for a v3 iteration to incorporate this feedback.
