This thread is about adding Bash completion for the new `git history` command. Vincent Mailhol posted a second version of his four-patch series to implement completion for the command’s subcommands (`--empty`, `--update-refs`, and `split` pathspecs), addressing earlier feedback on revision handling and test coverage.

The series is now functionally complete and under review. The main discussion has centered on two stylistic points: whether the completion script should check options before subcommands (Vincent’s current approach) or use a more idiomatic `case "$subcommand,$cur"` pattern (suggested by D. Ben Knoble), and where to place the `split` subcommand’s pathspec logic. Both reviewers agree the patch works correctly, but Knoble prefers the idiomatic pattern for consistency with the rest of Git’s completion codebase. Vincent has defended his approach as cleaner and more maintainable, but Knoble’s latest reply suggests the `case` pattern might achieve the same goal without duplication.

A separate maintainability concern about the `__git_history_has_revision` helper—flagged by Patrick Steinhardt as fragile due to hardcoded options—has largely been resolved in favor of a simplified approach (option 2) that ignores the rare and undocumented `--option <value>` syntax. Both Vincent and Knoble now support this, citing consistency with Git’s documentation and existing completion logic. The series is likely to see a third iteration incorporating these stylistic tweaks before it’s ready for merging. No maintainer decision has been reported yet.
