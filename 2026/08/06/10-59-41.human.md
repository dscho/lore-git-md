This is the final patch in the ready-to-merge `paint_down_to_common()` optimization series, removing the commit-date ordering fallback entirely. The patch is a performance and correctness win, eliminating a long-standing workaround that was introduced in 2018 to avoid regressions on v1 commit-graphs (which lack corrected commit dates).

The fallback was originally added because v1 topo levels could cause asymmetric queries (like `git merge-base v4.8 v4.9` on the Linux kernel repo) to walk 636k commits instead of 167k. The side-exhaustion optimization introduced earlier in the series solves this problem more elegantly: by terminating the walk as soon as one side of the history is exhausted, it prevents deep traversals regardless of queue ordering. Benchmarks show the new approach reduces step counts from 167k to just 5k on v1 graphs, a **30x improvement over the old fallback** and a **1000x improvement over the unoptimized baseline**.

The patch also fixes a correctness issue: the date-ordering fallback broke the `min_generation` invariant, allowing premature termination when the queue was date-ordered but contained commits with finite topo levels. With generation ordering now always active, the early-exit logic works correctly across all commit-graph versions. For v1 graphs, the patch introduces a topological ceiling (`GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX`) to ensure saturated commits are treated as unordered, preventing premature termination in the INFINITY region.

The documentation is updated to remove the now-obsolete "date-ordering fallback" section, and the test suite is adjusted to reflect the reduced step counts (e.g., `test_paint_down_steps 81 9 57 37` instead of `81 9 57 81`). The changes are minimal and focused: the patch touches only `commit-reach.c`, the technical documentation, and test expectations, with no new symbols or behavior changes beyond removing the fallback.
