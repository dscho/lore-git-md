This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) on Pablo Sabater’s GSoC v4 series extending `git cat-file --batch-command` to support `%(objecttype)` in remote-object-info queries. Peff proposes a targeted refactoring to improve the design of the transport layer’s object-info handling, addressing a latent bug and clarifying ownership semantics.

The patch Peff provides is a follow-up to Pablo’s patches 3/9 and 4/9, which refactored how object-info data is stored and passed between functions. Peff’s critique focuses on the transport struct’s role: currently, the `git_transport_options` struct holds pointers to the OID array and results struct, but these are only valid for a single request. This creates a subtle risk—if the transport is reused (e.g., for multiple object-info requests), the struct could retain dangling pointers to stack variables from the previous call. While harmless today (the transport is disconnected immediately after use), Peff argues this design is conceptually flawed and could cause issues if the transport layer evolves to support connection reuse.

The proposed fix removes the `object_info_oids` and `object_info_results` fields from `git_transport_options` and instead passes them as explicit parameters to `transport_fetch_object_info()` and its vtable implementations. This makes ownership clearer: the caller retains responsibility for the OID array and results, and the transport no longer needs to manage their lifetimes. Peff also marks the OID array as `const` to emphasize its input-only role. The change is mechanical (25 insertions, 19 deletions) and touches seven files, primarily `transport.c`, `transport-helper.c`, and `builtin/cat-file.c`.

Peff’s review is substantive and tested—he provides a complete, signed-off patch with a clear rationale and notes that the protocol changes are still unreleased, making this a safe time to adjust the design. The patch does not alter behavior but reduces technical debt and prepares the codebase for future enhancements, such as reusing transport connections for multiple object-info requests. While Peff speculates about protocol-level improvements (e.g., making the remote name stateful), he treats those as separate ideas and focuses his patch on the immediate cleanup.

Key symbols introduced or modified: `transport_fetch_object_info()` now takes explicit `oids` and `results` parameters; the `fetch_object_info()` helper and its vtable entry are updated to match. The `git_transport_options` struct no longer holds object-info fields. No on-disk or protocol changes are involved.

This feedback is actionable and aligns with the series’ broader goal of robust, maintainable design. Pablo can integrate Peff’s patch as-is or adapt its approach into his existing refactoring. The change is uncontroversial and likely to be merged once incorporated.
