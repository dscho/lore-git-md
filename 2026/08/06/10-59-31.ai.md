This is the ready-to-merge v7 iteration of a performance optimization series targeting Git’s commit-reach subsystem, specifically the `paint_down_to_common()` function used in merge-base calculations. The series teaches the algorithm to terminate early when one side of the history is exhausted, eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). Benchmarks show **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric queries, with no regressions on smaller repositories.

The series is **queued in Junio’s tree** and procedurally unblocked, but review bandwidth remains the primary bottleneck. The final patch removes the commit-date ordering fallback entirely, ensuring the queue remains generation-ordered regardless of graph version.

Key technical details include new instrumentation (`trace2_data_intmax`) to report step counts, deterministic test assertions for all commit-graph modes (none/full/half/no-gdat), and coverage for edge cases like clock-skew topologies and mixed finite/INFINITY generation numbers. The series touches `commit-reach.c`, `Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc`, and test scripts (`t6099-merge-base-side-exhaustion.sh`, `t6600-test-reach.sh`), introducing symbols like `paint_state` (with counters `parent1_count`, `parent2_count`, `mb_candidate_count`) and removing dead code (`nonstale_queue_put_dedup()`, `corrected_commit_dates_enabled()`).
