This is a reply in the ongoing ODB abstraction series, part of Patrick Steinhardt’s effort to make on-disk object database structures pluggable. The thread centers on the fourth patch in the six-patch v3 series, which defers ODB creation until `create_object_database()` to keep the ODB and its on-disk structures in sync.

Toon Claes’s review proposed an alternative design: remove the `APPLY_REPOSITORY_FORMAT_SKIP_ODB_CREATION` flag and make ODB creation an explicit step at every call site, reverting the refactoring in this patch. His proof-of-concept diff showed the change is mechanical and affects only the initialization sequence. Patrick initially resisted this approach, fearing it would complicate a future move of `apply_repository_format()` into `repo_init()`, but in this reply he concedes the point. He now agrees to adopt Toon’s simpler API, which will require a v4 of the series.

The concession resolves the last open architectural question in the series. The first patch (a correctness fix for loose-object map loading) is already slated for `next`, and the remaining patches are uncontroversial. This change affects only the initialization sequence and does not alter the series’ core goal of making on-disk ODB structures pluggable. The shift to explicit ODB creation aligns with Justin Tobler’s earlier suggestion and simplifies the API, though it may slightly increase the burden on callers. No new technical objections are raised; the discussion is now purely about implementation trade-offs.
