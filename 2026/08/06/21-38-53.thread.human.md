This thread is about Justin Tobler’s six-patch series to make `git receive-pack` work with pluggable object database (ODB) backends by replacing its hardcoded use of `git index-pack` and `git unpack-objects` with a new generic transaction interface. The series is part of Patrick Steinhardt’s broader ODB abstraction effort and is currently under active review at version 1.

The second patch, just posted, is a preparatory refactoring that removes reliance on the global `alt_shallow_file` variable by passing the shallow file path explicitly through the call chain in `builtin/receive-pack.c`. This is a mechanical change that doesn’t alter behavior but paves the way for the later switch to the generic `odb_transaction_write_pack()` interface. The patch looks clean and uncontroversial, though reviewers may weigh in on whether the explicit parameter passing is the right abstraction and whether error handling remains robust.

So far, only Justin has weighed in, acknowledging design uncertainty around the transaction lifecycle and seeking feedback. Patrick Steinhardt, who leads the ODB abstraction effort, hasn’t reviewed yet, but the series is a key part of his roadmap. The main open questions revolve around the transaction lifecycle design—specifically whether `odb_transaction_release()` is the right place for post-commit cleanup—and whether the new `odb_transaction_write_pack()` interface will be sufficiently generic. No objections or conditional approvals have been recorded yet.
