Justin Tobler’s six-patch series refactors `git receive-pack` to use the ODB transaction system for packfile writes, replacing its hardcoded reliance on `git index-pack` and `git unpack-objects` with a new generic interface, `odb_transaction_write_pack()`. This is a key step in Patrick Steinhardt’s broader ODB abstraction effort, enabling alternative backends (like reftable or cloud storage) to handle incoming packfiles without being tied to the traditional files backend. The series remains at its initial v1, under active review, with no merge status change—still cooking in `seen` or earlier.

The technical work is largely plumbing: introducing new symbols like `odb_transaction_write_pack()` and `odb_transaction_release()`, bundling previously global unpack options into structs, and shifting error reporting to `strbuf` parameters. The first five patches prepare the groundwork without changing observable behavior, while the sixth patch completes the switch by moving packfile ingestion logic into `object-file.c`, where it becomes the "files" backend implementation. Patrick Steinhardt’s review of the final patch is overwhelmingly positive, calling it "nicely done" and confirming the core payoff—ownership of `.keep` lockfiles now belongs to the transaction, ensuring cleanup happens in the backend’s `release` callback after references are updated.

Two open design questions remain. First, whether `odb_reprepare()` (which refreshes the entire object database after packfile ingestion) is overly broad now that the logic is part of an ODB transaction—Steinhardt suggests repreparing only the transaction’s source ODB as a future optimization. Second, whether `unpack_limit` (a threshold for loose vs. packed storage) belongs in the generic `odb_transaction_write_pack_opts` struct, given its irrelevance to backends that don’t use loose/packed storage. Steinhardt also raised ergonomic concerns in earlier patches about struct initialization and mutation patterns, but these are accepted as necessary trade-offs for the larger goal. The series is uncontroversial in direction, with reviewers focused on robustness and scalability of the new interface.
