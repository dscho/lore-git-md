This is the final patch in the ready-to-merge `paint_down_to_common()` optimization series, removing the commit-date ordering fallback entirely. The patch is a **correctness and performance cleanup** that eliminates a long-standing workaround for v1 commit-graphs, now rendered obsolete by the side-exhaustion optimization introduced earlier in the series.

The fallback was originally added to avoid a performance regression in v1 commit-graphs (where topological levels are unbalanced), but it introduced its own correctness issues: the date-ordered queue could violate topological invariants, causing premature termination or incorrect merge-base selection. The side-exhaustion optimization (added in prior patches) solves the performance problem more elegantly by terminating the walk as soon as one side of the history is exhausted, regardless of queue ordering. Benchmarks on the Linux kernel repo show the new approach reduces step counts from **167k to 5.7k** (v1 graph) and **3.9k** (v2 graph), far exceeding the fallback’s performance.

The patch replaces the fallback with a **topological ceiling** (`GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX`) for v1 commit-graphs, ensuring saturated generation numbers are treated as unordered. This preserves the early-exit optimization’s correctness while maintaining generation ordering as the sole queue comparator. The change touches `commit-reach.c` (removing `gen_ordered` and adding `topo_ceiling` to `paint_state`), updates the technical documentation to remove the now-obsolete "date-ordering fallback" section, and adjusts test expectations to reflect the reduced step counts (e.g., `test_paint_down_steps 81 9 57 37` instead of `81 9 57 81`).
