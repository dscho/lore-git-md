This is a policy discussion email from Junio C Hamano in response to brian m. carlson’s objection to the `utimensat()` migration series on AI-generated content grounds. Junio acknowledges the legal prudence of brian’s stance but raises a broader question: whether the project’s current approach to AI-generated contributions effectively creates a double standard for new contributors.

Junio frames the core issue as one of trust and risk. The objection hinges on the idea that a contributor who uses AI tools cannot validly certify the origin of their code under the Developer’s Certificate of Origin (DCO), because they cannot know whether the AI copied protected material. Junio agrees this is a legitimate concern—if the code later turns out to be infringing, the project bears the cost of removal, which grows the longer the code remains in the tree. However, he points out that the same risk exists with any unknown contributor who claims their work is original: if their code is later found to be copied, the project is still liable. The difference, in his view, is one of transparency—AI users disclose their process, while others may not.

The email does not resolve the policy question or signal a path forward. Instead, it highlights a tension: the project’s current policy may unintentionally penalize transparency (by rejecting AI-assisted contributions outright) while doing little to mitigate the underlying risk of infringement from any unknown contributor. Junio’s tone is measured, not dismissive—he is not advocating for a change, but he is clearly uncomfortable with the implication that the project might be treating new contributors differently based on whether they disclose AI use.

For now, the series remains blocked pending further discussion, likely from the Project Leadership Committee. The technical merits of the `utimensat()` migration—well-motivated, cross-platform, and introducing nanosecond precision—are not in dispute; the entire thread has pivoted to a meta-level debate about contribution policy. Readers following this thread will want to watch for PLC input or a "What's cooking" update that clarifies whether the project will adjust its stance on AI-generated content.
