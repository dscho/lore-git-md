This is a **policy discussion** in the ongoing `utimensat()` migration thread, specifically brian m. carlson’s reply to Junio C Hamano’s framing of the AI-generated content dilemma.

Brian reinforces the legal and ethical rationale behind the project’s current policy: the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) requires contributors to know the provenance of their code, and AI-generated patches—even when manually verified—do not provide that certainty. He cites active global litigation around LLM outputs and the risk that AI tools may regurgitate copyrighted training data, exposing the project to legal liability. The distinction between “knowingly” and “unknowingly” distributing infringing code is critical: jurisdictions like Canada’s Copyright Act impose stricter penalties for willful infringement, so accepting AI-generated work could amplify the project’s legal exposure.

The reply also clarifies the ethical dimension. Brian contrasts two scenarios: a contributor who discloses AI use (acting in good faith, even if the code cannot be accepted) versus one who misrepresents the origin of their work (acting in bad faith and likely to be banned). This framing positions the policy not as a barrier to new contributors but as a baseline expectation of honesty.
