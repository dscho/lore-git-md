This thread introduces a small but potentially disruptive change: Harald Nordgren proposes to prevent `git branch -d` from deleting a local branch if it is the upstream of another local branch, requiring `-D` (force) instead. The series is under review, with no prior versions.

Junio C Hamano has raised a significant backward-compatibility concern. The patch alters the long-standing behavior of `-d`, which currently only checks whether a branch is merged. Junio argues that this change could break existing workflows, as users who previously relied on `-d` to delete branches (including `@{u}`, the upstream branch) would now be forced to use `-D` even when the branch is fully merged. He rejects the idea of a configuration toggle, emphasizing the need for predictable behavior. The review focuses on the user-facing impact rather than the implementation, leaving the author to address the compatibility issue before the patch can proceed. No other reviews have been posted yet.
