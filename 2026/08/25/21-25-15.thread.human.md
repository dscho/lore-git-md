This thread is about a patch series by Harald Nordgren that aims to prevent accidental deletion of local branches via `git branch -d` when those branches serve as upstreams for other local branches. The series consists of two patches: a preparatory refactoring of helper functions in `builtin/branch.c` and the core logic that implements the protection.

The key debate centers on whether changing the long-standing behavior of `git branch -d` is justified. Junio C Hamano has raised a substantive backward-compatibility objection, arguing that the change could break existing workflows by forcing users to switch to `-D` (force deletion) even when the original safety checks (like merged status) would have allowed deletion. Harald acknowledged the concern but has not yet proposed a resolution.

Tuomas Ahola recently clarified a critical nuance in Junio’s earlier feedback: Junio had suggested two possible behaviors for protecting upstream branches, neither of which fully aligns with the current patch. The first ("block deletion") matches Harald’s implementation, while the second ("retarget dependent branches to origin/master") introduces a more complex and user-friendly alternative that the patch does not address. This shifts the discussion from whether the feature is desirable to *how* it should work, raising the bar for the patch’s design. The series remains under review, with no new version posted yet.
