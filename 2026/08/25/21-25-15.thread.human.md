This thread is about a proposed change to `git branch -d` that would prevent accidental deletion of a local branch if it’s the upstream of another local branch, breaking the dependency chain. The series, authored by Harald Nordgren, consists of two patches: a preparatory refactoring and the core logic implementing the protection. The idea has support in principle, but Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has raised a significant backward-compatibility concern—changing the long-standing behavior of `-d` could disrupt existing workflows.

The discussion has clarified that Junio’s earlier feedback was a brainstorm of possible approaches (blocking deletion or retargeting dependent branches) rather than an endorsement of the current implementation. Harald acknowledged this but hasn’t yet proposed a resolution. Junio’s latest email reaffirms his concern, emphasizing the need to balance protection with compatibility, but doesn’t suggest a specific alternative. The thread is now waiting for Harald to either revise the patch to address the compatibility issue or provide a stronger justification for the current design. No new version has been posted, and the series remains under review.
