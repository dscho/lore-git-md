This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing design discussion for Elijah Newren’s patch 2/2, which fixes a race condition in Git’s packed-object backend when a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a removed packfile. Elijah confirms that he has adopted Jeff King’s (Peff) latest refinement in the upcoming `v2` of the patch: the recovery mechanism will now **only trigger during `SECOND_READ`** (the retry phase after a refresh), rather than on the initial lookup. This change addresses Peff’s performance concern by ensuring that `QUICK` callers (like `git replay`) do not incur the cost of scanning MIDX-covered packs unless the object lookup fails and a refresh is attempted. The adjustment preserves the fix’s correctness while optimizing the common case where the object is in a newer, non-MIDX pack.

The email does not include new code or further rationale, but it signals that the tri-state design and `SECOND_READ`-gated fallback are now the agreed-upon approach. The remaining open questions—such as whether geometric repacking should adopt a grace period for redundant packfiles (per Derrick Stolee’s feedback) or how to optimize the scan order of MIDX-covered packs—are unaffected by this change. The patch is now one step closer to finalization, with no unresolved technical objections to the core fix. The next iteration (`v2`) will likely focus on integrating this refinement and addressing any remaining feedback from Patrick Steinhardt or Stolee.
