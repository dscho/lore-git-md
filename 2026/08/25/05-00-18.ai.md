This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Elijah Newren’s patch to improve `git push` performance from shallow clones. Elijah responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to enable `push.negotiate` by default (or conditionally for shallow clones) with a detailed rebuttal, arguing that negotiation alone does not fully solve the problem.

Elijah outlines four scenarios where `push.negotiate` fails to address the redundant tree-transfer issue: (a) shallow clones of rebased pull requests with sensitive data removed, (b) shallow clones pushed to unrelated repositories, (c) users who explicitly disable `push.negotiate`, and (d) setups where negotiation silently fails due to protocol limitations (e.g., SSH without `GIT_PROTOCOL=version=2` support, corporate policies, or Windows clients using plink/putty). He emphasizes that while `push.negotiate` helps in many cases, it does not eliminate the need for his patch, which directly targets the pack-generation logic in `send-pack`.

Elijah also acknowledges Patrick’s suggestion of a `push.negotiate=shallow` mode but remains unconvinced it fully resolves the problem. He suggests instead that `push.negotiate` could default to `true` for large repositories (not just shallow ones) but reiterates that his patch is still necessary for edge cases. Additionally, he mentions plans to split his follow-up work on shallow graft handling into two series: one for `git commit --amend` foot-guns and another for broader shallow-related fixes.

The discussion remains constructive, with both contributors exploring trade-offs between negotiation-based and pack-generation-based solutions. The key tension is between reducing latency (Patrick’s preference) and minimizing redundant data transfer (Elijah’s focus). The thread’s next steps will likely involve further debate over defaults and whether a hybrid approach is feasible.
