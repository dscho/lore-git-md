This is a review of a new feature patch introducing `git whoami`, a built-in command to display Git’s current identity and signing configuration. The reviewer, brian m. carlson, raises several surface-level and substantive points.

The review is **substantive** but not deeply technical. It focuses on design choices, usability, and code hygiene rather than algorithmic or correctness issues. The reviewer suggests improvements to the command’s output format (machine-readable output, possibly NUL-terminated), questions the use of `the_repository` (noting it should be avoided in favor of explicit repository arguments), and asks for clarification on X.509 key support. They also point out minor issues: the author’s name in the commit and sign-off should match their real name, and the motivation section should move to the commit message.

The reviewer is **neutral to mildly positive** on the utility of the command, acknowledging it could be useful but expressing no strong personal need for it. They invite broader community input on whether the feature is compelling enough for Git core. The patch itself is well-structured, but the review highlights areas where the design could be more robust (e.g., machine-readable output) or aligned with project conventions (e.g., avoiding `the_repository`). No fundamental objections are raised, but the discussion is just beginning.
