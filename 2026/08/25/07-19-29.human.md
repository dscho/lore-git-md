This is Elijah Newren’s **revised implementation** of the second patch in his two-patch bugfix series for the geometric repacking race condition. The patch now adopts Jeff King’s (Peff) suggested tri-state return value from `fill_midx_entry()` to distinguish between objects not in the MIDX, objects in the MIDX but unavailable (e.g., due to a missing packfile), and objects available in the MIDX. This replaces the original approach of embedding recovery logic directly in `find_pack_entry()` and instead shifts responsibility for handling missing packfiles to callers, avoiding an unnecessary `bsearch()` call in the recovery path.

The change is a direct response to Peff’s **substantive review**, which questioned the architectural placement of the fix and proposed the tri-state design as a cleaner alternative. Elijah’s adoption of this approach resolves the primary design concern raised in the thread, though the broader discussion about performance implications and the interaction with `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` callers remains open. The revised patch is likely to be more palatable to reviewers, as it aligns with Peff’s preference for caller-controlled recovery and avoids baking transient-failure handling into the backend.

The patch still targets the same core issue: a race condition where a MIDX references a packfile removed by geometric repacking, causing processes using the stale MIDX to fail to locate objects. The fix ensures that when an object’s recorded owner in the MIDX becomes unavailable, the backend scans the MIDX’s packs directly to recover the object, preserving correctness for all object lookups. The tri-state design makes this recovery more efficient by eliminating redundant lookups and clarifying the backend’s contract with callers.

This revision does not address Derrick Stolee’s **substantive feedback** about the existing `multi-pack-index expire` mechanism, which mitigates similar races by deferring packfile deletion until all processes have finished using them. Stolee’s suggestion—that geometric repacking could adopt a similar grace period—remains an open architectural question, though it is now somewhat orthogonal to the immediate fix. Similarly, the debate over whether the recovery logic should be tied to the `SECOND_READ` phase (as Peff later suggested) is not resolved here, but the tri-state design leaves the door open for such optimizations in the future.

The patch is part of a high-priority series that has already received **substantive reviews** from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt, with no remaining technical objections to the core fix. The revised implementation is likely to accelerate the series toward merging, though follow-up discussion may still refine the scan order or timing of the recovery logic. Elijah’s planned follow-up patches to address additional race-condition scenarios may also be affected by the outcome of these discussions.
