This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in Derrick Stolee’s seven-part bugfix and hardening series for the trace2 subsystem. Elijah Newren raises two technical concerns about the implementation of `banned-die.h`, the new enforcement mechanism introduced to block `die()`-triggering functions in trace2 code.

First, Elijah points out a **naming inconsistency**: the patch uses `banned(die)` instead of `BANNED(die)`, which deviates from Git’s existing convention (e.g., `NO_THE_REPOSITORY_H`, `NO_THE_HASH_ALGO`). This mismatch could lead to a confusing compiler error ("implicit declaration of function 'banned'") instead of the intended clear message ("sorry_die_is_a_banned_function"). The fix is trivial—uppercase the macro name—but the inconsistency risks undermining the header’s clarity and maintainability.

Second, Elijah identifies a **subtle ordering risk**: the patch adds `#include "banned-die.h"` in the middle of the include list in `trace2.c`. If future contributors add new includes *after* `banned-die.h`, any functions from those headers that later get banned would be silently ignored because the enforcement macro would already have been expanded. This could create a false sense of security, where banned functions appear to be blocked but are actually usable. The solution is to move `banned-die.h` to the *end* of the include list, ensuring it catches all prior declarations.

Neither issue questions the series’ overall goal—hardening trace2 against `die()`-triggering functions—but both are **architectural concerns** that could affect the mechanism’s long-term reliability. The review is focused and technical, engaging with the patch’s design rather than its motivation or scope. No objections to the broader series are raised, and the feedback is framed as a suggestion for improvement rather than a blocker. The tone is constructive, and the concerns are specific enough to be actionable in a v3.
