Andrew Pleeter’s `git whoami` patch, introducing a built-in command to display Git’s identity and signing configuration, is now under review by both the maintainer and the list. The initial version (v1) proposes a single command to consolidate what currently requires multiple `git config` or `git var` queries, targeting both interactive use and scripting with flags like `--author`, `--committer`, `--name`, and `--email`.

Junio C Hamano’s review raises two key concerns. First, he questions whether Git needs yet another subcommand, echoing brian m. carlson’s earlier skepticism about the feature’s necessity in core Git. Second, he flags a usability issue: the `--name` and `--email` flags override `--author` and `--committer`, creating an inconsistent experience where the latter two aren’t treated as equals. Junio suggests a more additive design, where flags combine predictably (e.g., `-a -e -n` for full author identity), aligning with Git’s existing conventions.

The patch itself touches `builtin/whoami.c`, adds tests, and avoids on-disk changes, but brian m. carlson’s prior review highlighted technical gaps: `the_repository` usage, missing X.509 signing key support, and a lack of machine-readable/NUL-terminated output for scripting. The thread’s focus has now shifted toward justifying the command’s inclusion and improving its flag behavior, with no decisions yet on merging. The series remains under review, with no indication of movement to `next` or `master`.
