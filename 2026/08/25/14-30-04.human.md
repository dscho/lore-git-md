This is the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s 10-patch series making Git’s object-database fsck checks pluggable. It is a small, focused refactoring that merges two nearly identical functions in `builtin/fsck.c`: `fsck_obj_buffer()` and `fsck_obj()`.

The patch eliminates redundancy by folding the logic of `fsck_obj()` into `fsck_obj_buffer()`. The only difference between the two was that `fsck_obj_buffer()` parsed the object buffer before calling `fsck_obj()`, and `fsck_obj()` had no other callers. The change also removes an unnecessary early return in `fsck_obj()` that checked for the `SEEN` flag, since the sole caller (`fsck_obj_buffer()`) already clears that flag before invoking it. The result is a single, clearer function that handles both parsing and verification.

The diff is confined to `builtin/fsck.c` and removes 27 lines while adding 20, for a net reduction of 7 lines. No behavior changes are introduced, and the patch is purely preparatory for later steps in the series that will move fsck logic into individual ODB backends. The commit message is concise and explains the motivation well. This looks like a clean, well-justified refactoring that should be uncontroversial.
