This is the sixth and final patch in brian m. carlson’s RFC series proposing a Git 3.0 breaking change to enforce lowercase-only hex object IDs. Junio C Hamano’s reply focuses narrowly on the test adjustment in `t5324-split-commit-graph.sh`, questioning the rationale behind changing the corruption character from "A" to "b" in one case and to "a" in another.

The confusion stems from the test’s dual purpose: it must verify that `git commit-graph verify` fails when the commit-graph chain is corrupted, but in SHA-256 mode, the "correct" (uncorrupted) character at position 70 happens to be "a". If the test corrupts that position to "A", the command would fail *not* because of the corruption, but because the series now rejects uppercase hex. The change to "b" ensures the test fails for the intended reason (corruption) rather than the new enforcement. The first hunk (changing "A" to "a") is a no-op in terms of the test’s logic, as both characters are valid lowercase hex, but it aligns the test with the series’ lowercase-only requirement.

Junio’s review is surface-level, addressing only the test’s clarity rather than the patch’s substance or the series’ broader design. The question is procedural: the test adjustment is correct, but the commit message could better explain why the specific characters were chosen. This does not challenge the series’ motivation or implementation, but it highlights a minor documentation gap that brian m. carlson may want to address in a future revision. The thread’s larger open questions—whether the project will accept the breaking change, and whether Junio’s proposed compromise (internal normalization) is viable—remain unresolved.
