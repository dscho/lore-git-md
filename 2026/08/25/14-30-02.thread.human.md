Patrick Steinhardt’s ten-patch series refactoring Git’s object integrity verification (fsck) to make it pluggable per ODB backend is now in its second week of review. The goal is to let each backend—loose, packed, multi-pack, etc.—implement its own consistency checks, paving the way for future pluggable storage systems. No user-visible behavior changes are intended; the series is pure infrastructure work, though it does fix a long-standing discrepancy in the `--full` flag’s documented versus actual behavior (patch 4/10) and a latent progress-meter bug in loose-object verification (patch 10/10). Karthik Nayak has reviewed the entire series and found only minor editorial nits, signaling general approval without raising substantive technical concerns. The series depends on two earlier topics (`ps/odb-eagerly-load-alternates` and `ps/odb-pluggable-pack-generation`) and remains under review, with no merge decisions yet. The only open question—whether `OPT_BIT` fully replicates `OPT_BOOL`’s `--no-full` semantics—was raised by Karthik in an earlier review but hasn’t been addressed by Patrick. All other feedback has been surface-level, leaving the series’s technical direction uncontested.
