This thread is Patrick Steinhardt’s ten-patch series refactoring Git’s object integrity verification (fsck) to make it pluggable per ODB backend. The goal is to prepare the codebase for future pluggable ODB backends—like reftable or alternate storage systems—without changing any user-visible behavior. The series is now complete with v2, posted on 26 August 2026, and addresses all feedback from the first iteration.

The refactoring moves fsck checks out of `builtin/fsck.c` and into their respective backends: loose objects into `odb/source-loose.c`, packfiles, reverse indices, bitmaps, and multi-pack indices into `odb/source-packed.c`. Along the way, it fixes a long-standing discrepancy in the `--full` flag’s behavior (ensuring it controls loose objects in alternates as documented) and a latent progress-meter bug in loose-object verification. The scaffolding for pluggable checks is now fully in place, with `odb_fsck()` as the sole entry point for object verification.

Karthik Nayak reviewed the series, focusing on minor wording and spelling nits in commit messages and diffs. His only substantive question—about `OPT_BIT` versus `OPT_BOOL` for the `--full` flag—was resolved by Patrick, who confirmed equivalent `--no-full` functionality. No deep technical objections or edge-case concerns were raised. The series depends on two earlier topics (`ps/odb-eagerly-load-alternates` and `ps/odb-pluggable-pack-generation`) and is based on Junio’s integration branch as of 24 August 2026.
