Patrick Steinhardt’s ten-patch series to make Git’s object integrity checks (fsck) pluggable for each ODB backend is under active review, with the first substantive technical feedback now in. The series is a pure refactoring that prepares the codebase for future pluggable ODB backends—such as reftable or alternate storage systems—without changing user-visible behavior. Karthik Nayak has begun reviewing the patches, starting with the foundational fifth patch, which introduces the infrastructure for pluggable fsck checks: a new `fsck` callback in the ODB source vtable, empty stubs for all existing source types, and the `odb_fsck_options` structure with the `ODB_FSCK_FULL` flag.

Karthik’s review engages with the patch’s design, asking about the choice of `OPT_BIT` over `OPT_BOOL` for the `--full` flag (it works the same way via bitwise negation) and confirming that the new `odb_fsck()` helper will centralize the fsck logic before it’s moved into backend-specific callbacks. His feedback is observational rather than critical, noting that the scaffolding “looks good” and aligns with the patch’s goals. Earlier reviews of patches 1–4 were surface-level, focusing on minor wording or code organization without engaging with technical correctness or edge cases. The series remains in its early review phase, with no open questions or objections yet raised. It depends on two earlier topics (`ps/odb-eagerly-load-alternates` and `ps/odb-pluggable-pack-generation`) and is based on Junio’s integration branch as of late August.
