This is the final patch in Derrick Stolee’s seven-part **bugfix and hardening series** for the trace2 subsystem, completing the elimination of all `die()`-triggering helpers by removing `xcalloc()`. The series as a whole addresses a critical stability issue: trace2, a low-level telemetry layer, must never crash Git—even under memory pressure or system call failures.

This patch replaces `xcalloc()` in `tr2tls_create_self()` with `calloc()` and introduces a **statically allocated fallback context** (`tr2tls_thread_fallback`) that uses the thread name `"unknown"` to signal allocation failure. When allocation fails, the code gracefully degrades by returning this fallback context instead of crashing. The new `tr2tls_is_fallback()` helper allows callers to detect and handle the fallback state, ensuring no modifications are attempted on the read-only fallback context. The patch also updates all trace2 counter and timer functions (`tr2_ctr.c`, `tr2_tmr.c`) to short-circuit when operating on a fallback context, preserving process stability at the cost of degraded telemetry (e.g., dropped counter/timer events).

The change is **self-contained to the trace2 subsystem** and touches five files: `banned-die.h` (updated to block `xcalloc()`), `trace2/tr2_ctr.c`, `trace2/tr2_tls.c`, `trace2/tr2_tls.h`, and `trace2/tr2_tmr.c`. The fallback mechanism is simple but effective, avoiding complex error propagation while ensuring the process remains alive. The patch maintains the existing interface and does not alter on-disk formats or user-visible behavior—only telemetry consumers may notice missing or malformed data under failure conditions, which is an intentional trade-off.

This patch, like the rest of the series, **lacks test coverage** due to the difficulty of reproducing transient allocation failures, but the hardening is well-motivated and aligns with the thread’s agreed goal of prioritizing stability over telemetry completeness. The introduction of `banned-die.h` in the first patch and its incremental updates (here blocking `xcalloc()`) signal an architectural shift toward systematic enforcement of "no `die()` in trace2," which could extend to other low-level subsystems in the future.

No objections or technical concerns have been raised in the thread so far, and the series remains on track for review. The only remaining loose end is the **MinGW-specific follow-up** (Taylor Blau’s alternative to disable `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection for `xsnprintf()`), which Derrick confirms will be implemented separately. This patch completes the hardening effort, leaving trace2 resilient to allocation failures across all platforms.
