This is a **design discussion reply** in the ongoing ODB alternates refactoring series. Jeff King (Peff) and Patrick Steinhardt reach a clear consensus on patch 1’s design: the current case-insensitive deduplication logic should be dropped entirely in favor of simpler, case-sensitive path comparisons.

Peff reinforces his earlier critique, dismissing the risk of regressions as negligible. He argues that duplicate alternates (whether case-varied or otherwise) are vanishingly rare in practice, limited to manually edited alternates files or specialized scripts like those used by forges. Even in those cases, he suggests the impact would be minimal: Git would treat the duplicates as distinct, avoiding correctness issues at the cost of a minor performance hit (e.g., redundant object lookups). He cites GitHub’s and kernel.org’s historical usage as evidence that real-world alternates configurations are simple chains, not complex trees or loops.

Patrick concedes the point, acknowledging that `stat()`-based deduplication (Peff’s proposed alternative) is ineffective on Windows due to unreliable `st_ino`/`st_dev` values. With no robust cross-platform solution available, both agree that unconditional case-sensitive matching is the only viable simplification. The discussion now hinges on whether this change will be made in a v3 revision or as a follow-up, but the technical direction is settled.

This reply advances the thread toward resolution, removing the last substantive obstacle to the series’ integration. The remaining open question is procedural: will Junio Hamano accept the series as-is (with a `NEEDSWORK` note) or require a v3 to implement the agreed simplification? The performance implications of eager alternate loading (e.g., for `git fsck`) and edge-case handling (e.g., repositories with thousands of alternates) remain unaddressed but are now secondary to the design consensus.
