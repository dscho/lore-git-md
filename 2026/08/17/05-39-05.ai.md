This is a **design discussion reply** in the ongoing ODB alternates refactoring series. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King’s critique of patch 1’s case-sensitivity handling, engaging with the trade-offs between correctness, performance, and complexity.

Patrick acknowledges the performance risk of duplicate alternates but downplays other concerns, noting that housekeeping operations (e.g., `git gc`) should remain unaffected since they only consider the primary ODB source. He agrees that `core.ignoreCase` is an imperfect proxy for path equivalence, citing symlinks and filesystem-specific behaviors as additional complications. While he initially suggests `realpath(3p)` as a theoretical improvement, he concedes its limitations (e.g., inconsistent case canonicalization across platforms).

On Jeff’s proposal to drop case-insensitivity entirely, Patrick accepts the trade-off, arguing that pathological alternate configurations (e.g., multiple case-varied paths) are user errors. However, he raises a practical obstacle to Jeff’s alternative `stat()`-based deduplication: Windows filesystems often lack reliable `st_ino`/`st_dev` values, making this approach ineffective where case-insensitivity is most common. This leaves case-sensitive matching as the only viable simplification, which Patrick now appears ready to adopt.

The reply advances the discussion toward a consensus: the current patch’s complexity is unjustified, and a simpler case-sensitive approach is preferable. No revised implementation is posted yet, but the technical rationale for patch 1’s design is now clearly undermined. The unresolved question is whether this change will be made as a follow-up or as a v3 revision.
