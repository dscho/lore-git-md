This is a **substantive technical follow-up** in the packfile performance regression thread, focusing on the quadratic-time inefficiency in `packfile_list_prepend()`. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s observation that `prepend()` suffers from the same performance issue as `append()`, but clarifies that the MRU (most-recently-used) adjustment in `find_pack_entry()`—a key `prepend()` caller—is not the root cause of the observed slowdowns. Peff’s analysis reveals that the quadratic growth in `git rev-list --count` with many packs is inherent to pack lookup, not list management, and that the MRU optimization already mitigates the worst-case behavior for `prepend()`.

The email introduces a **new synthetic test** demonstrating the quadratic scaling of `git rev-list --count` with up to 16,000 packs, but Peff cautions that this test primarily measures lookup overhead rather than list manipulation. His key insight is that the MRU adjustment in `find_pack_entry()` avoids the need for a hashmap-based duplicate check in `prepend()`, as the doubly-linked list structure allows O(1) removal when the pack is already at the front. This undermines the case for Patrick’s proposed hashmap refactoring, at least for the `prepend()` use case, and suggests that the existing fast-path fix (now in `next`) is sufficient for the immediate regression.
