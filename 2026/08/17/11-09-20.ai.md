This is a refactoring patch series targeting the object database (ODB) subsystem, specifically the handling of alternate object databases. The v3 revision of Patrick Steinhardt’s series now consists of five patches (up from four in v2) and addresses the key design and timing concerns raised during the v2 review.

The core goal remains the same: remove lazy-loading of alternates to simplify the codebase and prepare for future work moving alternates into the "files" backend. The series now includes a new first patch that reorders repository initialization to ensure the object database is created *after* the full repository configuration is written, fixing a correctness issue with `core.ignoreCase` lookups. This change directly addresses Jeff King’s concern about the timing of configuration loading and eliminates the need for Patrick’s earlier proposal to cherry-pick a patch from a later series.

Patch 2/5 (formerly 1/4) has been revised to retain case-insensitive deduplication of alternates but now documents the complexity as a `NEEDSWORK` and explicitly calls out the dubious value of handling `core.ignoreCase` in this context. The commit message now acknowledges the consensus that this mechanism may be removed in the future, aligning with Jeff King and Junio Hamano’s preference for simplicity. The patch also migrates from `khash` to `hashmap` to support dynamic comparison functions, a change that was previously debated but is now justified as necessary for decoupling from `the_repository`.

Patches 3–5 (formerly 2–4) are unchanged from v2: they eagerly initialize alternates during ODB setup, remove the `loaded_alternates` field, and drop the `alternates_db` field, respectively. The series touches 13 files, with a net reduction of 1 line of code (91 insertions, 92 deletions). Key symbols removed include `odb_prepare_alternates()` (usage eliminated; made static) and the `loaded_alternates`/`alternates_db` fields.

The series is built on top of `ps/odb-make-creation-pluggable` and is part of Patrick’s broader ODB abstraction effort. It is queued in Junio’s `seen` branch, though the v3 revision may require re-queuing. The changes are mechanical and well-motivated, with no intended user-visible behavior changes, though the removal of lazy-loading could affect performance in edge cases (e.g., repositories with thousands of alternates). The new first patch is a critical correctness fix, and the revised second patch reflects the design consensus reached in the v2 discussion. The series looks ready for integration, pending any new feedback on the v3 changes.
