This is a follow-up in the ongoing ODB alternates refactoring series, specifically about the design of the first patch. Patrick Steinhardt now proposes to **defer the simplification of case-sensitivity handling** entirely, leaving the current behavior unchanged for now. He argues that while the refactoring itself is sound, the risk of regressions (particularly performance-related) outweighs the benefits of removing the complexity.

The original patch decoupled alternate path comparisons from `the_repository` but retained case-insensitive deduplication via `core.ignoreCase`. Jeff King (Peff) and Junio Hamano had previously argued for dropping case-insensitivity entirely, accepting occasional duplicates as a low-risk trade-off. Patrick initially agreed but now reverses course, citing unresolved concerns about performance (e.g., `git fsck` with thousands of alternates) and the lack of a reliable cross-platform deduplication mechanism (e.g., `stat()`-based checks fail on Windows). He suggests documenting the issue as `NEEDSWORK` instead of addressing it in this series.

This shift leaves the series’ core goal intact—removing lazy-loading of alternates—but punts the case-sensitivity question to a future effort. The patch remains queued in Junio’s `seen` branch, but this discussion may delay its progression to `next` until the design is finalized. For readers tracking the ODB abstraction work, this is a notable pivot: the series is now purely a cleanup, not a behavioral change.
