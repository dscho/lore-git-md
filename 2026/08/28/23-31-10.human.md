This is Junio’s biweekly “What’s cooking in git.git” report for August 2026, summarizing the state of 50+ in-flight topics.

The most notable graduation is the **reftable optimization** (kn/reftable-optimize-reloading) that landed in `next` and will soon reach `master`.  It cuts the number of `newfstatat` syscalls from linear to constant when writing refs, a measurable win for high-ref-count repositories.

Two small but user-visible changes are also on track: `git worktree add` now refuses to DWIM when `-b` or `-B` is given (yn/worktree-add-no-dwim-with-b), and the deprecated-command warning has been reworded to be more assertive (jc/you-still-use-that).

On the stalled front, **git-svn migration noise** (ws/squelch-svn-migrate) is the only new topic that needs review; the rest of the stalled list is unchanged from previous reports.  The **ODB abstraction** series (ps/odb-alternates-at-creation and ps/odb-pluggable-fsck) continue to receive feedback; they are the most ambitious refactoring currently cooking.

The **experimental `git history squash`** subcommand (hn/history-squash) is still iterating, as is the **external diff driver hunk provider** (mm/diff-process-hunks), which promises to let tools like language servers feed Git their notion of changed lines while leaving all formatting to Git.

Finally, the **Git 3.0 preparatory work** (bc/restrict-hex-to-lowercase) is still waiting for a reroll; it will enforce lowercase-only object IDs in the breaking-changes mode.
