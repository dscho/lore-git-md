This is the **fourth patch in a five-patch bugfix series** targeting the merge machinery. It extends Git's existing protection against unsafe `git commit --amend` operations during conflict resolution to cover `git am`, `git revert`, and all forms of `git rebase` (both apply and merge backends). The patch is **technically sound**, **well-tested**, and **uncontroversial** at this stage.

The patch introduces a new `sequencer_ongoing_operation()` helper (defined in patch 1/5) to detect ongoing operations that record their results as new commits on top of HEAD, during which amending HEAD is almost always a mistake. For `git am`, `git revert`, and the apply backend of `git rebase`, the check is straightforward: if the operation is in progress, `git commit --amend` is blocked. For the merge backend of `git rebase`, the logic is more nuanced to avoid interfering with interactive rebase directives (`squash`, `reword`, `edit`, `break`). The patch uses the presence of `rebase-merge/stopped-sha` (indicating a genuine stop) and the absence of `rebase-merge/amend` (indicating a conflicted stop) to distinguish between conflict resolution and clean interactive rebase stops.

The commit message is **detailed and clear**, explaining the rationale, the edge cases, and the on-disk state used to make the determination. The patch touches `builtin/commit.c`, `sequencer.c`, and `sequencer.h`, and adds **comprehensive test coverage** in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh`, `t3507-cherry-pick-conflict.sh`, and `t4151-am-abort.sh`. The tests verify that `git commit --amend` is correctly blocked during conflict resolution for all relevant commands while remaining allowed for clean interactive rebase stops.

This patch is **ready for final review** and likely to proceed to `next`. The preparatory patches (1-2/5) have already addressed Junio C Hamano's mechanical feedback, and the remaining patches (3-5/5) are narrowly focused on extending the existing protection logic. The series as a whole addresses a real usability hazard and aligns with the project's ongoing efforts to improve safety during conflict resolution.
