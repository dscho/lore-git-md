This is the final patch (5/5) in Elijah Newren’s **bugfix series** extending Git’s protection against unsafe commit operations during conflict resolution. It blocks partial commits (`git commit <paths>`) during conflict resolution for `git am`, `git revert`, and all forms of `git rebase`, mirroring the existing protection for merges and cherry-picks. The patch reuses the `sequencer_ongoing_operation()` helper introduced earlier in the series to detect ongoing operations and distinguish between conflict stops (where partial commits are unsafe) and clean rebase stops (where they are permitted). Test coverage is added for all newly protected commands, including both the apply and merge backends of `git rebase`.

The patch is **technically sound** and addresses a real usability hazard: partial commits during conflict resolution can corrupt repository state by recording only a subset of the intended resolution. The logic is narrowly scoped to conflict resolution, deliberately allowing partial commits during clean rebase stops (e.g., when a pick becomes empty). The error messages are clear and specific to each operation. The patch aligns with the series’ overall goal of preventing foot-guns without restricting legitimate workflows.

This patch, like the rest of the series, is **ready for final review** and likely to proceed to `next`. The preparatory patches (1-2/5) have already addressed Junio’s and Phillip Wood’s feedback, and the substantive technical question about partial commits for empty commits was resolved in favor of allowing them. The only remaining loose end is Junio’s stylistic preference for a less verbose error message, but this is unlikely to block progress. The series as a whole is a well-motivated, uncontroversial improvement to Git’s safety mechanisms.
