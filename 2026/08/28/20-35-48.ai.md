This is a bugfix patch targeting the pathspec handling in `git ls-files` and `git add`. The issue is that when calculating the common prefix of positive pathspecs, Git incorrectly strips that prefix from *all* pathspecs—including negative ones (exclusions). This can cause negative pathspecs to degenerate into empty strings (matching everything) or, worse, read out of bounds when the negative pathspec is shorter than the prefix.

The patch adds a simple guard in `do_match_pathspec()` to skip prefix-stripping for negative pathspecs (`exclude` flag). The fix is minimal (11 lines in `dir.c`) and includes three new tests in `t/t6132-pathspec-exclude.sh` that cover edge cases: negative pathspecs matching the prefix length, shorter than the prefix, and the interaction with `git add` (which handles trailing slashes differently). The commit message clearly traces the bug to its introduction in 2013 (ef79b1f870) and references a prior report by Thomas Haller.

The patch looks well-motivated and narrowly scoped. The tests are thorough and demonstrate the problem and fix concretely. No controversy is evident, and the change aligns with the principle that negative pathspecs should not be affected by positive-path optimizations. The author (Diogo Castro) and reporter (Thomas Haller) are not frequent contributors, but the patch follows project conventions and addresses a real, user-visible bug.
