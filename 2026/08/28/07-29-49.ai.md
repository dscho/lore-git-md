This is the **final patch in Elijah Newren’s three-patch bugfix series** addressing a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. The patch implements the core recovery logic for cases where a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a packfile that has been removed by a concurrent repack, causing processes like `git replay` to crash or report missing objects. The fix introduces a tri-state return value (`MIDX_FILL_MISS`, `MIDX_FILL_HIT`, `MIDX_FILL_OWNER_UNAVAILABLE`) for `fill_midx_entry()` and gates the recovery fallback on `OBJECT_INFO_SECOND_READ` to optimize performance for `QUICK` callers.

The email is a **reply to Jeff King (Peff)’s latest review** of the patch, which raised three minor points: (1) the unchanged name of `fill_midx_entry()` despite its new tri-state semantics, (2) the potential redundancy of the `stale_packs_detected` flag (now moot after the previous patch 3/4 was dropped), and (3) a superfluous comment rewrite. Elijah acknowledges all three points—agreeing to rename the function (though no specific alternative is settled), confirming the flag is no longer needed, and conceding the comment rewrite was unnecessary. The exchange is **collaborative and converging**, with no unresolved technical disagreements. Peff’s feedback is framed as preferences rather than blockers, and the `SECOND_READ`-gated recovery logic is explicitly endorsed as correct.

Key technical details remain unchanged from the v2 summary: the patch touches `odb/source-packed.c` and `midx.c`/`midx.h`, introduces no on-disk format changes, and adds test coverage for MIDX recovery scenarios. The series is now **rebased on `ps/odb-generic-corrupt-objects`** and simplified by the removal of the earlier patch 3/4, which addressed `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` misuse in `git mktree --batch`. The remaining open questions are cosmetic (function naming, comment wording) and do not affect correctness or performance. The patch is **effectively merge-ready**, pending Elijah’s response to the naming suggestion and any final adjustments to the commit message. The broader architectural debate—whether geometric repacking should adopt a grace period for redundant packfiles to reduce race frequency—remains a follow-up discussion.
