This is a **bugfix** patch series (5 patches) targeting the merge machinery. Elijah Newren extends Git's existing protections against unsafe commit operations during conflict resolution to cover `git commit --amend` and `git commit <paths>` for all operations that can produce conflicts: `git am`, `git revert`, and every variant of `git rebase`. The goal is to prevent users from accidentally amending the wrong commit or committing only a subset of paths while resolving conflicts, which can corrupt repository state.

The series is now in its third iteration (v3) and incorporates feedback from Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood. Two new preparatory patches address Junio’s request to clarify naming (`FROM_REBASE_PICK` → `FROM_REBASE_EMPTY_PICK`, `is_from_rebase()` → `is_from_rebase_empty()`) and resolve the **substantive technical question** about whether blocking partial commits for empty commits is necessary. The second patch explicitly allows partial commits when a rebase pick becomes empty, acknowledging that this case does not pose the same risk as conflict resolution. The error message for amending an empty commit has also been reworded for clarity ("The now-empty commit has been dropped -- cannot amend.").

The core logic remains unchanged: the series introduces `sequencer_ongoing_operation()` to consolidate detection of ongoing operations, distinguishing between conflict stops (where amending or partial commits are unsafe) and clean interactive rebase stops (where they are permitted). Test coverage is thorough, spanning `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh`, `t3507-cherry-pick-conflict.sh`, and `t4151-am-abort.sh`. The series is well-motivated, carefully scoped, and addresses a real usability hazard. The remaining open question—whether the error message phrasing could be further improved—is minor and unlikely to block progress.

This looks like a **useful and uncontroversial improvement** to Git’s UI. The preparatory patches and resolution of the empty-commit question make v3 a stronger, more coherent version of the series. It is ready for final review and likely to be merged soon.
