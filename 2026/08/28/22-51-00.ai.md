This is a follow-up in the architectural discussion about Git’s built-in command interfaces, specifically clarifying the meaning of "libification" in response to D. Ben Knoble’s earlier question. Junio C Hamano corrects his own earlier phrasing, distinguishing between two distinct design flaws: (1) the "cheating" pattern where new commands like `git switch` and `git restore` were bolted onto `git checkout` via a monolithic `checkout_main()` helper, and (2) the deeper anti-pattern of treating `cmd_foo()` functions as reusable entry points when they are actually one-time orchestrators (like `main()` in ordinary programs) that perform initialization and finalization unsuitable for repeated calls.

Junio redefines the libification goal: instead of calling `cmd_switch()` from another command, the project should refactor reusable logic out of `cmd_checkout()` into standalone helper functions that accept a `struct repository *` parameter. These helpers can then be called by both `cmd_checkout()` and new consumers (e.g., `cmd_switch()` or external programs), placing all callers on equal footing. This approach aligns with the broader architectural shift introduced by Junio’s patch, which replaces the `struct repository *` parameter in built-in commands with a `bool has_repo` flag to prevent segfaults and clarify that built-ins are not intended for direct reuse.

The clarification does not alter the technical direction but sharpens the rationale for the `bool has_repo` design and the redirection of the `the_repository` removal effort toward utility code outside `builtin/`. It also implicitly addresses the open question about follow-up work: the next step is to extract reusable logic from built-in commands into libified helpers, a task that may require coordination with contributors like René Scharfe and Patrick Steinhardt. No new objections or alternatives are raised; the focus remains on community consensus for Junio’s proposal and the mechanical implications of the architectural shift.
