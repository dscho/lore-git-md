This is Jeff King (Peff)'s **substantive review** of the now-dropped patch 3/4 in Elijah Newren’s four-patch bugfix series addressing a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. The series targets crashes in `git replay` and missing-object errors caused by MIDX-packfile mismatches.

Peff’s review dissects the patch’s core assumptions and separates three distinct concerns: (1) whether the "packfile ... index unavailable" log message is misleading or useful, (2) whether `git mktree --batch` is racily broken due to its use of `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK`, and (3) whether `QUICK`’s current behavior—skipping reprepare even when an object was known to exist—is a worthwhile optimization tradeoff. He argues that the log message is often noise, that `mktree --batch` misuses `QUICK`, and that the patch’s architectural approach (forcing reprepare under `QUICK`) is misplaced. Peff suggests three separate patches to address each concern independently, but ultimately endorses Elijah’s plan to **drop the patch entirely** and instead fix `mktree --batch` by removing `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` while preserving `SKIP_FETCH_OBJECT`. This change will allow `mktree` to recover via the normal reprepare mechanism, eliminating the need for the `stale_packs_detected` flag and the associated `packfile.c` changes. The `.idx`/`.pack` race condition Peff identified in `pack_index_is_missing()` will also disappear as a side effect.

Key technical details: the review clarifies that `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK`’s contract is to optimize for genuine misses, not to recover from races, and that `mktree --batch`’s 2022 rewrite (commit `817b0f6027`) misapplied the flag. Peff’s feedback directly led to the patch’s removal and the targeted fix for `mktree` in the series’ v3. The review also surfaces a follow-up question about whether the log message should be demoted to trace2 or reworded to better reflect the recovery mechanism. No unresolved technical disagreements remain; the thread is converging.
