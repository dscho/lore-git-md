This is a refactoring patch discussion in the ongoing `die_for_incompatible_opts()` helper series. René Scharfe joins the thread with a technical proposal to address Jeff King’s concern about compile-time safety in the varargs interface.

René suggests two ways to retain compile-time checking while keeping the varargs flexibility. The first is a macro-based approach that enforces both `EOF` and `NULL` termination, effectively guaranteeing the argument list is properly terminated by construction. The second is a more elaborate type-safe variant using a compound literal of `struct used_option` to ensure every argument is a name/flag pair. Both designs preserve the existing call sites while making the helper safer to use.

The proposal is narrowly scoped: it does not change the error-message formatting, the set of callers, or the overall goal of replacing the fixed-arity family with a single varargs function. It is a direct response to Peff’s objection that the current `EOF`-only design sacrifices compile-time protection. René’s examples are concrete and ready to drop into the codebase, but he does not advocate for either approach over the other—he simply lays out the options. The ball is now back in Junio’s court to decide whether the added complexity is worth the safety gain.
