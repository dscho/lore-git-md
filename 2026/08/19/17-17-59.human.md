This is Junio C Hamano's review of the second patch in Karthik Nayak's three-patch series optimizing reftable stack reloads during transactions. The series targets a performance bottleneck in the reftable backend where excessive `fstat()` calls during bulk reference operations were identified by Jeff King.

Junio raises a safety concern about the patch's refactoring: moving the list lock from `struct reftable_addition` to `struct reftable_stack`. His question centers on whether the API provides sufficient protection against nested or concurrent additions on the same stack. He points out that if a caller already holds a lock (after calling `reftable_stack_init_addition()`) and then attempts to open another `reftable_addition` on the same stack, the second call to `flock_acquire(add->stack->list_lock)` would fail because the lock is now shared across all additions on that stack. This could lead to premature lock release in `reftable_addition_close()`, potentially disrupting other operations on the same stack.

The concern is substantive, touching on thread safety and API design rather than implementation details. Junio does not object to the optimization goal but wants assurance that the refactoring does not introduce new failure modes. The patch itself is mechanical and preparatory for the final optimization in patch 3, but this review suggests the change may need additional safeguards or documentation to prevent misuse. No resolution is proposed in this email; the ball is in Karthik's court to address the safety question.
