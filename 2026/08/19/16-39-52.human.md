This is the second patch in Karthik Nayak’s three-part series optimizing reftable stack reloads during transactions. The patch moves the list lock from `struct reftable_addition` into `struct reftable_stack`, a mechanical refactoring that enables the performance optimization in patch 3. Justin Tobler’s review confirms the change is sound: the lock logically belongs in the stack structure, and the relocation allows future optimizations by making the lock state accessible to read paths operating on `reftable_stack`.

The patch itself is straightforward, updating field references and removing an unused header include. No behavior changes are introduced yet, but the relocation is necessary for patch 3’s core optimization—skipping redundant stack reloads when the stack is already locked. The review is brief but substantive, focusing on the conceptual correctness of the lock’s new location rather than surface-level details.

This series targets a real performance bottleneck identified by Jeff King, where excessive `fstat()` calls during bulk ref creation were consuming significant time. The optimization is well-motivated and appears technically solid, with updated unit tests validating the new locking logic. The patch is part of the ongoing reftable backend work led by Patrick Steinhardt and Karthik Nayak, and the changes are confined to the reftable subsystem with no on-disk format modifications.
