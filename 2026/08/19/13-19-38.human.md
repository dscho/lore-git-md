This is the second patch in Karthik Nayak’s three-part series optimizing reftable stack reloads during transactions. The patch moves the list-file lock from `struct reftable_addition` into `struct reftable_stack` itself, enabling the follow-up optimization (patch 3) that skips redundant stack reloads when the stack is already locked.

The change is mechanical: the `reftable_flock` field is relocated, and all call sites in `reftable/stack.c` are updated to reference the new location. A small cleanup removes an unused `#include` from `reftable/stack.h`. The commit message explains the motivation clearly: the lock’s scope belongs with the stack, not the transient addition object, and this refactoring unlocks future optimizations that would otherwise be missed in read paths.

This patch is a straightforward refactoring with no behavior change on its own. It looks well-crafted, with a clean diff and a clear rationale. The real payoff comes in patch 3, but this step is necessary and uncontroversial.
