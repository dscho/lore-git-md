This is a substantive review of the final patch in Karthik Nayak's three-part series optimizing reftable stack reloads during transactions. The series targets the reftable backend, specifically reducing redundant `fstat()` calls that scale linearly with ref count during bulk operations.

Justin Tobler's review engages directly with the implementation: he proposes an alternative approach to avoid changing the `stack_uptodate()` function signature by skipping the call entirely when the stack is locked, rather than adding a `skip_if_locked` parameter. This suggestion preserves the existing API while achieving the same optimization goal. The review is technical and focused, addressing the patch's core logic without questioning its motivation or broader design.

The patch itself is a well-scoped performance optimization, building on the earlier refactoring in patches 1 and 2. It reduces `newfstatat()` syscalls from O(n) to a constant ~55, yielding a modest but consistent 1-2% clock-time improvement in `git-update-ref` benchmarks. The change is confined to `reftable/stack.c` and does not affect the on-disk format or other Git subsystems. Justin's feedback is the first substantive review of this patch, and his alternative approach—if adopted—would simplify the implementation while maintaining the same performance benefits.

The series as a whole is motivated by Jeff King's profiling data and addresses a real bottleneck in the reftable backend. Earlier patches in the series (removing a redundant flag and relocating the lock state) have already received positive feedback from Justin, suggesting the foundation is solid. This final patch completes the optimization and appears ready for iteration based on the review.
