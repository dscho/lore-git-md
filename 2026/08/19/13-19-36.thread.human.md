This thread is about a three-patch series from Karthik Nayak that optimizes the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The goal is to address a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls were slowing down bulk ref creation. The series is under review, with no prior versions, and touches the reftable stack implementation in `refs/reftable-backend.c` and related files.

The key change is reducing `newfstatat()` syscalls from O(n) to a constant ~55 calls, regardless of ref count, by skipping redundant reloads when the stack is locked during a transaction. Jeff King’s independent benchmarks confirmed a consistent 25% speedup across ref counts from 10,000 to 50,000, a larger improvement than initially reported due to differences in test methodology. The series has received substantive reviews from Justin Tobler and Junio C Hamano, with minor cosmetic feedback from Patrick Steinhardt. All technical concerns—including a safety issue about lock relocation and premature release—have been resolved in principle, though some implementation details (like per-addition lock tracking) remain open for discussion.

Karthik has agreed to address Patrick’s naming suggestion in a separate commit and has offered to update libgit2 for compatibility. The only remaining loose ends are minor: whether to embed lock-checking logic in `stack_uptodate()` (Karthik’s preference) or expose it to callers (Justin’s alternative), and the final implementation of per-addition lock tracking. The series is uncontroversial in its goals and performance impact, with no objections to merging once these details are settled.
