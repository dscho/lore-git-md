This thread is about a performance optimization in Git's reftable backend, specifically eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The series, now at v2, was authored by Karthik Nayak and addresses a bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls were slowing down bulk ref creation.

The key change reduces `newfstatat()` syscalls from scaling linearly with ref count to a constant ~55 calls, delivering a consistent 15-20% performance improvement. This was confirmed by Jeff King (Peff), who independently benchmarked the series and found a 25% speedup across ref counts from 10,000 to 50,000. The discrepancy between Peff's results and the original 1-2% improvement was traced to test methodology—using `refs/tags/` (which triggers extra stack reloads) instead of `refs/heads/`.

All substantive feedback from v1 has been addressed. Junio C Hamano's safety concern about premature lock release was resolved with per-addition lock tracking, validated by a new unit test. Patrick Steinhardt's cautionary feedback about lock state tracking was acknowledged, but no concrete alternative was proposed. The series is technically complete, with Peff endorsing the core idea and Justin Tobler and Patrick Steinhardt offering no objections. The only remaining step is Junio's final assessment.
