This thread is a three-patch series from Karthik Nayak aimed at optimizing reftable stack reloads during reference transactions. The series targets a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls—scaling linearly with the number of references—were consuming significant time during bulk ref creation. The final patch, just posted, implements the core optimization: skipping redundant stack reloads when the stack is already locked, reducing `newfstatat()` syscalls from linear growth to a constant ~55 calls regardless of ref count. Benchmarks show a modest but consistent 1-2% clock-time improvement in `git-update-ref` operations.

The series is now complete and under review. Karthik frames it as a targeted, mechanical optimization motivated by Jeff King’s profiling data. The first two patches laid the groundwork by removing a redundant flag and relocating the list lock from `reftable_addition` to `reftable_stack`, while the third patch introduces the actual optimization. The changes are confined to the reftable backend (`refs/reftable-backend.c`, `reftable/stack.{c,h}`) and unit tests, with no impact on the on-disk format or other Git subsystems. No objections or open questions have been raised yet, and the series appears self-contained and ready for feedback. Jeff King, who identified the bottleneck, has not yet reviewed the patches.
