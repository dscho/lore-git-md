This thread is about a three-patch series from Karthik Nayak that optimizes the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The goal is to address a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls were slowing down bulk ref creation. The series is under active review, with no prior versions posted.

The key change in the final patch reduces `newfstatat()` syscalls from scaling linearly with ref count to a constant ~55 calls, regardless of ref volume. Karthik initially reported a 1-2% performance improvement, but Jeff King (Peff) has now shared benchmark results showing a consistent 25% speedup across ref counts from 10,000 to 50,000. The discrepancy appears to stem from differences in test methodology, but both sets of data confirm the optimization’s effectiveness. Peff’s results also demonstrate the improvement scales well across workloads, including smaller repositories.

The series has received substantive feedback from Justin Tobler and Junio C Hamano. Justin’s reviews engage deeply with the patches’ logic, confirming mechanical correctness and proposing an alternative implementation for the final patch to avoid changing a function signature. Junio raised a safety concern about the lock relocation in patch 2, warning that concurrent `reftable_addition` instances on the same stack could lead to premature lock release. This issue remains unresolved and must be addressed before the series can proceed. Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback on patch 1 was minor, focusing on API naming consistency and external compatibility with libgit2. With Peff’s benchmark results now backing the series, the technical case for inclusion is stronger, but the open safety question in patch 2 still needs resolution.
