This thread is about a three-patch series from Karthik Nayak that optimizes the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The goal is to address a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls—scaling linearly with the number of references—were consuming significant time during bulk ref creation. Jeff King independently confirmed a consistent 25% speedup across ref counts from 10,000 to 50,000, reinforcing the optimization’s effectiveness.

The series is under review, with all substantive feedback now resolved except for minor design preferences. Patch 1 removes a redundant flag and simplifies the API, patch 2 relocates a lock to enable the optimization in patch 3, and patch 3 implements the core performance improvement by reducing `fstat()` calls from O(n) to a constant ~55. The only remaining open question is a design choice in patch 3: whether to keep the lock-checking logic inside `stack_uptodate()` (Karthik’s approach) or expose it to callers (Justin Tobler’s alternative). Karthik argues that embedding the logic in the function reduces caller error risk, while Justin’s proposal avoids changing the function signature. The discussion is narrow but substantive, focusing on API design rather than correctness or performance.

Key participants include Karthik (author), Jeff King (who identified the bottleneck and provided benchmarks), Justin Tobler (substantive review of all patches), Junio C Hamano (raised and later resolved a safety concern about lock handling), and Patrick Steinhardt (cosmetic naming feedback). The series is uncontroversial in its goals, and the current design is likely to proceed unless further objections arise. Karthik has also offered to send a compatibility pull request to libgit2 once the patch lands.
