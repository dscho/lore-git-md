This thread is about a three-patch series from Karthik Nayak that optimizes the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The goal is to address a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls—scaling linearly with the number of references—were consuming significant time during bulk ref creation.

The series is under review, with the first two patches now receiving substantive feedback. Justin Tobler reviewed both patches, confirming the mechanical correctness of the changes and endorsing the design decisions. In patch 1, he verified that removing the `REFTABLE_STACK_NEW_ADDITION_RELOAD` flag was safe and that redundant checks were no longer needed. In patch 2, he agreed with the relocation of the list lock from `reftable_addition` to `reftable_stack`, a prerequisite for the optimization in patch 3. Jeff King, who originally identified the bottleneck, has not yet reviewed the series.

The key technical change is in patch 3, which uses the lock state to skip unnecessary reloads when the stack is locked during a transaction, reducing `newfstatat()` syscalls from O(n) to a constant ~55 calls regardless of ref count. Benchmarks show a 1-2% clock time improvement in `git-update-ref` operations. The series touches `refs/reftable-backend.c` and the reftable stack implementation, with updated unit tests validating the new locking logic. No on-disk format changes are involved.

The series is narrowly focused and appears ready for the next iteration, with no unresolved objections or open questions. The only remaining step is for Karthik to address any minor follow-ups before the series can proceed toward integration.
