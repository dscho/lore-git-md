This thread is about Karthik Nayak’s four-patch series optimizing the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The goal is to address a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls were consuming significant time during bulk ref creation. The series is now in its second version, with all substantive review feedback incorporated.

Key participants include Karthik (the author), Jeff King (who identified the bottleneck and provided benchmark results), Justin Tobler (who reviewed implementation details), Junio C Hamano (who raised a safety concern about lock handling), and Patrick Steinhardt (who suggested cosmetic improvements). The series has evolved from a reported 1-2% performance improvement in v1 to a consistent 15-20% speedup in v2, with the discrepancy attributed to test methodology differences. The core fix reduces `newfstatat()` syscalls from O(n) to a constant ~55, regardless of ref count, by skipping redundant reloads when the stack is locked.

All technical feedback has been addressed: Junio’s safety concern about premature lock release is resolved with per-addition lock tracking, Patrick’s naming feedback is incorporated, and the implementation now enforces consistent lock-checking logic. The series is under review, with no remaining open questions on design or implementation. The only uncertainty is whether Junio will accept the resolution of his safety concern, which has been validated by a new unit test. The patches are mechanical, targeted, and demonstrate clear performance gains without altering the on-disk format or public API.
