This thread is about a four-patch series (v2) from Karthik Nayak that optimizes the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The goal is to address a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls were slowing down bulk ref creation, with benchmarks now showing a consistent 15-20% speedup after adjusting the test methodology to better reflect real-world conditions.

The series is technically complete and under review. All substantive feedback from v1 has been addressed, including Junio C Hamano’s safety concern about premature lock release when multiple `reftable_addition` instances operate on the same stack (now resolved with per-addition lock tracking and validated by a new unit test). Patrick Steinhardt’s latest review of patch 3—a mechanical change moving the list-file lock into `struct reftable_stack`—raises mild unease about the fragility of tracking lock state via a bit field but does not identify a concrete flaw or propose an alternative. He endorses the series overall, leaving the only remaining open question as whether Junio will accept the safety resolution.

Key participants include Karthik (author), Jeff King (who confirmed the performance improvement), Justin Tobler (substantive review of v1), and Patrick (surface-level feedback on v1, now a narrowly scoped review of patch 3). The series touches `refs/reftable-backend.c`, `reftable/stack.h`, and `reftable/stack.c`, introducing a `locked` bit field and a `skip_if_locked` flag to reduce `newfstatat()` syscalls from O(n) to ~55 constant calls. The on-disk format remains unchanged, and libgit2 compatibility is preserved.
