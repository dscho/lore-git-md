This thread is about a patch series from Karthik Nayak that optimizes the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The series has evolved from its initial v1 submission to v2, now consisting of four patches instead of three, after incorporating feedback from reviewers.

The core issue is a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls—scaling linearly with the number of references—were consuming significant time during bulk ref creation. The v2 series addresses this by tracking lock acquisition per `reftable_addition` instance, preventing premature lock release when multiple additions operate on the same stack. This resolves a safety concern raised by Junio C Hamano in the v1 review. Benchmark results now show a consistent 15-20% performance improvement, with `newfstatat()` syscalls reduced from linear growth to a constant ~55 calls regardless of ref count. The on-disk format remains unchanged, and compatibility with libgit2 is preserved.

Key participants include Jeff King, who identified the bottleneck and provided independent benchmark results confirming the optimization’s effectiveness; Justin Tobler, who reviewed the patches substantively and proposed implementation alternatives; and Patrick Steinhardt, who suggested a cosmetic function rename that was incorporated in v2. The series is under active review, with no major objections remaining. The changes are mechanical and well-tested, focusing on eliminating redundant operations rather than introducing new behavior.
