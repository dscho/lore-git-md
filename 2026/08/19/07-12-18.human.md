This is a Google Summer of Code (GSoC) progress update from Siddharth Shrimali on his project to improve disk space recovery for partial clones. The email links to his weeks 11 and 12 blog posts, continuing the work on the `gc-promisor` maintenance task introduced earlier in the summer.

The latest updates refine the design and implementation of the `git maintenance run --task=gc-promisor` subcommand, which safely prunes unneeded promisor objects in partial clones. Key developments include expanded test coverage for edge cases (shallow clones, grafted history, alternate object directories) and performance benchmarks for repositories with more than 10 remotes. The per-remote `.keep`-file safety mechanism, introduced in earlier weeks, is now fully implemented, with timestamps to enforce the configurable grace period (default: 30 days). The integration with the `git maintenance` scheduler remains stable, with `gc-promisor` running after `gc` to avoid interfering with pack consolidation.

The blog posts also address open questions from prior updates. Performance testing now includes repositories with up to 20 remotes and promisor packs exceeding 1 GB, with fetch latency increases remaining under 5% post-prune. The author has begun documenting the interaction between CLI (`--grace-period=<days>`) and config (`maintenance.gcPromisor.gracePeriod`) settings, though this is not yet finalized. Edge-case handling for shallow clones and grafted history is still under evaluation, and the sufficiency of the `.keep`-file mechanism for recovery remains a discussion point.

No formal patches have been posted yet, and the thread remains an invitation for broader review. The work continues to target `promisor-remote.c`, `builtin/gc.c`, `builtin/maintenance.c`, and `packfile.c`, with new test coverage in `t/t5329-partial-clone-prune.sh`. The project is well-motivated and appears to be making steady progress, with clear attention to safety and performance. Contributors interested in partial clones, promisor packs, or the `git maintenance` machinery may want to review the blog posts and provide feedback before the design solidifies further.
