This thread began with a targeted bugfix by Johannes Schindelin (Dscho) to resolve a Windows-specific deadlock in the sequencer during interactive rebase, where the ODB lock was held while spawning `git commit`, causing child processes that triggered auto-maintenance to fail. The fix—releasing the ODB before spawning commands—was accepted by Junio C Hamano and marked for inclusion in `next`, with no outstanding concerns about the immediate change.

The discussion has since expanded into broader architectural questions. Phillip Wood and Junio explored two potential long-term improvements: disabling `gc.auto` during rebase (a 2007-era idea) and automatically closing the ODB before spawning any Git command. Phillip’s latest email refines this analysis, noting that the sequencer *does* invoke `git commit` (and thus auto-gc) mid-rebase in many real-world cases—such as when rebasing merges or large histories—undermining the assumption that a single gc at the end would suffice. This shifts the focus toward Junio’s preferred solution: a global ODB-release mechanism, which would eliminate the need for one-off fixes like Dscho’s patch. While no concrete follow-up patches have been proposed, the thread positions this as a promising direction for future refactoring.
