This is a **bugfix discussion** targeting the geometric repacking feature introduced in Git 2.54.0. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Stefan Haller’s detailed report about two critical issues: the `geometric-repack.auto` condition triggering too aggressively (256× more sensitive than documented) and concurrent repack/prune operations causing intermittent failures in real-world workflows.

Patrick acknowledges the scaling bug in the auto-condition logic and proposes a straightforward fix: raising the default threshold from 100 to 6700 (matching `gc.auto`). This change would prevent background repacks in tiny repositories (fewer than ~6,700 objects) and reduce the frequency of the concurrency races Stefan observed. The patch itself is minimal—just two lines in `Documentation/config/maintenance.adoc` and `odb/source-files.c`—but the implications are significant: it would effectively disable geometric repacking for most small repositories, aligning its behavior with the more conservative `gc` strategy.

The concurrency issue remains unresolved in this email, but Patrick frames it as a preexisting problem exacerbated by the new default. He suggests that reducing repack frequency (via the higher threshold) might mitigate the races, though he stops short of claiming it would eliminate them entirely. The tone is pragmatic: this is a targeted fix for the immediate pain point (over-aggressive repacks) while acknowledging that deeper concurrency hardening may still be needed. The proposal is uncontroversial—it mirrors an established default and requires no new machinery—but its impact on the geometric repacking feature’s intended benefits (reducing full repacks in large repos) is worth noting. For now, this looks like a reasonable stopgap.
