This is a Google Summer of Code (GSoC) progress update from Siddharth Shrimali on his project to improve disk space recovery for partial clones. The email links to his combined week 9 and 10 blog post, continuing the design and prototyping work for the proposed `git maintenance run --task=gc-promisor` subcommand.

The post appears to finalize key design decisions from earlier iterations. The `gc-promisor` task now runs *after* the existing `gc` task in the maintenance scheduler, avoiding interference with pack consolidation. The per-remote `.keep`-file safety mechanism has been implemented, using timestamps to enforce the configurable grace period (default 30 days). Both CLI (`--grace-period=<days>`) and config (`maintenance.gcPromisor.gracePeriod`) options are now supported for tuning the grace period. Performance testing on large promisor packs (up to 1 GB) shows fetch latency increases of less than 5% post-prune, though edge cases like shallow clones and repositories with more than 10 remotes remain under evaluation.

The work touches `promisor-remote.c`, `builtin/gc.c`, `builtin/maintenance.c`, and `packfile.c`, with new tests in `t/t5329-partial-clone-prune.sh`. The on-disk format remains backward-compatible, but `.keep` files now include remote-specific markers and timestamps. The author is explicitly seeking feedback on edge cases, safety mechanisms, and configurability before formal patch submission.

No formal patches have been posted yet, and the thread has not yet attracted review from other contributors. The design looks well-motivated and technically sound, but the project is still in the prototyping phase, so further refinements are likely. If you care about partial clones or promisor pack management, this is a good time to engage.
