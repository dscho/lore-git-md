This is a **follow-up discussion** in the ongoing bugfix series for `git maintenance`'s geometric repack task. The thread focuses on the first patch, which adjusts how the repack mode decision accounts for promisor packs.

Taylor Blau responds to Patrick Steinhardt's review, clarifying the intended behavior of the fix. The core issue is that the current logic could trigger unnecessary all-into-one repacks when non-promisor packs are small, even if they’re already in a geometric progression. Taylor confirms this was observed in practice, with users reporting that geometric maintenance appeared inactive despite many promisor packs being present.

Patrick’s concern about the *reverse* edge case—where the fix might *under*-trigger all-into-one repacks—is addressed directly. Taylor explains that the patch ensures geometric repacks will still occur when either promisor *or* non-promisor packs form a valid progression, and that non-promisor packs will still be compacted into a single pack if their count matches `geometry.split`. The discussion suggests the fix is narrowly scoped and unlikely to introduce new regressions, though Patrick’s note about combined thresholds hints at potential future refinements.

The exchange is technical but concise, with no new objections raised. The patch remains on track for acceptance, pending any further feedback. Readers following the series can expect the second patch (adjusting the `--auto` trigger) to proceed similarly, given Patrick’s earlier approval.
