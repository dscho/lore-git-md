This is a review reply in the ongoing Bash completion series for `git history`. D. Ben Knoble engages with the second patch of the v2 series, which adds completion for the `--empty` option’s values ("drop", "keep", "abort").

The review is substantive but narrowly scoped. Knoble questions whether the patch should support both `--empty=<value>` (the documented form) and `--empty <value>` (the undocumented, parse-options-accepted form). He cites Git’s `gitcli(7)` man page, which recommends the `--option=value` syntax, and notes that the completion codebase itself rarely handles the unstuck form. He provides a concrete example: `git commit --cleanup <tab>` does not complete, while `git commit --cleanup=<tab>` does. His concern is consistency—both with Git’s documentation and with the existing completion logic elsewhere in the codebase.

Knoble’s feedback does not challenge the patch’s correctness or implementation, but it does raise a design question that could affect the series’ long-term maintainability. The thread’s earlier discussion already flagged the fragility of hardcoding option-value pairs in the completion helper; Knoble’s observation adds another dimension to that concern. His preference for dropping the unstuck form aligns with the "option 2" simplification proposed in the thread summary, which would ignore `--option <value>` entirely to reduce complexity.

The review is not tested (no mention of running the code), but it is informed by direct inspection of the completion codebase and Git’s documentation. It advances the thread by sharpening the focus on consistency and maintainability, though it does not resolve the open question of which approach to adopt.
