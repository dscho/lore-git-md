This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s four-patch series to refactor Git’s object database (ODB) subsystem by removing lazy-loading of alternate object databases. The goal is to simplify the codebase by initializing all alternates upfront during ODB setup, reducing scattered calls to `odb_prepare_alternates()` and enabling future work on the ODB abstraction effort. The series is currently queued in Junio Hamano’s `seen` branch, awaiting Patrick’s verification of the merge result after a minor conflict resolution.

The most contentious part of the series is the first patch, which decouples alternate path comparisons from `the_repository` to resolve inconsistent case-sensitivity handling. Jeff King (Peff) has raised a substantive critique, arguing that the patch’s reliance on `core.ignoreCase` for deduplication is flawed because it reflects the repository’s filesystem, not the alternate’s. Peff proposed two alternatives: always using case-sensitive comparisons (accepting occasional duplicates) or using `stat()` to compare `st_dev`/`st_ino` for correctness. Junio Hamano recently weighed in, endorsing Peff’s preference for simplicity and correctness over complexity, though he expressed skepticism about `stat()`-based comparisons due to platform-specific quirks like Unicode normalization on macOS. This leaves the design question unresolved, with a growing consensus that the current approach may be over-engineered.

Other patches in the series are less controversial. The second patch centralizes alternate initialization, removing scattered calls to `odb_prepare_alternates()`, while the third and fourth patches clean up now-unused fields in the ODB structure. Reviews from Justin Tobler and Karthik Nayak have been largely surface-level, focusing on commit message clarity and mechanical correctness rather than deeper technical implications. The series has not yet been mentioned in Junio’s "What's cooking" report, so its integration status remains "queued in `seen`" until Patrick confirms the merge is correct. Open questions include the unresolved design debate in patch 1 and the untested performance implications of eager alternate loading for commands like `git fsck`.
