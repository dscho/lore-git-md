This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s patch series to remove lazy-loading of alternate object databases in Git’s ODB subsystem. The goal is to simplify the code by loading all alternates upfront during ODB setup, reducing scattered initialization calls and paving the way for future refactoring. The series, now at v2, is queued in Junio’s `seen` branch but faces unresolved design feedback on its first patch.

The key sticking point has been patch 1’s handling of case-insensitivity for alternate paths. Jeff King (Peff) and Junio Hamano argued that the added complexity isn’t justified, as the risk of duplicate alternates is low and user-configurable. Patrick initially defended the design but now concedes that case-sensitive matching is the pragmatic choice, despite its limitations on Windows. He also highlights that `stat()`-based deduplication—another proposed simplification—is unreliable on Windows due to inconsistent `st_ino`/`st_dev` values. The discussion has shifted toward revising the patch to drop case-insensitivity entirely, though no updated implementation has been posted yet.

The series touches 12 files, primarily in the `odb/` subsystem, and removes lazy-loading logic that had become redundant. While the changes are intended to be user-invisible, reviewers have raised unaddressed concerns about performance implications (e.g., for `git fsck`) and edge cases like repositories with thousands of alternates. Junio’s merge strategy—merging the dependency into the feature branch rather than using it as a base—also drew Patrick’s attention, but this procedural question remains unresolved. For now, the series remains queued in `seen` pending further design revisions.
