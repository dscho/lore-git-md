This thread is a four-part patch series from Patrick Steinhardt aimed at removing lazy-loading of alternate object databases in Git’s ODB subsystem. The goal is to simplify the codebase by loading all alternates upfront during ODB setup, rather than deferring their initialization until they are needed. This refactoring is part of Patrick’s broader effort to make Git’s object storage layer more modular and paves the way for future work, such as moving alternates into the "files" backend.

The first patch in the series has just been posted, addressing a long-standing issue with path comparisons for alternate ODB sources. Currently, these comparisons rely on `the_repository` and its `core.ignoreCase` setting, which can lead to inconsistent behavior when alternates are registered before the repository’s configuration is fully parsed. Patrick’s patch decouples these comparisons from `the_repository` by introducing a self-contained mechanism tied to the ODB instance itself. This involves replacing khash with a `struct hashmap` to allow custom hash and equality functions, and manually resolving `core.ignoreCase` during path comparisons. While the change is technically sound, it adds some complexity to handle case-sensitivity correctly, which may draw scrutiny during review.

The series is still in its early stages, with no reviews or objections recorded yet. The main open questions revolve around the performance implications of eager alternate loading—particularly for commands that previously avoided loading alternates entirely—and how edge cases, such as corrupt alternates, will be handled. The patches touch several key files in the ODB subsystem and build on Patrick’s prior ODB refactoring work, so reviewers familiar with that effort may be particularly interested in this series. For now, the thread is awaiting feedback from the community.
