This thread is a patch series from Patrick Steinhardt aimed at refactoring Git’s object database (ODB) subsystem to remove lazy-loading of alternate object databases. The series, now at version 2, consists of four patches and builds on Patrick’s broader ODB abstraction work. The core goal is to simplify the codebase by initializing all alternate ODBs upfront during primary ODB setup, eliminating scattered calls to `odb_prepare_alternates()` and paving the way for future architectural changes, such as moving alternates into the "files" backend.

The first patch, which this email covers, addresses a long-standing inconsistency in case-sensitivity handling when alternates are registered before repository configuration is parsed. It decouples path comparisons from `the_repository` by introducing a `struct hashmap` that tracks case-sensitivity per ODB, replacing the previous `khash`-based approach. While this adds some complexity (41 lines net), Patrick and reviewer Justin Tobler agree it’s necessary for correctness, even if the performance trade-off for repositories with many alternates remains a minor point of debate. The rest of the series focuses on removing lazy-loading (patch 2), followed by mechanical cleanups (patches 3 and 4), all of which have received Justin’s review with no substantive objections.

The series is under active review, with Justin Tobler as the primary reviewer. His feedback on v1 was largely positive, focusing on commit message clarity and mechanical correctness, though he raised a clarifying question about environment variable handling in patch 4, which Patrick addressed in v2. The only open question is whether the performance implications of eager alternate loading—particularly for commands like `git fsck` that previously avoided loading alternates when none were present—might need further validation. For now, the series appears ready for integration, with no major technical concerns outstanding.
