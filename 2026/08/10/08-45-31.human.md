He cites Jujutsu (JJ) as an example of how small, targeted improvements can significantly enhance usability without overcomplicating the tool. His argument hinges on the distinction between "core functionality" (e.g., hunk selection) and "full UI" (e.g., a complete history editor), framing the former as a reasonable scope for Git while leaving the latter to external tools.

This keeps the discussion focused on *how* to implement such improvements (e.g., ncurses-based interfaces) rather than *whether* they belong in Git at all.

For readers following the thread, Patrick’s reply signals that the debate is narrowing: the immediate priority is likely to be a lightweight, built-in solution for unified hunk triage (stage/discard/defer), while more ambitious UI features (e.g., full history editing) may remain out of scope for now.
