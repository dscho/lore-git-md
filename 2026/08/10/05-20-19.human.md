This is a follow-up in the geometric repacking series, specifically addressing Stefan Haller’s concern about background maintenance races affecting real-world usage beyond just Git’s test suite.

Patrick acknowledges that the race condition *does* affect ordinary users, but argues that it should be transparent to them—Git commands ought to work correctly even when maintenance runs concurrently. The issue only becomes visible in Git’s test suite because tests often inspect repository internals directly, making them sensitive to on-disk state changes caused by background maintenance. For regular users, the expectation is that maintenance operations shouldn’t interfere with their workflows, and any failure in that regard would warrant deeper investigation.

The exchange clarifies that the auto-detach mechanism (introduced in patch 1/8) is primarily a test infrastructure fix, not a user-facing change. While Stefan’s observation about lazygit’s test suite suggests the problem could surface in scripted workflows, Patrick’s response frames it as an edge case rather than a systemic flaw. The discussion doesn’t derail the series—it remains on track for merging—but it does highlight a potential area for future scrutiny if similar issues arise in other tools. The broader series goal (making geometric repacking the default) is unaffected.
