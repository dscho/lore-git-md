This is a **completion infrastructure review** in the ongoing Bash completion series for the `git history` command. D. Ben Knoble chimes in with a concise, substantive review that weighs in on the maintainability trade-offs discussed earlier between Vincent Mailhol and Patrick Steinhardt.

Ben endorses Vincent’s proposed compromise (option 2: ignoring the `--option <value>` syntax and only completing `--option=<value>`) as the pragmatic path forward. He notes that the Git documentation (notably `gitcli`) recommends the "stuck" form (`--option=value`), and that most existing completion code in the codebase already aligns with this pattern. His reasoning is that supporting the unstuck form (`--option value`) adds complexity for little practical gain, since users who rely on completion are likely to use the stuck form anyway. He suggests starting with the simpler approach and revisiting it only if users complain about missing support for the unstuck form.

He also agrees with Vincent’s fallback strategy for handling future subcommands that deviate from the "revision-first" pattern: dispatching them early via a `case` statement before checking for revisions. This mirrors the pattern already used in commands like `git stash`, and Ben sees it as a reasonable way to accommodate edge cases without over-engineering the general case. His feedback is brief but substantive, focusing on real-world usage patterns and codebase consistency rather than theoretical edge cases.
