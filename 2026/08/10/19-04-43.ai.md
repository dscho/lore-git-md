This is a Google Summer of Code (GSoC) progress update from Siddharth Shrimali on his project to improve disk space recovery for partial clones. The email links to his week 9 and 10 blog post, continuing the work on the `gc-promisor` maintenance task introduced in earlier updates.

The post builds on the design established in weeks 7–8, with no major shifts in direction. The `git maintenance run --task=gc-promisor` subcommand and its integration with the maintenance scheduler remain central, and the per-remote `.keep`-file safety mechanism is now implemented. The grace period (default 30 days) is confirmed as configurable via both config (`maintenance.gcPromisor.gracePeriod`) and CLI (`--grace-period=<days>`). Performance testing has begun, with initial results showing acceptable fetch latency post-prune for repositories with up to 10 remotes, though edge cases like shallow clones and grafted history are still under evaluation.

Key technical details include the scheduling order of `gc-promisor` (now running *after* `gc` to avoid interference), the use of timestamps in `.keep` files to enforce grace periods, and the addition of new test coverage in `t/t5329-partial-clone-prune.sh`. The on-disk format remains backward-compatible, and no changes to existing pack or index formats are introduced. Open questions remain around edge-case handling (e.g., alternate object directories, promisor packs from >10 remotes), the sufficiency of the `.keep`-file mechanism for recovery, and whether additional configurability (e.g., per-remote grace periods) is needed.

No formal patches have been posted yet, and the thread remains an invitation for wider review. The author explicitly seeks feedback on safety, configurability, and performance, signaling that the design is still open to refinement. The work touches `promisor-remote.c`, `builtin/gc.c`, `builtin/maintenance.c`, and `packfile.c`, with new symbols like `cmd_maintenance__gc_promisor()` and config keys `maintenance.gcPromisor.enabled`/`maintenance.gcPromisor.gracePeriod`. The project is part of the broader effort to improve partial clone usability, building on prior GSoC work and the existing `git maintenance` machinery.
