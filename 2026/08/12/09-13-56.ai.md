This is the v2 revision of Patrick Steinhardt’s four-patch series refactoring the object database (ODB) subsystem to eagerly load alternate object databases. The series is part of the broader ODB abstraction effort and builds on Patrick’s prior work (`ps/odb-make-creation-pluggable`).

The core goal remains unchanged: eliminate lazy-loading of alternates, which has become ineffective due to scattered eager parsing throughout the codebase. This simplifies the ODB code by centralizing alternate initialization in `odb_new()` and `odb_prepare()`, removing 13 calls to `odb_prepare_alternates()` across 10 files. The series also prepares the ground for moving alternates into the "files" backend.

Key updates in v2:
- Patch 2/4 fixes a minor commit message typo ("where read" → "where we read").
- Patch 4/4 adds a comment clarifying why `GIT_ALTERNATE_OBJECT_DIRECTORIES` does not need reprocessing during cache flushing, addressing Justin Tobler’s question from the v1 review. The explanation confirms that alternates are fully initialized during `odb_new()` and subsequent calls to `odb_prepare_alternates()` only re-read the alternates *file* (not the environment variable).

The series touches 12 files, primarily in `odb/` but also affecting higher-level code like `builtin/fsck.c` and `commit-graph.c`. The net code delta is a reduction of 16 lines (70 insertions, 86 deletions). No behavioral changes are intended, but the removal of lazy-loading may have subtle performance implications for commands like `git fsck` that previously avoided loading alternates when none were present. The v1 discussion surfaced no objections to this trade-off, and the v2 changes are minor clarifications rather than technical revisions.

Justin Tobler’s v1 reviews were substantive for patch 1 (validating the case-sensitivity fix) but surface-level for the rest, focusing on commit message clarity and mechanical correctness. The v2 updates address all feedback, and the series appears ready for further review or integration. The only open question—performance impact on edge cases—remains untested but is acknowledged as a low-risk trade-off in the commit messages.
