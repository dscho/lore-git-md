This thread began when Marcel Svitalský reported seeing four unexpected pseudo-commits—three labeled “Notes added by 'git notes append' - rewrite-analytics” and one “chatter: initialize notes ref - chatter”—at the top of the graph produced by his custom `git lg` alias (`git log --graph --all --oneline --decorate`). These commits were invisible in Sublime Merge and IntelliJ IDEA, appeared inconsistently after resets or new commits, and puzzled him since his enabled hooks list was empty.

The root cause was quickly identified: the `--all` flag in the alias includes all refs, including Git notes (`refs/notes/rewrite-analytics` and `refs/notes/chatter`), which form a disconnected graph. This is not a bug but an expected consequence of `--all`. D. Ben Knoble proposed two practical workarounds: replacing `--all` with `--branches --tags --remotes` (omitting notes, stash, and other refs) or using `--exclude='refs/notes/*' --all` (explicitly filtering notes while preserving `--all`’s broader coverage). Marcel confirmed both workarounds successfully suppress the spurious notes in his follow-up. He now attributes the notes to background tooling like lazygit or IntelliJ IDEA, accepting the behavior as environment-specific rather than a Git regression. The thread is effectively resolved, with no maintainer action expected.
