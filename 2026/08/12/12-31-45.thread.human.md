This thread began when Marcel Svitalský reported seeing four unexpected pseudo-commits—three labeled “Notes added by 'git notes append' - rewrite-analytics” and one “chatter: initialize notes ref - chatter”—at the top of the graph produced by his custom `git lg` alias (`git log --graph --all --oneline --decorate`). These commits were invisible in Sublime Merge and IntelliJ IDEA, appeared inconsistently after resets or new commits, and were initially suspected to originate from Git’s notes or hook machinery despite Marcel’s enabled hooks list being empty.

The root cause was quickly identified as the `--all` flag in the alias, which includes all refs—including Git notes (`refs/notes/rewrite-analytics` and `refs/notes/chatter`)—forming a disconnected graph. This behavior, while not a bug, was unintuitive for an end-user-facing command like `git log`. Marcel confirmed that workarounds—replacing `--all` with `--branches --tags --remotes` or using `--exclude='refs/notes/*' --all`—successfully suppressed the spurious notes refs. Junio C Hamano later noted that `--branches`, `--tags`, and `--remotes` are the standard narrower alternatives to `--all` for excluding notes, though he reflected that altering `--all`’s behavior at this stage would be impractical. The notes’ appearance was attributed to background tooling (e.g., lazygit or IntelliJ IDEA) and deemed environment-specific rather than a Git regression.

In his follow-up, Marcel acknowledged Junio’s suggestion but reiterated his preference for the `--exclude='refs/notes/*' --all` workaround, as it preserves the inclusion of stashes in the log output while filtering out the notes. The thread is now effectively closed, with no open questions or further action expected. The resolution serves as a useful reference for users encountering similar cosmetic issues with `--all` in custom aliases.
