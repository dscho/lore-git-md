This is a follow-up in a user-support thread about unexpected pseudo-commits appearing in a custom `git lg` alias. The thread remains under review, with no maintainer involvement.

D. Ben Knoble responds to Marcel Svitalský’s questions about why Git notes suddenly appeared in his `git log --graph --all` output. He explains that notes themselves are not new (they have existed in Git for years), but the appearance of these specific notes (`refs/notes/rewrite-analytics` and `refs/notes/chatter`) likely means a tool in Marcel’s environment recently started creating them. This could be anything from a Git hook, a background daemon (like `fsmonitor--daemon`), or a third-party integration (e.g., `rewrite-analytics` or `chatter`).

For a practical workaround, D. Ben suggests modifying the alias to explicitly exclude notes refs while preserving `--all`’s coverage of branches, tags, and remotes. His proposed flags—`--branches --tags --remotes` or `--exclude='refs/notes/*' --all`—are reasonable starting points, though he notes they omit `refs/stash` (which may or may not matter to Marcel). The response is concise and solution-oriented, avoiding speculation about which specific tool might be generating the notes. No other participants have weighed in on this suggestion yet.
