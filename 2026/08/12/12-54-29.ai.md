This is a surface-level review of a bug report about unexpected pseudo-commits in a custom `git lg` alias. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Marcel Svitalský’s report by identifying the root cause: the `--all` flag in the alias includes all refs, including Git notes like `refs/notes/rewrite-analytics` and `refs/notes/chatter`. These notes form their own disconnected graph, which explains why they appear unconnected to the project commits in the output.

The review clarifies that this behavior is not a bug but an expected consequence of using `--all`. The pseudo-commits are legitimate Git notes, likely generated by background tooling (e.g., `rewrite-analytics` or `chatter`), even though the user’s enabled hooks list is empty. The discrepancy between CLI and GUI tools (Sublime Merge, IntelliJ IDEA) may stem from how those tools filter or display refs by default, whereas the alias explicitly includes all refs.

Kristoffer’s response is brief and factual, focusing on the technical explanation rather than proposing a solution. The review does not engage with the broader questions raised in the report, such as why these notes are being appended or how to modify the alias to exclude them. It also does not address the potential role of background processes like `fsmonitor--daemon` or Rust-based tooling in generating the notes. The tone is neutral, framing the issue as a misunderstanding of `--all` rather than a defect in Git.
