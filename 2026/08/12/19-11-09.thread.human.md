This thread is about a severe performance regression in Git v2.53 that causes quadratic-time slowdowns when loading many packfiles, particularly affecting `git rev-parse --short HEAD` in Microsoft Git Scalar environments. The regression, introduced in October 2025, undoes a prior fix from 2019 and manifests in repositories with tens of thousands of packfiles due to Scalar’s "shared cache" architecture.

Johannes Schindelin proposed a fast-path optimization to skip duplicate checks for new packfiles, which Junio C Hamano endorsed as "clever and clean." However, Patrick Steinhardt has countered with a hashmap-based refactoring that would eliminate the O(N) duplicate check entirely, making both `append()` and `prepend()` operations O(1). The debate remains unresolved: Johannes argues the fast-path fix is sufficient and simpler, while Patrick’s approach offers a more comprehensive solution but with added complexity. Patrick has signaled he may pursue his refactoring independently, leaving the door open for either path to land first.

The patch is still under review, with no merge decision made. Key participants include Johannes (author), Junio (maintainer, approving the fast-path design), and Patrick (proposing the hashmap alternative). Jeff King (Peff) confirmed the regression’s severity and clarified the necessity of the new performance test, while D. Ben Knoble’s question about `git maintenance` as a mitigation was resolved—it’s not reliable for Scalar’s use case. The unresolved CI clone-time regression (unrelated to this patch) remains unaddressed. The next steps depend on whether the project prioritizes immediate simplicity or future-proofing.
