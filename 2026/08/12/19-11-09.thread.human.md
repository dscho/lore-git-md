This thread addresses a severe performance regression in Git v2.53 that causes quadratic-time slowdowns when loading many packfiles, affecting common operations like `git rev-parse --short HEAD` and `git clone`. The regression, introduced in October 2025, undoes a prior fix from 2019 and has real-world impacts, slowing shell prompts from 0.4s to 4.5s and clones from under 2 minutes to over 30 minutes in some cases.

Johannes Schindelin proposed a fix that adds a fast-path optimization to skip duplicate checks for new packfiles, which Junio C Hamano endorsed as "clever and clean." Jeff King confirmed the regression's severity and the fix's reasonableness but questioned the necessity of a new performance test. Meanwhile, Patrick Steinhardt suggested a more ambitious alternative: replacing the linked-list `packfile_list` with a hashmap to eliminate quadratic-time behavior entirely. This design debate remains unresolved, though Patrick's latest email shifts focus to CI benchmarking gaps, noting GitLab's flaky infrastructure failed to catch the regression due to inconsistent runner specs and lack of many-pack coverage.

The patch is still under review, with no merge to any integration branch. Open questions include whether the hashmap approach is justified, whether the new performance test is redundant, and whether `git maintenance` (repacking) could mitigate the issue at the infrastructure level. Key participants—Johannes, Junio, Peff, and Patrick—have not yet converged on these points.
