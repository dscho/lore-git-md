This thread is about a critical performance regression in Git’s packfile handling that was introduced in v2.53. The bug causes quadratic-time slowdowns in common operations like `git rev-parse --short HEAD` and `git clone`, turning a 0.4-second operation into 4.5 seconds and a 2-minute clone into 30+ minutes. Johannes Schindelin identified the root cause—a linear scan for duplicates in `packfile_store_add_pack()`—and proposed a minimal fix that adds a fast path to skip the scan when the packfile is known to be new.

The patch has received maintainer approval from Junio Hamano, who called the design "clever and clean" and endorsed the optimization. Jeff King (Peff) recently reviewed the patch and confirmed the regression is real, citing historical context and perf-suite data showing a 246% slowdown in an existing test. However, he questioned the necessity of the new performance test, arguing it duplicates coverage already provided by another test. The core fix itself remains uncontested, and the patch is still under review, with the author likely to address the test-coverage feedback. No other open questions or objections have been raised.
