This thread is about a critical performance regression in Git’s packfile handling that was introduced in v2.53. The bug causes quadratic-time slowdowns in common operations like `git rev-parse --short HEAD` and `git clone`, turning sub-second operations into multi-second delays and multi-minute clones into half-hour ordeals. Johannes Schindelin identified the root cause—a linear scan for duplicates in `packfile_store_add_pack()`—and proposed a minimal fix that adds a fast path to skip the scan when the packfile is known to be new.

The patch is now under review and has received a strong endorsement from Junio Hamano, the project maintainer. Junio called the fix "clever and clean" and explicitly approved the design, which avoids unnecessary duplicate checks when the caller guarantees the packfile is new. His review was brief but substantive, confirming the algorithmic correctness without raising any concerns or requesting changes. While the patch hasn’t been merged yet, this maintainer approval effectively moves it to the "ready" column. No objections or open questions remain, and the patch includes a new performance test to prevent regressions. The fix is targeted at the packfile subsystem and doesn’t change on-disk formats or user-facing behavior.
