This thread is about a critical performance regression in Git’s packfile handling that caused `git rev-parse --short HEAD` (commonly used in shell prompts) to slow down from 0.4s to 4.5s in Microsoft Git Scalar environments. The regression, introduced in Git v2.53, stems from a quadratic-time scan in `packfile_store_add_pack()` when loading many packfiles, undoing a prior fix from 2019. Johannes Schindelin’s patch adds a targeted fast-path optimization to skip duplicate checks for packfiles the caller guarantees are new, eliminating the O(N²) behavior.

The patch is now ready for merging to `next`. Junio Hamano endorsed the design as "clever and clean," and Patrick Steinhardt, who initially proposed a more complex hashmap-based refactoring, has conceded that the fast-path is the correct immediate fix. The only remaining loose ends are deferred work—Patrick’s hashmap refactoring and broader CI benchmarking gaps—but neither blocks this patch. Jeff King confirmed the regression’s severity and the fix’s reasonableness, while D. Ben Knoble’s follow-up about `git maintenance` as a potential workaround was resolved: maintenance isn’t a reliable solution for Scalar’s shared cache repositories, where fetches outpace repacking. The patch includes a new perf test to catch similar regressions in the future.
