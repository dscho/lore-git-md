This is a follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread about adding filesystem-level copy-on-write (CoW) or block cloning support to `git worktree add`. The author, Peter Morris, acknowledges Junio C Hamano’s substantive technical feedback and confirms that the proposed optimization is architecturally feasible but beyond his own expertise to implement. He expresses hope that someone with deeper Git internals knowledge will take up the challenge.

The email does not advance the technical discussion or address the critical correctness issue raised by brian m. carlson regarding ReFS block cloning on Windows. Instead, it reiterates the motivation: reducing SSD wear and enabling worktree use for large repositories (e.g., 80GB of static image files). This use case—concurrent worktrees for AI coding agents or other scenarios with mostly unchanging files—remains the primary justification for the effort.

For readers tracking the thread, this reply signals that the RFC is now in a holding pattern. The proposal is technically viable but blocked by two unresolved challenges: the architectural complexity of refactoring the checkout machinery (as outlined by Junio) and the ReFS correctness issue (as raised by brian m. carlson). Without a contributor willing to tackle these, the optimization is unlikely to progress. The email’s tone is optimistic but realistic—Morris is not pushing for immediate action, merely keeping the idea alive for future consideration.
