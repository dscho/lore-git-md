This is Junio’s biweekly "What’s cooking in git.git" report for August 2026, summarizing the state of 40+ in-flight topics. It’s the project’s heartbeat: what’s about to graduate, what’s cooking, what’s stalled, and what’s been discarded.

The most immediate news is that the 13th batch of topics has just graduated to `master`, bringing the total since Git 2.55 to 424 non-merge commits. That batch is now irrevocably part of the next release; everything else is still in play.

In the **cooking** section, the topics are split into three integration branches:

- **`next`** (82 commits): these are the topics Junio considers stable enough to be candidates for the next release. They’re being integration-tested together and may still be dropped or rewound if problems surface.
- **`seen`** (286 commits): the ephemeral branch that merges *everything* currently in flight, including half-baked and experimental work. It’s rebuilt from scratch frequently and carries no promise of stability.
- **`maint`** is not mentioned, so nothing new is targeted for the maintenance track.

A few highlights worth skimming:

- **Pluggable ODB progress**: Patrick Steinhardt’s `ps/odb-pluggable-pack-generation` (5 commits) and Justin Tobler’s `jt/receive-pack-pluggable-writes` (9 commits) are both in `seen` and marked “Expecting a reroll”. They’re part of the long-running effort to make the object database backend-agnostic, allowing alternative ODBs to generate packfiles directly instead of shelling out to `pack-objects`. If you care about pluggable storage, these are the two to watch.

- **`the_repository` removal**: Taylor Yu’s `ty/repository-fetch-if-missing` (2 commits) moves the global `fetch_if_missing` variable into `struct repository`, another small step in the ongoing libification effort. It’s in `seen` and waiting for response.

- **Performance**: Johannes Sixt’s `js/packfile-fast-append` (1 commit) fixes a quadratic complexity regression when adding many new packfiles. It’s in `next` and looks ready to graduate.

- **New commands**: The experimental `git format-rev` (Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s `kh/format-rev-more-options`, 4 commits) is gaining more formatting options (`--abbrev`, `--color`, `--date`). It’s still marked experimental and waiting for review.

- **Documentation**: Jean-Noël Avila’s `kh/doc-refs-migrate-limitations` (2 commits) and Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s `kh/doc-trailers` (11 commits) have both graduated to `next` and are likely to reach `master` soon. The former improves the visibility of ref-migration warnings; the latter is a major rewrite of the `git interpret-trailers` documentation.

- **Security**: GitLab’s Gordon Gordon’s `gg/http-ssl-verify-status` (2 commits) adds support for stapled OCSP responses via `http.sslVerifyStatus`. It’s in `seen` and waiting for response.

- **Windows build**: Johannes Schindelin’s `js/mingw-build-updates` (12 commits) upstream a batch of Git-for-Windows patches, simplifying the build and allowing `git.exe` to be used directly without a wrapper. It’s in `seen` and needs review.

- **Experimental commands**: Toon Claes’s `tc/last-modified-bloom` (6 commits) and Hugo Nobrega’s `hn/history-squash` (9 commits) are both in `seen`. The former optimizes `git last-modified` with Bloom filters; the latter adds a `git history squash` subcommand to fold a range of commits into one. Both are marked experimental and waiting for feedback.

- **Stalled topics**: Several topics have been in limbo for months (`bl/t7412-use-test-path-helpers`, `tb/repack-geometric-cruft`, `hn/checkout-track-fetch`, `za/completion-hide-dotfiles`). Junio has either asked for a response and not received one, or explicitly marked them as “Will discard”. If you’re the author, now is the time to either reroll or accept that the topic may be dropped.

- **Discarded**: Two topics were retracted (`td/fsmonitor-darwin-cookie-flush` and `tb/send-pack-no-ref-delta`). The former was withdrawn by the author; the latter was set aside for now.

If you’re a casual observer, the topics in `next` are the safest bet for what will ship in the next release. The ones in `seen` are more speculative; some will graduate, others will be dropped or rewritten. The stalled list is a graveyard of topics that need fresh energy to revive.
