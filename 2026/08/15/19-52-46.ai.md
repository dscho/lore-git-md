This is a **follow-up in the bash completion performance regression thread**, clarifying the scope and limitations of `feature.manyFiles` as a mitigation.

Matthew Hughes responds to D. Ben Knoble’s suggestion by noting that his test repository is already managed by `git-scalar`, which explicitly disables `feature.manyFiles` (setting it to `false`) under the assumption that Scalar’s own configuration provides equivalent optimizations. When he tested `feature.manyFiles` in a fresh repository without Scalar, he observed no measurable performance improvement for the completion slowdown. He also raises a technical question: whether `git ls-files --exclude-standard --others --directory` (the command invoked by the completion script) is even aware of fsmonitor or the untracked cache, or if those optimizations are limited to commands like `git status`. His skepticism is based on qualitative experience rather than code inspection, but it highlights a potential gap in how completion-related filesystem scans interact with Git’s performance features. The reply narrows the discussion by ruling out `feature.manyFiles` as a solution and refocusing attention on the completion script’s direct use of `git ls-files` and whether that command can be optimized or made smarter about large repositories.
