This is a follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread about filesystem-level copy-on-write (CoW) or block cloning optimizations for `git worktree add`. The author, Peter Morris, acknowledges Junio C Hamano’s substantive technical feedback and confirms that the proposed approach is architecturally viable but beyond his current familiarity with Git’s internals. He expresses hope that someone with deeper expertise might take up the implementation.

The email does not introduce new technical details or counterarguments to the challenges raised earlier (e.g., the checkout machinery’s lack of cross-worktree visibility, TOCTOU races, or the ReFS correctness issue reported by brian m. carlson). Instead, it reiterates the motivation: reducing SSD wear and enabling worktree use for large repositories with static assets (e.g., 80GB of images). This use case—avoiding redundant copies of unchanging files—remains the primary justification for the optimization, though it is framed as niche (e.g., AI coding agents, large media repositories).

The thread’s status is now effectively stalled pending a contributor willing to tackle the architectural refactoring Junio outlined. The ReFS corruption issue remains an unresolved blocker for Windows support, and no progress has been made on the broader design questions (e.g., TOCTOU mitigation, side effects on other commands). For readers tracking this effort, the email signals that the proposal is unlikely to advance without a new champion. The core technical challenges and platform-specific risks identified in prior discussion remain unaddressed.
