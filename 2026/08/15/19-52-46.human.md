This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the bash completion performance regression in large repositories. Matthew Hughes responds to D. Ben Knoble’s suggestion to enable `feature.manyFiles` as a potential mitigation.

Hughes clarifies that the repository in question already uses `git-scalar`, which explicitly disables `feature.manyFiles` under the assumption that Scalar’s own optimizations (like fsmonitor) are sufficient. He tested the scenario again in a fresh repository with `feature.manyFiles` enabled but observed no meaningful performance improvement. This suggests that the slowdown in `git ls-files`—the root cause of the completion lag—may not benefit from fsmonitor or other optimizations bundled under `feature.manyFiles`, at least not in the way `git status` does. Hughes speculates (with appropriate caution) that `git ls-files` might not be wired to use fsmonitor or the untracked cache, though he lacks the familiarity with the codebase to confirm this.

The exchange narrows the scope of possible fixes: while `feature.manyFiles` is a useful optimization for some operations, it doesn’t appear to address the specific bottleneck in bash completion. The thread remains focused on diagnosing the root cause—why `git ls-files` with wildcard patterns triggers excessive filesystem scans—and whether the existing workaround (disabling index-based completion via `GIT_COMPLETION_NO_COMPLETE_INDEX`) is an acceptable trade-off or if a deeper fix is needed. No new patches or benchmarks are introduced here, but the discussion helps rule out one potential mitigation.
