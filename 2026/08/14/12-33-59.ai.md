This is the v2 cover letter for a three-patch series that converts Git’s build-time `USE_NSEC` macro into a runtime configuration option (`core.useNanosec`). The goal is to let users enable nanosecond precision on supported filesystems without recompiling Git, reducing "racy Git" problems.

The v2 iteration addresses the performance regression identified in v1: instead of calling `repo_config_get_bool()` on every `is_racy_stat()` invocation (which caused a ~41× slowdown in `git diff` benchmarks), the value is now cached in the repository’s `repo_config_values` struct. A CI run confirms the test suite passes with this approach, and the author’s partial control-flow analysis shows that all 214 call paths to `repo_config_values()` currently terminate at `the_repository`, meaning the `the_repository`-only limitation is already exposed and may grow with this change. The architectural trade-off—whether to use `repo_config_values` (modern but limited to `the_repository`), `repo_settings` (supports per-repository variation), or a global variable—remains open, but the author and Patrick Steinhardt have framed it as a pragmatic choice: land with `repo_config_values` now and revisit the broader `the_repository` issue later, or use a global variable as a temporary measure.

The series is based on master (2c78326f81) and consists of three patches: (1) a Meson build-system fix (uncontroversial after Patrick Steinhardt’s review), (2) a no-op refactor of `repo_config_values_init`, and (3) the core change introducing `core.useNanosec` and removing `USE_NSEC`. The conflict with `ty/repo-config-cleanups` in `seen` persists, as both series touch `struct repo_config_values`. The author has moved the `Best-viewed-with: --ignore-space-change` hint into the commit message body as requested by SZEDER Gábor, and the benchmark results from v1 (showing a ~57× improvement for `git diff` in new worktrees when `core.useNanosec` is enabled) are now referenced rather than repeated.

The series is ready for review, with the performance fix confirmed and the cache location decision still pending. The author is open to either `repo_config_values` or a global variable, depending on the project’s preference. Testing remains a loose end, as the platform-dependent nature of the change complicates test updates. The naming question (`core.useNanosec` vs a more abstract name like `core.useExtendedPrecision`) is also still open.
