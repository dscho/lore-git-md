This is a substantive design discussion in the ongoing RFC thread about adding filesystem-level copy-on-write (CoW) or block cloning to `git worktree add`. Junio C Hamano, the project maintainer, responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s pointer to prior art (`git clone --block-clone`) with a detailed technical analysis of the architectural challenges and a sketch of how such a feature might be implemented.

Junio clarifies two key constraints: dirty working tree files cannot be safely CoW-copied, and multiple worktrees cannot share the same checked-out branch. However, he notes these are surmountable if the optimization is limited to cleanly checked-out files. The core challenge, he explains, is that `git worktree add` materializes files from the object database via `checkout_entry()`, which has no visibility into other worktrees. To make CoW work, he proposes a two-phase approach: first, build a mapping of cleanly checked-out blob objects across all worktrees, then hook `checkout_entry()` to intercept writes and perform CoW instead of full copies. He flags a potential TOCTOU (time-of-check-to-time-of-use) race condition as a major hurdle, suggesting post-CoW verification as a mitigation.

The tone is cautious but not dismissive. Junio acknowledges the feature’s potential value while highlighting its complexity, particularly the TOCTOU issue, which he implies would require significant effort to resolve safely. His sketch is a starting point for further discussion rather than a final design, and he explicitly states he has no interest in implementing it himself. The response is likely to shape the thread’s direction, as it shifts the focus from high-level goals to concrete architectural concerns. Readers should note that this is still an RFC, and no implementation exists yet.
