This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) of the first patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s ODB alternates refactoring series. Peff questions the fundamental design choice to use `core.ignoreCase` for deduplicating alternate object database paths, arguing it is both technically incorrect and unnecessarily complex.

The core issue is that `core.ignoreCase` reflects the case-sensitivity of the *current* repository’s filesystem, but alternates may reside on entirely different filesystems with different rules. This can lead to false positives (suppressing non-duplicates) or false negatives (failing to suppress true duplicates), though both are rare in practice. Peff suggests two alternatives: (1) always use case-sensitive comparisons, accepting that duplicates may occasionally slip through but avoiding suppression of non-duplicates, or (2) use a more thorough check that compares `st_dev` and `st_ino` values via `stat()` when paths appear to match case-insensitively. The first option would eliminate the patch’s added complexity entirely; the second would preserve correctness at the cost of slightly more code.

Peff frames the trade-off as one of correctness versus simplicity. The current patch errs on the side of caution by using `core.ignoreCase`, but Peff argues the edge cases are so unlikely that the simpler approach (case-sensitive comparisons) might be preferable. He also notes that even `core.ignoreCase` is an imperfect proxy for filesystem behavior, as filesystems may perform other transformations like Unicode normalization.

This review challenges the series’ foundational assumption and could lead to a significant redesign of the first patch. The discussion is now open: whether to accept the current complexity for theoretical correctness, simplify the code at the risk of rare duplicates, or implement a more robust (but heavier) solution. The outcome will likely influence whether the series proceeds as-is or requires a v3.
