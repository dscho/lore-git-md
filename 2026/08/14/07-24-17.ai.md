This is a refactoring patch series (2 patches) continuing the `the_repository` removal effort. The goal is to move the global `fetch_if_missing` variable into `struct repository`, making it a per-repository flag that controls whether missing objects trigger lazy fetches from promisor remotes. This is the fifth iteration of the patch, addressing the five remaining `the_repository->fetch_if_missing` references identified in Junio’s v4 review.

The series now fully eliminates all direct uses of `the_repository` for `fetch_if_missing`, converting the last holdouts in `builtin/fetch-pack.c`, `builtin/pack-objects.c`, `builtin/rev-list.c`, `common-init.c`, and `git.c` to use the caller-supplied `repo` parameter. The first patch ("pack-objects: give fetch_if_missing call sites access to 'repo'") is preparatory, ensuring the `repo` parameter is passed through the call graph in `pack-objects.c` so the second patch can safely replace the global. The second patch ("repository: move fetch_if_missing into struct repository") is the core change, moving the variable into `struct repository` and updating all call sites.

The patch preserves existing behavior: the default value remains `1` (enabled), and initialization occurs in `initialize_repository()`. Special handling in `fsck.c` and `index-pack.c` (deferring operations until after parameter parsing) and the ternary fallback in `index-pack.c` (`(repo ? repo : the_repository)->fetch_if_missing`) remain unchanged. The partial clone documentation is updated to reflect the new per-repository nature of the flag.

This iteration resolves the mechanical blockers Junio identified in v4, making the patch technically complete for the immediate goal. The broader architectural question of whether `fetch_if_missing` belongs in `struct repository` (or should instead use a global "basic settings" structure for repository-less invocations) remains open, but the author has deferred that discussion until subsystems are fully prepared to handle `repo` instead of `the_repository`. No new test coverage is introduced, but the changes are expected to preserve existing behavior.

The series is well-scoped and addresses the feedback directly. The remaining open question is whether Junio will request further architectural clarification before queuing, but the patch is now mechanically ready for review. It is part of the ongoing libification and `the_repository` removal efforts, and the author has coordinated with other topics to avoid conflicts.
