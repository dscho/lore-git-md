This is the sixth patch in Derrick Stolee’s seven-part series hardening the trace2 subsystem against crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers. The patch removes `ALLOC_GROW()` from `trace2/tr2_tls.c` and replaces it with manual reallocation logic that degrades gracefully under memory pressure instead of crashing the process.

The change is surgical: it adds a new `nr_skipped_regions` counter to track how many times a region was entered but could not be added to the stack due to allocation failures. When `realloc()` fails, the code increments this counter and skips recording the region, allowing the process to continue with a shallower stack. The counter is decremented as regions are exited, preserving the expected nesting behavior for any regions that *were* successfully recorded. If the counter is non-zero, the `tr2tls_region_elasped_self()` helper returns `0` to signal degraded operation to callers.

The patch also extends `banned-die.h` to block `ALLOC_GROW()` in trace2, preventing future regressions. The manual reallocation logic includes an overflow check to avoid undefined behavior when doubling the allocation size, a detail that was implicit in `ALLOC_GROW()` but must now be handled explicitly.

This is a well-crafted, low-risk change that directly addresses the series’ goal of eliminating crash vectors in trace2. The fallback behavior (skipping regions) is a pragmatic trade-off that prioritizes process stability over telemetry completeness, and the new counter ensures the stack remains consistent even when some regions are skipped. The patch is part of a larger effort but stands on its own as a meaningful improvement. Worth attention if you care about Git’s stability or telemetry infrastructure.
