This is the final patch in Toon Claes’s six-part series optimizing the experimental `git last-modified` command by reusing Git’s Bloom filters. It addresses a specific edge case: wildcard pathspecs like `*.c` lose their Bloom filter settings during `prepare_revision_walk()`, causing the per-path Bloom filter lookups to fail and forcing a fallback to expensive diff operations.

The patch restores `bloom_filter_settings` after `prepare_revision_walk()` clears it, ensuring the per-path Bloom filter logic continues to work for wildcards. It also adds a trace2 counter (`bloom_queries`) to instrument how often the per-path check runs, and a new test in `t/t8020-last-modified.sh` that verifies the counter increments exactly twice for a top-level wildcard query (`*.c`). The test writes a commit-graph with `--changed-paths`, disables the commit-graph to establish a baseline, then re-enables it and checks that the Bloom filter fast-path is triggered (counter = 2) while preserving the correct output.

This is a targeted fix for a test gap identified in v2. The instrumentation is lightweight and non-intrusive, and the change itself is minimal: a 10-line conditional restore of `bloom_filter_settings` and a 6-line trace2 counter update. The maintainability concern about restoring settings after `prepare_revision_walk()` was acknowledged in earlier discussion but accepted as sufficient; Jeff King proposed an alternative design but did not insist on it. The patch is ready for integration and completes the series.
