This is a performance optimization for the experimental `git last-modified` command, now in its third iteration (v3). The series reuses Git’s Bloom filter machinery to speed up queries about the last commit that modified files matching a pathspec, including wildcards like `*.c`. The v3 reroll adds a trace2 counter to verify that Bloom filter queries are actually being used for top-level wildcard pathspecs, addressing Junio’s request for test coverage of this edge case.

The benchmarks in the cover letter show a 3.7× speedup without `--show-trees` (from 60.7 ms to 16.2 ms) and a 1.95× speedup with `--show-trees` (from 61.9 ms to 31.8 ms) on the ziglang/zig repository, bringing `git last-modified` nearly to parity with `git log`. The changes are confined to the revision and Bloom filter subsystems, plus the `last-modified` built-in and its test suite. The new trace2 counter (`bloom_queries`) and accompanying test ensure that the optimization is working as intended for wildcard pathspecs, which was the last open question from v2.
