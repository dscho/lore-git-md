This is a **bugfix and hardening series** targeting the trace2 subsystem, now in its third iteration. Derrick Stolee expands the scope beyond the original crash fix to systematically eliminate all `die()`-triggering helpers (e.g., `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, `ALLOC_ARRAY()`, `xstrfmt()`, `ALLOC_GROW()`, `xcalloc()`) from trace2, ensuring telemetry operations never crash Git—even under memory pressure or system call failures. The series introduces `banned-die.h`, a new enforcement mechanism that blocks these functions at compile time, and replaces them with defensive fallbacks (e.g., hardcoded timestamps, `NULL` returns, or static fallback contexts) to prioritize process stability over telemetry completeness.

The v3 update addresses feedback from Jeff King and Elijah Newren. Key fixes include correcting a naming inconsistency (`BANNED(die)` instead of `banned(die)`), fixing an off-by-one error in redaction logic (now properly including the trailing `>` in `:<REDACTED>`), and resolving a regression in the sixth patch by merging it with the seventh. The config parsing path now degrades gracefully by returning `0` (not `-1`) on allocation failure, avoiding misleading error messages. Tests are updated to explicitly validate redaction behavior. Jeff King’s broader critique—that the series doesn’t address indirect `die()` calls via helpers like `strbuf`—is acknowledged but not resolved; Stolee frames the series as a pragmatic first step and plans to investigate a "safe strbuf" library separately.

The series touches 17 files, primarily within trace2, and is self-contained except for the new `banned-die.h` header, which is positioned for future reuse in other subsystems (e.g., to enforce `the_repository` or `the_hash_algo` constraints). No on-disk format changes are made, but trace consumers may see degraded telemetry (e.g., truncated messages, dropped events) under failure conditions. The lack of test coverage for allocation failures is noted as a limitation, though the redaction logic is now better validated. This looks like a well-motivated, incremental hardening effort with clear trade-offs, though Jeff King’s concerns about completeness suggest further work may be needed to fully eliminate crash risks. The series is under active review and appears likely to proceed, with the MinGW-specific follow-up (Taylor Blau’s alternative) still planned as a separate patch.
