This is Junio’s "What’s cooking" report for August 2026, summarizing the state of Git’s integration branches. It’s the project’s heartbeat, listing which topics have graduated to `master`, which are cooking in `next`, which are stalled, and which are new or discarded.

The big news: 21 topics graduated to `master` this cycle, bringing the total since Git 2.55 to 601 non-merge commits. The highlights include Patrick Steinhardt’s ODB pluggability work (`jt/receive-pack-pluggable-writes`, `ps/odb-pluggable-pack-generation`, `ps/odb-alternates-at-creation`, `ps/odb-pluggable-fsck`), which is now complete enough that `receive-pack`, `bundle`, `send-pack`, and `upload-pack` all generate packfiles through the ODB interface rather than hard-coding `pack-objects`. The alternates mechanism is now initialized eagerly at ODB creation time, eliminating scattered lazy-loading calls. The `fsck` builtin has been refactored to move backend-specific checks into the ODB source layers, making them truly pluggable. These changes are foundational for the ongoing ODB abstraction effort and are well-motivated; the series were reviewed thoroughly and the graduation is uncontroversial.

Other graduates: `fr/pack-objects-trace-pack-bytes` adds trace2 output for pack bytes written, useful for performance tuning; `kh/trailers-no-urls` stops mistaking URLs for trailers; `ps/odb-geometric-repack-loose-threshold` aligns geometric repacking with `gc --auto`; `vm/complete-history` adds zsh/bash completion for the experimental `git history` command; `yn/worktree-add-no-dwim-with-b` fixes a DWIM bug when `-b` or `-B` is given.

New topics cooking in `next` or `seen` include `jc/checkout-refactor`, a large refactoring of the checkout/switch/restore front-end that extracts shared helpers and paves the way for libification. It’s a clean, well-structured series with good commit messages, but it touches a lot of code and will need careful review. `ps/odb-alternates-at-creation` and `ps/odb-pluggable-fsck` are follow-ups to the graduated ODB work and look ready for `next`. `en/no-amend-during-conflicts` teaches `commit --amend` and partial commits to refuse during conflict resolution, aligning them with `cherry-pick` and `merge`; the motivation is clear and the implementation looks solid.

Stalled topics include `ec/commit-fixup-options` (waiting for author response since May), `ps/libgit-in-subdir` (ejected due to evil merges), and `tb/pack-with-duplicates` (stalled since July). `tc/replay-linearize` is new and needs review; it adds `--linearize` to `git replay` to drop merge commits, mimicking `rebase --no-rebase-merges`.

The report also lists discarded topics: `kh/format-patch-range-diff-notes` was retracted after discussion, and `jc/die-for-incompatible-opts` was withdrawn in favor of a simpler approach.

Overall, the ODB pluggability work is the standout this cycle; it’s a major architectural change that’s now largely complete and ready for wider use. The checkout refactoring and conflict-resolution safety improvements are also worth watching. The stalled topics are mostly waiting on author follow-up rather than technical objections.
