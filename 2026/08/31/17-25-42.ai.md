This is the sixth patch in Derrick Stolee’s seven-part v3 series hardening the trace2 subsystem against crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers. It removes `ALLOC_GROW()` from `trace2/tr2_tls.c` and replaces it with manual reallocation logic that degrades gracefully under memory pressure.

The patch extends `banned-die.h` to block `ALLOC_GROW()` in trace2, preventing future regressions. In `tr2tls_push_self()`, it replaces the banned helper with a defensive implementation that checks for integer overflow, attempts `realloc()`, and increments a new `nr_skipped_regions` counter on failure. This counter tracks how many regions were entered but not recorded due to allocation failures, ensuring the stack does not grow beyond available memory. When `nr_skipped_regions` is non-zero, `tr2tls_pop_self()` and `tr2tls_region_elasped_self()` short-circuit to avoid operating on an incomplete stack, returning early with safe defaults (e.g., `0` for elapsed time).

The new `nr_skipped_regions` field in `struct tr2tls_thread_ctx` is the key mechanism, allowing trace2 to continue operating (albeit with degraded telemetry) instead of crashing. This aligns with the series’ goal of prioritizing process stability over telemetry completeness. The patch also resolves the v2 splitting error by folding the logic into this patch rather than leaving it for the seventh patch, ensuring no regressions if applied in isolation.
