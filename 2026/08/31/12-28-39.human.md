This is the first patch in Derrick Stolee’s seven-part bugfix and hardening series for the trace2 subsystem, now updated in response to Junio C Hamano’s feedback. The patch introduces `banned-die.h`, a new header designed to block functions that can trigger `die()`—such as `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and `ALLOC_ARRAY()`—from being used in trace2 code. The goal is to prevent Git crashes during telemetry operations, particularly in contexts like `atexit()` handlers where `die()` is unsafe.

Stolee’s reply to Junio expands on the header’s broader potential, framing it as a general-purpose enforcement mechanism that could be reused to ban other traits—like direct use of `the_repository` or `the_hash_algo`—in specific subsystems. He suggests this could help both *declare* a subsystem as free of globals (e.g., for multi-repo or multi-hash readiness) and *prevent* reintroduction of those globals into already-clean code. The tone is exploratory, acknowledging the need for both directions and hinting at future tooling (e.g., static analysis) to automate enforcement.

The patch itself is well-motivated and narrowly scoped, touching only the new header and its inclusion in trace2 files. Junio’s earlier feedback positioned the header as a template for future hardening efforts, and Stolee’s response aligns with that vision. However, Jeff King’s recent review raises a critical architectural concern: the mechanism only blocks *direct* calls to banned functions, missing indirect calls via helpers like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`, which could still trigger `die()`. This limitation isn’t addressed here, but Stolee’s reply doesn’t engage with it—likely deferring to a later iteration.

For readers tracking the series, this patch is a foundational step, but the broader discussion about completeness (and whether a ground-up rewrite of trace2 is needed) remains unresolved. The header’s placement at the root of the tree signals intent for reuse, but its effectiveness hinges on addressing Peff’s critique. No test coverage is added, as the issue depends on transient failures, but the trade-off is documented. The series is under active review, with Elijah Newren’s feedback on later patches already identifying correctness issues (e.g., an off-by-one error in redaction logic). This patch is uncontroversial on its own, but its long-term value depends on how the project responds to the deeper questions raised.
