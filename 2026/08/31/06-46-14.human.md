This is the second iteration of a 10-patch refactoring series from Patrick Steinhardt that makes Git’s object database (ODB) consistency checks pluggable. The goal is to restructure `git fsck` so each ODB backend (loose, packed, multi-pack, etc.) can implement its own verification logic, rather than relying on a monolithic function in `builtin/fsck.c`. This is pure infrastructure work with no user-visible behavior change, but it lays the groundwork for future pluggable ODB backends like reftable or alternate storage systems.

The v2 update addresses all feedback from the initial submission. Changes are minor and editorial: commit message wording tweaks (e.g., “Almost the exact same steps” → “The exact same steps”, “early” → “early return”), a spelling fix (“focussed” → “focused”), and a correction to Karthik Nayak’s restatement of the `--full` flag’s intended behavior (“latter” → “former”). The sole technical question—whether `OPT_BIT` provides equivalent `--no-full` functionality to `OPT_BOOL`—was resolved in v1; Patrick confirmed that bitwise negation replicates the desired semantics.

The series is well-structured and incremental. Each patch moves a specific type of verification (packfiles, reverse indices, bitmaps, MIDX, loose objects) into its respective ODB backend, replacing dedicated error bits (`ERROR_PACK`, `ERROR_BITMAP`, etc.) with the generic `ERROR_OBJECT` bit. Tests are added or extended to verify that the new backend-specific checks respect flags like `--no-full` and `--connectivity-only`. Patch 10/10 also fixes a latent progress-meter bug where the meter jumped from 0 to 256 due to premature capture in the callback data.

Karthik Nayak’s reviews were surface-level, focusing on wording and spelling rather than technical correctness or edge cases. His feedback was fully addressed, and he stated the series “looks good” with no substantive concerns. The series depends on two earlier topics (`ps/odb-eagerly-load-alternates` and `ps/odb-pluggable-pack-generation`) and is based on Junio’s integration branch as of 2026-08-24. No maintainer decision has been recorded, but the refactoring appears uncontroversial and aligns with the ongoing ODB abstraction effort.
