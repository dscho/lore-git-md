This is a brief but substantive follow-up in the ongoing trace2 hardening thread. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio C Hamano’s musing about whether the new `banned-die.h` pattern could be extended to other global variables like `the_repository` or `the_hash_algo`. Patrick points out that Git already uses a similar mechanism for `the_repository` via the `USE_THE_REPOSITORY_VARIABLE` macro, which conditionally hides function declarations that depend on the global. He suggests that the same incremental approach—adding `#ifdef` guards to hide more functions over time—could work for `die()`-prone functions, though he acknowledges the current mechanism isn’t perfect because it doesn’t catch implicit dependencies.

The observation is constructive and aligns with the thread’s broader architectural discussion. It doesn’t propose a concrete change to Derrick Stolee’s series but reinforces the idea that `banned-die.h` could evolve into a reusable pattern for other hardening efforts, such as the ongoing `the_repository` removal. The tone is pragmatic, and the reply doesn’t challenge the current series or introduce new objections. It’s a small but useful data point for anyone tracking how Git’s enforcement mechanisms might scale beyond trace2.
