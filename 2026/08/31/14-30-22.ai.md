This is a follow-up in the ongoing bugfix thread about negative pathspec handling in `git ls-files` and `git add`. The author (Diogo Castro) clarifies his position on the performance implications of Junio C Hamano's proposal to recalculate the common prefix to include negative pathspecs. He argues that the "common prefix" optimization is purely a performance feature for positive pathspecs and that extending it to negatives could degrade performance in cases like `git add -- a/b/c a/b/d ':!a/**/x'`, where the common prefix would shrink from `a/b/` to `a/`, forcing a broader working-directory walk.

The reply is substantive and technical, directly addressing the design trade-off between correctness and performance. Diogo provides concrete examples to illustrate how Junio's proposal could hurt performance without improving correctness, reinforcing his original patch's approach of leaving negative pathspecs unaffected by the positive-path prefix optimization. The discussion remains unresolved, with no alternative implementation proposed yet, but this reply sharpens the focus on the performance consequences of the two competing designs.
