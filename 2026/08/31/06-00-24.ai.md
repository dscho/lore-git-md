This is a reply in the ongoing refactoring series that makes Git’s ODB fsck checks pluggable. The email is a short exchange between Patrick Steinhardt (author) and Karthik Nayak (reviewer) about the first patch in the 10-patch series, which replaces open-coded loose-object verification in `builtin/fsck.c` with a call to the existing `fsck_obj_buffer()` helper.

The discussion is purely editorial. Karthik suggests dropping the word “Almost” from the commit message (“Almost the exact same steps” → “the exact same steps”), observes that the `free(contents)` call has moved to after the `fsck_obj_buffer()` invocation (which is harmless), and notes that `fsck_obj_buffer()` also sets the `ERROR_OBJECT` flag (which is redundant but not problematic). Patrick accepts the wording nit and explains why he kept the redundant flag-setting: to avoid calling the function without checking its return value, even though the flag is already set internally. No technical concerns, edge cases, or design questions are raised; the feedback is surface-level and the patch’s correctness is not in dispute. The series remains on track for eventual integration, pending resolution of the minor `OPT_BIT` vs `OPT_BOOL` question in patch 5/10.
