This is the v2 cover letter for Patrick Steinhardt’s 10-patch series that makes Git’s object-database consistency checks pluggable. The series is part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort and refactors `git fsck` to move verification logic out of `builtin/fsck.c` and into the respective ODB source implementations (loose, packed, multi-pack, etc.). No user-visible behavior changes; the goal is to prepare for future pluggable ODB backends.

v2 incorporates only minor editorial feedback from Karthik Nayak: commit-message wording tweaks (“Almost” dropped in patch 1, “early” → “early return” in patch 2, “focussed” → “focused” in patch 8) and a correction to Karthik’s restatement of the `--full` flag’s intended behavior in patch 4 (“latter” → “former”). The sole technical question—whether `OPT_BIT` provides equivalent `--no-full` functionality to `OPT_BOOL`—was resolved in v1; Patrick confirmed that `OPT_BIT`’s bitwise negation replicates the desired semantics.

The series touches 14 files, removing 259 lines from `builtin/fsck.c` and adding 394 lines across `odb/source-*.c`, `odb.h`, and test scripts. Key symbols introduced: `fsck_obj_buffer()` (the sole entry point for object verification), backend-specific verification hooks (`odb_source_fsck_fn`), `struct odb_fsck_options`, and flags `ODB_FSCK_FULL` and `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE`. Patch 10/10 also fixes a latent progress-meter bug that caused the meter to jump from 0 to 256 at completion. Tests verify that the new backend-specific checks respect the `--no-full` and `--connectivity-only` flags.

All feedback has been addressed; the series is now a clean refactoring with no open technical questions. Karthik’s reviews were surface-level, focusing on wording and spelling rather than substantive correctness or edge cases. The series depends on two earlier topics (`ps/odb-eagerly-load-alternates` and `ps/odb-pluggable-pack-generation`) and is based on Junio’s integration branch as of 2026-08-24.
