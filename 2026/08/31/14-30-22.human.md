This is a follow-up in the ongoing bugfix thread about negative pathspec handling in `git ls-files` and `git add`. The author, Diogo Castro, clarifies his position on the performance implications of Junio C Hamano’s proposal to recalculate the common prefix to include negative pathspecs.

Diogo argues that the "common prefix" optimization is purely a performance feature for positive pathspecs and should not be extended to negatives. He provides two examples to illustrate the trade-offs: in `git add -- a/b/c a/b/d ':!a/b/x'`, the current approach limits the working-directory walk to `a/b/`, while Junio’s proposal would not change this. However, in `git add -- a/b/c a/b/d ':!a/**/x'`, Junio’s approach would force a broader walk (`a/` instead of `a/b/`), hurting performance. Diogo’s patch preserves the current optimization by leaving negative pathspecs unaffected by prefix-stripping.

The discussion remains unresolved. Junio has not yet responded to this clarification, and no alternative implementation has been proposed. The key question is whether negative pathspecs should be treated as absolute (Diogo’s approach) or relative to the full tree when they share a prefix with positives (Junio’s proposal). The thread is now focused on the performance implications of these design choices.
