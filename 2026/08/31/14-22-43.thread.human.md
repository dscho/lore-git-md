This thread is about an inconsistency in Git’s branch name validation: porcelain commands like `checkout -b`, `switch -c`, and `branch` allow creating a branch named `@`, even though `@` is rejected by `git check-ref-format` and cannot later be checked out (since `@` is interpreted as `HEAD`). The discussion has crystallized around a design trade-off: should Git tighten validation to reject bare `@` as a branch name (preventing user mistakes) or preserve it as a convenient shorthand for `HEAD` in complex expressions?

Key participants include Bence Csókás, who reported the issue and now argues for preserving `@`’s usability, and Jeff King (Peff), who supports stricter validation, citing precedent (e.g., `HEAD` is already rejected in `--branch` mode) and proposing to treat bare `@` similarly while allowing compound names like `foo@bar`. D. Ben Knoble and Kristoffer Haugsbakk have weighed in with surface-level endorsements of the convenience and deprecation arguments, respectively, but neither has moved the discussion forward. The thread remains split, with no patch proposed yet, and the next step will likely hinge on a patch that either implements stricter validation or documents the status quo, addressing both the usability trap and the convenience trade-off.
