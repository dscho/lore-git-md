This thread is a design discussion about an inconsistency in Git’s branch name validation: porcelain commands like `git checkout -b` and `git switch -c` allow creating a branch named `@`, even though `@` is rejected by `git check-ref-format` and cannot be checked out afterward (because `@` is interpreted as `HEAD`). The debate centers on whether to tighten validation to prevent accidental branch creation or preserve `@` as a convenient shorthand for `HEAD` in complex expressions.

Key participants include Bence Csókás (who reported the issue), Jeff King (Peff), and Junio C Hamano. Peff argues for treating bare `@` like `HEAD`—rejecting it in `--branch` mode while preserving compound names like `foo@bar`—citing this as a targeted fix. Junio’s recent clarification that his 2024 stance on deprecating `@` was incomplete has reset the discussion to evaluating the trade-offs on their merits, with no clear consensus yet. D. Ben Knoble and Kristoffer Haugsbakk have also contributed, the latter retracting an earlier citation that had muddied the debate.

The thread remains under review with no patch proposed. The core tension is between usability (keeping `@` as a shorthand) and safety (preventing the usability trap of an uncheckoutable branch). The inconsistency stems from `git check-ref-format --branch` incorrectly accepting `@`, and any fix would likely target this validation logic in `refs.c` and `check-ref-format.c`. For now, the discussion is stuck on whether the convenience outweighs the risk.
