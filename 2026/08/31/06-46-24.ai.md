This is the final patch (10/10) in Patrick Steinhardt’s v2 series making Git’s ODB fsck checks pluggable. It moves loose-object verification from `builtin/fsck.c` into the loose ODB backend (`odb/source-loose.c`), completing the refactoring begun in earlier patches. The patch removes 90 lines from `cmd_fsck()` and adds 89 lines to `odb/source-loose.c`, introducing a new `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE` flag to preserve the original behavior of printing status messages when `--verbose` is passed. It also fixes a latent bug in the progress-meter logic: the old code captured the progress meter in the callback data before it was initialized, causing the meter to jump from 0 to 256 at completion; the new code initializes the meter first, restoring incremental updates.

The patch is a pure refactoring with no user-visible behavior changes. It threads the `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE` and `ODB_FSCK_PROGRESS` flags through the new `struct odb_fsck_options`, ensuring the loose backend respects caller preferences without exposing backend details. The `fsck_loose()` helper is relocated and adapted to use the new `struct fsck_loose_data`, which consolidates the callback data and error state. The patch also removes the last direct use of `for_each_loose_file_in_source()` from `builtin/fsck.c`, leaving `odb_fsck()` as the sole entry point for object verification.

This patch depends on the scaffolding introduced in patches 1–9, particularly the `odb_fsck_options` structure and the `odb_source_fsck_fn` callback. With this patch, all fsck checks are now implemented in their respective ODB backends, and `builtin/fsck.c` no longer contains any backend-specific verification logic. The series is now complete and ready for integration, with all feedback from v1 and v2 addressed. Karthik Nayak’s reviews of earlier patches were surface-level, focusing on wording and spelling rather than substantive correctness or edge cases. No open technical questions remain.
