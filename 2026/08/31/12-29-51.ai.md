This is a **bugfix follow-up** to the first patch in Derrick Stolee’s seven-part hardening series for the trace2 subsystem. The email addresses two technical concerns raised by Elijah Newren in his substantive review of the `banned-die.h` enforcement mechanism: a **naming inconsistency** and an **include-ordering risk**.

Derrick acknowledges both issues matter-of-factly. The naming error (`banned(die)` instead of `BANNED(die)`) was introduced during a rebase and will be fixed in the next iteration. The include-ordering risk—where future includes added *after* `banned-die.h` could silently bypass enforcement—is real; Derrick notes the header already documents this requirement but agrees a local comment in `trace2.c` would help. Neither change alters the series’ core goal (eliminating `die()`-triggering functions from trace2), but both are small, actionable improvements that tighten the mechanism’s long-term reliability.

The tone is collaborative and low-key, treating the feedback as routine polish rather than a fundamental critique. No new architectural questions are raised, and the discussion remains narrowly focused on the two mechanical issues. This reply does not engage with Jeff King’s broader concern about indirect `die()`-prone dependencies (e.g., via `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`), which remains an open question for the series.
