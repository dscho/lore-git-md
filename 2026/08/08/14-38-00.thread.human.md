This thread is a provocative critique of Git’s `--signoff` (`-s`) option and the `Signed-off-by:` trailer it adds to commit messages. Дилян Палаузов argues that the sign-off convention—meant to certify copyright ownership and transfer—is legally redundant and practically counterproductive. He contends that copyright law already protects published patches, making the trailer unnecessary, and that its enforcement can create unintended barriers: patches without sign-offs might block others from fixing similar bugs until the original author’s copyright expires (typically 70 years after death). He also questions whether deleted code can even be copyrighted, calling the idea "insane."

Beyond the legal critique, Палаузов frames sign-off requirements as a form of gatekeeping, suggesting they disproportionately burden contributors who may not fully understand the legal implications—particularly non-native English speakers. He cites examples where projects retroactively added sign-offs to others’ patches, undermining the trailer’s intended purpose. The email is deliberately confrontational, positioning the sign-off as bureaucratic rather than protective, and is likely to spark debate about Git’s contribution policies and the assumptions behind `Signed-off-by:`. So far, no patches or concrete proposals have been made; the discussion remains conceptual.
