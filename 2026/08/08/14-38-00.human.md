This is a discussion thread, not a patch. The author, Дилян Палаузов, argues that the `-s` (`--signoff`) option for `git commit` is problematic and makes little sense in its current form.

The core of the argument is that the `Signed-off-by:` trailer is widely interpreted as a copyright transfer mechanism, but this interpretation is legally dubious and practically harmful. The author contends that requiring sign-offs can block bug fixes if the original patch was published without one, even if the fix is the only reasonable way to address the issue. They also question whether deleted code can be copyrighted and point out that people often add sign-off lines without fully understanding their legal implications, especially non-native English speakers.

The email cites real-world examples where projects have insisted on sign-offs, leading to awkward workarounds (like adding redundant sign-off lines to others' patches) or even including unrelated text in commit messages to satisfy project policies. The author suggests that the requirement is often followed mechanically rather than meaningfully, and that it creates an uneven playing field for contributors who are paid to comply versus those who are not.

This is a philosophical critique rather than a technical one, and it’s likely to spark debate about Git’s contribution policies and the legal assumptions behind them. The thread may interest readers who follow project governance, licensing, or contributor workflow issues, but it doesn’t propose a code change or call for action—just reflection.
