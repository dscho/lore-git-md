This is a discussion thread opener questioning the rationale behind the `-s` (`--signoff`) option in `git commit`.

The author, Дилян Палаузов, argues that the `Signed-off-by:` trailer—intended to certify copyright ownership and transfer—is conceptually flawed and practically problematic. They contend that copyright law already protects published patches, making the sign-off redundant or even harmful: if a patch is published without a sign-off, its copyright remains with the author, potentially blocking others from fixing the same bug in a similar way until the author’s death (or until copyright expires). They also question whether deleted code can be copyrighted at all, calling the notion "insane."

The email further criticizes projects that enforce sign-off requirements as a gatekeeping mechanism, suggesting contributors may add the trailer without understanding its legal implications—especially non-native English speakers. The author implies this creates an uneven playing field, where some contributors are paid to comply while others are not. They cite real-world examples where sign-off enforcement led to awkward workarounds, like retroactively adding the trailer to others' patches.

No code changes or patches are proposed; this is purely a conceptual critique of the sign-off convention. The tone is skeptical and provocative, framing the sign-off requirement as a bureaucratic hurdle rather than a meaningful safeguard.
