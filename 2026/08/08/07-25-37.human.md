This is the **behavior-changing patch** in Christian Couder’s `git fast-import` libification series, and Elijah Newren’s **substantive review** of it. The patch switches the command-line parser from a hand-written loop to the standard `parse_options()` API, removing support for five undocumented options (`--alias`, `--get-mark`, `--ls`, `--notes`, and `--cat-blob`) on the command line and enabling abbreviated options like `--dep=5`.

Elijah’s review identifies **three unlisted behavioral changes** that the patch introduces. First, a bare `--` is now accepted as the end-of-options marker, whereas the old parser treated it as a usage error. Second, value-taking options now accept the space-separated `--opt value` form (e.g., `--depth 5`) in addition to `--opt=value`. Both of these are conventional `parse_options()` behaviors and are improvements, but they were not mentioned in the commit message.

The third change is more subtle and potentially problematic: the handling of `--allow-unsafe-features` now disagrees between the early scan (which looks for the exact spelling and stops at the first non-option token) and `parse_options()` (which accepts abbreviations and space-separated values). This can cause the in-stream feature to be rejected even though the command-line option itself is accepted, surprising users. Elijah suggests documenting this as a known shortcoming or adjusting the early scan to match `parse_options()` behavior, possibly by requiring `--allow-unsafe-features` to be the first argument.

The rest of the patch is uncontroversial, and Elijah’s feedback is narrowly focused on these behavioral edge cases rather than the overall direction. The series remains under review, and this review is the first substantive technical engagement beyond Junio’s initial feedback. If the author addresses the `--allow-unsafe-features` inconsistency, the patch is likely to proceed smoothly.
