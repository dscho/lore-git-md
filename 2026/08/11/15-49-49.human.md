This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Taylor Blau’s bugfix series for `git maintenance`’s geometric repack task. The thread focuses on the first patch, which modifies how promisor and non-promisor packs are evaluated for repacking.

Taylor responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s concern that the current logic might under-trigger all-into-one repacks, potentially leaving unreachable objects unpruned. Taylor argues that the geometric repacking logic is designed to eventually converge both promisor and non-promisor packs toward a single pack, at which point an all-into-one repack would occur. He acknowledges that if the two progressions are "perfectly out of phase," the fallback might not trigger, but considers this an unlikely edge case. He maintains that the current behavior is still an improvement over the regression introduced by the earlier promisor pack support.

The discussion remains open, but Taylor’s response suggests he is not yet convinced the edge case warrants a change. No new code or test adjustments are proposed here—this is purely a conceptual exchange about the trade-offs between geometric efficiency and garbage collection. The second patch in the series (adjusting the `--auto` trigger) remains uncontested.
