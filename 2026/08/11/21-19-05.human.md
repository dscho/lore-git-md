This is a **security-adjacent configuration debate** in the ongoing thread about `http.sslVerifyHost`. Brian M. Carlson joins Patrick Steinhardt in **opposing the patch**, reinforcing the argument that the proposed option creates a false sense of security.

Carlson’s reply is blunt: he agrees with Steinhardt that the option oversells its benefits and is easy to misuse. He goes further, dismissing the unsecured Wi-Fi use case entirely—ARP spoofing means even passive eavesdroppers can become active attackers, so the distinction between passive and active threats is moot. His technical point about modern TLS (subjectAltName over CN) undercuts the original motivation: self-signed certificates without valid hostnames are already a solved problem with ACME or DANE, making the patch’s use case obsolete.

The tone is decisive: Carlson doesn’t just question the patch’s security model—he argues it shouldn’t exist at all. This shifts the thread from a documentation discussion to a fundamental rejection, with two respected voices now aligned against the feature. The author, Ron Nazarov, hasn’t yet responded, so the next move is theirs—but the bar for justification just got higher.
