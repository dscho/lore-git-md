This is a **security-adjacent configuration option** discussion, specifically a reply in the `http.sslVerifyHost` thread. Brian M. Carlson joins Patrick Steinhardt in **strongly opposing** the proposed `http.sslVerifyHost` option, reinforcing the skepticism about its security model and expanding on the technical and practical reasons for rejection.

Carlson’s response is **substantive and technical**, directly challenging the premise that the option provides meaningful security. He argues that **any unsecured network (including Wi-Fi) allows active man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks via ARP spoofing**, meaning that even passive eavesdropping scenarios are not safe. This undermines the original use case—decentralized setups with self-signed certificates—by asserting that the proposed knob offers no real protection against the most likely threats. His position is that **the option would create a false sense of security** without addressing the core vulnerability.

Beyond the security critique, Carlson raises **practical and modern alternatives**: ACME (Let’s Encrypt) and DANE make it easy to deploy properly configured TLS certificates, eliminating the need for self-signed certificates with invalid Common Names (CNs). He also notes that **CNs are obsolete** in modern TLS verification (replaced by subjectAltName), further weakening the argument for accommodating broken certificates. His conclusion is unequivocal: **Git should not accept this option** because it is unnecessary, misleading, and prone to misuse.

The email does not engage with implementation details (e.g., default values, backward compatibility) or suggest amendments to the patch. Instead, it **rejects the feature outright** on both security and practical grounds. This shifts the thread from a discussion about *how* to implement the option to whether it should exist at all. Future replies may need to address Carlson’s points about ACME/DANE adoption or argue for niche use cases where those alternatives are genuinely unavailable.

Key technical details for follow-up discussion:
- **ARP spoofing** as a vector for active MITM on unsecured networks.
- **subjectAltName** as the modern replacement for CN in TLS certificates.
- **ACME and DANE** as accessible solutions for decentralized TLS deployment.
- The **false sense of security** risk posed by partial verification knobs.
