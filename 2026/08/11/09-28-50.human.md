This is the final patch in a now-ready-to-merge performance series targeting Git’s commit-reach subsystem. It implements the core optimization: **early termination of `paint_down_to_common()` when one side of the merge-base query exhausts its commit queue**, eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories.

The patch adds the termination condition to `paint_queue_get()`: once the walk enters the **ordered region** (where topological ordering holds), it exits early if either `parent1_count` or `parent2_count` drops to zero, no pending merge-base candidates remain, and the current commit’s generation is below the topological ceiling (`GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX` for v1 commit-graphs, `GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY` for v2). This gate ensures the optimization only fires when it is safe—no new merge-base can form without both sides meeting.

The change is **measurable**: in a git.git query (`merge-base --all v2.55.0-rc1 v2.55.0-rc1~5`), step counts drop from 110 to 7, a **15x speedup**.
