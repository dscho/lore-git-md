This thread is about a small but important fix to Git's auto-maintenance feature. Patrick Steinhardt identified that the geometric repacking strategy was triggering too frequently—whenever there were just 100 loose objects, compared to `git gc`'s much higher threshold of 6,700. This mismatch was causing instability in busy repositories due to race conditions between repacking and loose object pruning.

The fix itself is straightforward: a single patch that changes the default threshold from 100 to 6,700, aligning it with `git gc`. Patrick provided historical context showing that 6,700 was chosen back in 2007 to balance pragmatism and stability, and both he and the reviewer, Justin Tobler, agree this is the simplest and most conservative solution. Elijah Newren has now also reviewed the patch, confirming its correctness and adding a minor technical clarification about the loose object count heuristic. No one has raised any objections or requested changes, and the patch is now flagged for Junio's attention, though it hasn't yet appeared in a "What's cooking" report.

The discussion is effectively closed—everyone involved agrees the patch is ready to go. The only loose end is whether Junio will pick it up, but there’s nothing more for the author or reviewers to do at this point. If you’ve been away from the list, this is a low-risk, high-impact fix that should land soon.
