This thread is about a small but important fix to Git's auto-maintenance feature. Patrick Steinhardt identified that the geometric repacking strategy was triggering too often—whenever there were just 100 loose objects—compared to `git gc`'s much higher threshold of 6,700. This mismatch was causing instability in busy repositories due to race conditions between repacking and pruning loose objects.

The fix itself is straightforward: change the default threshold from 100 to 6,700 to match `git gc`. Justin Tobler reviewed the patch and called it "trivially correct," though he noted the commit message could better explain why 6,700 was chosen. Patrick responded by clarifying that the issue isn't the estimation method (which counts objects in just one fan-out directory and multiplies by 256) but the overly aggressive default. Even a perfect count would still trigger repacks too often at 100 objects, so aligning with `git gc`'s long-standing default is the simplest and safest fix.

The patch is now under review with no outstanding technical objections. It touches just two files (`Documentation/config/maintenance.adoc` and `odb/source-files.c`) and changes only a configuration default, with no on-disk format or algorithmic changes. The discussion remains focused on balancing repack frequency with stability in high-concurrency environments.
