This is the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s four-part refactoring of Git’s object database (ODB) subsystem to remove lazy-loading of alternate object databases. The patch centralizes alternate initialization by loading them eagerly during ODB creation (`odb_new()`) and cache flushing (`odb_prepare()`), eliminating 13 scattered calls to `odb_prepare_alternates()` across 10 files. The goal is to simplify the codebase and reduce the risk of bugs from missing or redundant calls.

Justin Tobler’s review is brief but substantive. He confirms the patch’s direction ("This sounds like the right direction and overall much simpler") and validates the two key changes: eager initialization at ODB creation and during cache flushing. His only nit is a minor grammar suggestion in the commit message ("where we read" instead of "where read"), which Patrick can trivially address. The review is positive and raises no concerns about correctness, performance, or edge cases.

The patch itself is mechanically clean, with a clear diff showing the removal of redundant calls. It builds on the preceding patch (1/4), which resolved the historical blocker for eager initialization—case-sensitivity handling for alternate paths—by decoupling path comparisons from `the_repository`. While the change may affect performance in edge cases (e.g., commands like `git fsck` that previously avoided loading alternates when none were present), the simplification and reduced bug surface area appear to justify the trade-off.

This patch is part of Patrick’s broader ODB abstraction effort, which aims to make Git’s object storage layer more modular. The series is under active review, and this patch looks ready to advance. Reviewers may still want to scrutinize the performance implications, but Justin’s feedback suggests no major objections.
