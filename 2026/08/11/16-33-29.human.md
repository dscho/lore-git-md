This is Junio C Hamano’s latest reply in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. Junio pushes back on Patrick’s argument that the reorganization helps newcomers, countering that the current layout—with commands in `builtin/`, subsystems like `refs/` and `odb/` in their own directories, and the rest at the root—already provides meaningful organization. He contrasts the proposed mass move to `lib/` with past subsystem relocations (e.g., `builtin/`, `refs/`, `odb/`), which were motivated by substantive updates and delivered clear organizational benefits. In his view, the `lib/` directory is an arbitrary intermediate layer that fails to improve discoverability, as it would still require further classification to be useful. The implication is that the patch introduces churn without addressing the root problem: Git’s complexity is inherent to its 20-year history, not its file layout.

The email advances the thread by **sharpening the structural critique** of the `lib/` approach. Junio’s argument hinges on the idea that meaningful reorganization requires targeted, subsystem-specific moves (e.g., carving out a new directory for a major update) rather than a mechanical mass rename. This aligns with his earlier preference for incremental changes and suggests he sees no path forward for the current patch unless it adopts a more granular approach. The debate remains deadlocked, with Junio’s maintainer perspective likely to carry significant weight in the final decision.
