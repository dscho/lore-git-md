This is the sixth patch in the v8 series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. The series is now fully reviewed, technically complete, and queued in Junio’s tree, with this patch adding critical test coverage for clock-skew edge cases that could break the side-exhaustion optimization.

The patch introduces two new topologies (`se-*` and `se2-*`) to test how the algorithm behaves when commit dates violate topological ordering (e.g., a parent with a lower date than its child). These scenarios are common in repositories with clock skew or import grafts, and the optimization’s early termination could previously fire too early, missing valid merge bases or returning overly deep candidates. The tests validate that generation-number ordering (used by the new code) correctly handles these cases, while commit-date ordering (the old fallback) would fail.

Additionally, the patch adds step-count assertions to existing edge-case tests, including a new "mixed finite/INFINITY" topology where one tip is outside the commit-graph (generation `INFINITY`) and the other is inside (finite generation). This exercises the transition between generation-number regions, ensuring the optimization works across the entire history. The step counts (e.g., `test_paint_down_steps 6 4 6 6` for `se-*`) provide deterministic validation of the performance improvements, replacing wall-clock benchmarks with observable Trace2 metrics.

The test improvements are directly tied to the series’ core goal: hardening the side-exhaustion optimization against real-world edge cases. The `se2-*` topology, for example, ensures that redundant ancestors (like `se2-MB2`) don’t cause the algorithm to terminate prematurely before finding the correct merge base (`se2-MB1`). These tests were explicitly requested in review to address concerns about clock-skew invariants, and their inclusion closes the last major technical gap in the series.

For a reader tracking the series, this patch confirms that the optimization is robust across all commit-graph modes (none/full/half/no-gdat) and edge cases. The step counts also serve as a baseline for future performance comparisons.
