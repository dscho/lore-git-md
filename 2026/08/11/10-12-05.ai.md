This is the fifth patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s v2 series unifying the ODB streaming API, and it is the centerpiece of the refactoring. The patch merges `struct odb_read_stream` and `struct odb_write_stream` into a single `struct odb_stream` that can handle both directions, eliminating redundancy and preparing the codebase for pluggable ODB backends.

The change is mostly mechanical, but two sites—`builtin/unpack-objects.c` and `odb/streaming.c`—require special handling. Instead of embedding the old `struct odb_write_stream` directly, these now allocate a wrapper structure (`struct input_zstream_data` and `struct read_object_fd_data`, respectively) that embeds the new `struct odb_stream` base. The wrapper’s data is accessed via `container_of()`, replacing the old `stream->data` pointer. The rest of the patch updates type names and function signatures across 14 files, replacing `struct odb_write_stream *` with `struct odb_stream *` and renaming `odb_write_stream_read()` to `odb_stream_read()`.

Karthik Nayak’s review is **surface-level**. He notes the trade-off in the new design: the unified `char *` buffer type loses some type safety (previously, read streams used `char *` and write streams used `unsigned char *`) but gains flexibility. He does not raise any edge cases, performance concerns, or correctness issues, and his feedback is limited to validating the structural intent. The wrapper structures remain misnamed for now; these will be addressed in subsequent patches (Patch 6/8 and Patch 7/8).

The patch is uncontroversial and ready for integration. Reviewers familiar with the ODB may focus on the wrapper allocation logic and whether the `container_of` pattern is used correctly, but the patch itself introduces no new behavior or on-disk format changes. It is a pure refactoring that aligns the read and write stream designs, completing the preparatory work for the ODB abstraction effort.
