This is Patrick Steinhardt’s reply to Taylor Blau’s substantive review of the `lib/` directory reorganization patch. The email refines the patch’s motivation, shifting focus from improving individual file discoverability within `lib/` to **reducing root-directory clutter** to make non-library files (e.g., `README.md`, `SECURITY.md`, `Makefile`, and subsystem directories like `refs/` and `builtin/`) more visible to newcomers. Patrick acknowledges Taylor’s critique—that the `lib/` directory does little to clarify the project’s structure for contributors not yet thinking in terms of `libgit.a`—but argues that the reorganization’s primary benefit is **filtering out noise** rather than organizing library code. He concedes that the `lib/` name may be misleading (since many moved files are not suitable for external linking) but maintains that the distinction between library and non-library files is valuable in itself.

The reply does not address Junio’s or Taylor’s broader structural concerns—such as the `lib/` directory’s arbitrary intermediate layer or the preference for incremental subsystem-by-subsystem moves—but instead **narrows the patch’s scope** to a more modest goal: improving discoverability of non-library files. This framing may resonate with contributors who see root-directory clutter as a real problem, but it risks leaving unresolved the **ongoing disruption** to tools like `git log --follow` and the **persistent mental overhead** of file moves (e.g., `wt-status.c` → `lib/wt-status.c`). The email does not propose concrete mitigations for these workflow issues, nor does it engage with Kaartic Sivaraam’s `git show` hint proposal or Junio’s incremental reorganization alternative. The debate remains **polarized**, with Patrick’s refined rationale unlikely to sway critics who prioritize stability over structural cleanup.
