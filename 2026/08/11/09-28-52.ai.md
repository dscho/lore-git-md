This is the final patch in a now-queued performance series targeting Git’s commit-reach subsystem. It removes the long-standing commit-date ordering fallback in `paint_down_to_common()`, completing the transition to pure generation-number ordering that the rest of the series enables.

The fallback was introduced in 2018 to mitigate a performance regression on the Linux kernel repo when v1 commit-graphs (topo levels only) caused asymmetric queries like `git merge-base v4.8 v4.9` to walk 636k commits instead of 167k. The problem arose because a side branch with a low topo level stayed in the queue behind a long chain, preventing early STALE propagation. The series’ side-exhaustion optimization (added in earlier patches) now solves this differently: it terminates the walk as soon as one paint side empties, reducing step counts far below what the date-ordering fallback achieved (e.g., 5.7k steps for v1 graphs, 3.9k for v2, vs. 167k baseline).

Removing the fallback simplifies the code by eliminating the `gen_ordered` flag and the conditional queue ordering. The `topo_ceiling` field (introduced earlier in the series) already handles v1 graph saturation (`GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX`), so the early-exit logic remains correct. The patch also updates the technical document (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`) to remove outdated sections about the fallback’s limitations (e.g., disabling single-result and side-exhaustion optimizations). Test expectations in `t6600-test-reach.sh` are adjusted to reflect the reduced step counts from pure generation ordering (e.g., `merge-base --all` drops from 81 to 37 steps in no-gdat mode).

The change is low-risk: the side-exhaustion optimization and deterministic step-count tests ensure the walk terminates correctly, and the series has been fully reviewed and queued in Junio’s tree. No further action is needed.
