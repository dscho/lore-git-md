This is the v3 cover letter for Christian Couder’s 12-patch series refactoring `git fast-import`. The series is now fully reviewed by Junio and Elijah Newren; all feedback has been addressed and the series is ready for broader attention.

The goal is twofold: bring the command’s usage string into line with its SYNOPSIS documentation, and lay the groundwork for libification by reducing global variables and switching to the parse-options API. The first patch introduces a new `OPT_HIDDEN_GROUP` macro that will be useful for other commands; patches 2–3 expand the parse-options API documentation. Patches 4–10 are mechanical refactorings—localizing loop counters, factoring out option-parsing helpers, introducing a `struct fast_import_state` to encapsulate globals, and converting the usage string to `struct option`. Patch 11 is the behavior-changing core: it switches the command-line parser from a hand-written loop to `parse_options()`, rejecting five undocumented options (`--alias`, `--get-mark`, `--ls`, `--notes`, `--cat-blob`) on the command line (they are stream-only) and enabling abbreviated options, space-separated values, and bare `--`. The final patch is a cleanup that drops a redundant `from_stream` argument.

The only known limitation is a subtle inconsistency with `--allow-unsafe-features`: the early scan for this option (needed to process in-stream “feature” lines) is stricter than `parse_options()`, so command lines like `--allow-unsafe` or `--depth 5 --allow-unsafe-features` are accepted by the parser but still reject unsafe features from the stream. This is now documented in the man page and commit message, and flagged with a `NEEDSWORK` comment; the author plans a follow-up series to teach the early scan about value-taking options. CI passes except for a likely unrelated Windows+Meson failure. The series is well-structured, thoroughly tested, and explicitly scoped to leave deeper libification for future work.
