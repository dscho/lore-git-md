This is a refactoring series from Junio C Hamano that restructures the `git checkout`, `git switch`, and `git restore` code to eliminate the monolithic `checkout_main()` choke point. The series is now in its second iteration (`v2`), addressing feedback on the first version and making two key corrections: fixing argument ordering in `die_for_incompatible_optN()` calls (patch 7) and updating `post_checkout_hook()` to accept a `struct repository *` parameter (patch 8), removing its dependency on `the_repository`.

The refactoring is part of the broader libification effort, where reusable logic is extracted from built-in commands into standalone helpers that accept a `struct repository *` parameter. This aligns with the architectural direction established in earlier threads: built-in commands are not libified, but their reusable internals are. The series is explicitly positioned as an early draft for discussion, not yet intended for merging to `seen` due to potential conflicts with other in-flight topics.

The first six patches are mechanical refactorings that extract utility functions for option validation, pathspec handling, and branch setup. Patch 6 is the core architectural change, rewriting the three command entry points (`cmd_checkout()`, `cmd_switch()`, `cmd_restore()`) to call the extracted helpers directly, bypassing `checkout_main()`. Patch 7 is a style cleanup, and patch 8 moves `post_checkout_hook()` to a new top-level `checkout.c` file, making it available to other subsystems like `git worktree`. The series touches `builtin/checkout.c` heavily (557 lines changed) and introduces new files `checkout.c` and `checkout.h`, but does not change CLI behavior or on-disk formats.

The series is uncontroversial and passes the test suite, but its scale (328 insertions, 267 deletions) and potential for conflicts with other checkout-related work make it a candidate for careful review. Junio has addressed the feedback from the first version, and the changes in `v2` improve consistency and multi-repository support. The first seven patches could be merged independently if the eighth is deferred, but the series remains a discussion starter for now. No objections or alternatives have been raised yet, but broader feedback from contributors like René Scharfe or Patrick Steinhardt is still pending.
