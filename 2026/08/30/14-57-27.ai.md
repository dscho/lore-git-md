This is a reply in the ongoing bugfix thread about negative pathspec handling in `git ls-files` and `git add`. The author (Diogo Castro) pushes back against Junio C Hamano's suggestion to recalculate the common prefix to include negative pathspecs. Diogo argues that the "strip the common prefix" optimization is purely a performance feature for positive pathspecs, and extending it to negative pathspecs could negate its benefits. He gives the example `git add -- a/b/c a/b/d ':!*.md'`, where no common prefix exists across all pathspecs (positive and negative), forcing a full working-directory walk even though the positive pathspecs alone would allow limiting the walk to `a/b/`.

The reply is substantive: it directly addresses the design question raised in the parent review, provides a technical rationale, and introduces a new example that illustrates the trade-off between correctness and performance. The discussion is now at an impasse: the maintainer's suggestion would change the semantics of negative pathspecs (making them relative to the full tree), while the author's approach preserves the current semantics (treating negative pathspecs as absolute) and maintains the performance optimization. No alternative implementation has been proposed yet, and the thread remains open.
