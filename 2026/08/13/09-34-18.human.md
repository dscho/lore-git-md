This is a **refactoring** discussion in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. Johannes Schindelin’s reply reinforces Patrick’s argument that a cleaner root directory improves discoverability for newcomers, framing the current disorganized layout as an "ongoing burden" that outweighs the one-time disruption of the patch.

Schindelin’s email advances the thread by **dismissing critics’ objections as short-sighted** and emphasizing the long-term benefits of structural cleanup. He argues that the reorganization is a necessary first step—even if further subdivision of `lib/` is needed later—citing past refactorings (e.g., ODB, merge machinery) where naming and organization evolved over time. His key points: (1) incremental improvements are better than stalled efforts, (2) naming is inherently iterative, and (3) the disruption caused by this patch highlights gaps in Git’s tooling (e.g., rename detection), which could spur future improvements. He also notes that the maintenance pain of integrating this change with in-flight topics is not unique to this patch but a recurring issue for maintainers, including himself.

The reply does not engage with Junio’s **structural critique**—that the `lib/` directory is an arbitrary intermediate layer that fails to clarify the codebase’s architecture—or his preference for incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem moves. Instead, Schindelin aligns with Patrick’s focus on **root-directory clarity**, arguing that reducing clutter makes entry points like `builtin/` and `Documentation/` more visible. He gives an example of the `RelNotes` symlink, suggesting that even experienced contributors might overlook top-level files in the current layout.

The email’s tone is **advocacy-driven**, prioritizing the perceived benefits of a tidier root directory over the practical concerns raised by critics (e.g., rebasing challenges, `git log --follow` disruptions). While Schindelin acknowledges that further reorganization may be needed, he frames the current patch as a **worthwhile first step** that should not be blocked by perfectionism. His argument hinges on the idea that the patch’s disruption is justified by the permanent improvement in discoverability, even if the `lib/` directory itself is not semantically meaningful.

For a busy reader, this reply signals that the **pro-reorganization camp remains steadfast** in its position, but it does little to address the **maintainer-level skepticism** from Junio and Taylor Blau. The patch’s fate likely depends on whether Patrick or others can propose a compromise (e.g., incremental moves or tooling mitigations) or whether the community coalesces around an alternative. The timing of the series—sent close to the upcoming release—suggests Patrick aims to land this early in the next cycle if accepted, but Junio’s and Taylor’s objections may prove decisive.
