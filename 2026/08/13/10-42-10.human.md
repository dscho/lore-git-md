This is a **follow-up in the ongoing packfile performance regression thread**, focusing on the design debate between Johannes Schindelin’s fast-path optimization and Patrick Steinhardt’s proposed hashmap refactoring.

Johannes and Patrick reach a small compromise on naming: Johannes agrees `is_new` is too narrow and proposes `skip_dup_check` instead, which Patrick accepts. The substance of the patch remains unchanged, but the new name better reflects the parameter’s purpose—skipping duplicate checks for packfiles the caller guarantees are new. This is a minor but clarifying adjustment.

The core disagreement persists. Patrick reiterates his intent to pursue a hashmap-based refactoring independently, arguing it would significantly speed up packfile reordering (a common operation in large repositories). Johannes counters that while a hashmap is faster than a linear scan, it still can’t match the performance of skipping the search entirely—his patch’s key advantage. Neither backs down, but Patrick concedes Johannes’s fix can land first, leaving the door open for his refactoring to follow later.

The thread remains unresolved on the long-term design direction. Junio C Hamano’s earlier endorsement of the fast-path approach still stands, but Patrick’s counterproposal is now a concrete alternative rather than a vague suggestion. For now, the patch is ready to advance with the naming tweak, but the broader question of whether Git should adopt a more complex, future-proof solution lingers. The regression’s impact in Microsoft Git Scalar environments keeps the urgency high, but the debate over the right fix is far from settled.
