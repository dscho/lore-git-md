This is a **maintainer review** of Johannes Schindelin’s packfile performance regression fix, specifically addressing Patrick Steinhardt’s counterproposal and the design of the `is_new`/`skip_dup_check` parameter. Junio C Hamano engages with the core trade-off between the fast-path optimization and the hashmap alternative, raising a **substantive concern** about the long-term maintainability of the fast-path approach.

Junio echoes Patrick’s observation that the `is_new` parameter effectively relaxes the uniqueness invariant of `packfile_list`, but frames it as a **risk**: while current callers may uphold the "new pack" guarantee, future changes could break this assumption, leading to duplicate entries. He questions whether the consequences of duplicates are fully understood, noting that while duplicates might make existence checks slightly faster (if they appear early in the list), they would make non-existence checks even less efficient (requiring a full scan). This suggests that the fast-path optimization, while effective for the immediate regression, could introduce subtle correctness or performance issues down the line.

The review does not endorse or reject either approach outright, but the tone implies skepticism about the fast-path’s robustness. Junio’s focus on future maintainability aligns with Patrick’s earlier argument for the hashmap refactoring, which would eliminate the need for caller discipline entirely. This adds weight to the deferred hashmap work, even though the fast-path is currently the agreed-upon fix for merging to `next`.

No new technical details or alternatives are proposed; the email is purely a design-level critique. The discussion remains open, but Junio’s concern may prompt further iteration or accelerate Patrick’s planned hashmap refactoring.
