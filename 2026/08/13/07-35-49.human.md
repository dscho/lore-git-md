This is a **substantive review** of Johannes Schindelin’s packfile performance regression fix. Patrick Steinhardt engages deeply with the patch’s design and proposes an alternative implementation that would eliminate the quadratic-time behavior more thoroughly.

Patrick starts with a minor nit (a typo in the commit message) but quickly moves to the core issue. He acknowledges the severity of the regression (38,000 packfiles is "a lot") and the cleverness of the fast-path fix, but questions whether the `is_new` parameter is the right abstraction. He suggests renaming it to `accept_duplicates` to better reflect its behavior, since the function now allows duplicate entries rather than enforcing uniqueness.

More significantly, Patrick proposes a **hashmap-based rewrite** of the packfile list management. His alternative patch replaces the linked list with a hashmap to track seen packfiles, making both `append()` and `prepend()` operations O(1) instead of O(N). This would also allow moving existing entries to the front or back of the list in logarithmic time if the list were doubly-linked. The patch touches several files (`http-push.c`, `http-walker.c`, `odb/source-packed.c`) to initialize the new hashmap field, demonstrating that the change would require broader coordination.

Patrick is candid about the trade-offs: while the hashmap approach is more complex, it would eliminate the quadratic-time issue entirely and remove the need for callers to reason about whether a packfile is new. He does not insist on the alternative, but his detailed counter-proposal signals that the current fix, while correct, may be a short-term solution.
