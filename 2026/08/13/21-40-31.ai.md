This is a progress update in the `core.useNanosec` series, specifically addressing the unresolved architectural question of where to cache the runtime config value (`repo_config_values` vs `repo_settings`). The author, D. Ben Knoble, reports on two lines of investigation: a CI run confirming the test suite passes when using `repo_config_values` (with no evidence of non-`the_repository` usage), and a partial control-flow analysis of 214 call paths leading to `repo_config_values()`. The analysis reveals that some edges touch public `libgit.a` interfaces, meaning the "must only use `the_repository`" surface area is larger than initially assumed and may grow further with this change. The author expresses concern that requiring `the_repository`-cleanliness could block the series indefinitely unless the project accepts `repo_settings` as a stopgap, even if it isn’t fully `the_repository`-clean. No strong preference is stated, but the tone suggests readiness to proceed with `repo_config_values` if the project is satisfied with the CI results. The next iteration (v2) is promised within 24 hours, pending feedback. Key technical details: the CI run (GitHub Actions) validates the change across multiple configurations, and the control-flow graph highlights the risk of expanding the `the_repository`-only limitation. The open question remains whether to prioritize architectural purity or timely landing of the feature.
