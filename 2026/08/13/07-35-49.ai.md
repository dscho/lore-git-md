This is a **substantive review** of Johannes Schindelin’s packfile performance regression fix, authored by Patrick Steinhardt. The review engages deeply with the patch’s design and proposes an alternative implementation that would eliminate the quadratic-time behavior entirely, rather than just optimizing the fast path.

Patrick begins with a minor nit (a typo in the commit message) but quickly pivots to the core issue: the patch’s approach of adding an `is_new` parameter to `packfile_list_append()` to skip duplicate checks for known-new packfiles. He suggests renaming the parameter to `accept_duplicates` to better reflect its semantics, arguing that the current design effectively allows duplicates rather than enforcing uniqueness.

The bulk of the review is a **counterproposal**: replacing the linked-list implementation of `packfile_list` with a hashmap. The provided patch demonstrates how this would work, converting the list into a hybrid structure that tracks seen packfiles in a hashmap while maintaining the existing MRU (most-recently-used) ordering via a linked list. This change would make both `append()` and `prepend()` operations O(1) for duplicate checks (vs. the current O(N)), and it would eliminate the need for callers to reason about whether a packfile is new. The patch also includes the necessary initialization/cleanup plumbing for the hashmap and updates all callers to use the new `packfile_list_init()` function.

Patrick acknowledges the trade-off: the hashmap approach adds complexity (new data structures, initialization logic, and memory overhead) but offers broader benefits (covering `prepend()` as well as `append()`, and potentially enabling O(log N) reordering if the list were doubly-linked). He leaves the decision open, noting that the added complexity may not be justified for this specific regression.

The review does not address the earlier discussion about test redundancy (raised by Peff) or the strategic question about `git maintenance` as a mitigation (raised by D. Ben Knoble). It focuses solely on the technical design of the fix itself. The tone is collaborative and exploratory, framing the hashmap alternative as a thought experiment rather than a demand for a rewrite.
