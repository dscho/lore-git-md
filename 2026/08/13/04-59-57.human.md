This is a follow-up discussion in the resolved thread about unexpected pseudo-commits appearing in a custom `git lg` alias. Kristoffer Haugsbakk replies to Junio C Hamano’s observation that `git log --all` includes all refs—including Git notes and stash refs—despite feeling unintuitive for an end-user command.

Kristoffer speculates that `--all`’s broad scope may have been intentional from the start, designed to include branches, tags, remote-tracking branches, and any custom refs users might create, even if those refs are internal bookkeeping data (like notes or stash). He notes that while branches and tags are easily shared, Git notes require explicit refspecs to push or fetch, making them less likely to surprise users unless they’re generated by third-party tools (e.g., IDE integrations or analytics scripts). He also confirms Junio’s suspicion that `git log --all -p` does include stash refs, reinforcing the point that `--all`’s behavior is consistent but potentially unexpected for users who assume it only covers "standard" refs.

The tone is analytical rather than critical, and the discussion remains low-stakes—no one is proposing changes, just reflecting on Git’s design choices. For a busy reader, this exchange underscores that `--all`’s behavior is working as intended, even if it’s not always intuitive, and that users who want narrower output should use `--branches --tags --remotes` or `--exclude='refs/notes/*'` as workarounds.
