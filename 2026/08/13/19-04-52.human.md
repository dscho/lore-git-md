This is a **refactoring discussion** in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving core files into a `lib/` directory. Michael Montalbo, a newer contributor, weighs in with a data-driven proposal that attempts to bridge the deadlock between proponents of the sweeping `lib/` move and Junio C Hamano’s preference for incremental, concept-driven reorganization.

Montalbo acknowledges the appeal of the `lib/` approach—reducing root-directory clutter to improve newcomer discoverability—but ultimately sides with Junio’s critique: the `lib/` directory is too coarse and lacks clear semantics. Instead, he proposes a **mechanical, history-informed method** for identifying *specific* conceptual groupings of files, using shared internal header dependencies as a proxy for cohesion. His analysis suggests that Git’s existing carved-out directories (e.g., `refs/`, `odb/`) exhibit high internal cohesion (scoring 1.0–3.1 on his metric), while the root directory scores only 0.3. Applying the same metric to the current root files, he identifies five candidate subsystems—wire protocol, index, revision, diff, and pack—each scoring 6.0–6.9, far exceeding the cohesion of existing directories.

The proposal is notable for its **principled, repeatable approach**: rather than hand-picking groupings or dumping files into an arbitrary `lib/`, Montalbo’s method mines Git’s own history to surface natural clusters. This addresses Junio’s concern that the `lib/` move is premature abstraction while providing a concrete roadmap for incremental reorganization. The script (included in the email) is simple but effective: it counts shared "distinctive" headers (those included by ≤12% of root files) and uses average-linkage clustering to group files until cohesion drops below a threshold. The results align with past successful moves (e.g., `odb/` was flagged as a cohesive group even before its creation) and suggest actionable next steps, such as carving out a `wire/` or `index/` directory.

Montalbo’s contribution is **substantive and constructive**, offering a middle ground that could break the deadlock. It validates Junio’s incrementalist philosophy while providing a tool to make such increments *deliberate* rather than ad-hoc. The email does not engage with the tooling or workflow disruptions (e.g., `git log --follow`) that critics like SZEDER Gábor have raised, but it shifts the debate from "should we reorganize?" to "how should we reorganize?"—a more productive framing. Whether the community adopts this specific metric or refines it further, the proposal injects a fresh, evidence-based perspective into a polarized discussion. For now, it stands as a compelling alternative to both the `lib/` move and the status quo.
