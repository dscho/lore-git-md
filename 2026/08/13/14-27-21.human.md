This is a **refactoring debate** in the ongoing thread about Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. Junio C Hamano’s latest reply directly counters Johannes Schindelin’s incrementalist argument, reinforcing his preference for **subsystem-by-subsystem moves** over a sweeping `lib/` reorganization.

Junio’s core objection remains structural: the `lib/` directory is an **arbitrary intermediate layer** that fails to deliver meaningful organization. He contrasts it with past successful moves (e.g., `builtin/`, `refs/`, `odb/`), which were tied to *substantial updates* and created clear, intuitive groupings. His proposed alternative—**incremental, focused moves** (e.g., carving out `refs/` or `odb/` when they undergo major changes)—avoids the "hodgepodge" problem by ensuring each directory has a well-defined purpose from the start. This approach also sidesteps the disruption of a mass rename, as each step would be self-contained and motivated by a specific technical need.

The reply underscores Junio’s skepticism about the `lib/` patch’s long-term value. He argues that moving everything to `lib/` now would force contributors to re-learn the structure later when further reorganization becomes necessary, creating **persistent mental overhead**. His emphasis on **naming clarity** ("come up with one clear concept, name it well") suggests he sees the current proposal as premature—lacking a coherent vision for how the `lib/` directory would evolve beyond its initial state.

This exchange leaves the thread at an impasse. Schindelin’s incrementalist framing (start with `lib/`, refine later) clashes with Junio’s insistence on **motivated, self-contained steps**. The lack of engagement with Junio’s alternative proposal—subsystem-by-subsystem moves—suggests the pro-reorganization camp remains focused on the `lib/` approach, while Junio’s maintainer-level concerns continue to block progress. The patch’s fate likely hinges on whether Patrick or Schindelin can address Junio’s structural critique or pivot to an incremental strategy. For now, the debate remains **polarized and deadlocked**.
