This is a review by Junio C Hamano of the second patch in Delilah Ashley Wu’s three-patch series fixing Git’s global config inconsistency. The patch introduces infrastructure to track whether any config files were successfully read and optionally fail if none were.

Junio’s feedback is narrowly focused on code style and naming. He suggests shortening the overly long helper function name `attempt_git_config_from_file_with_options` to something like `try_config`, arguing that shorter names improve readability and keep lines under 70 columns. He also questions whether the `success_count` counter is strictly necessary at this stage, though he concedes it may become useful in the next patch. His comments are mechanical rather than substantive: he does not dispute the patch’s logic or design, only its presentation.

The review is surface-level. Junio does not engage with the patch’s core purpose (enabling scoped config reads) or its error-handling strategy, nor does he raise any technical concerns about correctness or edge cases. His feedback is limited to naming and line length, both of which are easily addressed in a follow-up iteration. The patch remains uncontroversial and technically sound; Johannes Schindelin’s Reviewed-by from v1 still applies to the underlying implementation. The only open question is whether the author will adopt Junio’s suggested naming changes before the series moves forward.
