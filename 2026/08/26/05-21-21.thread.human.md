This thread is about a three-patch bugfix series from Elijah Newren that tightens Git's protections against unsafe commit operations during conflict resolution. The series prevents users from accidentally corrupting repository state by running `git commit --amend` or `git commit <paths>` during conflict stops in `git am`, `git revert`, and all forms of `git rebase`—except for interactive rebase directives like `squash` or `edit` that explicitly require amending.

The first patch in the v2 series, just posted, is a small but important clarification: it rewords error messages to specify that the restriction applies only when resolving a commit that became *empty* during rebase, not during rebase in general. This avoids misleading users into thinking the restriction applies to all rebase stops, such as `edit` or `break` directives where amending or partial commits are valid. The change is purely textual—four lines in `builtin/commit.c` and corresponding test updates in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh`—and sets the stage for the two follow-up patches, which extend the protection to additional commands and operations.

The series has strong support from both Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood, who reviewed the v1 and provided feedback that Elijah incorporated into v2. Junio endorsed the series as correct and well-reasoned, while Phillip acknowledged the maintainability improvements in the v2 approach. The core logic is now consolidated into a new helper function, `sequencer_ongoing_operation()`, in `sequencer.c`, and the series aligns with ongoing efforts to remove the `the_repository` global variable. No open questions remain, and the series appears ready for integration.
