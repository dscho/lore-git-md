This thread is about a five-patch bugfix series from Elijah Newren that tightens Git's safety net during conflict resolution. The goal is to prevent users from accidentally corrupting repository state by running `git commit --amend` or `git commit <paths>` while resolving conflicts in `git am`, `git revert`, or any form of `git rebase`. The series is now complete at version 4, with all substantive feedback addressed.

The core logic distinguishes between genuine conflict stops (where amending or partial commits would corrupt state) and clean interactive rebase stops (like `squash` or `edit`), allowing the latter while blocking the former. Junio C Hamano raised and resolved a key technical question about whether partial commits should be blocked for empty commits; the restriction was removed after he noted the result would still be an empty commit and thus harmless. The series also refines error messages to clarify context, such as "The now-empty commit has been dropped -- cannot amend."

All five patches have Junio's explicit approval, and Phillip Wood, the other reviewer, called the series "pretty sound" and a "useful improvement to our UI." The changes touch `builtin/commit.c`, `sequencer.c`, and test scripts, with no on-disk format changes. The series is technically complete and ready for integration, likely to move to `next` soon.
