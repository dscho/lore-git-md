This thread is about a bugfix patch from Elijah Newren that extends Git’s existing protection against `git commit --amend` during conflict resolution to cover `git am`, `git revert`, and all forms of `git rebase`. The patch prevents users from accidentally amending the wrong commit and corrupting repository state, while still allowing `--amend` for interactive rebase operations like `squash` or `reword` that explicitly require it.

The patch is under review and currently uncontroversial in its goal. Phillip Wood provided a substantive review, praising the motivation but suggesting a structural improvement: moving the conflict-resolution checks out of `builtin/commit.c` and into `sequencer_determine_whence()` to avoid hardcoded paths and improve maintainability. This change would also make the logic cleaner for `git am` and the apply-based rebase backend. Phillip’s feedback is technical and constructive, focusing on code organization rather than correctness, and he explicitly notes that the current implementation is sound. No other participants have weighed in yet, and the thread remains in the early review phase. If Elijah agrees with the suggestion, a v2 patch may follow.
