This thread is about a five-patch bugfix series by Elijah Newren that extends Git's existing protections against unsafe commit operations during conflict resolution. The series prevents users from accidentally running `git commit --amend` or `git commit <paths>` during conflict resolution for `git am`, `git revert`, and all forms of `git rebase`, which could corrupt repository state. The protection logic reliably distinguishes between genuine conflict stops (where these operations are unsafe) and internal `--amend` usage by interactive rebase directives like `squash` or `reword`.

The series is now complete at version 3, with all substantive feedback addressed. Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood reviewed earlier versions, raising questions about the necessity of blocking partial commits for empty commits (resolved by removing the restriction) and maintainability concerns about hardcoded paths (addressed by consolidating logic in a helper function). Junio provided surface-level feedback on naming and error message phrasing, which Elijah incorporated, and explicitly approved the final wording. The fifth and final patch, which Junio just reviewed, replaces hardcoded checks with a cleaner `switch` statement and is ready for integration. No open questions or objections remain, and the series is likely to proceed to Junio's `next` branch soon.
