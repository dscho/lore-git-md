Thread identity
Subject: [PATCH 0/2] Introduce die_for_incompatible_opts() helper
Series version: withdrawn (v2 abandoned)
Originating author: Junio C Hamano
Thread type: patch series (refactoring)

Core problem or goal
Replace the fixed-arity `die_for_incompatible_optN()` family (N = 2, 3, 4) with a single varargs helper `die_for_incompatible_opts()` that can handle an unbounded number of mutually exclusive command-line options. The change was purely internal API refactoring with no intended user-visible behavior change.

Current status
Abandoned. Junio withdrew the v2 series after concluding that the revised design (EOF sentinel, static inline wrappers) was unsatisfactory and that the preliminary parameter-reordering step from v1 was even less appealing. The withdrawal follows Jeff King’s substantive review advocating for compile-time safety via `LAST_ARG_MUST_BE_NULL` over the `EOF` sentinel. No further iterations or alternatives are proposed; the topic is closed pending a potential future redesign.

Key technical details
- Files touched: `parse-options.c`, `parse-options.h`; 14 built-in commands and the `revision` subsystem were updated to use the new helper through existing wrappers in v2.
- New symbol: `die_for_incompatible_opts()` (varargs, EOF-terminated) was implemented but will not be merged.
- Renamed symbols: none; the fixed-arity helpers remain with their original signatures.
- Old-vs-new behavior: identical in v2; the withdrawal restores the status quo ante.
- On-disk format: unaffected.
- Test coverage: none mentioned; the change was plumbing-only.

Open questions and loose ends
- **Compile-time safety vs. flexibility trade-off**: The unresolved debate between compile-time safety (advocated by Jeff King via `LAST_ARG_MUST_BE_NULL`) and varargs flexibility (implemented via `EOF` sentinel) remains. René Scharfe’s proposals (EOF + NULL sentinel macro, type-safe compound literal) offered actionable paths to reconcile the two, but neither was adopted. The withdrawal leaves this tension unresolved for any future redesign.
- **Sentinel choice rationale**: Junio’s retrospective observation that `LAST_ARG_MUST_BE_NULL` (or an integer equivalent) would have been a better initial design is preserved, but the withdrawal does not revisit this trade-off.
- **Hard-coded error-message formatting**: The hard-coded formatting for four options (to avoid i18n breakage) was accepted by Peff and remains a constraint for any future redesign.
- **Future-proofing**: The varargs helper was designed to handle more than four options, but no callers currently need this capability. The withdrawal does not address whether this flexibility is worth pursuing.

Participant positions
- **Jeff King (Peff)**: Substantive review engaging with the compile-time safety vs. flexibility trade-off. Advocated for `LAST_ARG_MUST_BE_NULL` as a safer sentinel, arguing that the varargs helper sacrifices compile-time protection. Accepted Junio’s rationale for retaining hard-coded error-message formatting and the `EOF` sentinel for backward compatibility, but maintained that the design could have been more future-proof. Feedback remains a design preference rather than a correctness issue; the withdrawal does not alter this stance.
- **Elijah Newren**: Substantive review identifying the `va_end()` omission in v1. No other concerns raised; the rest of the patch (parameter reordering, error message formatting, call-site updates) was implicitly accepted. The withdrawal does not affect this position.
- **René Scharfe**: Substantive review proposing two technical alternatives to improve compile-time safety while preserving varargs flexibility. The first approach combines `EOF` and `NULL` sentinels via a macro to prevent direct calls to the internal function. The second introduces a type-safe compound literal to enforce correct argument patterns at compile time. Neither proposal requires call-site changes. René’s feedback remains exploratory and actionable for any future redesign.
- **Junio C Hamano**: Originating author and withdrawer. Acknowledged the `va_end()` omission and incorporated the fix in v2. Proposed and implemented the revised design (EOF sentinel, dropped parameter reordering). Retained the hard-coded error-message formatting to avoid i18n breakage. Conceded that the original API design was not future-proof for varargs but defended the `EOF` sentinel as a pragmatic choice for backward compatibility. Withdrew the series after concluding the design was unsatisfactory, leaving the compile-time safety debate unresolved. No objections to the core goal, but no longer pursuing the current implementation.
