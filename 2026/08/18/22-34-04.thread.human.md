This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series from Elijah Newren that addresses a race condition in Git's geometric repacking mechanism. The issue occurs when a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a packfile that has been removed by a concurrent repack, causing processes like `git replay` to crash when they can't locate objects through the stale MIDX. Patch 1 ensures `git replay` fails gracefully when merge inputs are unreadable, while patch 2 modifies the packed-object backend to recover from MIDX-packfile mismatches by scanning all packs covered by the MIDX when the normal lookup fails.

The series was previously considered ready for integration after substantive reviews from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt, with no remaining technical objections. However, Jeff King (Peff) has now raised a fundamental design question about patch 2, challenging whether the fix belongs in the packed-object backend or should instead be handled at the caller level. Peff argues that `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` callers are already aware of the false-negative trade-off and should avoid the flag if they cannot tolerate missing objects, noting that `is_pack_valid()` already handles some race conditions. This has shifted the discussion to architectural trade-offs, with the series' future depending on whether Elijah's approach (making the backend more resilient) or Peff's preference (caller-level handling) prevails. The series remains a high-priority candidate for merging, but the outcome may require revisions to patch 2. Elijah has not yet responded to Peff's critique.
