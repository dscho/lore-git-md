This thread is about a critical bugfix series by Elijah Newren that addresses a race condition in Git's geometric repacking mechanism. The issue causes objects to be incorrectly reported as missing when a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a packfile that has been removed by repacking, leading to crashes in `git replay` and errors in other server-side operations. The two-patch series introduces graceful failure logic in `git replay` and a recovery mechanism in the packed-object backend to handle MIDX-packfile mismatches.

The fix is widely seen as necessary, but its implementation—particularly the performance implications of the fallback logic—has sparked a detailed design discussion. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have reviewed the series substantively, with no remaining technical objections to the core fix. Jeff King (Peff) has raised architectural and performance concerns, focusing on whether the recovery logic should be tied to the `SECOND_READ` phase or remain as currently implemented. The latest exchange between Peff and Patrick clarifies that the fallback's overhead is limited to edge cases, as reverse-mtime sorting in `SECOND_READ` prioritizes newer packs not covered by the MIDX.

Derrick Stolee's recent review adds a new dimension to the discussion, noting that the race condition is already partially mitigated by `git multi-pack-index expire`. This mechanism avoids breaking existing processes by leaving redundant data in place temporarily, a design particularly important on Windows due to file-handle constraints. Stolee suggests that geometric repacking could adopt a similar approach to reduce reliance on the fallback logic, framing the fix as a safeguard rather than a primary solution. While he doesn't propose specific changes, his input underscores the need to balance correctness, performance, and operational simplicity, especially across platforms.

The series is now at version 2, with the latest iteration addressing Junio's feedback on test adjustments. The open questions revolve around optimizing the fallback logic's scan order and timing, and whether to defer its invocation to `SECOND_READ`. Elijah has not yet responded to the latest design critiques, but the discussion remains constructive and focused on refining the implementation. The fix is a high-priority candidate for merging, with no dependencies on other in-flight topics, though a minor merge conflict with an unrelated API change is noted.
