This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series from Elijah Newren that addresses a race condition in Git's geometric repacking mechanism. The issue causes objects to be incorrectly reported as missing when a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a packfile that has been removed by a concurrent repack, leading to crashes in `git replay` and errors in other server-side operations like `git merge-tree` and `git diff`.

The series is effectively ready for integration. Junio C Hamano reviewed the first patch substantively, endorsing the fix for `git replay`'s graceful failure logic and requesting a minor test adjustment, which Elijah addressed in the second version. The second patch, which modifies the packed-object backend to recover from MIDX-packfile mismatches, received a surface-level acknowledgment from Junio, who noted a mechanical merge conflict with an unrelated in-flight topic that he will resolve during integration.

Patrick Steinhardt recently provided a substantive review of the second patch, engaging deeply with the technical details. He confirmed the problem and praised the fix's targeted recovery logic, which scans all packs covered by the MIDX only when necessary. Patrick raised a nuanced question about whether the fix is strictly needed for callers like `cat-file` or `rev-list` that don’t use the `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` flag (and thus already reload packfiles on failure), but he did not request changes—just flagged it as a point for potential clarification. His feedback reinforces the patch’s design and leaves no technical objections unresolved.

The series touches `replay.c` and `odb/source-packed.c`, with new tests verifying the graceful failure and recovery behavior. No on-disk format changes or new symbols are introduced. The only loose end is Patrick’s question about `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK`, but this is unlikely to block merging given the patch’s clear benefit for server-side stability. Junio’s earlier feedback has been addressed, and the series is a high-priority candidate for `seen` and eventual graduation to `next`.
