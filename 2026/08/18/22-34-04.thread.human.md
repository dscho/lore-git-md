This thread is about a four-patch series from Elijah Newren that fixes a race condition in Git's geometric repacking mechanism, where objects can be incorrectly reported as missing due to stale multi-pack-index (MIDX) references. The issue causes crashes in `git replay` and errors in other operations like `git merge-tree` and `git diff`. The series is now at v2 and under active debate, particularly around the architectural approach to handling `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` lookups and a `stale_packs_detected` flag introduced in patch 3/4.

The core fix (patch 4/4) has evolved to adopt Jeff King (Peff)'s tri-state design for `fill_midx_entry()`, which gates recovery logic on `SECOND_READ` to optimize performance for `QUICK` callers. However, Peff's latest review challenges the necessity of the `stale_packs_detected` flag, arguing that the tri-state design alone should suffice. He also raises minor concerns about function naming and unnecessary comment changes. Earlier feedback from Peff on patch 3/4 questioned whether `git mktree --batch` is misusing the `QUICK` flag and suggested that the patch violates its intended semantics, potentially introducing unnecessary overhead. Derrick Stolee had previously suggested that geometric repacking could adopt a grace period for redundant packfiles (similar to `multi-pack-index expire`) to reduce the race condition's frequency, though this remains a follow-up item.

Patches 1/4 (v2) and 2/4 (v2) are uncontroversial: the former addresses a redundant test check, and the latter fixes a memory leak in `git mktree --batch`. Peff noted that `git mktree --batch` has lacked dedicated test coverage since its introduction in 2009 and suggested adding basic tests as a follow-up. The series is rebased on `ps/odb-generic-corrupt-objects` and includes comprehensive test coverage for the crash scenario, MIDX recovery logic, and `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` readers. Key participants include Elijah Newren (author), Junio C Hamano (maintainer), Patrick Steinhardt, Peff, and Stolee, with Peff's latest feedback re-opening the architectural debate around patch 3/4. The outcome of this discussion will likely determine whether the `stale_packs_detected` flag is retained or redesigned.
