This thread is about a four-patch bugfix series by Elijah Newren that addresses a race condition in Git's geometric repacking mechanism, where objects become incorrectly reported as missing due to stale multi-pack-index (MIDX) references. The issue causes crashes in `git replay` and errors in other operations like `git merge-tree` and `git diff`. The series, now at v2, includes fixes for the core race condition, a memory leak in `git mktree --batch`, and a recovery mechanism for `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` lookups when packfiles vanish mid-operation.

The series was largely ready for merging, with the core fix (patch 4/4) adopting Jeff King (Peff)'s tri-state design for MIDX recovery and gating the fallback logic on `SECOND_READ` for performance. However, Peff's latest review of patch 3/4 challenges its architectural premise, arguing that `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` is being misused. He contends that the flag is meant to optimize for genuine misses, not to recover from transient races, and suggests that `git mktree --batch` (the primary affected command) should avoid the flag or use `SKIP_FETCH_OBJECT` instead. Peff also raises concerns about misleading error messages and a race condition in the patch's logic where `stale_packs_detected` might not be set correctly. This critique puts the series' convergence in question, as it targets a foundational assumption of the current approach and could lead to a redesign or removal of patch 3/4.

Key participants include Elijah Newren (author), who has incorporated feedback from Junio C Hamano (maintainer) and Peff, and Derrick Stolee, who suggested follow-up work to reduce the race condition's frequency. Patrick Steinhardt has also reviewed the series but has not yet responded to the latest changes. The series remains a high-priority candidate for merging, but Peff's feedback introduces a significant open question about the correct handling of `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` lookups.
