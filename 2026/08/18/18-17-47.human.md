This is a review reply in the ongoing ODB refactoring series from Patrick Steinhardt. Junio C Hamano responds to patch 3/7, which fixes an inconsistency in the packed-object backend where already-corrupt objects were incorrectly reported as missing rather than corrupt.

Junio opens by praising the general direction—he has long found it awkward that Git treats corrupt objects as missing, silently recovering if another copy exists, which makes `fsck` the only reliable way to detect corruption. His tone is constructive, but he immediately flags a conceptual concern: the patch’s logic marks an entire pack as "bad" if even one object in it is corrupt. He questions whether this is too coarse-grained, asking whether the presence of one bad object should taint the entire pack, given that other parts might still be valid.

The review is substantive but narrowly focused on this one design choice. Junio does not engage with the patch’s mechanical correctness or its alignment with the series’ broader goals; instead, he zeroes in on the policy implied by the `bad_pack` logic. His question is open-ended—he does not demand a change, but the implication is that the current approach may be overly conservative and could needlessly penalize otherwise healthy parts of a pack. This is a classic Junio move: he surfaces a second-order consequence that might not be obvious to the author, leaving the resolution open for discussion.
