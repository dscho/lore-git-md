This is a bugfix targeting the `git history` command, specifically addressing a worktree-corruption issue in `git history fixup`. Patrick Steinhardt, one of the most prolific Git contributors, has weighed in on the ongoing discussion and formally scheduled the fix for the next release cycle.

The bug, previously reported by Toon Claes and analyzed by Phillip Wood, causes `git history fixup` to corrupt the index in other worktrees when rewriting commits. The root cause is clear: the command updates branch pointers in other worktrees without updating their index or working copy, leaving them in a dirty state. The discussion has centered on two potential fixes: refusing to rewrite branches checked out in other worktrees (the safer, `git rebase --update-refs`-style approach) or attempting a "best-effort" update of their index and working copy if clean (a more user-friendly option).

Patrick comes down firmly on the side of the safer approach, aligning with Phillip Wood’s earlier suggestion. He has created a GitLab work item to track the fix, signaling that this is now a priority for the next release. While he welcomes contributions from others, his explicit scheduling suggests the fix is likely to land soon, possibly implemented by his team at GitLab. The thread remains in the design phase, but the direction is now settled: `git history fixup` will refuse to rewrite branches checked out in other worktrees, preventing the corruption issue at the cost of requiring users to check out branches in the current worktree before rewriting them.
