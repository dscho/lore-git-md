This is a review of the fourth patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s six-patch series making packfile generation pluggable in Git’s ODB layer. The patch refactors `git bundle`’s progress-meter option handling, and Karthik Nayak’s review is surface-level but positive.

Karthik describes the refactoring as "much nicer to read," praising the simplification of the command-line interface. The patch consolidates four related options (`--quiet`, `--progress`, `--all-progress`, and `--all-progress-implied`) into a single boolean `progress` flag, eliminating redundant option handling while preserving backward compatibility. Karthik’s only substantive observation is a tangential note about the `git-pack-objects` documentation: it lists only the `-q` short form for `--quiet`, not the long form. This is not a critique of the patch itself, which remains uncontroversial and well-motivated.

The patch is a mechanical cleanup that prepares `git bundle` for the upcoming ODB migration in patch 6/6. It touches only `builtin/bundle.c`, replacing `OPT_PASSTHRU_ARGV` macros with `OPT_BIT`, `OPT_NEGBIT`, and `OPT_NOOP_NOARG` to manage the boolean state directly. The logic for determining whether to show progress (defaulting to `isatty(STDERR_FILENO)`) is preserved, and the resulting boolean is used to push either `--progress` or `--quiet` into the `pack_opts` strvec. The change is purely internal and does not alter behavior.

This is a minor but necessary step in the larger ODB abstraction effort, which aims to decouple Git’s transport layer from the "files" backend. The series as a whole remains queued in `seen` and under active review, with no major objections to this patch. Karthik’s review does not raise any technical concerns, and the patch appears ready to proceed.
