This is a **substantive review follow-up** in the `git pack-objects` Trace2 byte-counting patch thread. Patrick Steinhardt and Jeff King (Peff) converge on a technical consensus about hash algorithm consistency and performance, but keep the discussion narrowly scoped to avoid derailing the original patch.

The exchange confirms that the patch’s current use of `the_repository->hash_algo->rawsz` is functionally correct, but both reviewers now agree that switching to `f->algop->rawsz` (the hashfile’s algorithm) is the more consistent and future-proof choice. Patrick explicitly endorses Peff’s refactoring snippet, which would make all three conditional blocks in `write_pack_file()` use the hashfile’s algorithm pointer. This change is small, mechanical, and uncontroversial; the author is likely to adopt it in v3.

Beyond the immediate patch, the reviewers briefly explore a broader performance curiosity: the current code recomputes the packfile’s hash three times (once during writing, once during `fixup_pack_header_footer()`, and once during verification on read-back). Both Patrick and Peff call this "absurdly pessimal" and "awful," noting that Git does not re-verify other on-disk data structures like loose objects or refs. They agree that switching to the unsafe hash variant (used by hashfiles) could yield measurable speedups, but explicitly defer this discussion to a separate effort. The tone is collaborative, with no disagreement on the technical merits—only on whether the optimization is worth pursuing now.

The thread remains focused on the Trace2 instrumentation. The open question from v2—whether to use `f->algop->rawsz` or `the_repository->hash_algo->rawsz`—is now resolved in favor of the former. The author’s v3 will likely include Peff’s refactoring, and the patch’s core goal (adding a `write_pack_file/wrote_bytes` Trace2 event) is unaffected. Key files are still `builtin/pack-objects.c` and `t/t5300-pack-object.sh`; the test coverage (single packs, split packs, empty packs) remains unchanged.
