This is the v4 iteration of D. Ben Knoble’s three-patch series that converts the build-time `USE_NSEC` macro into a runtime configuration option (`core.useNanosec`). The series is now cooking in `next` under the topic `dk/use-nsec-runtime` and is effectively ready to graduate to `master` after the final polish incorporated in this version.

The core change—introducing `core.useNanosec`—remains the same: users can now enable nanosecond precision on supported filesystems without recompiling Git, reducing “racy Git” problems. The v4 update addresses the last technical concerns raised in v3 by Junio and Patrick Steinhardt. Specifically, the `#ifdef NO_NSEC` strategy has been simplified: the `use_nanosec` field in `struct repo_config_values` is now unconditionally present but ignored (treated as false) when `NO_NSEC` is defined, eliminating any risk of uninitialized reads. The documentation for `core.useNanosec` has also been strengthened with a direct warning about platform/filesystem limitations, complementing the existing link to the Racy Git technical note.

The first two patches—a Meson build-system fix and a no-op refactor of `repo_config_values_init`—are unchanged from v3 and remain uncontroversial. The series still touches 12 files, including `Documentation/config/core.adoc`, `environment.c`, `read-cache.c`, and `statinfo.c`. The only remaining loose ends are the platform-dependent test coverage (still unaddressed) and the potential naming issue (`core.useNanosec` might be too specific if future platforms offer extended precision beyond nanoseconds), but neither is blocking graduation.

The conflict with `ty/repo-config-cleanups` in `seen` (which removes comments in `struct repo_config_values`) persists, but it is purely textual and will be trivial to resolve if both topics advance. The series is based on master (2c78326f81) and carries forward the performance fix from v2: caching the `use_nanosec` value in `repo_config_values` restores baseline performance (21 ms vs 20 ms in `git diff` benchmarks) after the v1 regression (~41× slowdown). Junio and Patrick Steinhardt have both confirmed that the v4 changes address their final polish requests, and the author has explicitly declined to add a runtime warning for `NO_NSEC` builds, aligning with the project’s preference for shared-configuration compatibility.
