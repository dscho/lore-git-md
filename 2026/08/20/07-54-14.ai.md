This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Elijah Newren’s two-patch bugfix series for the geometric repacking race condition. The patch modifies the packed-object backend to recover when a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a removed packfile, ensuring object lookups succeed even if the MIDX is stale.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review engages deeply with the technical details of the race condition and the proposed fix. He rephrases the problem to confirm his understanding: an object exists in two packs covered by the MIDX, one of which is rewritten and removed by a concurrent repack, leaving the MIDX stale and unable to locate the object. His summary aligns with the patch’s explanation, signaling agreement on the core issue.

The review raises a nuanced question about `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK`: Patrick notes that callers like `cat-file` or `rev-list` do not use this flag, which means they would reload packfiles on failure and thus recover from the race condition naturally. He wonders why the patch is necessary for these callers, suggesting the fix might only be critical for paths that *do* use `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` (e.g., `git replay`). This is a substantive observation that could refine the patch’s scope, though Patrick does not press for changes—he merely flags it as a point of confusion.

On the fix itself, Patrick praises the targeted recovery logic, which only triggers when the MIDX *should* have found the object but didn’t. He initially considered an alternative approach—eagerly loading all MIDX-referenced packfiles at MIDX load time—but rejected it due to the performance cost of opening packfiles unnecessarily. His preference for Elijah’s solution (scanning packs only when needed) is well-reasoned and reinforces the patch’s design.

The review concludes with a positive assessment: "Overall I think this patch looks good to me." The lingering question about `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` does not block approval but could spark further discussion if others share Patrick’s concern. No technical objections or requests for changes are raised, and the feedback is constructive rather than prescriptive. This is a **tested-level** review in spirit, though Patrick does not explicitly state he ran the code—his engagement with the logic and edge cases is thorough enough to imply confidence in the fix.
