This is the final patch in D. Ben Knoble’s three-part series converting the build-time `USE_NSEC` macro into a runtime configuration option (`core.useNanosec`). The series is cooking in `next` under the topic `dk/use-nsec-runtime` and is effectively ready to graduate to `master` after the polish incorporated in this version.

The patch replaces the `#ifdef USE_NSEC` preprocessor logic with a runtime check controlled by the new `core.useNanosec` config key (defaults to false). The `use_nanosec` field is unconditionally added to `struct repo_config_values` and initialized to `0` in `repo_config_values_init()`, ensuring no uninitialized reads even on platforms where `NO_NSEC` is defined. The nested `#ifndef NO_NSEC` guards in `is_racy_stat()` are simplified to a single ternary expression, improving readability while preserving the existing behavior on unsupported platforms. Documentation for `core.useNanosec` now includes a direct warning about platform/filesystem limitations and lists known-working filesystems (ext2, ext4, XFS, CEPH, CIFS, NTFS, UFS, FUSE, FAT).

The patch touches 10 files, including `read-cache.c` (where the racy-timestamp logic lives), `environment.c` (config parsing), and `Documentation/config/core.adoc` (user-facing documentation). The commit message links to benchmarks confirming the performance regression from v1 (~41× slowdown in `git diff`) is resolved by caching the value in `repo_config_values`. The only remaining loose ends are platform-dependent test coverage (still unaddressed) and a potential naming issue (`core.useNanosec` might be too specific if future platforms offer extended precision), but neither is blocking.

This patch is uncontroversial and aligns with the project’s preference for runtime configurability over build-time knobs. The cross-platform correctness blocker (silent corruption risk on `NO_NSEC` platforms) is resolved by treating `use_nanosec` as false when `NO_NSEC` is defined, and the decision to omit a runtime warning for this case—agreed by Junio and Patrick Steinhardt—avoids user friction in shared-configuration scenarios. The conflict with `ty/repo-config-cleanups` in `seen` (removing comments in `struct repo_config_values`) is purely textual and will be trivial to resolve if both topics advance.
