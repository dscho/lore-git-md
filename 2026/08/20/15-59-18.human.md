This is a **maintainer response** in the ongoing `git pull --hard` discussion. Junio C Hamano pushes back on Phillip Wood’s proposed `--reset` redesign, questioning the real-world use case for a workflow that values uncommitted changes enough to protect them by default, yet is willing to discard them in favor of upstream changes.

Junio’s reply centers on the tension between convenience and safety. He reiterates that `git pull --ff-only` already serves users who want to keep a local repository in sync with upstream without integrating local development. The proposed `--reset` design (which would error out by default if uncommitted changes exist) targets a narrower audience: users who care about uncommitted changes but are still willing to discard them wholesale. Junio suggests this is a niche use case, implying the feature may not justify its complexity or the risk of encouraging destructive workflows.

The response does not outright reject the redesign, but it signals skepticism about its necessity. The thread now hinges on whether Artur Bieniek (the original author) or other participants can articulate a compelling use case that would overcome Junio’s philosophical objection. Until then, the feature’s prospects remain uncertain.
