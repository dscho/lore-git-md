This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) on Junio C Hamano’s `git add --resolved` series (v3). Peff engages directly with the feature’s design and edge cases, raising two potential footguns but ultimately concluding they are either non-issues or manageable. His review focuses on the workflow implications of `--resolved` and whether it might mislead users into staging incomplete resolutions.

Peff’s first concern—accidentally staging unrelated local changes in a conflicted file—he dismisses after investigating: most merge strategies (except `ours`, which doesn’t produce conflicts) refuse to operate on dirty files, so the scenario is unlikely. The second concern is more nuanced: `--resolved` only stages files with resolved conflict markers, ignoring related changes (e.g., updates to callers of a modified function) that are logically part of the merge resolution. While this isn’t unique to `--resolved` (users already face this with manual staging), Peff worries the option could create a false sense of completeness, leading users to overlook these related changes.

The review is thoughtful but not alarmist. Peff doesn’t propose blocking the feature or suggest major redesign; instead, he flags a usability risk that might warrant a note in the documentation or a warning in the command’s output. His feedback is narrowly scoped to the user experience and doesn’t revisit the broader interface debate (e.g., Michael Montalbo’s `--unmerged` proposal). Reviewers should verify whether the documentation (`git-add.txt`) adequately addresses these edge cases, and whether the test suite (`t/t2207-add-resolved.sh`) covers scenarios where related changes exist outside conflicted files. The series remains technically sound, but Peff’s points highlight a subtle workflow pitfall worth mitigating.
