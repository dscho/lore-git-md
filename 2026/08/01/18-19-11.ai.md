This is a follow-up in the RFC v2 series introducing `git repack --drop-filtered` for partial clones. The email is a reply from the author, Siddharth Shrimali, to Junio C Hamano’s substantive review questions about the series’ core design and safety mechanisms.

Siddharth clarifies three key technical points raised by Junio. First, he confirms that the `ODB_FOR_EACH_OBJECT_PROMISOR_ONLY` traversal is trusted to correctly identify promisor objects, but acknowledges that real-world validation from other partial-clone users would be valuable. Second, he details how the repack machinery avoids lazy fetches: enumeration uses `OBJECT_INFO_SKIP_FETCH_OBJECT`, and the rebuild step only repacks locally available promisor objects, demonstrated by testing with the promisor remote disabled. He also explains that the current implementation performs two separate promisor-object walks (one for enumeration, one for repacking), rather than reusing the first list, but notes this could be optimized in the future.

The most consequential exchange concerns the necessity of the safety guards. Siddharth concedes that the merge/rebase/cherry-pick checks and index validation are not correctness measures—since lazy-fetching would transparently restore dropped objects—but rather conveniences to avoid redundant network operations during paused operations. He defers to the list’s preference on whether to retain or remove these guards, framing them as optional optimizations rather than essential protections. This clarification significantly refines the series’ design rationale and may reduce implementation complexity if the guards are dropped.

The discussion also touches on future extensibility: Siddharth outlines how additional drop criteria (e.g., object age, path, or reachability) could plug into the existing "enumerate-then-filter" framework without structural changes. The tone remains collaborative, with Siddharth actively engaging with Junio’s skepticism while standing by the core approach. No new patches are attached, but this exchange will likely inform the next revision’s design decisions.
