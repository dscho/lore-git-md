This is a follow-up in the RFC v2 series introducing `git repack --drop-filtered` for partial clones. The email is a response from the author, Siddharth Shrimali, to Junio C Hamano’s review questions about the series’ core design and safety mechanisms.

Siddharth clarifies three key technical points. First, he confirms that the promisor-object enumeration (`ODB_FOR_EACH_OBJECT_PROMISOR_ONLY`) is reliable "by construction" — objects are recoverable as long as the promisor remote keeps its promise — but acknowledges that real-world validation from other partial-clone users would be valuable. Second, he details how the repack machinery avoids lazy fetches: enumeration uses `OBJECT_INFO_SKIP_FETCH_OBJECT`, and the rebuild only repacks locally available promisor objects, confirmed by testing with the promisor remote disabled. However, he notes a current inefficiency: the rebuild performs a second promisor-only walk rather than reusing the pre-enumerated list, which could be optimized in the future.

The most significant exchange concerns the necessity of the safety guards (merge/rebase/cherry-pick checks and index validation). Siddharth concedes that the guards are not correctness measures — since lazy-fetching will transparently restore dropped objects — but rather conveniences to avoid "wasted work" (immediate re-downloads) and disruptive network fetches during operations like merges. He defers to the list’s preference on whether to keep them, framing them as optional user-experience improvements rather than hard requirements. This clarification may reduce the perceived complexity of the feature, as the guards were previously presented as critical safety mechanisms.

The discussion also touches on future extensibility: Siddharth notes that the current design (enumerate promisor objects, then narrow the set with a filter) could easily accommodate additional drop criteria like object age, path, or reachability, without requiring architectural changes. This positions the feature as a flexible framework for partial-clone maintenance, not just a one-off optimization.

The tone is collaborative and pragmatic, with Siddharth readily addressing Junio’s concerns while leaving room for community input on the safety guards. The series remains an RFC, with no indication of integration branch status, but the technical questions raised in v1 appear largely resolved. The next steps likely involve gathering feedback from partial-clone users on the promisor-object enumeration and deciding whether to retain the safety guards.
