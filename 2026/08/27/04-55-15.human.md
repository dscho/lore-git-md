This is a refactoring patch in Junio C Hamano’s two-part series introducing `die_for_incompatible_opts()`, a varargs helper that replaces the fixed-arity `die_for_incompatible_optN()` family. Jeff King (Peff) reviews the second patch, which implements the new helper and updates all call sites.

Peff’s review is substantive but narrowly focused. He raises two technical concerns: first, that the varargs interface risks runtime errors from missing NULL terminators (suggesting a `LAST_ARG_MUST_BE_NULL` macro in the header as a safeguard), and second, that the hard-coded switch cases for error message formatting could complicate future changes to the four-option cap. He also notes that the current callers do not actually need more than four options, making the varargs interface’s practical benefit unclear—though he accepts the design choice to cap reported options at four rather than two.

The review is constructive and matter-of-fact, with no objections to the core refactoring. The `va_end()` omission Elijah Newren flagged in the previous email remains the only blocking issue; Peff’s points are about robustness and maintainability rather than correctness. The patch itself is mechanical, well-scoped, and touches only the option-parsing plumbing in `parse-options.c` and `parse-options.h`. No behavior changes or test failures are expected.
