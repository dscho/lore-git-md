This thread is a refactoring effort led by Junio C Hamano to overhaul the internals of `git checkout`, `git switch`, and `git restore`. The goal is to eliminate the monolithic `checkout_main()` function, which was a shared entry point that forced the newer `switch` and `restore` commands to "cheat" by piggybacking on `checkout`'s logic. Instead, the series extracts reusable logic into standalone utility functions that all three commands can call directly, placing them on equal footing.

The series is now in its second iteration as an early draft, with eight patches that apply cleanly to `master` and pass the test suite. The first five patches progressively extract initialization, validation, and branch-handling logic into smaller, focused helpers, reducing `checkout_main()`'s complexity by roughly 150 lines. The sixth patch, which Karthik Nayak recently reviewed, completes the elimination of `checkout_main()` by rewriting the three command entry points to call the extracted helpers directly. This patch is mechanical and uncontroversial, though it leaves open a design question from the first patch about whether to keep or eliminate the `cb_option` parameter. The seventh patch improves readability by wrapping long lines and using helper macros for option-compatibility checks, while the eighth moves the `post_checkout_hook()` function to a new top-level file, making it reusable by other subsystems like `git worktree`.

Karthik Nayak has been the primary reviewer so far, raising two substantive points: a design question about the `cb_option` parameter and a correctness issue in the third patch regarding option-compatibility checks. The latter was resolved after Junio confirmed the fix, though a minor detail about gating the check on `opts->pathspec.nr` remains open. D. Ben Knoble has also weighed in, agreeing with the architectural direction. The series is explicitly not ready for merging due to potential conflicts with other in-flight topics, but the first seven patches could likely be merged independently if the eighth is deferred. No broader feedback from other key contributors (e.g., René Scharfe or Patrick Steinhardt) has appeared yet, so the architectural direction remains uncontested for now. The unresolved `cb_option` question and the coordination with other refactoring efforts (like `the_repository` removal) are the main loose ends.
