This thread is about a refactoring of Git’s `checkout`, `switch`, and `restore` commands to eliminate a monolithic helper function (`checkout_main()`) and extract reusable logic into standalone utility functions. Junio C Hamano posted an 8-patch series as an early draft to illustrate the architectural direction agreed in a parent thread: built-in commands should not be libified, but their internals should be factored into helpers that accept a `struct repository *` parameter, placing all callers on equal footing.

The series is now complete and applies cleanly to `master`, passing the test suite. The first six patches extracted initialization, validation, pathspec handling, and branch setup logic, while the sixth patch eliminated the `checkout_main()` choke point by rewriting the three command entry points to call the new helpers directly. The seventh patch, just posted, is a purely stylistic cleanup: it wraps long lines and replaces manual option-compatibility checks with helper macros. A minor typo (`new_new_branch_force`) was noted but does not affect correctness. The eighth patch moves the `post_checkout_hook()` function to a new top-level file, making it available to other subsystems like `git worktree`.

No objections or alternatives have been raised yet, but the series remains a discussion starter rather than a merge candidate. The first seven patches are uncontroversial and could be merged independently if the eighth is deferred. Open questions include how this refactoring will coordinate with other in-flight topics (e.g., `the_repository` removal), whether the community will accept the extracted helpers, and what follow-up work remains to complete the libification effort. Junio’s position is definitive: the monolithic `checkout_main()` is a "cheating" pattern that should be eliminated, and the series demonstrates the path forward.
