This is the first patch in Elijah Newren’s three-part **bugfix series** targeting the merge machinery. It rewords two error messages that appear when users attempt unsafe commit operations (`git commit --amend` or `git commit <paths>`) during a rebase stop caused by an empty commit.

The current messages—“You are in the middle of a rebase—cannot amend” and “cannot do a partial commit during a rebase”—are misleading: they suggest the restriction applies to *any* rebase stop, when in fact it only applies to stops triggered by a commit that became empty. The patch replaces both messages with more specific phrasing: “You are resolving a commit that became empty—cannot amend” and “cannot do a partial commit while resolving a commit that became empty.” The change is purely textual, touching only `builtin/commit.c` and the corresponding test expectations in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh`.

The motivation is clear: generic “during a rebase” messages could confuse users into thinking the restriction is arbitrary, whereas the new wording explains the actual hazard—recording conflict resolution requires capturing the entire tree state, not rewriting HEAD or committing a subset of paths. The patch is uncontroversial and aligns with the series’ overall goal of making Git’s safety checks more precise and user-friendly. No code logic changes; this is purely a documentation improvement.
