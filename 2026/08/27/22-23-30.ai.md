This is Elijah Newren’s reply to Jeff King’s **substantive review** of patch 3/4 in the four-patch bugfix series addressing a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. The series targets a crash in `git replay` and missing-object errors in other operations caused by MIDX-packfile mismatches.

Elijah concedes that his architectural approach in patch 3/4 was flawed. The patch attempted to force `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` lookups to recover from vanished packfiles by introducing a `stale_packs_detected` flag, but Peff’s critique argued that this violated the `QUICK` flag’s intended semantics—its purpose is to optimize for genuine misses, not to recover from races. Elijah now agrees and will **drop patch 3/4 entirely**, replacing it with a targeted fix for `git mktree --batch` that removes the `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` flag while retaining `SKIP_FETCH_OBJECT`. This change will allow `mktree` to recover via the normal reprepare mechanism, eliminating the need for the `stale_packs_detected` flag and the associated packfile.c changes. The `.idx`/`.pack` race condition Peff identified in `pack_index_is_missing()` will also disappear as a result.

The decision simplifies the series and aligns with Peff’s suggestion that `mktree --batch` was misusing `QUICK` since its 2022 rewrite (commit `817b0f6027`). The remaining patches (1/4, 2/4, and 4/4) are unaffected, though patch 4/4’s `stale_packs_detected` flag may still face scrutiny in Peff’s follow-up review. The core MIDX recovery logic in patch 4/4 remains the primary fix for the race condition, and the series is now one patch shorter. Elijah’s response signals a willingness to revisit assumptions and refocus the fix on the caller (`mktree`) rather than the object database layer. The thread’s convergence is now more likely, though Peff’s pending feedback on patch 4/4 could still prompt further adjustments.
