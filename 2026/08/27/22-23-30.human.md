This is Elijah Newren’s reply to Jeff King’s substantive critique of **patch 3/4** in the four-patch bugfix series addressing a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. The series targets a crash in `git replay` and missing-object errors in other operations caused by MIDX-packfile mismatches.

Elijah concedes the architectural point: the patch’s attempt to force a second read for `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` lookups when packfiles vanish violates the flag’s intended semantics. Instead of defending the current approach, he proposes dropping patch 3/4 entirely and replacing it with a minimal fix to `git mktree --batch` that removes the `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` flag while retaining `SKIP_FETCH_OBJECT`. This change would allow `mktree` to recover from vanished packfiles via the normal object-database reprepare mechanism, just like other non-`QUICK` readers. The decision removes the need for the `stale_packs_detected` flag and the associated complexity in `packfile.c`, including the race condition Peff identified where the `.idx` file’s presence masked the `.pack` file’s disappearance.

The shift simplifies the series and aligns with Peff’s feedback, but it leaves the broader question of `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` misuse in other commands (like `git upload-pack`) unaddressed. Elijah’s reply does not engage with Peff’s suggestion to suppress or reword the "packfile ... index unavailable" error message, nor does it revisit the architectural debate about whether geometric repacking should adopt a grace period for redundant packfiles (per Derrick Stolee’s feedback). The focus is now squarely on fixing `git mktree --batch` as the immediate source of the test failures, with follow-up work deferred.

The series’ convergence appears closer, but the removal of patch 3/4 may require adjustments to the test cases in `t/t5336-repack-reader-race.sh`, which were designed to exercise the `stale_packs_detected` logic. The remaining patches (1/4, 2/4, and 4/4) remain uncontroversial, with patch 4/4’s tri-state MIDX recovery logic now the centerpiece of the fix.
