This thread tackles a long-standing performance problem in Git: when pushing from a shallow clone, the entire toplevel tree is resent even for tiny changes, ballooning transfer sizes in large repositories. Elijah Newren’s patch introduces `push.shallowExcludeBoundary` (defaulting to `true`) to treat shallow grafts as uninteresting tips during pack generation, cutting redundant transfers. The series is now at v2, with minor style fixes and a rebase onto Patrick Steinhardt’s ODB branch.

The discussion has sharpened around correctness after Derrick Stolee identified a critical flaw in v2: in multi-ref pushes, the patch could over-exclude objects needed by accepted refs if one ref is rejected for shallow-update reasons. Elijah has since clarified that this is a false-positive failure (rejecting valid refs) rather than repository corruption, as `receive-pack` enforces connectivity checks regardless of protocol. He’s also highlighted a new failure mode for `push.negotiate=true`—a race between client checks and server repacking that forces redundant transfers—bolstering his argument that negotiation alone isn’t sufficient. Stolee’s alternative (repurposing `push.negotiate` for shallow repos) avoids the flaw but may introduce latency.

Key participants remain divided on approach. Elijah defends his patch’s assumptions (e.g., servers retaining unadvertised objects) with a six-case matrix, arguing the trade-offs favor performance. Patrick Steinhardt prefers a negotiation-based solution, while Stolee’s review has shifted focus to safety. The next steps likely hinge on whether Stolee accepts Elijah’s refutation of the corruption risk or presses for further changes. If the false-positive failure is deemed acceptable, a v3 patch could emerge soon; otherwise, the thread may pivot to Stolee’s alternative.
