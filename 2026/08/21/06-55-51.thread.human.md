This thread is about a patch from Elijah Newren that improves `git push` performance from shallow clones by avoiding redundant transfers of the entire toplevel tree. The patch introduces a new config option, `push.shallowExcludeBoundary` (default: `true`), which tells `send-pack` to treat shallow grafts as uninteresting tips during pack generation, reducing unnecessary data transfer.

Elijah has addressed feedback from Patrick Steinhardt, clarifying the patch’s scope and making minor implementation tweaks. The discussion has also touched on whether the new config should default to `true` or require opt-in, given the trade-off between bandwidth savings and potential latency from an extra round-trip. A separate issue—Git’s current behavior of treating locally amended shallow grafts as new root commits—was acknowledged as a foot-gun but deferred for a future series.

In a recent follow-up, Elijah clarified why `--depth=2` clones don’t reliably solve the redundant tree-transfer problem unless combined with `push.negotiate=true`. Even then, the workaround is fragile and breaks under common workflows (e.g., GitHub’s hidden `refs/pull/` or squash merges). This reinforces the need for the proposed config, which directly addresses the negotiation gap. The thread remains under review, with no blocking objections but ongoing discussion about defaults and edge cases.
