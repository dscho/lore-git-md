This thread tackles a performance bottleneck in `git push` from shallow clones, where Git resends the entire toplevel tree (often gigabytes) even for tiny changes. Elijah Newren’s patch series—now at v2—introduces a new config option, `push.shallowExcludeBoundary`, to treat shallow grafts as uninteresting tips during pack generation, reducing redundant transfers. The series has sparked debate over three competing approaches: Elijah’s config-based solution, Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to enable `push.negotiate` by default for shallow clones, and Derrick Stolee’s alternative to repurpose `push.negotiate` for shallow repos without a new config.

The discussion took a sharp turn when Stolee identified a critical flaw in Elijah’s v2 patch: it risks corrupting the receiver’s repository by over-excluding objects needed by other refs in a multi-ref push when one ref is rejected for shallow-update reasons. This flaw, demonstrated in Stolee’s test case, is now the primary blocker for merging. Stolee’s latest email doubles down on the severity of the issue, arguing that Elijah’s assumptions about shared objects or delayed garbage collection aren’t reasonable defaults and that users need clearer recovery mechanisms. While Elijah has rebutted Patrick’s negotiation-based approach—citing scenarios like rebased pull requests, unrelated repos, and protocol limitations—Stolee’s review shifts the focus to correctness over performance trade-offs. The thread is now at a crossroads: Elijah may need to address the over-exclusion flaw in a v3, or the group may pivot toward Stolee’s safer, negotiation-based alternative. For now, the series remains under review, with repository safety as the dominant concern.
