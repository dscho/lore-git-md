This is a follow-up in the thread where Elijah Newren refines his explanation of why `--depth=2` does not reliably solve the `git push` performance problem from shallow clones. The patch under discussion introduces `push.shallowExcludeBoundary` to avoid sending redundant tree objects, and this email clarifies a common misconception about shallow clone depth.

Elijah corrects his earlier oversimplification: while `--depth=2` does not generally help with negotiation (as he previously demonstrated with test numbers), it *can* work for users whose workflows guarantee a stable ref pointing to the extra commit. He outlines the specific conditions required—forge customers who never push directly to main branches, use actual merges (not squash or rebase), and whose forges advertise merge-request refs. However, he notes that GitHub’s configuration (hiding `refs/pull/` and users deleting branches on merge) breaks this assumption, and even if it didn’t, squash merges would still prevent the optimization. The takeaway is that `--depth=2` is not a robust solution, reinforcing the need for the proposed `push.shallowExcludeBoundary` config.

The email is a minor but useful clarification, aimed at reviewers and users who might assume `--depth=2` is a workaround. It does not alter the patch’s design or address the open questions about default behavior or graft modification, but it underscores why the patch’s approach is necessary. The tone remains technical and collaborative, typical of the thread’s constructive discussion.
