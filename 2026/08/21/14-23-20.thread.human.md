This thread began as a technical patch series to replace the deprecated `utime()` function with `utimensat()` for POSIX.1-2024 compatibility, authored by Alexey Samsonov using GitGitGadget. The series is technically complete, introducing a `git_utimensat()` wrapper, replacing all `utime()` calls, and ensuring cross-platform support, including MinGW. However, the effort is now blocked by a policy dispute over AI-generated content: the patches were created with Gemini Flash and manually verified, but Git’s `SubmittingPatches` policy prohibits significant AI-generated contributions due to legal and ethical concerns about code provenance and potential copyright risks.

Junio C Hamano initially acknowledged the series positively but later highlighted the legal risks of accepting AI-generated work, citing the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) and the challenge of verifying code origin. Brian m. carlson raised a formal process objection, reinforcing the policy’s rationale and blocking further consideration until the Project Leadership Committee (PLC) or community clarifies the rules. The latest reply from Weijie Yuan complicates the discussion by pointing out that the project has already accepted commits with `Assisted-by` trailers (e.g., from Johannes Schindelin), suggesting a gray area in practice. Weijie quotes Linus Torvalds’ trust-based approach from the Linux kernel but stops short of proposing a resolution, leaving the thread stalled pending a policy decision. No technical review of the patches has advanced beyond the initial framing.
