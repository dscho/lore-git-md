This thread is about a patch series from Alexey Samsonov that replaces the deprecated `utime()` function with `utimensat()` for POSIX.1-2024 compatibility, enabling nanosecond-precision timestamps across Git’s codebase. The series is technically complete, with a new `git_utimensat()` wrapper, MinGW support, and updates to 23 files, but it has hit a roadblock: the patches were generated by an AI tool (Gemini Flash) and manually verified by the author. brian m. carlson raised a process objection, citing Git’s `SubmittingPatches` policy, which prohibits significant AI-generated content due to unclear legal implications under the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO).

Junio C Hamano has acknowledged the legal prudence of brian’s stance—AI-generated code, even if verified, may not satisfy the DCO’s requirement that contributors know the origin of their work—but also highlighted a pragmatic dilemma: the project already faces uncertainty when accepting patches from unfamiliar contributors who *don’t* disclose AI use. The distinction between rejecting AI-generated work and distrusting new contributors may be narrower than it seems, potentially creating an unintended barrier to participation. The thread is now focused on whether the project will adjust its policy to allow AI-assisted contributions, with the series’ fate hanging on input from the Project Leadership Committee (PLC) or a broader community discussion. Until then, the series remains blocked, despite its technical readiness and Junio’s earlier positive note about cross-platform support for `utimensat()`. No substantive technical review has taken place since the policy objection was raised.
