This thread began as a straightforward patch series to replace the deprecated `utime()` function with `utimensat()` for POSIX.1-2024 compatibility, authored by Alexey Samsonov. The series is technically complete, introducing a `git_utimensat()` wrapper, replacing all `utime()` calls, and ensuring MinGW compatibility. However, the discussion has shifted entirely to a **policy debate** about AI-generated content after brian m. carlson raised a process objection, citing Git’s `SubmittingPatches` policy and legal risks around LLM outputs.

The impasse centers on whether the project should accept AI-generated contributions—even with manual verification—or require rewriting the series without AI assistance. Junio C Hamano has acknowledged the technical merits but framed the legal risks as a barrier, while also questioning the policy’s strictness and suggesting a middle ground: the project might tolerate AI-assisted contributions from **trusted contributors who disclose their use of tools**. Weijie Yuan has twice highlighted a precedent—Johannes Schindelin’s prior commits with `Assisted-by` trailers—suggesting the project has already navigated this gray area. Junio’s latest email reinforces this, implying that enforcement may depend on contributor trust rather than a blanket prohibition.

No formal resolution has emerged. The series remains blocked pending clarification from the Project Leadership Committee (PLC) or broader community on whether to amend `SubmittingPatches` to reflect a more nuanced stance. The discussion has not yet escalated to the PLC, but the precedent of `Assisted-by` trailers may accelerate the need for formal guidance. Until then, the technical review of the `utimensat()` series is on hold, and the focus remains on governance, enforcement consistency, and the balance between legal caution and practical trust.
