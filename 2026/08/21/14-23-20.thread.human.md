This thread began as a straightforward patch series replacing the deprecated `utime()` function with `utimensat()` for POSIX.1-2024 compatibility, but has since become a pivotal policy discussion about AI-generated contributions in Git. The series, authored by Alexey Samsonov, is technically complete—introducing a `git_utimensat()` wrapper, replacing all `utime()` calls, and ensuring MinGW compatibility—but is blocked by a process objection from brian m. carlson. The objection centers on Git’s `SubmittingPatches` policy, which prohibits significant AI-generated content due to legal and ethical concerns, including potential copyright risks from LLM outputs and the DCO’s requirement for contributors to know the origin of their code.

Junio C Hamano has acknowledged the technical merits of the series but framed the legal risks as a barrier, though his latest emails suggest a potential middle ground: the project might tolerate AI-assisted contributions from *trusted contributors who disclose their use of tools*, balancing policy enforcement with practical trust. This shift has reframed the discussion around governance—whether the PLC or broader community must clarify or amend the policy—but no explicit path forward has been proposed. Meanwhile, Weijie Yuan’s recent email sharpens the stakes by questioning whether disclosure itself (e.g., via `Assisted-by` trailers) creates legal exposure, contrasting Junio’s earlier quip about avoiding disclosure with the kernel community’s preference for transparency. The thread remains in limbo, with the technical work on hold until the project resolves whether to accept AI-assisted contributions, how to enforce the policy consistently, and whether disclosure is a liability or a safeguard. No timeline or process for this resolution has been suggested.
