The thread is about a patch series from Alexey Samsonov that replaces the deprecated `utime()` function with `utimensat()` for POSIX.1-2024 compatibility, enabling nanosecond-precision timestamps. The series is technically complete, with a new `git_utimensat()` wrapper, MinGW support, and updates across 23 files. However, it has hit a **policy roadblock**: the patches were generated by an AI tool (Gemini Flash) and manually verified, which violates Git’s `SubmittingPatches` policy prohibiting significant AI-generated content due to legal and ethical concerns.

Junio C Hamano initially noted the series’ technical merits (e.g., macOS support for `utimensat()`) but later emphasized the legal risks, citing the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) requirement that contributors must know the origin of their code. Brian m. carlson reinforced this stance, arguing that AI-generated patches—even verified—could expose the project to copyright litigation and ethical breaches, as LLMs may regurgitate copyrighted training data. The policy discussion now hinges on whether the Project Leadership Committee (PLC) or broader community will amend `SubmittingPatches` to allow AI-assisted contributions or require the series to be rewritten without AI. Until then, the series remains blocked, with no technical review or merge path forward. Key participants are Junio (maintainer) and Brian (policy advocate); no other reviewers have weighed in on the patches’ substance.
