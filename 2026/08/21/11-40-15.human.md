This is a follow-up discussion in the thread about Patrick Steinhardt’s bugfix patch adjusting the default threshold for geometric repacking in Git’s auto-maintenance. The patch itself is ready for integration (as previously summarized), but this email digs into a deeper, orthogonal question raised by Elijah Newren: whether Git should stop pruning loose object sharding directories entirely.

Patrick responds thoughtfully, weighing two trade-offs. On one hand, pruning empty shard directories slightly speeds up lookups for nonexistent objects and reduces the number of directories scanned during full object database iterations. On the other, not pruning them could reduce write churn and eliminate the race conditions that motivated the original patch—where concurrent processes trip over disappearing directories while trying to create loose objects. His gut feeling leans toward the latter, noting that the performance benefits of pruning are marginal (since Git prioritizes packfiles for lookups and rarely iterates over all loose objects), while the write churn and race risks are real and frequent. He leaves the door open for unknown reasons behind the current pruning behavior but clearly sees merit in exploring a change.

This exchange doesn’t alter the status of the original patch—it remains a well-motivated, uncontroversial bugfix—but it plants the seed for a potential future improvement. The discussion is substantive and technical, engaging directly with Git’s object storage mechanics, and could evolve into a separate patch series if either contributor decides to pursue it. For now, it’s a candid, low-stakes exploration of a design question, typical of the mailing list’s collaborative style.
