Thread identity: Patch series to replace deprecated `utime()` with `utimensat()` for POSIX.1-2024 compatibility. Series version: initial (v1). Originating author: Alexey Samsonov (via GitGitGadget). Thread type: patch series, now a **policy discussion** regarding AI-generated content, project precedents, enforcement consistency, governance of `SubmittingPatches`, contributor trust, due diligence standards, and the legal implications of disclosure practices.

Core problem or goal: Remove all uses of the deprecated `utime()` function (removed in POSIX.1-2024) and replace it with `utimensat()` to ensure long-term compatibility and enable nanosecond-precision timestamps. The series introduces a `git_utimensat()` wrapper, replaces all `utime()` calls, removes legacy headers/shims, and includes MinGW-specific support with full interface compatibility (e.g., `UTIME_NOW`, `UTIME_OMIT`, `AT_FDCWD`). The **primary blocker is policy-related**: the patches were generated by an AI tool (Gemini Flash) and manually verified, but Git's `SubmittingPatches` policy prohibits significant AI-generated content due to legal and ethical concerns. The discussion has expanded to include **precedent** (prior `Assisted-by` trailers), **legal risks** (copyright litigation around LLM outputs and the implications of disclosure), **practical trust** in contributors (analogies to GPL/BSD contamination), **enforcement consistency**, **due diligence standards**, and **governance process** (whether the PLC or broader community must clarify or amend the policy).

Current status: **Blocked pending policy resolution**. The series is technically complete and under review, but a **process objection** from brian m. carlson has raised legal/policy concerns about AI-generated content, citing the DCO and potential copyright risks.
