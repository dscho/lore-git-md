Thread identity: Patch series to replace deprecated `utime()` with `utimensat()` for POSIX.1-2024 compatibility. Series version: initial (v1). Originating author: Alexey Samsonov (via GitGitGadget). Thread type: patch series, now a **policy discussion** regarding AI-generated content, project precedents, enforcement consistency, governance of `SubmittingPatches`, contributor trust, due diligence standards, and the legal implications of disclosure practices and code provenance.

Core problem or goal: Remove all uses of the deprecated `utime()` function (removed in POSIX.1-2024) and replace it with `utimensat()` to ensure long-term compatibility and enable nanosecond-precision timestamps. The series introduces a `git_utimensat()` wrapper, replaces all `utime()` calls, removes legacy headers/shims, and includes MinGW-specific support with full interface compatibility (e.g., `UTIME_NOW`, `UTIME_OMIT`, `AT_FDCWD`). The **primary blocker is policy-related**: the patches were generated by an AI tool (Gemini Flash) and manually verified, but Git's `SubmittingPatches` policy prohibits significant AI-generated content due to legal and ethical concerns. The discussion has expanded to include **precedent** (prior `Assisted-by` trailers), **legal risks** (copyright litigation around LLM outputs and the implications of disclosure), **practical trust** in contributors (analogies to GPL/BSD contamination), **enforcement consistency**, **due diligence standards**, and **governance process** (whether the PLC or broader community must clarify or amend the policy).

Current status: **Blocked pending policy resolution**. The series is technically complete and under review, but a **process objection** from brian m. carlson has raised legal/policy concerns about AI-generated content, citing the DCO and potential copyright risks. Junio C Hamano has acknowledged the technical merits but framed the legal risks as a barrier, while also questioning whether the policy's strictness is justified. His latest emails reframed the discussion around **contributor trust and due diligence**, suggesting the project may tolerate AI-assisted contributions from **trusted contributors who disclose their use of tools and demonstrate sufficient due diligence** (e.g., verifying the code was derived from acceptable sources like Git's own codebase or permissively licensed projects). However, Oswald Buddenhagen's reply **rejects this middle ground**, arguing that **AI tools inherently undermine the ability to verify code provenance**—even for trusted contributors—due to the risk of license contamination or copyright infringement from training data. No explicit approval, merge intent, or formal path forward has been stated. The series cannot proceed until the PLC or broader community clarifies or amends the AI-generated content policy in `SubmittingPatches`. No prior versions exist.
