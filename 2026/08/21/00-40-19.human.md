This is a **substantive technical discussion** in the ongoing review of the `git pack-objects` Trace2 byte-counting patch. The email is a follow-up exchange between Jeff King (Peff) and Patrick Steinhardt, digging deeper into a performance curiosity raised earlier: the "absurdly pessimal" triple-hashing of packfiles when `--max-pack-size` triggers a split.

Peff clarifies why the triple-hash happens and why it’s hard to eliminate. The three hashes are: (a) the hash computed during writing via `struct hashfile`, (b) the hash recomputed during read-back verification, and (c) the final hash after `fixup_pack_header_footer()` adjusts the header. The code path is only triggered when `--max-pack-size` forces a split, which is rare in practice, so the performance impact has gone unnoticed. Peff suggests the best immediate fix is to drop the read-back verification (b), but notes that the real solution—removing `--max-pack-size` entirely—would be a much larger change with unknown compatibility risks.

Both reviewers agree that the current behavior is overly paranoid. Git doesn’t re-verify other on-disk data like loose objects or refs, so triple-hashing packfiles feels inconsistent. Peff traces this to Git’s early design philosophy of aggressive checksumming, which has since been relaxed in other areas (e.g., `PARSE_OBJECT_SKIP_HASH_CHECK` and the commit graph). The discussion remains collaborative and exploratory, with no disagreement on the technical merits—only on whether the optimization is worth pursuing now.

This exchange doesn’t block the original patch (which adds a `write_pack_file/wrote_bytes` Trace2 event) but highlights a potential follow-up performance improvement. The author of the original patch is likely to proceed with v3 incorporating the earlier consensus on hash algorithm consistency (`f->algop->rawsz`), while this triple-hash discussion may resurface in a future series. For now, the patch remains under review, focused on its core observability goal.
