This is the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s five-patch v3 series refactoring the object database (ODB) subsystem to remove lazy-loading of alternates. The email is a follow-up discussion between Patrick and Junio C Hamano about the design and correctness of the deduplication mechanism for alternate ODB sources, specifically the use of `core.ignoreCase` for case-insensitive path comparisons.

The exchange clarifies the practical trade-offs of the current approach. Patrick argues that while the mechanism is imperfect—it doesn’t resolve symlinks or mountpoints, and `core.ignoreCase` may not always reflect filesystem behavior—it has worked reliably for two decades without reported issues. He acknowledges that the hashmap-based deduplication is likely overkill for typical use cases (fewer than a dozen alternates) but defends retaining it to avoid regressing edge cases, such as repositories with thousands of alternates. Junio’s concerns about inconsistent behavior across filesystems with mixed case sensitivity are met with skepticism: Patrick notes that no real-world problems have been reported, and retroactively imposing limitations (e.g., declaring repositories cannot span such filesystems) could break existing setups.

On the implementation side, Patrick confirms that his earlier refactoring in `setup.c` (patch 1/5) ensures the repository is fully initialized before ODB creation, addressing Junio’s worry about `o->repo->initialized` being false in `odb_source_paths_cmp()`. He demonstrates this with a proof-of-concept `BUG()` check that passes all tests except the in-memory ODB unit test (which intentionally skips full initialization). The discussion leaves the `NEEDSWORK` note in the patch intact, signaling that the case-insensitive deduplication mechanism may still be removed in the future, but no immediate changes are proposed.

The unresolved tension here is between simplicity and backward compatibility. Junio’s preference for dropping the mechanism entirely (or at least defaulting to case-sensitive comparisons) clashes with Patrick’s reluctance to regress even theoretical edge cases. The thread does not resolve this, but the v3 patch retains the current behavior while documenting its limitations. The broader series remains queued in Junio’s `seen` branch, pending resolution of the `core.ignoreCase` timing constraints in patch 1/5.
