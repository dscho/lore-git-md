This is a substantive review of Elijah Newren's performance patch for `git push` from shallow clones. Patrick Steinhardt engages deeply with the technical design, raising several questions and suggesting minor improvements.

The review focuses on the core logic in `send-pack.c`, particularly the new `append_reachable_shallow_grafts()` function. Patrick questions whether the current behavior when a shallow graft is locally modified (e.g., via `git commit --amend`) is safe, and whether the patch's assumption that the server already has the shallow graft could lead to insufficient data being sent in such cases. He also explores whether the negotiation could be extended to include tree objects to verify graft integrity, though he acknowledges potential compatibility risks with `git-receive-pack` and other clients.

On implementation details, Patrick suggests marking input-only parameters as `const` and combining the two loops over `refs` for efficiency. He also asks whether higher-level commit-graph interfaces like `repo_is_descendant_of()` could simplify the reachability walk, though he concedes the current approach may be sufficient given shallow clones' limited history.

The review requests additional test coverage for cases where the grafted commit is modified, to clarify current behavior and how the patch changes it. Patrick also questions whether the new `push.shallowExcludeBoundary` config should default to `true` outright, rather than requiring users to opt in.

No objections block the patch, but the questions about graft modification and negotiation scope could lead to design refinements. The review is thorough and technical, engaging with both the patch's correctness and its broader implications for shallow clone workflows.
