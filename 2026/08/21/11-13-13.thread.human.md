This thread is a user inquiry into why Git’s `--ext-diff` option exists and why external diff drivers (configured via `diff.<driver>.command` or `diff.external`) are not enabled by default in all commands that show diffs, such as `git show`. The question, raised by Anselm Schüler, seeks to understand whether this behavior stems from security concerns, performance trade-offs, or historical oversight.

Jeff King (Peff) provided the first substantive response, explaining that external diff drivers are suppressed in some commands (like `git show`) to avoid disruptive side effects, such as spawning GUI windows during operations like `git log`. He suggested that the distinction between `git diff` (which allows external diffs by default) and `git show` (which does not) might be an oversight rather than a deliberate design choice. Peff also recommended `textconv` as a safer, more integrated alternative for most use cases, though he acknowledged his bias as its author.

Junio C Hamano, the maintainer and original author of `--ext-diff`, has now weighed in to endorse Peff’s explanation. Junio agrees that `textconv` is generally a better fit for most users due to its constrained and integrated design, despite his own creation of `--ext-diff`. He notes that his *only* remaining practical use for `--ext-diff` is in maintaining the "What's cooking" report drafts, and even that could likely be replaced with a combination of `textconv`, diff hunk header patterns, and the `-W` (function-context) option. This reinforces the idea that `--ext-diff`’s role is largely niche and that its suppression in commands like `git show` is not a significant limitation for most workflows.

The thread remains at the inquiry stage, with no patches or RFCs proposed. The key takeaway is that the current behavior—where external diffs are allowed by default in `git diff` but not in `git show`—is likely intentional, even if the rationale is not explicitly documented. Future discussion might explore whether this behavior should be rationalized (e.g., via documentation or a config option), but Junio’s response suggests that any changes would need a compelling use case to justify breaking existing workflows. For now, the thread has clarified the design intent behind `--ext-diff` and its default behavior, though no actionable changes are on the horizon.
