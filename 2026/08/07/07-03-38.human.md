This is a **substantive review** of the third patch in Justin Tobler’s six-patch series making `git receive-pack` backend-agnostic via ODB transactions. The patch mechanically lifts global state out of the `unpack()` function by introducing a `struct unpack_opts` that bundles previously global variables (`fsck_msg_types`, `shallow_file`, `max_input_size`, `fsck_objects`, `unpack_limit`, `reject_thin`, `err_fd`, and `quiet`) and passes them explicitly.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review is thorough and constructive. He catches a minor typo in the commit message ("can no rely" → "can no longer rely") and notes that the refactoring is hard to audit for completeness—though he assumes the subsequent patch (which moves `unpack()` to a different file) will surface any missed conversions. His main concern is ergonomic: the initialization of `fsck_objects` in `unpack_with_sideband()` is visually cluttered, and the need to modify the `opts` struct after construction (to set `err_fd`) feels awkward. He accepts these trade-offs but flags them as potential maintenance pain points as the interface evolves.

The review does not raise correctness issues or suggest blocking changes, but it highlights areas where future callers might struggle with the API’s design. This patch remains a preparatory step; the real test will come in patch 6/6, where `odb_transaction_write_pack()` is introduced. For now, the refactoring is clean and aligns with the series’ goal of eliminating globals.
