This is the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s v2 series reintroducing a `writev(3p)`-like wrapper for performance. It adds the core plumbing: `writev_in_full()` in `wrapper.c` and `writev_or_die()` in `write-or-die.c`, plus their declarations in the corresponding headers.

The patch is a straightforward infrastructure piece. `writev_in_full()` mimics the familiar `write_in_full()` pattern: it loops until all `iovec` entries are written, handling `EINTR`/`EAGAIN` and partial writes by adjusting the `iov` array in place. `writev_or_die()` is the die-on-failure variant, identical in spirit to `write_or_die()`. The commit message is minimal, but the code is clean and the intent is clear—these are the generic wrappers the project now expects for batched I/O.

No behavior change is introduced yet; the actual call sites come in later patches. The patch is uncontroversial on its own, but it sits in the middle of a series whose overall value (performance gain vs. added complexity) is still under maintainer assessment. If the series lands, this will be the plumbing everyone uses; if not, it’s dead code.
