This is a test-only patch in the v7 series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. It adds `t6099-merge-base-side-exhaustion.sh`, a new regression test targeting the side-exhaustion optimization introduced earlier in the series.

The test verifies that `git merge-base --all` correctly excludes redundant merge-base candidates when one is an ancestor of another, even when the side-exhaustion optimization exits early (before STALE propagation removes the ancestor). The topology is a diamond-shaped graph where `B` and `C` are both merge bases of `A` and `X`, but only `B` should appear in the output. The test runs with and without commit-graph to ensure the optimization behaves correctly in both scenarios.

Elijah Newren’s review suggests a minor improvement to the ASCII graph in the test header for clarity (switching to a fixed-width font representation), but this is purely cosmetic and does not affect the test’s correctness or coverage. He also notes that the test could be strengthened by adding Trace2 region verification to confirm which codepath is exercised, though he does not consider this worth a re-roll.

The patch is well-motivated and directly addresses a correctness concern raised during review of the optimization. The test infrastructure is clean and follows Git’s conventions, making it easy to understand and maintain. This is a targeted addition to the series’ test coverage and looks ready to advance.
