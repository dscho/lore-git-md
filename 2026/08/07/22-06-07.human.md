This is a platform-compatibility patch in Johannes Schindelin’s twelve-part series converting size-related fields in Git’s delta and pack machinery from `unsigned long` to `size_t`. The series targets 64-bit Windows, where `unsigned long` is 32-bit and can truncate values larger than 4 GiB, but the changes are motivated by type consistency and correctness across all platforms.

This specific patch widens two tightly coupled types: the `*left` output parameter of `use_pack()` and the `avail_in`/`avail_out` fields in `struct git_zstream`. The change is mechanical and preparatory, enabling the next patch in the series to remove the last `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` shim. The commit message explains that these fields are already effectively bounded by `zlib_buf_cap()`’s 1 GiB cap, so the widening does not introduce new >4 GiB capabilities but aligns the type system with existing behavior.

Junio C Hamano’s reply raises two technical questions about potential truncation risks that could undermine the series’ goal of full type consistency. First, `zlib_buf_cap()` still returns `unsigned long`, and `zlib_pre_call()` passes the now-wider `avail_in`/`avail_out` values to it. Second, `unpack_object_header_buffer()` still takes an `unsigned long` length parameter, while `oe_get_size_slow()` passes a `size_t` value to it. Both cases could trigger truncation before comparison with constants like `ZLIB_BUF_MAX`, and Junio notes that a static checker might flag them. He describes the risks as “probably harmless in practice” but “concerning from the standpoint of type consistency.”

The series as a whole remains uncontroversial and is already queued in `next` for integration testing. However, these two details may require follow-up patches to fully resolve the type system alignment. The questions are narrowly scoped and do not challenge the series’ direction or motivation, but they do highlight that the conversion is not yet complete. For a busy reader, this patch is worth tracking if you care about platform compatibility or the ongoing `unsigned long` -> `size_t` effort, but the open questions suggest it may not graduate to `master` without additional work.
