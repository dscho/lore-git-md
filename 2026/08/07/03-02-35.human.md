This is the final patch in the `paint_down_to_common()` optimization series, now at v7. It implements the core performance improvement: early termination of the merge-base walk when one side of the history is exhausted. The patch adds a termination check to `paint_queue_get()` that activates once the walk enters the finite-generation region (where topological ordering guarantees hold) and no merge-base candidates remain. This eliminates unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories, delivering the advertised 100-1000x speedup in asymmetric queries—most visibly in the `merge-base --all commit-9-9 commit-9-1` test, where step counts drop from 81 to 9 in full commit-graph mode.

The patch is technically sound and well-motivated, with clear documentation updates explaining the new "side-exhaustion" termination condition and why the optimization is skipped in the INFINITY region (where commit-date ordering can violate topological guarantees). Elijah Newren’s review raises a minor but important point about `GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX`: the documentation and code currently gloss over how v1 commit-graphs (which use saturated generation numbers) interact with the optimization. This is addressed in the next two patches, which remove the commit-date fallback entirely and introduce a topological ceiling for v1 graphs. The split feels slightly awkward—this patch could arguably include the v1 handling—but the separation doesn’t affect correctness, and the series remains procedurally unblocked.

It’s a clear win for repositories with asymmetric histories (e.g., import grafts or shallow clones) and requires no further action from the author unless additional review feedback surfaces.
