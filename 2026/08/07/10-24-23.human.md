This is a CI/build system discussion following up on Jeff King’s two-patch series to bump the Ubuntu image for Git’s static-analysis job. The series itself is already approved in principle by Junio and is on track for integration, but Patrick Steinhardt raises a broader point about Coccinelle maintenance.

Patrick notes that while upgrading Coccinelle to a newer version (which the series does) is a quick win for performance, Git’s collection of Coccinelle semantic patches has grown over time and likely contains rules that are no longer useful. He lists several candidates for removal—`refs.cocci`, `object_id.cocci`, `the_repository.cocci`, `git_config_number.cocci`, `index-compatibility.cocci`, and `context_fn_ctx.cocci`—arguing that the migrations they were designed to help with are long complete and the rules are probably just adding overhead. He hasn’t measured the impact but suggests that pruning obsolete rules could further speed up the static-analysis job.

The observation is substantive but not a blocker for the current series; it’s a separate cleanup idea that could be pursued later. No action is required from Jeff or Junio at this stage, and the original patches remain ready for queuing.
