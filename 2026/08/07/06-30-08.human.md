This is a surface-level review of the first patch in Vincent Mailhol’s four-part series adding Bash completion for the `git history` command. Patrick Steinhardt points out a maintainability concern in the helper function `__git_history_has_revision`: it hard-codes the list of options that take arguments (`--empty` and `--update-refs`), making it brittle if new options are added later. He suggests a simpler approach—always complete references unless a `--` has been seen—using the existing `__git_hash_doubledash` helper. This would trade some precision for robustness and ease of maintenance, but Patrick notes it would still be a major improvement over the current lack of completion.

The review is narrowly focused on a single implementation detail and does not question the overall design or necessity of the series. No alternative technical solutions are proposed beyond the suggested simplification, and the tone is pragmatic rather than critical. The patch itself remains uncontroversial and well-scoped, targeting only the completion logic for `git history` subcommands. If the author adopts Patrick’s suggestion, the change should be minimal and unlikely to disrupt the rest of the series.
