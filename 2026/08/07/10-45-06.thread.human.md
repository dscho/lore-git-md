This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s six-patch series v2 that makes Git’s packfile generation pluggable, a key step in his larger ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to decouple the transport layer (fetch, push, bundle) from the "files" ODB backend, enabling future support for alternative backends. The series has now completed its stated scope: all pack streams leaving the repository delegate generation to the ODB layer via a new `odb_generate_pack()` interface.

The series is in its second iteration, posted as `[PATCH v2 0/6]`, and is queued in Junio C Hamano’s `seen` branch after Junio resolved a merge conflict with the `--no-ref-delta` topic. While no prior versions have been merged to `next` or `master`, the series is under active review, with Junio and Patrick addressing feedback. The latest review from Junio focuses on a narrow but important detail in patch 5/6: the use of an `extern` declaration for `get_log_output_encoding()` as a workaround for its implicit dependency on `the_repository`. Junio objected to this approach, as it bypasses the compile-time guard (`USE_THE_REPOSITORY_VARIABLE`) meant to prevent reintroducing global repository references. He suggested omitting the `extern` hunks while keeping the rest of the patch, which mechanically replaces `the_repository` with explicit repository parameters.

Patrick has already addressed Junio’s earlier feedback from v1 by splitting the original patch 5/5 into three patches (progress-meter refactoring, `the_repository` removal, and ODB migration) and eliminating most global repository references. The remaining loose end—the `get_log_output_encoding()` dependency—is now the primary open question, though it’s unlikely to block the series. Other pending items include deeper testing of edge cases (e.g., shallow clones, partial clones, error handling) and potential performance regressions in transport operations. The long-term plan for `git-pack-objects` and repository maintenance remains deferred to a future `git-objects` command. For now, the series is on track, with Junio’s latest review serving as a targeted correction rather than a broader objection.
