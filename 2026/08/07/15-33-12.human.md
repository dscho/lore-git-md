This is the third patch in Justin Tobler’s six-patch series making `git receive-pack` backend-agnostic by replacing its hardcoded use of `git index-pack` and `git unpack-objects` with a new ODB transaction interface. The series is part of Patrick Steinhardt’s ongoing ODB abstraction effort, which aims to enable alternative object storage backends (e.g., reftable, cloud storage) to handle incoming packfiles without being tied to the traditional files backend.

This patch lifts eight global variables (`fsck_msg_types`, `shallow_file`, `max_input_size`, `fsck_objects`, `unpack_limit`, `reject_thin`, `err_fd`, `quiet`) out of the `unpack()` function and into a new `struct unpack_opts`, which is passed explicitly through the call chain. The change is mechanical and does not alter observable behavior, but it is a necessary step toward the larger goal of making packfile ingestion backend-agnostic. The patch is well-motivated and aligns with the series’ overall direction, though the commit message could be clearer about why this refactoring is needed (it currently assumes the reader already knows the series’ goal).

Patrick Steinhardt’s review is substantive but focused on ergonomics rather than correctness. He notes that the diff is hard to audit for completeness because the old global references are scattered, making it difficult to verify that all globals have been lifted. He also flags two specific friction points: the ternary initialization of `fsck_objects` inside the struct initializer is visually awkward, and the late assignment of `err_fd` in `unpack_with_sideband()` forces mutation of the passed-in struct, which feels unergonomic. Steinhardt accepts these trade-offs as necessary for the larger goal but invites future scrutiny when the function moves to a different file in a later patch. No functional bugs are identified.

Justin Tobler’s reply is brief and acknowledges the feedback. He agrees to fix the typo in the commit message and notes that he experimented with alternative ways to initialize `fsck_objects` but found them no more readable. The exchange is constructive and suggests that the next iteration will address the minor issues raised without changing the patch’s core approach.

The patch is clearly part of a well-structured series and looks like a solid step toward the ODB abstraction goal. The ergonomic concerns raised are real but not showstoppers, and the author’s willingness to iterate suggests the next version will be an improvement. For anyone following the ODB abstraction effort, this patch is worth tracking, but it is unlikely to be controversial on its own.
