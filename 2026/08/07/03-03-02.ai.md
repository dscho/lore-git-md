This is the final patch in the `paint_down_to_common()` optimization series, removing the commit-date ordering fallback entirely. The patch is a **cleanup and correctness fix** that eliminates a long-standing workaround for v1 commit-graphs, now made obsolete by the side-exhaustion optimization introduced earlier in the series.

The fallback was originally added to avoid a performance regression in v1 commit-graphs, where topological levels could cause asymmetric queries (e.g., `git merge-base v4.8 v4.9` in the Linux kernel repo) to walk hundreds of thousands of commits. However, the fallback introduced its own correctness issues by violating topological invariants, potentially causing premature termination or incorrect merge-base selection. The side-exhaustion optimization solves the performance problem more elegantly by terminating the walk as soon as one side of the history is exhausted, reducing step counts from **167k to 5.7k** (v1 graph) and **3.9k** (v2 graph) in the Linux kernel repo.

The patch replaces the fallback with a **topological ceiling** (`GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX`) for v1 commit-graphs, ensuring saturated generation numbers are treated as unordered while preserving the early-exit optimization’s correctness. The change removes the `gen_ordered` flag from `paint_state`, adds `topo_ceiling` to enforce the ceiling for v1 graphs, and updates the technical documentation to remove the now-obsolete "date-ordering fallback" section. Test expectations are adjusted to reflect the reduced step counts (e.g., `test_paint_down_steps 81 9 57 37` instead of `81 9 57 81`).
