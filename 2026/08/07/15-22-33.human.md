This is a review of the second patch in Son Luong Ngoc’s v3 series fixing `git rebase --update-refs` handling of symbolic references (symrefs). Phillip Wood’s feedback is substantive but narrow: he raises two specific questions about the implementation’s interaction with worktree state and symref resolution logic, suggesting the patch might be over-engineered for edge cases that rarely occur in practice.

Phillip’s first question asks why the patch resolves symrefs when preparing the list of checked-out branches, given that the first patch already skips symrefs during todo-list generation. He suspects this could lead to inconsistencies if a branch and its symref alias are checked out in different worktrees, and wonders if the earlier part of the function should instead store both the symref and its target when walking worktree HEADs. His second question is more procedural: he proposes deferring this patch entirely, arguing that the first patch (which handles the common case of branch-to-branch symrefs) is sufficient for now, and that the edge case of multiple symrefs pointing outside `refs/heads/` can wait until it proves problematic in real workflows.

The review is constructive but signals caution about overcomplicating the fix. Phillip’s tone suggests he sees this patch as a “nice-to-have” rather than a critical bugfix, and his questions imply the current implementation may be solving a problem that doesn’t yet exist at scale. The thread’s earlier consensus—retaining robustness for symrefs pointing outside `refs/heads/`—remains intact, but this review introduces a pragmatic note: the project might prefer a simpler, staged approach. The author will likely need to either justify the edge case’s real-world relevance or accept deferring this patch to a future iteration.
