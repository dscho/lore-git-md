This thread is about D. Ben Knoble’s three-patch series converting Git’s build-time `USE_NSEC` macro into a runtime configuration option, `core.useNanosec`. The goal is to let users enable nanosecond precision on supported filesystems without recompiling Git, reducing "racy Git" problems. The series is now in its fourth iteration (`v4`) and is cooking in Junio’s `next` branch under the topic `dk/use-nsec-runtime`, effectively ready to graduate to `master` after final polish.

The core change replaces `#ifdef USE_NSEC` with a runtime check controlled by `core.useNanosec` (defaulting to false). The `use_nanosec` field is unconditionally added to `struct repo_config_values` and initialized to `0`, ensuring no uninitialized reads even on platforms where `NO_NSEC` is defined. The nested preprocessor guards in `is_racy_stat()` are simplified to a single ternary expression for clarity. Documentation now warns users about platform/filesystem limitations and lists supported filesystems. A performance regression in the first version (~41× slowdown in `git diff`) was fixed by caching the value in `repo_config_values`. Junio and Patrick Steinhardt agreed to omit a runtime warning for `NO_NSEC` platforms where `core.useNanosec=true` is set, prioritizing user experience for shared configurations. The only remaining loose ends are platform-dependent test coverage and a potential naming issue (`core.useNanosec` might be too specific if future platforms offer extended precision), but neither is blocking.

The series also includes two uncontroversial patches: a Meson build-system fix (exposing `MAN_BASE_URL` as a configurable knob) and a no-op refactor of `repo_config_values_init()` to group assignments by config section. A textual conflict exists with `ty/repo-config-cleanups` in `seen`, but it’s trivial to resolve. The series is based on `master` and touches files in `read-cache.c`, `environment.c`, and `Documentation/`. All review feedback from Junio and Patrick has been addressed, and the series is now effectively complete.
