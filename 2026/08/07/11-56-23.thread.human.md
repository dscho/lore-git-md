This thread is about a three-patch series from D. Ben Knoble that replaces Git’s build-time `USE_NSEC` macro with a runtime configuration option (`core.useNanosec`) to enable nanosecond precision without recompiling. The series also includes a Meson build-system fix and a no-op refactor of `repo_config_values_init`.

The first patch—a small Meson build-system fix to expose `man_base_url` for downstream flexibility—has now been confirmed as correct by Patrick Steinhardt. This aligns Meson’s behavior with the existing Makefile and fixes broken relative links in man pages. The change is uncontroversial and ready to advance.

The core patch introducing `core.useNanosec` hit a major performance snag in its initial version, causing a 41× slowdown in `git diff` benchmarks due to repeated config lookups. The author has since benchmarked a fix that caches the value in the repository’s `repo_settings` struct, restoring performance. However, Patrick Steinhardt raised an architectural question: whether to cache the setting in `repo_config_values` (modern but limited to `the_repository`) or `repo_settings` (supports per-repository variation, e.g., for submodules). The author is preparing a v2 that will include the performance fix, a cosmetic commit-message tweak, and a decision on the cache location.

The series is under active review, with the Meson fix now resolved and the core patch’s performance regression addressed. The remaining open questions are architectural (cache location) and logistical (conflict with `ty/repo-config-cleanups` in `seen`). Junio and Patrick have both weighed in on the technical trade-offs, while SZEDER Gábor’s review was limited to commit-message formatting. The next iteration should clarify the caching strategy and resolve the textual conflict.
