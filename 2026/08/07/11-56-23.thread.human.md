This thread is about converting Git’s build-time `USE_NSEC` macro into a runtime configuration option, `core.useNanosec`, allowing users to enable nanosecond precision on supported filesystems without recompiling Git. The series, now at version 5 (`v5`), is effectively complete and ready to graduate to `master` under the topic `dk/use-nsec-runtime`. The originating author, D. Ben Knoble, has addressed all prior review feedback, including a performance regression in the initial implementation and a cross-platform correctness blocker that risked silent data corruption on platforms lacking nanosecond support.

Key participants include Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Patrick Steinhardt, and SZEDER Gábor. Junio and Patrick identified critical issues in earlier versions—such as the performance flaw and the risk of uninitialized reads on `NO_NSEC` platforms—and their concerns have been fully resolved. The series now unconditionally initializes the `use_nanosec` field in `struct repo_config_values` and simplifies the preprocessor guards in `is_racy_stat()` to a single ternary expression. Junio and Patrick also rejected the idea of a runtime warning for unsupported platforms, prioritizing user experience for shared configurations. The only remaining loose ends are platform-dependent test coverage (complicated by filesystem variability) and a routine conflict with `ty/repo-config-cleanups` in `seen`.

The core change introduces `core.useNanosec` (defaulting to false) and caches its value in `repo_config_values` for performance. The patch preserves backward compatibility by treating the field as false on `NO_NSEC` platforms, eliminating any risk of silent corruption. While the new dependency on `repo_config_values()` in `is_racy_stat()` introduces a forward-looking constraint—limiting future in-process submodule operations—this is not a regression and does not block graduation. The series is uncontroversial, with all technical objections addressed, and is now awaiting routine integration.
