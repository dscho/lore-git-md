This thread replaces Git’s build-time `USE_NSEC` macro with a runtime configuration option, `core.useNanosec`, allowing users to enable nanosecond precision on supported filesystems without recompiling. The series, now at v5, is effectively complete and ready to graduate to `master`. The core change resolves "racy Git" problems by making nanosecond precision configurable at runtime, while preserving backward compatibility on platforms lacking nanosecond support (where the option is silently ignored). A performance regression in early versions (~41× slowdown in `git diff`) was fixed by caching the config value in the repository’s `repo_config_values` struct, restoring baseline performance.

Key participants—D. Ben Knoble (author), Junio C Hamano (maintainer), and Patrick Steinhardt—have resolved all technical concerns. The cross-platform correctness blocker (silent corruption risk on `NO_NSEC` platforms) was addressed by unconditionally including the `use_nanosec` field in the struct but treating it as false when `NO_NSEC` is defined. Junio and Patrick also rejected a runtime warning for unsupported platforms, prioritizing shared-configuration usability. The only remaining open items are minor: a trivial commit-message tweak (changing "defined" to "enabled" in a comment) and a textual conflict with `ty/repo-config-cleanups` in `seen`, neither of which block graduation. The series is uncontroversial, well-tested, and praised for reducing preprocessor directives. Expect it to land in `master` shortly after the final cosmetic fix.
