This is a review comment from Junio C Hamano on the seventh patch in Johannes Schindelin’s twelve-patch series widening size fields in Git’s delta and pack machinery from `unsigned long` to `size_t`. The series targets platform compatibility, specifically addressing truncation risks on 64-bit Windows where `unsigned long` is 32-bit.

Junio’s feedback focuses on two potential truncation risks introduced by the patch. First, he notes that `zlib_buf_cap()` remains `unsigned long`, and the widened `avail_in`/`avail_out` fields in `struct git_zstream` are passed to it via `zlib_pre_call()`. This could cause truncation if the values exceed `ULONG_MAX`, even though `zlib_buf_cap()` caps them at `ZLIB_BUF_MAX` (1 GiB). Second, he points out that `unpack_object_header_buffer()` still takes an `unsigned long` length parameter, and `oe_get_size_slow()` in `builtin/pack-objects.c` passes a `size_t` value to it, which could also truncate if the value exceeds `ULONG_MAX`.

The review is substantive, engaging with the patch’s technical implications rather than surface-level details. Junio’s questions suggest he is concerned about whether the type widening could introduce new truncation risks in practice, despite the patch’s stated goal of improving type consistency. His feedback does not reject the patch but requests clarification on whether these truncation risks are real or theoretical, and whether the author’s static checker would flag them. The series as a whole remains uncontroversial and ready for integration, but this specific patch may require a follow-up to address these concerns.
