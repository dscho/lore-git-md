This is a **substantive review** from Jeff King (Peff) on patch 2/2 of Elijah Newren’s two-patch series fixing a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. The series targets a crash in `git replay` and missing-object errors in server-side operations caused by multi-pack-index (MIDX) references to removed packfiles.

Peff’s review challenges the core premise of the patch: whether the `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` flag’s behavior is actually a bug or working as designed. He argues that `QUICK` callers (like `upload-pack` and `mktree --batch`) explicitly accept false negatives to avoid the performance cost of reloading packfiles, and that the current patch’s recovery logic undermines this trade-off. His critique hinges on the distinction between *false negatives* (expected under `QUICK`) and *permanent failures* (the bug being fixed). Peff suggests that if callers cannot tolerate false negatives, the fix belongs in those callers—not in the low-level lookup code.

The review also surfaces a subtle technical point: the patch’s recovery logic (triggered when a `.idx` file fails to open) misses cases where the `.idx` is still mmapped but the `.pack` file is gone. Peff notes that Git’s existing `is_pack_valid()` check already handles this scenario for regular packfiles, implying the MIDX recovery logic may be redundant or incomplete.

Elijah’s prior response to Patrick Steinhardt acknowledged the `QUICK` caller distinction and mentioned three follow-up patches to address broader race conditions, but Peff’s feedback suggests the current patch’s scope may need rethinking. The discussion is now at a crossroads: is the patch a targeted fix for a real bug, or does it risk reintroducing performance regressions for `QUICK` callers? The outcome could shape how Git handles object-lookup failures in high-concurrency scenarios. No resolution is reached in this email, but the technical depth of the exchange makes it a critical datapoint for the series’ future.
