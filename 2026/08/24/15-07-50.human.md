This is a short exchange in the ongoing `git history squash` thread, focusing on the final unresolved issue: **how strictly to enforce case conventions for commit OIDs in autosquash markers** (`fixup!`, `squash!`, `amend!`). Junio C Hamano pushes back against Phillip Wood’s suggestion that accepting all-uppercase OIDs might be reasonable, pointing to an earlier topic (`bc/restrict-hex-to-lowercase`) where brian m. carlson advocated **banning uppercase OIDs entirely**. Junio argues that since Git itself never emits uppercase hexadecimal, `starts_with()` (case-sensitive matching) is sufficient and `istarts_with()` (case-insensitive) is unnecessary.

The exchange clarifies the stakes: the choice is now between **strict case-sensitive matching** (rejecting all uppercase OIDs, aligning with brian’s position) or **permissive matching** (accepting all-uppercase OIDs, matching `git rebase --autosquash`). Junio’s preference for `starts_with()` suggests he favors the stricter approach, but the thread remains open—this is a policy question, not a technical one, and may require broader input before Harald Nordgren rerolls the series for final integration.
