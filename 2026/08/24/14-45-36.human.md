This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Elijah Newren’s two-patch bugfix series for a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. Derrick Stolee’s email steps back from the implementation details to discuss the broader architectural context and trade-offs.

Stolee acknowledges the race condition—where a stale multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a packfile removed by a concurrent geometric repack—as a known challenge, particularly on Windows where open file handles can block deletions. He notes that Git already has a mitigation for this: the `git multi-pack-index expire` command, which removes packfiles whose objects are fully covered by other packs while preserving them in the MIDX for a grace period. This mechanism is used in incremental repacks (e.g., `git multi-pack-index repack`) to allow existing processes to continue using stale MIDX entries without disruption. Stolee suggests that geometric repacking could adopt a similar approach to reduce the frequency of the race condition, rather than relying solely on the fallback logic introduced in Elijah’s patch.

While Stolee praises the robustness of Elijah’s fix, he frames it as a last-resort solution. His key point is that the race condition is inherently complex, and the project should aim to *avoid* triggering the fallback logic whenever possible. He advocates for a design that tolerates redundant data (e.g., keeping packfiles referenced by stale MIDX entries for a short period) to minimize the need for recovery mechanisms. This aligns with his broader focus on large-repository performance, where the overhead of scanning multiple packs—even in edge cases—could become noticeable.

The email does not propose a specific alternative or request changes to the patch, but it introduces a strategic consideration for future work: whether to prioritize immediate cleanup (and accept the risk of races) or to embrace temporary redundancy (to reduce the need for recovery logic). This perspective adds nuance to the ongoing discussion between Jeff King (Peff), Patrick Steinhardt, and Elijah about the performance implications of the fallback mechanism. Stolee’s input suggests that the project may eventually need to balance the fix’s correctness with proactive measures to prevent the race from occurring in the first place.
