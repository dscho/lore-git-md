This is a **substantive follow-up** in the design discussion about Elijah Newren’s patch 2/2, which fixes a race condition in Git’s packed-object backend when a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a removed packfile. Jeff King (Peff) acknowledges Patrick Steinhardt’s clarification that the patch addresses a real bug—objects disappearing from the MIDX when the preferred pack is removed—and accepts the need for a targeted fix. However, he raises a **performance concern** about the current implementation: the fallback logic, which scans all packs covered by the MIDX when the preferred pack is missing, could become expensive if the MIDX spans many packs (e.g., a million).

Peff suggests that the fallback workaround should be **gated by `SECOND_READ`** (the flag indicating a retry after a refresh), arguing that `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` callers are already prepared to tolerate false negatives and shouldn’t incur the cost of a full MIDX pack scan. He acknowledges that the performance impact is likely minimal in practice (since the fallback only triggers when the MIDX *should* have found the object), but the principle of avoiding unnecessary work in `QUICK` mode remains. The discussion now centers on whether to **restrict the fallback to non-`QUICK` callers** or retain the current behavior, which applies the fix unconditionally.

No code changes are proposed here, but the outcome of this trade-off—correctness vs. performance—could influence the final implementation. The patch remains a high-priority fix for a crash-inducing race condition, but the design may need adjustment to address Peff’s concern. The thread is now focused on refining the fallback logic rather than questioning the fix’s necessity.
