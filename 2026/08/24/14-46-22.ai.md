This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Elijah Newren’s two-patch bugfix series addressing a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. Derrick Stolee’s email introduces a new architectural consideration: the existing `git multi-pack-index expire` mechanism, which already mitigates similar race conditions by leaving redundant packfiles in place until all processes using them have finished. Stolee suggests that geometric repacking could adopt a similar approach to reduce the frequency of the race condition, thereby minimizing reliance on the fallback logic introduced in patch 2/2.

The key insight here is that `multi-pack-index expire` is designed to handle the same class of race conditions—where a packfile is removed while still being referenced by a stale multi-pack-index (MIDX)—by deferring deletion until all processes have finished using the pack. This is particularly critical on Windows, where file locks can prevent deletions entirely. Stolee’s feedback implies that the fallback logic in patch 2/2, while correct, may be less necessary if geometric repacking were to adopt a grace period for redundant packfiles, similar to the `expire` mechanism. This would reduce the runtime cost of the fallback by making the race condition itself rarer.

Stolee’s review does not challenge the correctness of the fix or the need for it, but it does shift the architectural discussion toward a broader strategy for managing packfile lifecycles. The current patch 2/2 focuses on recovery *after* the race has occurred, while Stolee’s suggestion aims to prevent the race from happening in the first place. This aligns with the ongoing debate between Jeff King (Peff) and Patrick Steinhardt about the performance implications of the fallback logic, particularly whether it should be tied to the `SECOND_READ` phase or remain as currently implemented. Stolee’s feedback adds a new dimension: the fallback’s cost could be further reduced by adopting a grace period for redundant packfiles, making the recovery logic a last-resort mechanism rather than a routine one.
