This is a design clarification reply in an ongoing user inquiry about Git’s `--ext-diff` option.

Jeff King (Peff) explains that external diff drivers are not enabled by default in all commands because they can execute arbitrary code—including opening GUI windows or performing other side effects. This could be disruptive in commands like `git log`, where iterating over many commits might spawn dozens of windows. He suggests that `git show` likely inherited its default behavior from `git log --no-walk` without much deliberate consideration, and that the distinction between `git diff` (which allows external diffs by default) and `git show` (which does not) may be an oversight rather than a deliberate design choice.

Peff also notes that `textconv` (a mechanism for converting binary files to text for diffing) is often a better fit for most use cases, as it is more constrained and integrates more smoothly with Git’s existing commands. He acknowledges his bias as the author of `textconv` but frames this as a practical observation rather than a definitive recommendation. The reply does not propose any code changes or patches—it is purely an explanation of Git’s current behavior and the trade-offs involved.
