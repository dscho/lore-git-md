This is a **substantive follow-up** in the ongoing design discussion about Elijah Newren’s patch 2/2, which fixes a race condition in Git’s packed-object backend where a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a removed packfile. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s refinement of the tri-state design, clarifying the performance implications of scanning MIDX-covered packs when the preferred pack is missing.

Peff agrees with Patrick’s diagnosis of the root cause—when an object exists in multiple packs covered by a MIDX and the *preferred* pack is removed, the current code fails to find the object in the remaining packs—but raises a **performance concern** about the proposed fallback logic. Specifically, he argues that scanning *all* MIDX-covered packs (as the current patch does) is inefficient when the object is likely to be in a non-MIDX pack after the preferred pack is removed. Instead, he suggests that the fallback should prioritize searching non-MIDX packs first, reserving a full MIDX-pack scan for cases where the object is known to be in the MIDX (e.g., via the `bsearch()` check). This aligns with his earlier tri-state proposal but refines the order of operations to minimize unnecessary work.

The discussion now centers on **implementation trade-offs**: Patrick’s approach (scanning only MIDX-covered packs when the preferred pack is missing) ensures correctness but may incur a performance penalty in large repositories, while Peff’s suggestion (prioritizing non-MIDX packs) optimizes for the common case but risks missing objects if the MIDX is stale. Neither questions the necessity of the fix, but the outcome may influence how the recovery logic is structured in `find_pack_entry()`. The key technical detail is the interaction between the MIDX lookup and the regular pack fallback, particularly in `odb/source-packed.c`. No new files or symbols are introduced, but the debate may lead to revisions in patch 2/2 before merging. The series remains a high-priority candidate, with the focus now on balancing correctness and performance.
