His example of Johannes Schindelin’s (`Dscho`) prior work (a commit that reused a pattern from an earlier Git patch) illustrates how the project trusts contributors to perform "sufficient due diligence" when reusing or adapting code, regardless of whether AI was involved.

Junio’s response suggests a potential middle ground for the policy impasse: the project might tolerate AI-assisted contributions from **trusted contributors who disclose their use of tools and demonstrate manual verification**. This shifts the focus from the tool itself to the contributor’s diligence, aligning with his earlier analogies to prior art exposure (e.g., GPL/BSD contamination). However, he does not propose a formal policy change or explicitly approve the `utimensat()` series; the discussion remains unresolved, with the ball now in the PLC’s or broader community’s court to clarify or amend `SubmittingPatches`. The technical merits of the series are still unaddressed, and no merge intent has been signaled. The key takeaway is that Junio sees the policy’s strictness as potentially unjustified, but the path forward requires formal guidance.
