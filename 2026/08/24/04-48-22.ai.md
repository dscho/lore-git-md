This is a **substantive review** by Jeff King (Peff) in the ongoing thread about Elijah Newren’s two-patch series fixing a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. The series targets a crash in `git replay` and missing-object errors in other server-side operations caused by a stale multi-pack-index (MIDX) referencing a removed packfile.

Peff’s email challenges the necessity of the current patch (2/2) and the design of a planned follow-up fix. He argues that the `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` flag is explicitly intended to allow false negatives for performance reasons, and that callers using it (like `upload-pack` and `mktree --batch`) are already aware of this trade-off. His core point: if those callers cannot tolerate false negatives, they should not use `QUICK` at all, and the fix should be applied at the caller level rather than in the packed-object backend. He also notes that the existing `is_pack_valid()` check in `packfile_fill_entry()` already handles the case where the `.pack` file disappears but the `.idx` remains mmapped, making the planned follow-up patch’s hole less critical than Elijah feared.

The email does not dispute the existence of the race condition but questions whether the proposed solution is the right layer for the fix. It introduces a potential design tension: should the packed-object backend be made more resilient to transient failures (Elijah’s approach), or should callers be responsible for choosing the right flags and handling failures themselves (Peff’s preference)? This is a **substantive** review that could shift the direction of the series if Elijah revises his approach. No new code or test changes are proposed yet, but the discussion is now focused on architectural trade-offs rather than implementation details.
