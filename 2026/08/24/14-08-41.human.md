This is a surface-level review of the seventh patch in Michael Montalbo’s RFC series introducing `git organize`, which moves Git’s ODB (object database) sources into an `odb/` subdirectory. Oswald Buddenhagen’s reply focuses on two minor but notable points.

First, he suggests the ODB files should go into `lib/odb/` rather than just `odb/`, aligning with a potential future structure where subsystems are nested under `lib/`. This is a forward-looking comment about directory naming, not a critique of the patch’s mechanics.

Second, he points out that the `#include` style in the patch (`#include "odb/object-file.h"`) is inconsistent with Git’s existing conventions, which typically use angle brackets (`#include <odb/object-file.h>`) for non-local headers. While this inconsistency predates the patch, Oswald notes that the large-scale changes here present an opportunity to standardize the style.

The review is brief and does not engage with the patch’s core functionality or the broader `git organize` tool. It is purely about naming and style, so it does not signal deeper technical concerns. Reviewers following the thread may want to weigh in on whether the `lib/` prefix is worth adopting now, but the patch itself remains a clean, mechanical reorganization.
