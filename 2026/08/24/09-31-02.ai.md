This is the final patch in Karthik Nayak’s four-patch v2 series optimizing the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. It implements the core performance fix: `stack_uptodate()` now skips reloading the stack if it is already locked, reducing `newfstatat()` syscalls from O(n) to a constant ~55 regardless of ref count. The patch embeds the lock-checking logic inside `stack_uptodate()` (renamed to accept a `skip_if_locked` flag) to enforce consistent application and prevent caller error, addressing the open design question from the v1 review cycle.

The change is purely internal to `reftable/stack.c` and does not alter the on-disk format or public API. Benchmark results show a consistent 15-20% speedup across ref counts from 2,000 to 50,000, with the improvement definitively attributed to the elimination of redundant reloads during the locked phase of a transaction. The discrepancy from the original 1-2% improvement reported in v1 is explained by test methodology: using `refs/tags/` (which triggers an extra stack reload due to reflog checks) instead of `refs/heads/` (which shortcuts reflog creation).

The patch is well-motivated by Jeff King’s profiling data and demonstrates clear performance gains with no behavioral changes. The implementation is mechanical and targeted, focusing on eliminating redundant operations rather than introducing new logic. The series as a whole is now fully resolved on the technical side, with all substantive review feedback incorporated and validated by updated unit tests. The only remaining open question is whether Junio C Hamano will accept the resolution of his safety concern (premature lock release when multiple `reftable_addition` instances operate on the same stack), which is addressed in patch 3 and validated by a new unit test.
