This is a **substantive follow-up** in the design discussion for Elijah Newren’s patch 2/2, which fixes a race condition in Git’s packed-object backend when a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a removed packfile. Jeff King (Peff) refines his earlier performance concern about the fallback logic’s scan order, clarifying a key detail: when an object exists in a *newer* pack (not covered by the MIDX), the `SECOND_READ` phase will prioritize that pack due to reverse-mtime sorting, avoiding the need to scan all MIDX-covered packs. This means the fallback’s cost is only incurred when the object is *not* in a newer pack—i.e., when it truly requires scanning the MIDX-covered packs to recover from the race condition.

The email corrects a minor inaccuracy in Peff’s previous message (the "final sentence" typo) but does not alter the core argument. The discussion remains focused on whether the recovery logic should be tied to `SECOND_READ` to optimize performance for `QUICK` callers (like `git replay`), which are already prepared to tolerate false negatives. Peff’s observation about reverse-mtime sorting strengthens his case that the fallback’s overhead is limited to edge cases, but the debate over *when* to trigger it (first pass vs. `SECOND_READ`) is still unresolved. The outcome may influence whether Elijah resubmits the patch with adjusted logic or leaves the change to be handled during integration. No new objections to the fix’s correctness have been raised.
