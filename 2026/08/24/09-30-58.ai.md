This is a performance-focused refactoring of the reftable backend, specifically targeting redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The v2 series expands the original three-patch set to four patches, incorporating feedback from the v1 review cycle and addressing edge cases around lock management.

The core optimization remains the same: eliminating unnecessary stack reloads when the stack is already locked during a transaction. The key change in v2 is the addition of a `locked` bit field to `struct reftable_addition` to track which addition instance holds the lock, preventing premature lock release when multiple additions operate on the same stack. This resolves Junio C Hamano's safety concern from v1 and is validated by a new unit test in `t/unit-tests/u-reftable-stack.c`.

The series now shows a consistent 15-20% performance improvement in benchmarks (up from the 1-2% reported in v1), with the discrepancy attributed to test methodology differences—using `refs/tags/` instead of `refs/heads/` to trigger reflog checks. Syscall counts for `newfstatat()` are reduced from linear growth to a constant ~55 calls regardless of ref count. The on-disk format remains unchanged, and libgit2 compatibility is preserved.

Patch 2 is new in v2 and addresses Patrick Steinhardt's cosmetic feedback by renaming `reftable_stack_new_addition()` to `reftable_stack_addition_new()` for naming consistency. The remaining patches correspond to v1 with updated commit messages and implementation details. The series is well-motivated by Jeff King's profiling data and demonstrates clear performance gains with thorough test coverage. The changes are mechanical and targeted, focusing on eliminating redundant operations rather than introducing new behavior.
