This is a **substantive follow-up** in the ongoing design discussion about Elijah Newren’s patch 2/2, which fixes a race condition in Git’s packed-object backend where a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a removed packfile. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King’s (Peff) critique, clarifying the root cause of the bug and proposing a refined approach that aligns with Peff’s tri-state design suggestion.

Patrick confirms that the issue is more fundamental than Peff initially described: when an object exists in multiple packs covered by a MIDX and the *preferred* pack (the one the MIDX would normally use) is removed, the current code fails to find the object in the remaining packs. This happens because the non-MIDX lookup skips *all* packs covered by the MIDX, and the MIDX lookup fails when the preferred pack is missing. The result is a false negative even for callers that do not use `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK`, breaking correctness guarantees. Patrick’s proposed fix—scanning only the packs covered by the MIDX when the preferred pack is missing—avoids the performance penalty of a full pack scan while addressing the correctness issue. This approach is equivalent to Peff’s tri-state suggestion, where `fill_midx_entry()` would distinguish between "object not in MIDX," "object in MIDX but unavailable," and "object available."

The email narrows the scope of the disagreement: Peff’s critique focused on whether `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` callers should handle false negatives themselves, but Patrick’s clarification shows the bug affects *all* object lookups, not just performance-sensitive ones. The proposed tri-state design remains the most promising path forward, though the implementation details (e.g., whether to scan all MIDX’d packs or only the relevant subset) are still open. The series is still a high-priority candidate for merging, but the outcome of this discussion may require revisions to patch 2/2. Key technical details include the `find_pack_entry()` function in `odb/source-packed.c`, the `fill_midx_entry()` helper, and the interaction between MIDX lookups and the regular pack fallback. No new files or symbols are introduced, but the recovery logic’s placement and scope are now the central point of debate.
