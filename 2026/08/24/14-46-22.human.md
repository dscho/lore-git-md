This command deletes packfiles only after ensuring all their objects are referenced by other packs, and it intentionally leaves stale packs in the MIDX for a grace period. This design, critical for Windows stability (where open file handles block deletions), allows existing processes to continue using the stale MIDX while preventing new processes from opening the deleted pack. Stolee suggests that geometric repacking could adopt a similar approach to reduce reliance on the fallback logic introduced in Elijah’s patch.

The email does not object to the fix itself but implies a preference for minimizing its runtime cost by reducing the frequency of the race condition. Stolee’s feedback introduces a new dimension to the ongoing design debate: whether to optimize the fallback logic (as Peff and Patrick Steinhardt are discussing) or to avoid triggering it altogether by adopting a grace period for redundant packfiles. This aligns with the broader discussion about balancing correctness, performance, and operational simplicity, but it shifts the focus from *how* to recover from the race to *how often* the race should occur.
