This is a follow-up in the ongoing design discussion about Elijah Newren’s patch 2/2, which fixes a race condition where Git’s multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a packfile that has been removed by geometric repacking. Jeff King (Peff) refines his earlier performance concern about the fallback logic’s scan order, now arguing that the recovery mechanism should only trigger during the `SECOND_READ` phase (a retry after refreshing the packfile list), not during the initial `QUICK` lookup.

Peff’s key insight is that the current implementation’s fallback—scanning all MIDX-covered packs when the preferred pack is missing—could be unnecessarily expensive for `QUICK` callers, who are already prepared to tolerate false negatives. He notes that in most cases, the object will either be found in a newer pack (which would be prioritized during `SECOND_READ`) or not at all, making the full MIDX scan redundant for `QUICK` lookups. The fallback should instead be reserved for `SECOND_READ`, where the cost is justified by the need for correctness. This suggestion aligns with the broader goal of optimizing the common case (objects in non-MIDX packs) while preserving the fix’s correctness guarantees.

The discussion remains focused on implementation details rather than the fix’s necessity, which is now widely accepted. Peff’s proposal is a targeted refinement, not a rejection, and the thread is still converging on a solution. Elijah Newren has not yet responded to this latest exchange, so the next steps will likely involve either adjusting the patch to gate the fallback on `SECOND_READ` or further debating the trade-offs. For now, this is a well-motivated technical tweak to an otherwise solid bugfix.
