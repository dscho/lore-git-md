This is a design clarification reply in the ongoing thread about Git’s `--ext-diff` option and its default behavior.

Junio C Hamano, the maintainer and original author of the `--ext-diff` feature, weighs in to endorse Jeff King’s explanation. He agrees that `textconv` is generally a better fit for most use cases due to its more constrained and integrated design, despite his own authorship of `--ext-diff`. Junio notes that his *only* remaining practical use for `--ext-diff` is in maintaining the "What's cooking" report drafts, and even that could likely be replaced with a combination of `textconv`, diff hunk header patterns, and the `-W` (function-context) option. This suggests that `--ext-diff`’s current role is largely niche and that its suppression in commands like `git show` may not be a significant limitation for most users.

The reply does not propose any code changes or challenge the existing behavior—it reinforces the idea that the current design is intentional, if not widely used. The thread remains at the inquiry stage, with no patches or RFCs on the horizon. The key takeaway is that the distinction between commands that allow external diffs by default (`git diff`) and those that do not (`git show`) is likely deliberate, even if the rationale is not explicitly documented. Future discussion might explore whether this behavior should be rationalized (e.g., via documentation or a config option), but Junio’s response implies that any such change would need a compelling use case to justify breaking existing workflows.
