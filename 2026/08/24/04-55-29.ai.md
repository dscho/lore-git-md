This is a **substantive review** from Jeff King (Peff) on the second patch of Elijah Newren’s two-patch bugfix series for a race condition in Git’s geometric repacking mechanism. The series targets a scenario where a multi-pack-index (MIDX) references a packfile that has been removed by a concurrent repack, causing processes using the stale MIDX to fail to locate objects.

Peff’s feedback challenges the architectural placement of the fix in the packed-object backend (`odb/source-packed.c`). He suggests an alternative approach: instead of adding recovery logic to `find_pack_entry()`, the function could return a tri-state value from `fill_midx_entry()` to distinguish between (1) the object not being in the MIDX, (2) the object being in the MIDX but unavailable (e.g., due to a missing packfile), and (3) the object being available. This would allow callers to handle case (2) explicitly, rather than baking the recovery logic into the backend. Peff notes that this distinction would avoid an unnecessary `bsearch()` call in the recovery path, as the MIDX lookup has already been performed by the time the fallback is considered.

The review also questions the broader necessity of the fix, pointing out that the same race condition (a `.idx` file referencing a missing `.pack` file) already exists for regular packfiles and is handled by `is_pack_valid()`. This suggests that the planned follow-up patches (addressing additional race conditions) may be less critical than initially thought, as callers using `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` are already expected to tolerate false negatives for performance reasons. Peff’s preference is for callers to avoid `OBJECT_INFO_QUICK` if they cannot tolerate missing objects, rather than making the backend more resilient.

The feedback does not reject the patch outright but raises a fundamental design question: should the backend be made more robust to transient failures, or should callers be responsible for handling such cases? This is the primary unresolved issue in the thread, and its resolution may affect the scope of Elijah’s planned follow-up work. The discussion is now focused on architectural trade-offs, with no clear consensus yet. The series remains a high-priority candidate for merging, but the outcome of this discussion could require revisions to patch 2/2.
