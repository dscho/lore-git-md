This is a policy discussion reply in the ongoing thread about AI-generated contributions and Git's `SubmittingPatches` policy. Oswald Buddenhagen challenges Junio C Hamano's framing of "contributor trust" as a sufficient safeguard for AI-generated code, arguing that **AI tools inherently undermine the ability to verify code provenance**—even for trusted contributors.

Oswald contends that while AI tools *might* detect outright plagiarism (and disclosure via trailers could provide some assurance), the reality is that **no contributor can realistically verify** whether AI-generated code contains verbatim or near-verbatim fragments from incompatible licenses (e.g., GPL-licensed code). He dismisses the idea that contributors would use multiple code search engines to audit AI outputs, calling it impractical. This directly counters Junio's earlier suggestion that trusted contributors like Johannes Schindelin could perform "sufficient due diligence" by ensuring code is copied from Git's own codebase or permissively licensed sources.

The email does not propose an alternative policy or resolution but **reinforces the legal and ethical risks** of AI-generated contributions, framing them as a systemic problem rather than one solvable by individual trust. It leaves the discussion open-ended, with no clear path forward for the `utimensat()` series or the broader policy question. The technical merits of the patch series remain unaddressed, as the focus stays on governance and risk.
