This is the final patch in Karthik Nayak’s four-part series optimizing the reftable backend by eliminating redundant stack reloads during reference transactions. The series targets a performance bottleneck where excessive `fstat()` calls (scaling linearly with ref count) were consuming significant time during bulk ref creation.

This patch embeds the lock-checking logic directly into `stack_uptodate()`, adding a `skip_if_locked` parameter to skip reloads when the stack is already locked. The change reduces `newfstatat()` syscalls from O(n) to a constant ~55 regardless of ref count, delivering a consistent 15-20% performance improvement in benchmarks. The optimization is well-validated: independent measurements by Jeff King confirm a 25% speedup, and the discrepancy from the original 1-2% improvement is now clearly attributed to test methodology (`refs/tags/*` vs `refs/heads/*`).

The implementation is clean and targeted, avoiding behavior changes while encapsulating the lock-checking logic to reduce caller error risk. Earlier patches in the series resolved safety concerns (premature lock release) and cosmetic feedback (function renaming), leaving this patch as a straightforward performance win. The series is under review but looks ready; the only remaining open question is whether to expose the lock-checking logic to callers (Justin Tobler’s alternative), but Karthik’s argument for encapsulation is persuasive. This is a well-crafted, low-risk optimization that addresses a real problem.
