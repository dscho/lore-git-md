This thread is about a bugfix series for `git rebase --update-refs` that addresses edge cases where symbolic references (symrefs) to branches or other refs cause rebase failures. The series, now in its third version (v3), is authored by Son Luong Ngoc and consists of two patches that handle symref decorations more robustly, particularly when HEAD or other refs point outside the `refs/heads/` namespace.

The key issue involves scenarios like branch rename migrations or cross-worktree workflows where symrefs (e.g., `main` pointing to `master`, or HEAD pointing to a tag) break rebase operations. A design debate between Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood over whether `git rebase` should handle such edge cases has been resolved in favor of robustness, after Phillip demonstrated that tools like TopGit actively rely on HEAD symrefs pointing to `refs/top-bases/{...}`. The current approach, using `resolved_ref` for HEAD comparison, will be retained in the upcoming v4.

The series touches `sequencer.c` and `branch.c`, with test coverage in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh` verifying symref chains, cross-worktree conflicts, and backend compatibility (files and reftable). A latent bug in `master`—where the `head_ref` buffer is clobbered during resolution, causing incorrect todo list entries in multi-worktree setups—was identified by Erik Cervin-Edin and will be addressed in v4. Junio’s latest email confirms the design resolution and shifts focus to fixing this remaining issue, with no further design changes expected. The series remains under review, with v4 anticipated soon.
