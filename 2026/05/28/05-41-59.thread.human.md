This thread is fixing edge cases in `git rebase --update-refs` when symbolic references (symrefs) are involved, particularly where HEAD or branches point to other branches or non-branch refs like tags. The series, now preparing its fourth version (v4), is authored by Son Luong Ngoc and addresses two main issues: rebase failures during symref updates and a latent bug in `master` where the `head_ref` buffer gets clobbered in multi-worktree setups, causing incorrect entries in the rebase todo list.

The third version (v3) of the two-patch series is functionally complete but has two unresolved technical questions from Phillip Wood’s review of the second patch. These focus on whether symref resolution in `prepare_checked_out_branches()` is redundant after the first patch and whether the deduplication logic for non-branch symrefs is overengineered. The latent `head_ref` bug, confirmed by Junio and Erik Cervin-Edin, remains a blocker for v4. Junio initially resisted handling edge cases like HEAD pointing outside `refs/heads/` but conceded after Phillip provided real-world examples (e.g., TopGit’s use of symrefs) showing the need for robustness.

The series touches `sequencer.c` and `branch.c`, with test coverage in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh` verifying symref chains, cross-worktree conflicts, and backend compatibility. Phillip’s latest email confirms the v2 regression is fixed in v3 and reiterates a request for a comment explaining why `resolved_ref` is used for HEAD comparison—this ensures symrefs pointing outside `refs/heads/` are handled correctly. The next steps for v4 are clear: address the `head_ref` bug, add the requested comment, and incorporate feedback on the second patch. The design debate is settled, and the focus is now on polish.
