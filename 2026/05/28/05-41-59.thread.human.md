Here’s the human-readable summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series (now at v3) by Son Luong Ngoc to fix edge cases in `git rebase --update-refs` when dealing with symbolic references (symrefs). The series addresses failures that occur when branches or other refs are symrefs—for example, when `main` points to `master` or when HEAD points outside `refs/heads/`. These issues affect branch rename migrations and cross-worktree workflows. The v3 series is functionally complete and under final review, with Junio C Hamano confirming that the only remaining blocker is a latent bug in `master` (a clobbered `head_ref` buffer during HEAD resolution), which will be fixed in v4.

The key technical work spans `sequencer.c` and `branch.c`, focusing on memory-safe symref resolution, deduplication of updates, and cross-worktree conflict prevention. Tests in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh` now cover HEAD aliases, secondary branch aliases, non-branch symrefs, and backend compatibility (files and reftable). The design debate—whether `git rebase` should handle symrefs pointing outside `refs/heads/`—was resolved in favor of robustness after Phillip Wood provided concrete evidence (TopGit’s use of such symrefs) that the edge case is actively relied upon.

Phillip Wood’s latest review raises two substantive questions about the second patch. First, he questions whether the `prepare_checked_out_branches()` function redundantly resolves symrefs, given that the first patch already skips them. He also flags a potential race condition where two worktrees could incorrectly reserve the same branch if one uses a symref. Second, he suggests the deduplication logic might be overcomplicating the problem, hinting that the first patch (fixing branch-to-branch symrefs) could suffice for now. These questions are the last open discussion points before the series can proceed. Son Luong Ngoc is expected to address them in v4, along with the latent `head_ref` bug. Other participants, including Junio and Erik Cervin-Edin, have already weighed in on the design and correctness of earlier versions.
