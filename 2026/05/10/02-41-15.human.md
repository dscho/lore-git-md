Junio Hamano weighs in on the type safety discussion in the Windows large-object handling series. This is a follow-up to Torsten Bögershausen's concern about memory alignment when reading varint-encoded data, suggesting byte-by-byte reading to avoid platform-specific issues.

Junio clarifies that the current code doesn't actually dereference misaligned pointers as Torsten feared, and traces the history back to a 2009 commit by Linus Torvalds that fixed sign-extension issues with unsigned char shifts. He acknowledges Torsten's point about platform safety but argues this is orthogonal to the immediate goal of fixing 32-bit truncation on Windows.

The maintainer's position is clear: the series should focus on its primary purpose (fixing Windows large-object handling) without being sidetracked by broader type safety cleanups, which can be addressed separately later. This keeps the scope tight for merging while leaving room for future improvements. The technical discussion shows the careful attention being paid to cross-platform considerations in this series.
