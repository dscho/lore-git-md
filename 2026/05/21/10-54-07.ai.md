This is patch 3/5 in Tuomas Ahola's series fixing edge cases in Git's date approximation logic ("approxidate"). The patch specifically addresses how special time formats like "noon" and "tea" interact with explicitly specified dates.

The core issue is that these special time formats were incorrectly rolling back the date by 1 day when combined with explicit date specifications (like "today at noon" or "13 May at tea"). The fix modifies the `date_time()` function to only apply the day rollback when no day has been explicitly set (indicated by tm_mday being negative).

The patch includes test cases demonstrating the corrected behavior:
- "today at noon" now correctly uses today's date
- "last Friday at noon" properly respects the specified day
- Explicit dates with "noon pm" maintain their specified date regardless of current time

This builds on the series' earlier work introducing negative tm_mday markers for deferred date adjustment. The change is small (3 lines of core logic) but fixes a long-standing quirk that dates back to at least 2006, as evidenced by Linus Torvalds' old report about "one year ago yesterday at tea-time" producing incorrect results.
