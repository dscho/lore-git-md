This is patch 3/5 in Tuomas Ahola's series fixing edge cases in Git's date approximation logic ("approxidate"). The patch specifically addresses how special time formats like "noon" and "tea" interact with explicitly specified dates.

The core issue is that these special time formats were incorrectly rolling back to the previous day even when a specific date was provided (e.g., "May 13 at noon" would incorrectly evaluate to May 12). The fix is straightforward - only apply the day rollback logic when no specific day has been set (indicated by tm_mday being negative). The patch includes new test cases that verify the corrected behavior for combinations like "today at noon" and "last Friday at noon".

This version appears to be the final iteration of this particular fix, having been accepted in previous rounds of review. The change is small (just 3 lines of core logic) but fixes a long-standing quirk that dates back to at least 2006, as evidenced by the referenced Linus Torvalds email. The test coverage is thorough, including cases with time offsets to ensure robustness.

As part of the larger series, this patch helps establish more intuitive behavior for Git's date parsing, particularly when combining relative dates ("today", "yesterday") with time specifications. The series as a whole seems ready for merging.
