Chris Torek weighs in on the ongoing discussion about documenting `git diff --word-diff`'s whitespace behavior, suggesting a middle path between full implementation documentation and no documentation at all. He proposes framing the explanation as an "Implementation Note" that acknowledges the current line-by-line approach while leaving room for future algorithmic improvements.

The note would explain that the current implementation may show larger diffs than strictly necessary due to its line-based approach, similar to how `--diff-algorithm` can affect output. Importantly, Torek emphasizes that any diff algorithm inherently shows just one possible interpretation of changes, not necessarily the exact editing sequence. This pragmatic framing avoids locking in implementation details while still giving users context for the behavior they observe.

This moves the discussion forward from the previous impasse, offering a concrete suggestion that addresses both the need for user guidance and the project's desire to maintain implementation flexibility. The proposal seems likely to satisfy both sides of the debate - those who wanted some documentation (like the original bug reporter) and those who worried about over-specifying (like Johannes Sixt).
