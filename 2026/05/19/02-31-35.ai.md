This is a documentation discussion thread about `git diff --word-diff` behavior. Chris Torek weighs in with a proposed "Implementation Note" that would explain the current word-diff algorithm's limitations while preserving flexibility for future improvements. His suggestion acknowledges that the line-by-line preprocessing can produce suboptimal word diffs for whitespace-only changes, but frames this as an implementation detail that may evolve rather than a behavior to be formally documented.

The note strikes a balance between transparency and maintainer flexibility - it informs users why they might see unexpected whitespace diffs without committing to the current implementation long-term. This moves the discussion forward from the previous impasse where participants were torn between overspecifying the algorithm and providing no guidance at all.

Key technical points in Chris's proposal:
- Explicitly states the current line-then-word processing approach
- Leaves room for algorithmic improvements by calling this the "currently" used method
- Draws a parallel to `--diff-algorithm` which also reserves the right to change outputs
- Includes a philosophical note about diffs inherently being approximations

The thread appears to be converging on this middle path between full documentation and no documentation, though Junio Hamano or other maintainers may still weigh in on whether even this level of implementation disclosure is desirable.
