Taylor Blau has completed his performance optimization work for Git's pack-bitmap-write subsystem, with the final patch now posted. The eight-part series targets bitmap generation speed in large repositories, building on his earlier pseudo-merge bitmap work. 

The optimizations fall into three main categories: core bitmap generation improvements (reducing time from 612 to 294 seconds in test repositories), better bitmap compression (shrinking output from 635MB to 176MB), and now - in the final patch - a restructured approach to pseudo-merge handling that processes them separately from regular commits. This last change cuts pseudo-merge generation time nearly in half (575s to 328s) while preserving all the traversal speed benefits. 

The series demonstrates Taylor's deep expertise in Git's pack and bitmap subsystems, with each patch building carefully on the last. The changes are all internal optimizations that don't affect user-facing behavior, focusing instead on algorithmic improvements and reduced redundant work. While the series hasn't yet received review feedback, the detailed performance measurements and focused scope suggest these are likely to be uncontroversial improvements for the next release cycle.
