Performance optimization for `git fetch` connectivity checks. Kristofer Karlsson from Spotify initially proposed passing transport parameters through `store_updated_refs()` to enable `check_connected()` optimizations, claiming significant speedups (22s->5s) during clone operations. Junio C Hamano approved after reviewing call sites. Jeff King (Peff) identified through substantive technical review that the optimization's `self_contained_and_connected` flag cannot activate during fetches, as it relies on pack properties only guaranteed during clones. 

Kristofer confirmed Peff's analysis and withdrew the patch, unable to reproduce initial benchmarks. Peff now explores whether similar optimizations could work for fetches in principle, analyzing technical constraints around verifying connectivity when fetched objects reference existing history. He notes potential theoretical speedups by checking only "edges" between new and existing objects, but highlights implementation complexity and differences from the original approach. Both acknowledge benchmarking challenges due to Git's internal transfer optimizations. The thread demonstrates Git's rigorous review culture where even withdrawn proposals spark deeper analysis, leaving open future directions for fetch performance work while clearly documenting why the initial approach was invalid. Key technical details remain: clone-specific pack properties, connectivity verification guarantees, and the non-trivial differences between clone and fetch object transfer semantics.
