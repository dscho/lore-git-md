This thread explored a proposed optimization for `git fetch` connectivity checks that initially promised significant speedups (22s to 5s in early benchmarks). Kristofer Karlsson from Spotify suggested passing transport parameters through `store_updated_refs()` to enable `check_connected()` optimizations, with Junio C Hamano initially approving after reviewing call sites. However, Jeff King (Peff) identified through substantive technical review that the optimization only worked for clone operations, not fetches, due to differences in how object connectivity is verified.

Kristofer later confirmed Peff's analysis, acknowledging the `self_contained_and_connected` flag couldn't activate during fetches and that his benchmark results were unverifiable, leading him to withdraw the patch. In a follow-up, Peff explored whether similar optimizations might theoretically work for fetches by checking only the "edges" between new and existing objects, though he noted this would require a different implementation approach. He also validated the challenges of accurately benchmarking fetch operations due to Git's internal transfer optimizations.

The discussion highlights Git's rigorous review culture, where even withdrawn proposals spark valuable technical analysis. While no immediate solution emerged, the exchange clarified constraints around packfile connectivity optimizations and identified potential future directions for fetch performance work. The key takeaway is that clone and fetch operations have fundamentally different connectivity verification requirements, making optimizations non-transferable between them without careful redesign.
