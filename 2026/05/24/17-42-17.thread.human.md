Kristofer Karlsson has completed a performance optimization series for Git's commit-reach.c algorithms, specifically targeting merge-base and ahead-behind calculations. The three-patch series introduces an O(1) solution using a `nonstale_queue` wrapper structure that eliminates queue scanning while maintaining significant speedups - showing 2-3x improvements in merge-base calculations and reducing paint_down_to_common() runtime from 50% to just 4% in benchmarks on a 3.7M commit monorepo.

The series has undergone thorough review, with key technical concerns addressed across all three patches. Junio Hamano positively reviewed the second patch's deduplication approach using an ENQUEUED flag, which forms the foundation for the final O(1) optimization. Jeff King proposed the core O(1) approach and verified the performance characteristics, while Derrick Stolee's earlier encapsulation concerns have been fully addressed.

In response to Junio's review, Kristofer provided additional technical context about four alternative implementations they considered before settling on the ENQUEUED flag approach, demonstrating the solution's superiority both in performance and generality (working without commit-graph data). This thorough exploration of the design space confirms the series is well-considered and ready for integration, with no outstanding technical objections remaining. The optimization maintains Git's behavior while delivering measurable performance gains, particularly valuable for large repositories.
