This is a performance optimization series targeting the commit-reach.c code, specifically the algorithms used by `git merge-base` and ahead-behind calculations. The patch series comes from Kristofer Karlsson and shows significant speedups (up to 2.5x) for certain workloads.

The core change replaces an O(n) linear scan of the commit queue (checking for non-stale commits) with an O(1) counter-based approach. The optimization is most impactful in repositories with wide commit graphs (like the tested 2.4M commit monorepo), where the queue grows large and the linear scan becomes expensive. For simpler cases with narrow commit graphs, the change shows no regression.

The series is structured as three patches:
1. First deduplicates queue entries in paint_down_to_common()
2. Then replaces the queue scan with a counter in paint_down_to_common()
3. Finally applies the same optimization to ahead_behind()

The cover letter includes detailed benchmark results showing the improvements across different repository sizes and graph shapes. The changes touch commit-reach.c and object.h, modifying how commit flags are tracked during graph walks. The approach appears well-motivated and the benchmarks demonstrate clear wins for the targeted cases without regressing simpler scenarios.
