This is the third patch in Tuomas Ahola's four-part series fixing edge cases in Git's date approximation logic (approxidate). The patch addresses a long-standing quirk where special time formats like "noon" and "tea" would incorrectly roll back the day even when an explicit date was specified.

The core fix is a simple 3-line change in date.c that makes the time-of-day logic respect explicitly specified days (tm_mday). The patch includes test cases demonstrating the corrected behavior for scenarios like "today at noon" and "last Friday at noon" that previously gave incorrect results. The commit message references a 2006 bug report from Linus Torvalds about similar date parsing issues.

This patch was already accepted in previous iterations of the series, with the v4 version incorporating Junio C Hamano's feedback about making "today" mean midnight rather than "now". The change is well-contained and clearly motivated, with comprehensive test coverage showing both the old incorrect behavior and new correct behavior.

The patch is part of a larger effort to clean up Git's date parsing quirks, with this particular issue having been known for nearly 20 years. The series as a whole appears ready for merging, with this patch being one of the more straightforward technical fixes in the set.
