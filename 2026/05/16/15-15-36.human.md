This is the fourth iteration of a bugfix series addressing edge cases in Git's date approximation logic ("approxidate"). The series, submitted by Tuomas Ahola, focuses on making special date/time formats like "noon" and "tea" work predictably when combined with explicit day specifications (e.g., "noon yesterday").

The key changes in v4:
1. A new first patch that makes "today" wrap to midnight by default) rather than meaning "now" (which was an accidental behavior)
2. The previously contentious "today" alias patch from v3 has been dropped
3. The deferred mday adjustment scheme (patch 4/4) now applies to "today" as well as "yesterday"

The series addresses a 2006 bug report from Linus Torvalds and makes several improvements:
- Special time strings now respect fixed day-of-month specifications
- The test infrastructure has been enhanced to support time offsets
- More intuitive behavior for combinations like "tea yesterday"

The v4 restructuring shows responsiveness to Junio's feedback - removing the debatable "today" alias while keeping the core fixes. The deferred adjustment approach in patch 4/4 appears technically sound but still awaits Junio's review. This series is now focused squarely on fixing actual bugs rather than adding new aliases, which should improve its chances of acceptance.
