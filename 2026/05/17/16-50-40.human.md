Performance optimization follow-up with benchmark data for the Git repository's own version ranges. René Scharfe provides concrete numbers showing the priority queue optimization for `limit_list()` yields consistent 1.16-1.28x speedups for `git rev-list --bisect` across Git's historical release ranges (v2.0.1 to v2.10.1 through v2.50.1). While not demonstrating the extreme 4x improvements seen in synthetic merge-heavy cases, these real-world measurements confirm the optimization provides meaningful gains even in Git's relatively linear history.

The benchmarks show the speedup scaling slightly with history depth - from 16% faster for the v2.10.1 range to 28% faster for v2.50.1 - suggesting the priority queue's O(log n) advantage becomes more pronounced as the commit count grows. This data strengthens the case for merging the optimization by showing it helps even in repositories without extreme merge density, though the largest benefits remain in more complex histories. The clean implementation and broad consensus from prior reviews (Stolee, Peff, Junio) suggest this is ready to proceed.
