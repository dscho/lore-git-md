# Thread Summary (Human Mode)

Derrick Stolee and Taylor Blau have sent v4 of their 13-patch series (up from 12 in v3) making `--path-walk` fully interoperable with Git's filter options. The series moves filter logic from `builtin/pack-objects.c` into the `path-walk.c` API, so that `blob:none`, `blob:limit`, `sparse:oid`, `tree:0`, `object:type`, and combined filters all work when path-walk is active. Performance remains impressive -- sparse-checkout packfile generation for the nodejs/node repo drops from over 8 minutes to under 30 seconds.

The headline change in v4 is the reworked `tree:0` filter handling (patch 11/13), which addresses the correctness bug that Stolee himself identified during v3 review. The original approach placed tagged trees in a `/tagged-trees` pseudo-path, but could silently drop directly-requested tagged trees that happened not to also be root trees of commits -- a case the v3 tests missed entirely. A new preparatory patch (patch 10/13) adds test coverage specifically for this scenario by tagging trees that have no other reference path, so any future regression will be caught. The v4 `tree:0` patch also clarifies how directly-requested trees in `/tagged-trees` are expanded.

Other v4 changes are cleanup: typos fixed, the performance test script made more robust against submodule edge cases, and minor wording improvements to commit messages.

The remaining open questions from earlier rounds -- the `strict_types` behavior for `object:type`, where to place the `GIT_TEST_PACK_PATH_WALK=1` CI flag, whether `sparse:oid`/`treesparse:oid` will work with servers or only local bundles, and the bitmap documentation follow-ups -- are not addressed in this cover letter. Stolee doesn't indicate whether those await separate patches or further discussion. The series looks well-structured and the critical `tree:0` correctness issue appears resolved, but those lingering questions mean the series may not be quite as close to the finish line as the cover letter's tone suggests.
