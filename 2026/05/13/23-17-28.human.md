This is a follow-up email from **Elijah Newren** responding to **Derrick Stolee's** earlier review of his batch-prefetch series for partial clones. The series (covering `git cherry` and `git grep`) was already merged to `next`, and Stolee had suggested some architectural improvements. Newren is now replying after a long delay that he blames on work emergencies.

Newren gently pushes back on Stolee's suggestion that the prefetch logic should be centralized in the diff or archive libraries rather than living in the individual builtins. He makes a concrete case: the key value in cherry's case is accumulating blob OIDs _across many commits_ before fetching, so a per-commit prefetch (which is what the diff library's existing `diff_queued_diff_prefetch()` does) would still produce hundreds of tiny fetches. The win comes from one big fetch -- that logic lives above the diff library, not inside it. He also points out that cherry has two filters (skip commits whose file list doesn't match, skip binary files) that are specific to patch-ID computation, not to diffing in general, and would not be natural to push into a shared helper.

For grep, he acknowledges archive as a close analog but notes that there is no prefetch logic in archive yet either, so there is no existing place to share code. He proposes waiting until a third consumer appears before factoring things out.

The response is well-argued and grounded in concrete details -- Newren has clearly thought through the architecture and can point to specific reasons why each existing callsite is too specialized to share code with cherry or grep. It is a substantive reply, not a dismissal; he engages with Stolee's points in good faith and offers clear reasoning for his current design choice. Whether readers find his position convincing or not, the discussion is productive and focused on real trade-offs.

The thread status remains "expecting a re-roll" for the pathspec test and the independently discovered redundant-fetch fix, both of which Newren has already committed to producing.
