This is the final patch (13/13) of the `--path-walk --filter` integration series from Derrick Stolee and Taylor Blau. It adds support for the `combine` filter type, which takes the intersection of multiple child filters. With this, every `--filter` option that the series has individually wired up can now be composed together.

The implementation is careful. The `combine` filter works because each supported child filter is a monotonic restriction -- you can apply them one after another without special coordination. The main mechanical change is splitting `prepare_filters()` into a recursive helper and a thin wrapper: the helper recurses over sub-filters without releasing them (the parent call handles release), and each sub-filter's contribution is applied as a further restriction. Most of the compositions are straightforward, but `blob:limit=N` requires explicit min-tracking so that `combine:blob:limit=10+blob:limit=5` correctly yields a limit of 5 regardless of ordering. The `sparse:oid` case is intentionally single-use -- a `combine` with two `sparse:oid` children gets rejected, matching standalone behavior.

The test coverage in `t6601-path-walk.sh` hits the edge cases worth testing: `blob:none+tree:0` (degenerates to `tree:0`), `object:type=blob+blob:limit=3` (exercises the min-limit logic), `object:type=blob+object:type=tree` (produces empty intersection), and `tree:1+blob:none` (confirms the unsupported-subfilter error bubbles up). Documentation in `git-pack-objects.adoc` is updated to list `combine:<spec>+<spec>`.

The patch is well-structured and the commit message clearly explains why the recursive split is needed and how each filter type composes. It completes the series' stated goal of making `--path-walk` work with all supported filter forms. No new open questions are introduced, and the `combine` support is the natural capstone after the per-filter patches that precede it. This looks ready to go -- the main remaining question for the series as a whole remains the lack of independent review on the more subtle intermediate patches (particularly the `tree:0` rewrite and `object:type` pending-tree logic), but that is a general concern about the series, not about this specific patch.
