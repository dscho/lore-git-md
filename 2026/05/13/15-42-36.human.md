This is a documentation patch. Michael Montalbo adds a short clarification to the `--word-diff` option's description in `Documentation/diff-options.adoc`.

The key point the patch addresses is that `--word-diff` does not compute a fresh word-by-word diff of the entire file. Instead, it operates in two stages: first, Git produces a line-level diff with its own hunk alignment, and then word-level highlighting is layered on top of those hunks. The line-level alignment therefore influences which lines get compared at the word level, which can produce surprising output when words appear unchanged but the surrounding line structure shifts.

The commit message cites what appears to be prior discussion involving Vincent Lefevre and Johannes Sixt, suggesting this confusion has come up before in practice. The patch itself is tiny -- five lines of documentation -- and the wording is clear and accurate. This looks like a genuinely useful clarification for a gotcha that users of `--word-diff` occasionally hit, and it seems unlikely to be controversial. Worth a quick read from anyone who maintains or documents diff-related features.
