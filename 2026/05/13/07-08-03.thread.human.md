This thread began with Jeff King (Peff) posting a small but important fix to Git’s handling of ANSI color codes in progress messages. The patch, originally written by Mantas Mikulėnas, tweaks the `sideband.c` parser to accept colons as well as semicolons in SGR sequences, enabling support for 256-color and true-color escape codes like `\e[38:5:NNm`. Johannes Schindelin quickly reviewed and acked the change, calling it “well-motivated and well-executed,” and Junio C Hamano, while mildly questioning Git’s long-standing whitelist-based design philosophy, raised no objections.

The patch itself is effectively approved and ready for Junio to queue. The only open procedural question is whether he’ll ask for test coverage—unlikely, given the additive nature of the change. Meanwhile, Mantas has responded to Junio’s design skepticism with a spirited defense of strict whitelisting, arguing that the current SGR filter is now exhaustive and that other escape sequences (cursor movement, hyperlinks, progress bars) have no legitimate place in Git’s sideband output. This exchange is purely design-level; it doesn’t block the patch, but it may inform future decisions about terminal output security. For now, the fix is on track to land as-is.
