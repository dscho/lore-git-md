This is v9 of Harald Nordgren's five-patch series adding `git branch --prune-merged` for safe, automated local branch cleanup, and it addresses the final outstanding objection from Junio C Hamano. The thread context tells us that v8's upstream-reachability safety model was strongly approved by Junio, but he objected to the `--force` override that allowed bypassing the mergedness check. Harald agreed to remove that behavior, and v9 does exactly that.

The core change is in patch 3: `--force` no longer has any special meaning with `--prune-merged`. The reachability check is always enforced. The synopsis drops `[-f]`, and the error message that previously said "re-run with --force to delete it anyway" now says "delete it with 'git branch -D' if you are sure." This matches how `git branch`'s other read/safe actions treat `--force` -- as a style preference for the `-d` vs `-D` deletion mode, not as a bypass of safety checks. The tests for `--prune-merged --force` have been removed, and the remaining tests that used `--force` in scenarios where it wasn't about bypassing the check (the protect-the-default-branch tests) have been updated to use `--prune-merged` without `--force`. Patch 4 (the `branch.<name>.pruneMerged` opt-out) also drops its `--force` test, and patch 5 (`--all-remotes`) updates the synopsis and parameter passing accordingly. Patches 1 and 2 are unchanged.

The series is now mechanically consistent with Junio's feedback. Harald also floated a separate `fetch.pruneLocalBranches` concept in his v8 response email, but that is not part of this cover letter and no reviewer has reacted to it yet -- it remains a separate proposal for thread-level awareness. The `--prune-merged` series itself looks ready; v9 appears to be a clean, targeted fix of the one remaining issue, and the range-diff shows exactly what changed.
