This is a maintainer response from Junio C Hamano to Harald Nordgren's v8 cover letter, and it contains the first significant pushback on the redesigned series.

Junio begins with clear approval of the core redesign -- the switch to upstream-reachability semantics matches his understanding of the original motivation "a lot better than the previous round." That is the good news for the series.

Then he raises a specific objection to patch 3's `--force` behavior. The current design lets `--force` skip the reachability check entirely, effectively turning `--prune-merged --force` into `--forked --delete` -- a nuclear delete-every-candidate-branch option. Junio thinks this is a bad idea. His reasoning is principled: the command is called `prune-merged`, and mergedness is what should determine deletion regardless of `--force`. He suggests that if someone wants the nuclear option, they should run the two commands separately (`git branch -D $(git branch --forked <remote>)`) rather than having `--prune-merged --force` silently bypass its own safety check. He also explicitly opposes adding a `--prune-forked` alias that would make the nuclear option even easier to trigger by confusion.

This is a clean, surgical objection from the maintainer on a single design point. The rest of the series appears to be on solid ground. Harald will need to either remove the `--force` short-circuit from patch 3 or find a different way to handle it -- possibly just letting `--force` override the worktree check and per-branch config opt-out as usual, but keeping the reachability check intact. The core feature, the documentation, the opt-out config, and the `--all-remotes` flag all look like they survived Junio's first look.
