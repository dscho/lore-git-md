This is a maintainer follow-up in the ongoing discussion about CI testing strategy for Windows platform support of large objects (>4GB). Junio Hamano responds to Derrick Stolee's question about monitoring CI failures on integration branches.

Junio clarifies his CI monitoring approach, emphasizing that breakages should ideally be caught before patches reach integration branches. He notes he actively monitors 'seen' branch failures to notify authors early, but stresses this doesn't scale well as a maintainer-driven process. The key points:

1. Breakage detection should happen earlier in contributors' forks, where problems are easier to isolate and fix
2. Integration branch CI primarily exists to catch inter-topic interactions, not basic faults
3. Maintainer bandwidth is limited - the current 'seen' branch bisecting approach doesn't scale
4. Better tooling for contributors to test their own forks would be more effective

The response reflects Git's culture of individual contributor responsibility for pre-submission testing. Junio also notes he'll be intermittently available, managing expectations about response times during his limited availability.

This continues the thread's exploration of balancing thorough CI coverage with practical workflow constraints, now focusing on where in the pipeline quality gates should be placed. The discussion remains relevant to the series' test strategy while also touching on broader project development workflow concerns.
