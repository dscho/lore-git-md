This is a substantive review of Johannes Schindelin's RFC patch series adding merge commit support to `git history`. Phillip Wood identifies a potential edge case in the merge replay logic where new conflicts could be silently introduced without proper detection.

The review focuses on a scenario where:
1. A merge commit O has parents B and D
2. The replay rewrites B onto E (creating E')
3. If E' and D conflict in ways that don't overlap with O's original conflicts, the current implementation would silently accept the new conflicts rather than detecting them

Phillip notes this aligns with Elijah Newren's design notes which suggest extending xdiff merge detection, but acknowledges that would be significant work. He proposes a possible workaround - requiring both merge parents to be rebased onto the new base (E in the example) to avoid such conflicts.

The review demonstrates deep understanding of the merge machinery and the specific challenges of preserving conflict resolutions across rewrites. It doesn't propose immediate changes but surfaces an important limitation that the authors should consider addressing, either through implementation changes or explicit documentation of the known edge case.

This feedback will likely prompt discussion about whether to:
1. Accept the limitation for now (since the series is RFC)
2. Add detection for this case
3. Document it as a known restriction
4. Implement the more complex xdiff solution Elijah suggested

The review is technically precise, citing specific commit relationships and clearly explaining the problematic scenario without being prescriptive about solutions.
