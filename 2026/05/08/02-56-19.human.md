This is Junio Hamano's response to Johannes Schindelin's patch series removing the nedmalloc allocator from Git's Windows builds. The maintainer acknowledges the technical constraints that forced the removal to be split across multiple patches due to mailing list size limits, but suggests two alternative approaches to present the changes more cleanly in the project history.

Junio proposes either:
1. Consolidating the logical description of the full removal in the first patch (2/6) and marking subsequent patches as "to be squashed", or
2. Handling the final removal as a single squashed commit after initial review of the early preparatory patches

The feedback reflects Junio's typical maintainer perspective - focusing on preserving a clean, logical history despite technical constraints of the mailing list workflow. He's not questioning the technical merit of the removal (which was already established in earlier discussion), but rather how to best represent the change in Git's commit history. This is a common pattern when large deletions must be split across multiple patches.
