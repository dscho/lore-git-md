This is a substantive review of Johannes Schindelin's RFC series adding merge commit support to `git history`. Phillip Wood identifies a potential edge case in the merge replay logic that could lead to undetected conflicts when replaying merge commits.

The review focuses on a scenario where:
1. A merge commit O is created from parents B and D
2. When replaying onto E (a descendant of B), if E and D conflict in ways that don't overlap with O's original conflicts
3. The current implementation might not detect these new conflicts because it only checks the resolution of O's original conflicts against the new merge

Phillip notes this case is acknowledged in Elijah Newren's original design notes, which suggest extending xdiff merge detection - a significant undertaking. He proposes a simpler solution: only allow merge replay when both parents have been rebased onto the target, ensuring any new conflicts would appear during the parent replay rather than the merge recreation.

The review demonstrates deep understanding of the merge machinery and identifies a real limitation in the current approach. Phillip's suggestion of constraining the allowed merge replay scenarios provides a practical path forward while avoiding the complexity of full conflict detection. This feedback will likely shape the next iteration of the RFC series.
