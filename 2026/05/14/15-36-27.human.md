This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the "today" alias patch in the approxidate series. Junio C Hamano clarifies his position on the controversial "today" alias proposal from Tuomas Ahola's v3 series.

Junio explains that his objection isn't about an edge case in the implementation, but rather about the fundamental semantics of making "today" mean "now" in Git's date parsing. He points out that this would make commands like `git log --since=today` behave counterintuitively by excluding all commits made earlier in the day, since "--since=now" means "strictly after this moment." 

The maintainer draws a distinction between this being an intentional design choice (which he might accept with proper justification) versus simply inheriting the default behavior that approxidate uses for unrecognized strings (like "decay" or "bogus"). The latter case, he implies, would be an unacceptable way to define such a common term.

This email doesn't introduce new technical changes but serves to clarify the maintainer's position on the semantic concern. The ball is now in Tuomas's court to either:
1) Drop the "today" alias patch
2) Propose it with different semantics (like meaning "midnight")
3) Provide a stronger justification for the current behavior

The rest of the series (patches 1, 3, and 4) remains unaffected by this discussion and could potentially proceed independently if the "today" alias is dropped.
