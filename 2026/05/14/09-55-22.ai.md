Vincent's point is simple but useful: he agrees with Junio that the algorithmic description alone is unhelpful, and proposes that the paragraph should instead recommend `--ignore-space-change` as a practical workaround. He is careful to distinguish `--ignore-space-change` from `--ignore-all-space` (`-w`), noting that Johannes Sixt's earlier suggestion of `-w` is too aggressive -- it would hide the difference between a file with one word and a file with three identical letters separated by spaces, which is a genuine structural change, not just whitespace. `--ignore-space-change` is more conservative: it only ignores whitespace *differences* (e.g., tabs vs. spaces, trailing whitespace) while still treating runs of whitespace as delimiters.

However, Vincent honestly admits that even `--ignore-space-change` is not a complete fix: if the only changes between two files are whitespace that affects line boundaries (as in his `"foo bar"` vs `"foo\nbar"` example), `--word-diff --ignore-space-change` would still show a diff. He estimates that such cases are rare in practice, and suggests that recommending `--ignore-space-change` is still a net improvement over the status quo.

This is a small, helpful contribution to the stalled thread -- Vincent is not a reviewer, but as the original reporter he has a clear stake in the outcome.
