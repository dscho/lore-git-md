This thread discusses a performance optimization for Git's revision walking code, specifically replacing a linked list with a priority queue in `limit_list()` to improve efficiency in merge-heavy repositories. The original patch series (v2) has already been reviewed and approved by Derrick Stolee, Jeff King, and Junio Hamano, with benchmarks showing 1.6-4.3x speedups, particularly for ancestry-path queries and commit-graph-enabled cases.

Kristofer Karlsson later suggested extending this approach to `get_revision_1()`, reporting potential 3.3x speedups in monorepos, though this would require more complex changes to the revision walking infrastructure. The discussion is now considering whether to pursue this as a separate effort.

René Scharfe has contributed new benchmark data showing consistent 1.16-1.28x speedups when testing the original optimization against Git's own version history using `git rev-list --bisect`. While these improvements are more modest than the best-case scenarios from the initial series, they demonstrate that the benefits extend beyond just pathological merge-heavy cases to more typical repository histories. This strengthens the case for merging the optimization while providing a more complete picture of its real-world impact. The core optimization appears ready for integration, with potential follow-on work to explore similar improvements elsewhere in the revision walking code.
