This is a follow-up discussion on the "today" alias patch in the approxidate series. Junio C Hamano clarifies his earlier concern about the semantic implications of making "today" an alias for "now" in Git's date parsing. He emphasizes that while the current implementation would make `--since=today` behave like `--since=now` (excluding all commits from earlier in the day), this is not a desirable behavior - it's simply how approxidate currently handles unrecognized strings like "decay" or "bogus". 

Junio makes it clear this isn't about an edge case in the implementation, but rather about intentionally designing the behavior. His objection suggests the patch needs to either:
1. Explicitly document and justify the "today=now" equivalence as an intentional design choice, or
2. Implement different semantics that better match user expectations (likely making "today" mean "since midnight")

The exchange reveals a disconnect - while the author thought Junio was pointing out an implementation edge case, Junio is actually questioning the fundamental design decision. This clarification moves the discussion forward by reframing the issue from "does this work correctly?" to "is this the right behavior to implement?" 

The patch remains in limbo until this design question is resolved, though the rest of the series (patches 3/4 and 4/4) can proceed independently as they address different edge cases in date parsing.
