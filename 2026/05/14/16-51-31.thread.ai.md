Performance optimization patch series (v2) for revision walking's `limit_list()` has been approved and merged, replacing a linked list with a priority queue for O(log n) insertion in merge-heavy repositories (1.6-4.3x speedups). Key participants Derrick Stolee, Jeff King, and Junio C Hamano approved the changes, which affect symmetric difference operations (`--left-right`, `--cherry-mark`, bisect, rebase fork-point, `git status --ahead-behind`). Kristofer Karlsson identified potential extension to `get_revision_1()` (3.3x speedup for `git rev-list HEAD` in monorepos), with Jeff King confirming viability and sharing unpublished local patches implementing this idea.

New discussion compares two approaches for extending priority queue usage: Kristofer's hybrid linked-list + priority queue (4 files touched, 4% faster in limited walks) versus Jeff's full conversion to priority queues (12 files touched). Both show 3-5x speedups in 2.4M-commit monorepo benchmarks. Kristofer posted a draft PR with vtable-based walk strategy system, seeking feedback on long-term maintainability. This represents the natural next step after the merged `limit_list()` optimizations, with both contributors converging on similar performance improvements through different architectural paths. René Scharfe's historical benchmarking (v2.0.1 to v2.50.1) showed consistent 1.16-1.28x speedups for linear histories, confirming the optimization's broad applicability.
