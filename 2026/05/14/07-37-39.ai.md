This is a reply from the maintainer (Junio C Hamano) to Michael Montalbo's proposed documentation patch. Junio is unconvinced that the patch achieves its goal.

The one-paragraph addition to `Documentation/diff-options.adoc` that Montalbo proposed explains that word diff works by first finding line-level changes and then looking for word-level differences within those hunks. Junio's objection is not about factual accuracy -- he concedes the text "may not say anything wrong" -- but about whether knowing that internal mechanism actually helps a user who is confused by the output. His implied question is: if a user sees unexpected word-level changes, does being told "this is because of how the two-step algorithm works" actually reduce their confusion, or does it just transfer the puzzle to a different layer of the explanation?

This is a substantive assessment from the maintainer, not a surface-level comment. Junio is asking the patch author to step back from describing _how_ the machinery works and instead consider _what_ a user needs to understand to avoid being surprised. The patch as written explains the algorithm, but it does not tell the user what to _do_ about the situation -- it does not say, for example, "if line alignment produces unexpected results, consider normalizing whitespace first" or "use `--word-diff-regex=` to limit what counts as a word", nor does it give a mental model that makes the behavior feel less arbitrary.
