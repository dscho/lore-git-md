Jeff King's further analysis:
- Opened by pushing back on Taylor's characterization of the "drop and re-acquire" alternative as unworkably racy: `git gc` already uses exactly that pattern (deletes the lockfile before daemonizing), and the only real risk is starvation, which is both unlikely and self-limiting (the final invoker will always win the race eventually). This calibrates the discussion: Peff is not arguing against Taylor's fix, but noting that the problem it solves is a correctness and design hygiene issue, not an absolute hard requirement.
- In response to Taylor's question about whether all tempfiles should be reassigned on fork, Peff argues that yes, *all* of them should be reassigned. His reasoning: "daemonize() is not about spawning a child... There is still a single contiguous flow of execution, and the fact that we switch pids and call exit() is an implementation detail." From that perspective, every tempfile and lockfile should follow the new process id, because there is not really a "parent" and "child" -- there is just one logical execution that happens to change pid. He extends the argument to atexit handlers in general, though notes the situation does not arise in practice because daemonize is called early.
- Most significant technical contribution: Peff identified a race condition in Taylor's implementation. The proposed patch has a window during which both parent and child could claim ownership (after the child takes ownership but before the parent finishes reassigning), or neither could (if the parent relinquishes before the child takes). The double-claim scenario can, in an elaborate sequence involving signals and a third process, cause a lock to be deleted out from under someone who just acquired it. The no-claim scenario just leaves a stale lock behind -- annoying but not worse than a `kill -9`. Peff offers no easy fix (synchronizing between parent and child with pipes would add significant complexity) and seems to conclude the race is unlikely enough to be acceptable.
- Implication: Taylor's solution is likely workable with minor polish, but the window between the two `reassign_tempfile_ownership()` calls may need to be made atomic or at least narrower, perhaps by having the parent reassign first and the child take over afterward, rather than having both directions in flight simultaneously.
