This thread has uncovered a serious issue in Git's maintenance subsystem where background operations can consume excessive resources and potentially corrupt repositories through concurrent repacks. The problem stems from maintenance running independently of traditional `gc.auto` settings, with no locking to prevent multiple instances.

Jeff King (Peff) confirmed the root cause lies in the maintenance subsystem's design rather than a configuration parsing bug. Two key issues emerged: whether `maintenance.auto` should respect `gc.auto` for backward compatibility, and the need for locking to prevent concurrent maintenance runs. Derrick Stolee, the subsystem's architect, has been CC'd for input.

Mikael Magnusson's recent response escalates the discussion by highlighting alarming usability concerns. He expresses shock that this potentially destructive behavior is enabled by default and questions the safety claims in Git's documentation given the demonstrated corruption risks. The email surfaces confusion about the interaction between different maintenance modes (`maintenance.auto` vs `git maintenance register`) and suggests the subsystem's design may be fundamentally flawed in how it presents these features to users.

While no new technical details were added, the response shifts focus to broader questions about default behaviors and documentation accuracy. The strong reaction ("horrific", "hard to believe") suggests this may evolve into a contentious discussion about the maintenance subsystem's design philosophy, especially as it affects users who knowingly enabled maintenance versus those who inherited it through default configurations.
