Here is the updated human-mode thread summary, integrating the new email from Jeff King.

This thread began as a bug report about `git maintenance --auto` causing repository corruption due to a broken locking mechanism when running in daemonized mode (`--detach`), a regression in Git v2.54. The core issue is that when the parent process daemonizes, it releases its lockfiles, but the child process continues working without them, creating a race condition that can corrupt the repository.

The discussion has crystallized around three potential solutions: a revert for the v2.54.1 stable release, a comprehensive fix from Taylor Blau that transfers ownership of *all* tempfiles to the child, and a narrower fix from Patrick Steinhardt that only targets lockfiles.

The newest email, from Jeff King (Peff), is a strong endorsement of Taylor Blau's comprehensive direction, though with a critical technical nuance. Peff argues that on philosophical grounds, *all* tempfiles should follow the daemonized process. He explains that daemonization isn't a parent-child spawn, but a single logical execution that just happens to change its PID. He also pushes back on Taylor's dismissal of an alternative "drop and re-acquire" pattern, noting that the existing `git gc` already uses this safely. Peff's most valuable contribution is identifying a subtle race condition in Taylor's proposed `reassign_tempfile_ownership()` implementation, where both the parent and child could briefly claim ownership of the same tempfile, or neither could. He deems the resulting failure modes acceptable in practice (stale lock or unlikely edge-case deletion), but suggests the fix could be polished.

The thread remains at a decision point. While Peff's analysis adds weight to the comprehensive solution, Derrick Stolee's earlier review still leans toward a safe revert for the point release. The community now needs to decide if Peff's confidence in the "good enough" approach overrides the desire for a perfectly atomic fix, and whether this fix is stable enough for a v2.54.1 or should wait for a later release. Junio C Hamano has not yet weighed in.
