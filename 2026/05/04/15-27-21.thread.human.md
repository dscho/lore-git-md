The thread began with a report that Git was ignoring resource-limiting configurations (`gc.auto=0`, `pack.threads=1`) during `git add` and `git commit`, spawning excessive pack-objects processes that consumed nearly all system resources. Jeff King (Peff) has now identified the root cause: this behavior comes from Git's newer `git-maintenance` subsystem, which operates independently of the `gc.auto` setting and lacks proper locking to prevent concurrent operations. 

Peff explains that maintenance tasks are controlled by `maintenance.auto` rather than `gc.auto`, and each maintenance operation can spawn its own set of threads, leading to the reported resource exhaustion. More concerningly, he demonstrates this can cause repository corruption through concurrent repacks. The discussion has shifted to two key questions: whether `maintenance.auto` should respect `gc.auto` for consistency, and how to implement proper locking in the maintenance subsystem to prevent these issues. Peff has brought in Derrick Stolee, the maintenance subsystem expert, to weigh in on these design questions. The discovery that this can lead to data corruption raises the stakes beyond just performance concerns.
