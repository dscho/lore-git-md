This is a technical clarification in Johannes Schindelin's ongoing series addressing Windows platform limitations with large objects (>4GB). The exchange focuses on test performance metrics for patch 8/11, which introduces precomputed packfiles to optimize testing of 4GB+1 byte objects.

Derrick Stolee had questioned whether the performance numbers (88s/81s/140s vs 14s/13s/15s) properly accounted for the unsafe SHA-1 hashing optimization from an earlier patch. Schindelin explains that the unsafe SHA-1 optimization is only available in certain CI configurations (specifically linux-TEST-vars jobs) and not on Windows builds, where the performance numbers remain unchanged. This clarifies that the precomputed pack optimization provides its speedup independently of the SHA-1 hashing changes.

The response highlights a platform-specific limitation - Windows builds cannot currently benefit from the unsafe SHA-1 optimization due to OpenSSL linking constraints. This maintains the technical focus of the series while addressing Stolee's substantive question about how the optimizations interact. The exchange demonstrates careful attention to performance measurement methodology in this sensitive area of large object handling.
