Review from Johannes Sixt on `fetch --prune-merged` functionality.

Johannes Sixt expresses reservations about the proposed `fetch --prune-merged` feature, which would automatically delete local branches after a fetch operation when their upstream branches have been pruned. While acknowledging the motivation for cleaning up obsolete local branches, he objects to the fundamental concept of `git fetch` modifying local branch state, even as an opt-in feature.

The review takes a philosophical stance rather than a technical one - Sixt argues that local branch management should remain the exclusive domain of `git branch` at the porcelain level. This contrasts with the series' approach of providing both low-level plumbing (`git branch --prune-merged`) and high-level automation (`fetch --prune-merged`).

This feedback represents a deeper architectural concern than previous discussions in the thread, which had focused more on implementation details and naming. It suggests a potential philosophical divide about the appropriate scope of `git fetch`'s responsibilities. The review is substantive in that it engages with the design principles rather than surface-level issues, though it doesn't propose specific technical alternatives.

The comment leaves open how the functionality might be redesigned to respect these boundaries while still providing convenient automation, and whether other contributors will share this perspective on command responsibilities.
