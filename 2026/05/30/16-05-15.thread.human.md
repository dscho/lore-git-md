This thread is about refactoring two core Git configuration flags—`trust_executable_bit` and `has_symlinks`—to eliminate their reliance on the global `the_repository` variable, completing a key phase of the broader effort to remove global state from Git’s codebase. The work is split into a 4-patch series (v5) by Tian Yuchen, with the first three patches serving as preparatory cleanups and the final patch reintroducing the previously deferred migration of `has_symlinks` into repository-scoped storage.

The earlier v4 iteration of this series (which migrated only `trust_executable_bit`) was merged into `master` after a smooth review period, and v5 now builds on that foundation by adding `has_symlinks` to the same repository-scoped configuration pattern. The changes are mechanical, preserving existing behavior while introducing new getter functions (`repo_has_symlinks()` and `repo_trust_executable_bit()`) and adjusting the mingw compatibility layer to use a global fallback variable. The series has attracted substantive review from established contributors like Patrick Steinhardt and Christian Couder, who have already weighed in on v4 and now offer targeted feedback on v5.

Christian’s recent review of the final patch suggests a minor rewording of the commit message to avoid redundancy but otherwise signals approval, leaving the core design—including the mingw compatibility workaround—unchallenged. The author has acknowledged some tension between the ideal of fully repository-scoped configuration and the practical need for early platform-specific overrides, but no major objections or open technical questions remain. The series is now under active review, with the migration pattern established here likely to serve as a template for future configuration refactoring efforts.
