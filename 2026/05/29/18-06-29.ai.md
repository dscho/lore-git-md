This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about managing fetch workflows in large repositories. Matthew Hughes shares a practical solution he implemented to address the tension between selective fetching and tracking colleagues' branches.

The approach involves two scripts:
1. A `git-fetch-other` command that fetches a specific branch, adds a wildcard-based refspec to track updates (avoiding errors when branches are deleted), and records the tracking relationship using a special ref.
2. A cleanup script that periodically checks for deleted remote branches and removes their tracking configurations.

The solution cleverly works around Git's behavior with deleted branches by using wildcards in refspecs while maintaining precise tracking through a separate ref namespace (`refs/tracked/`). Hughes acknowledges this feels like a "big ol' hack" but it effectively solves the original problem of maintaining fetch configurations without accumulating stale entries.

This represents a third approach in the thread's discussion, complementing the previous suggestions of either:
1. Manual selective fetching (original approach)
2. Fetch-all with maintenance optimizations (D. Ben Knoble's suggestion)

The implementation demonstrates thoughtful engagement with Git's refspec mechanics and shows how workflow pain points can drive creative solutions at the scripting level. The solution may interest others facing similar large-repo collaboration patterns, though its hackiness suggests there might be room for more built-in Git functionality in this area.
