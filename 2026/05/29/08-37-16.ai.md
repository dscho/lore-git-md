This is a review comment on a cleanup series removing deprecated commit list functions. Jeff King (Peff) acknowledges the correctness of Kristoffer Haugsbakk's v2 series but raises a philosophical question about Git's deprecation workflow.

Peff observes that while the current approach of marking functions deprecated and removing them later is working as intended, it may not be the most efficient path. He questions whether it would have been better to simply remove the old functions immediately back in Git 2.53.0, forcing in-flight topics to update right away rather than going through the deprecation period. The compiler would have caught any missed updates in either case, and the changes required are small mechanical updates.

The comment doesn't challenge the technical correctness of the current series (Peff explicitly says it "looks obviously correct") but rather reflects on whether Git project's standard deprecation workflow is optimal. This is a meta-level observation about project process rather than a technical review of the specific patches. The question is framed as open-ended ("just pondering") rather than as a criticism of the current approach.

This review doesn't require any action from the patch author since it's not about the current changes, though it may spark discussion about future deprecation policies. The comment comes from a respected long-time contributor with deep knowledge of Git's internals and development practices.
