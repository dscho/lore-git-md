This is the final patch in Taylor Blau's performance optimization series for the pack-bitmap-write subsystem, focusing on pseudo-merge bitmap generation. The patch restructures the bitmap generation process to handle pseudo-merges in a separate pass after regular bitmaps are built, reducing overhead from 5 minutes to just 34 seconds (11.7% penalty) while maintaining traversal speed benefits.

Jeff King (Peff) had previously reviewed the patch positively, praising its clear problem explanation and technical approach. In this follow-up, Taylor provides additional performance data showing how the optimization scales with different pseudo-merge configurations. The numbers reveal sub-linear growth in generation time as the number of pseudo-merges increases, though with notable jumps at higher counts (252 pseudo-merges take ~46 minutes versus 26 taking ~3.7 minutes).

The discussion also touches on operational considerations - how pseudo-merges might perform in real-world deployments where stable refs could allow bitmap reuse across generations. Taylor acknowledges this is hard to simulate but suggests practical experience will be the true test. He also notes this patch was originally part of a larger series about pseudo-merge APIs, explaining a stray reference in the commit message.

With Peff's thorough review complete and no outstanding technical concerns, this well-optimized series appears ready for integration. The changes demonstrate Taylor's deep expertise in Git's pack and bitmap subsystems, delivering substantial performance gains through careful algorithmic improvements.
