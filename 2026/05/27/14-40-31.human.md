This is a technical follow-up in Taylor Blau's performance optimization series for bitmap generation. The email provides additional clarification on patch 3/8, which speeds up bitmap generation by reusing stored bitmaps during commit traversal.

Taylor explains the key insight behind the optimization: while the bitmap writer processes commits from roots up, it only passes commit masks along first-parent relationships. This means common history shared via non-first parents (like merge bases) gets redundantly processed. The patch addresses this by detecting when a commit being traversed already has a stored bitmap, allowing the walk to short-circuit.

Jeff King (Peff) had expressed initial surprise that such an optimization would be needed, theorizing the existing algorithm should already handle commits optimally. Taylor's response clarifies the practical limitation - the first-parent-only bitmap propagation leaves optimization opportunities on the table for shared non-first-parent history.

The exchange demonstrates the patch's technical soundness, with Peff having already confirmed the impressive 47% speedup on linux.git in his review. Taylor notes the trace2 metrics he added (initially for debugging) proved useful enough to keep, providing visibility into how often the optimization triggers. This level of measurement and validation is characteristic of Taylor's careful approach to performance work.

The discussion remains focused on bitmap internals, with no new concerns raised about the change. Peff's earlier review approval stands, and this follow-up serves to deepen understanding of why the optimization works rather than to revisit any decisions.
