This is a substantive review of a performance optimization patch in Taylor Blau's bitmap generation series. Jeff King (Peff) examines the third patch, which speeds up bitmap generation by reusing stored bitmaps during commit traversal.

Peff confirms the impressive 47% speedup on linux.git test case, matching Taylor's reported 42% improvement. While initially surprised that this optimization is needed (since the bitmap-writer should theoretically process commits in an optimal order), he recognizes the practical reality that the current algorithm processes maximal commits independently, leading to redundant work that this patch eliminates.

The review digs into the implementation details, analyzing the hash lookup tradeoff and verifying the "c != commit" guard condition that prevents unnecessary lookups. Peff notes the patch is "obviously correct" and approves of the optional-but-helpful trace2 metrics for future tuning. His analysis shows deep familiarity with the bitmap generation internals while acknowledging the empirical results outweigh theoretical expectations.

This continues the pattern of thorough, expert review for this performance series - Peff's confirmation of both the correctness and measured benefits provides strong validation for the change. The interaction demonstrates how Git's review culture combines technical depth with pragmatic acceptance of real-world performance characteristics.
