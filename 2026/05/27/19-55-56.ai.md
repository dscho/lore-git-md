This is the third patch in Taylor Blau's performance optimization series for the pack-bitmap-write subsystem, building on his earlier pseudo-merge bitmap work. The patch focuses on reusing stored bitmaps during bitmap generation to avoid redundant work.

The key optimization detects when `fill_bitmap_commit()` encounters an ancestor commit that already has a stored bitmap (from being selected earlier in the process). Rather than walking that commit's tree and parents again, it simply ORs the existing bitmap into the current one. This provides a significant 42.3% speedup (562.8s->324.8s) in the test repository, with corresponding reductions in CPU cycles and instructions.

The implementation adds counters to track how often this optimization triggers - in the test case, it short-circuits about 9.5% of the calls to `fill_bitmap_commit()`. The patch maintains the series' pattern of careful measurement and instrumentation, adding trace2 counters to monitor the optimization's effectiveness in production.

This change fits naturally with the series' overall goal of optimizing bitmap generation for large repositories, particularly by reducing redundant work. The approach builds on the position-first optimizations from earlier patches while setting up for the remaining optimizations in the series. Jeff King's prior reviews of the technical approach suggest this is likely to be uncontroversial.
