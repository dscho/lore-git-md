This is a substantive review of the final patch in Taylor Blau's performance optimization series for the pack-bitmap-write subsystem. Jeff King (Peff) provides detailed technical feedback on the pseudo-merge bitmap generation changes.

Peff praises the clear explanation of why the original approach was inefficient for pseudo-merges, noting the key insight that pseudo-merges don't represent real commits like regular bitmaps do. He validates the new approach of building pseudo-merges in a separate pass after regular bitmaps, which reduces generation time by 43% while maintaining the same on-disk format and query performance.

The review digs into performance implications, speculating how the time might scale with different pseudo-merge configurations (number of chunks) and noting the lack of real-world operational data yet. Peff also points out that the patch references "following commits" when this is actually the final patch in the series - a minor documentation nit.

While Peff acknowledges he's less familiar with the pseudo-merge code specifics, he confirms the technical approach makes sense and trusts the test coverage. He specifically calls out the handling of unparsed commits from pseudo-merges as correct optimization that should still leverage the commit-graph when available.

This review continues Peff's pattern of thorough, technically-grounded feedback throughout the series, providing the maintainer perspective while deferring to the author's deeper expertise in this subsystem. The interaction demonstrates the project's culture of careful review even for optimizations from established contributors.
