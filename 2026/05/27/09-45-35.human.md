This is a substantive review of Taylor Blau's bitmap optimization patch from Jeff King (Peff), who brings deep expertise in Git's performance characteristics. The review focuses on patch 5/8 in the series, which introduces an object position cache to speed up bitmap generation.

Peff acknowledges the effectiveness of the cache approach (reporting a 26% speedup on linux.git) while thoughtfully questioning whether alternative designs might achieve similar gains. He explores whether converting the packlist to a hash table could provide comparable benefits, but recognizes the current solution's advantages given the specific access patterns. The review also considers edge cases like very large repositories (2^31+ objects) and potential applications to bitmap reading operations.

Notably, Peff validates the implementation quality ("the whole patch looked pretty cleanly done") while maintaining a critical eye on the architectural implications. His commentary reflects both immediate approval of this specific change and broader thinking about how similar optimizations might apply elsewhere in Git's codebase. The review exemplifies the project's culture of thorough, technically deep feedback even for performance-focused changes that show clear benefits.
