Performance optimization follow-up for `git fetch` connectivity checks.

Kristofer Karlsson responds to Jeff King's technical review of his proposed `git fetch` optimization, acknowledging that the optimization is fundamentally clone-only and does not work for regular fetches as originally claimed. After deeper analysis, Kristofer confirms that even manually setting the `self_contained_and_connected` flag in the fetch path doesn't enable the optimization, and that he was unable to reproduce his initial benchmark numbers. He withdraws the patch, recognizing it was incorrect.

This exchange demonstrates Git's rigorous review process in action - a seemingly straightforward performance optimization was caught to be ineffective through careful technical analysis of the control flow. The thread shows how even well-intentioned patches undergo substantive verification before merging, with Kristofer modeling good contributor behavior by transparently acknowledging the oversight and withdrawing the proposal. The discussion leaves open the question of whether similar optimizations could be made to work for fetches in the future, but confirms this particular approach doesn't achieve that goal.
