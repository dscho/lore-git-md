Here's the human-readable summary of the thread:

Kristoffer Haugsbakk has sent a two-patch series removing long-obsolete functions from Git's commit.h API, completing a deprecation process that began in Git 2.53.0. The series has Patrick Steinhardt's approval and updates all remaining callers to use the modern equivalents. While the cleanup itself is uncontroversial, it sparked a broader discussion about Git's deprecation workflow.

Jeff King (Peff) questioned whether the standard deprecation period was always necessary for straightforward cases like this, where the replacement API is a direct equivalent. Junio Hamano responded by defending the current approach, explaining that gradual transitions help distribute maintenance work across the community rather than creating bottlenecks for the maintainer at merge time. The exchange highlights different perspectives on balancing efficiency against maintainability, though neither side is pushing for immediate process changes.

The technical cleanup will proceed as planned, with the deprecated functions being removed in the next release. Meanwhile, the discussion may influence how future deprecations are handled, particularly for low-risk changes where the transition work is minimal. Patrick Steinhardt has suggested evaluating deprecation periods case-by-case rather than applying a one-size-fits-all policy.
