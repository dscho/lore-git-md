Kristoffer Haugsbakk has sent a two-part series removing deprecated commit list functions from commit.h, completing a deprecation process that began in Git 2.53.0. The first patch updates all remaining callers to use the modern equivalents, while the second removes the obsolete functions entirely. Patrick Steinhardt, who originally introduced both the old and new APIs, has reviewed and acked the changes, confirming the removal is complete and well-timed.

Jeff King (Peff) weighed in with a thoughtful observation about Git's deprecation workflow. While fully agreeing the current series is correct, he questioned whether the project's standard practice of marking functions deprecated before later removal is the most efficient approach. Peff suggested that immediate removal might have been equally effective, since the compiler would catch any missed updates either way. This meta-level comment doesn't require any changes to the current patches but may influence how future deprecations are handled.

The series remains uncontroversial and ready for merging, having received both technical validation from Patrick and this broader process reflection from Peff. The changes are purely mechanical - updating callers and removing the now-unused functions - with no impact on behavior. This cleanup represents another small but meaningful step in Git's ongoing codebase maintenance.
