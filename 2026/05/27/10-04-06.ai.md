This is a substantive review of Taylor Blau's bitmap optimization patch from Jeff King (Peff), focusing on the commit sorting approach for better XOR compression.

Peff confirms the technical correctness of the patch but raises several insightful questions about its historical context and practical impact. He notes that while the date-based sorting makes logical sense, he's surprised the bitmap generation code wasn't already producing optimal results given the commit processing order. His testing on linux.git shows no size difference between v2.30 and v2.31 (both ~67MB), suggesting this optimization may be more impactful for very large repositories like Taylor's test case (which saw a 72% reduction).

The review digs into two key follow-up areas: historical performance (suggesting testing with v2.30.0 and v2.31.1 to understand when the suboptimal behavior emerged) and read-side performance (noting that while fewer bytes should help, there could be hidden assumptions that might negatively impact read performance). These points demonstrate Peff's thorough approach - validating not just the code's present correctness but its evolution and broader system impact.

Peff also walks through the implementation details, confirming the QSORT() approach and the necessary hash map pointer updates are correctly handled. His "Looks correct" final assessment carries weight given his deep expertise in this area, though the unanswered questions about historical context and read performance suggest there may be more to explore about why this optimization works so well in some cases but not others.
