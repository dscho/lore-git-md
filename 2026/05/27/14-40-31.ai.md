This is a technical discussion thread about performance optimizations in Git's bitmap generation code, specifically addressing how to reuse stored bitmaps during commit traversal to avoid redundant work.

Taylor Blau explains the rationale behind his patch that teaches `fill_bitmap_commit()` to recognize when it encounters an ancestor that already has a stored bitmap, allowing it to skip redundant tree and parent walks. This optimization yielded a 42-47% speedup in testing, as confirmed by Jeff King (Peff) on linux.git.

Peff initially expressed surprise that such an optimization would be needed, given the theoretical optimality of the current bitmap writing approach. However, he recognizes the practical reality where the algorithm processes maximal commits independently, leading to redundant work that this patch eliminates. The discussion clarifies that the optimization particularly helps with non-first-parent history sharing in repositories with merges.

Taylor confirms Peff's understanding of the "c != commit" guard condition that prevents unnecessary hash lookups. Both agree the trace2 metrics, while not strictly necessary, provide useful data for future tuning. Peff concludes the patch is "obviously correct" in its implementation.
