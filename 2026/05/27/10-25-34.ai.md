This is a substantive review of the final patch in Taylor Blau's performance optimization series for the pack-bitmap-write subsystem. Jeff King (Peff) provides detailed technical feedback on the pseudo-merge bitmap generation changes.

Peff praises the clear explanation of the problem - that treating pseudo-merges like regular commits imposes significant slowdown (5 minute penalty for 50s traversal benefit). He agrees with the solution's approach: building regular bitmaps first, then processing pseudo-merges separately. The review notes this conceptually returns to pre-v2.31 bitmap generation methods but makes sense given pseudo-merges' non-overlapping nature.

The review examines the performance metrics showing 11.7% overhead for pseudo-merges (down from previous excessive costs) and considers how this might scale with various pseudo-merge configurations. Peff speculates about operational characteristics - that time may grow linearly with number of chunks, but hopes stable refs would allow reuse across generations. He acknowledges these behaviors will depend heavily on repository-specific factors.

While approving the technical approach, Peff notes he's less familiar with pseudo-merge internals and trusts the tests more than his review for verification. He does analyze one code section in detail - the commit parsing logic for pseudo-merge reaches - confirming it properly handles both parsed and unparsed commit cases.

The review concludes with Peff's characteristic sign-off, indicating approval while maintaining his role as reviewer rather than final arbiter. This continues the pattern seen throughout the series of thorough, technically-grounded feedback from one of Git's most experienced contributors.
