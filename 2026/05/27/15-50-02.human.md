Performance optimization for revision walking using priority queues.

This is the third and final patch in Kristofer Karlsson's series to optimize revision walking by replacing O(w) linked-list insertions with O(log w) priority queue operations. The series builds on a previous optimization that improved `limit_list()`, now targeting the streaming (non-limited) walk in `get_revision_1()`.

The patch introduces a `commit_queue` field in `rev_info` alongside the existing commit list, making the two representations mutually exclusive. The queue is lazily initialized on first use by draining the commit list. A new `REV_WALK_NO_WALK` enum value helps distinguish cases that still need list access. The changes touch core revision walking logic but maintain compatibility with existing callers.

Benchmarks in the cover letter show a 5.3x speedup for merge-heavy repositories with wide walk frontiers, while having no measurable impact on repositories like linux.git with narrow frontiers. The implementation appears carefully crafted - it removes the now-unused `commit_list_insert_by_date()` function and includes thorough updates to all walk modes. The patch follows Git's pattern of performance-sensitive changes being introduced after preparatory refactoring (patch 2's enum introduction).

This completes a well-motivated optimization that eliminates the last O(w) insertion bottleneck in revision walking. The series demonstrates good decomposition, with each patch standing on its own while building toward the final goal. No reviews have been posted yet, but the approach aligns with prior successful optimizations in this area.
