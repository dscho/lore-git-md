This is a design discussion about the final visualization approach for root commits in `git log --graph` output, part of Pablo Sabater's GSoC project to improve graph rendering of commits with excluded parents. The technical implementation (using `is_placeholder` flags) has been validated through multiple iterations (now at v3), with this email representing the last open question.

Phillip Wood responds to Pablo's analysis of two visualization approaches (indentation vs blank lines) by clarifying his blank-line proposal. He notes that blank lines wouldn't necessarily waste more vertical space than indentation when properly implemented, and provides concrete examples showing how blank lines could maintain visual grouping of related commits while still separating root commits. Phillip acknowledges that indentation could theoretically be more space-efficient if implemented "smarter" but maintains that blank lines provide clearer visual separation in complex cases.

The email reveals key insights:
1. The blank-line approach would treat root commits as the visual anomaly rather than their children
2. Both approaches should theoretically use similar vertical space when optimally implemented
3. Complex merge scenarios (like the "shower of commits" example) may benefit from blank lines' clearer separation

This represents the final design decision point before potential integration, with no remaining technical objections - only a preference choice between two valid visualization strategies that both satisfy the original requirements. The thread shows healthy debate about the subtle UX tradeoffs in graph rendering clarity versus space efficiency.
