This is a performance optimization patch for revision walking, part of an ongoing thread discussing the replacement of a linked list with a priority queue in `limit_list()`. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, responds to reviews from Junio C Hamano, Derrick Stolee, and Jeff King (Peff) with additional benchmark data and clarifications.

Key points from this follow-up:
- The optimization shows significant speedups (1.6-4.3x) in merge-heavy repositories, particularly when the commit-graph is disabled or when using ancestry-path queries.
- The improvement is less noticeable in linear histories like git.git's, where the queue remains narrow.
- Benchmark data now includes both commit-graph and non-commit-graph scenarios, addressing Junio's earlier question about when `limit_list()` is actually used.
- Peff's concern about `unsorted_input` is confirmed to be a non-issue since `limit_list()` isn't called in that case.
- The author considers adding a new perf test but notes the challenge of creating a representative merge-heavy test repository.

The technical approach has been validated by all reviewers, with the remaining discussion focusing on benchmarking methodology and potential test coverage. The patch appears well-justified and ready for the next iteration with the suggested commit message improvements.
