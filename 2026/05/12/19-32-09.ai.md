This is a reply from René Scharfe to Jeff King's review of patch 1/4, adding implementation background that was not in the original commit message. René reports that he actually benchmarked three formatting strategies before settling on the on-stack temporary buffer: (1) formatting directly into a pre-grown strbuf using memmove, (2) formatting into an on-stack buffer with memcpy, and (3) computing the digit count up front with extra divisions to write into the strbuf in-place. The on-stack buffer approach was essentially a tie on two different CPUs -- slightly ahead on Apple silicon, slightly behind on an AMD Ryzen -- and René chose it mainly because it is the same pattern used in standard printf implementations on BSD systems, making it defensible as the solid conservative choice.

The no-copy / memmove variant did not deliver the expected cache-line benefit; René speculates that memmove's overhead outweighs any savings. Likewise, precomputing the digit count (which would avoid both the buffer and the memcpy) came out a wash in practice. The reader should take away that the implementation was thoughtfully benchmarked against plausible alternatives and the chosen approach is not arbitrary. The exchange adds no new demands on the series and requires no action -- it is a pleasant technical aside between two contributors who enjoy discussing tradeoffs.
