This is the author's follow-up reply to Jeff King's review of patch 3 in the `strbuf_add_uint()` series. It is mostly a casual technical discussion rather than a response requiring action.

René confirms the performance numbers Peff asked about: the improvement is a modest 1.2 ms for the padded case (the unpadded case is cleaner), but noise is around 1.0 ms, so the gain is marginal. He then responds to Peff's musings about padding-handling alternatives by revealing that he initially tried a `struct numbuf` approach specifically to avoid the `memmove()`, but decided introducing a new concept for just two callers was overkill -- exactly the same conclusion Peff reached independently.

The conversation then drifts into a familiar hobby-horse for Peff: the idea of a custom `vsnprintf()` for Git. René points out that C99 provides `%ju` and `%zu` as cleaner alternatives to the `PRIuMAX` machinery, and notes that coding-guideline concerns about MinGW support for these format specifiers may be outdated -- the project already has a `%zu` use as of January 2026 (commit 82c36fa0a9), and `compat/mimalloc/` in Git for Windows uses them freely. He adds an idle thought about how an extensible formatter could produce structured output like tar headers, but concludes he never found a compelling enough reason to justify the complexity.

This is a clean, conversational close to the thread. No changes are being made to the patches. The author and reviewer agree on the technical trade-offs, the series is ready for pickup, and the discussion that follows is low-stakes background chatter about potential future directions. Any suggestion to the reader that a v2 is coming would be misleading.
