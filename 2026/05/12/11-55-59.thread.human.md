This is a thread about a small optimization series: René Scharfe proposed adding a `strbuf_add_uint()` convenience function that formats unsigned integers as decimal strings using a simple division loop instead of the full printf machinery. The series converts three call sites in `cat-file`, `ls-files`, and `ls-tree` to use it, and Jeff King (Peff) reviewed and approved all four patches. Jeff confirmed the change is both a performance win and a readability improvement -- `cat-file --batch-all-objects` is about 4% faster on one benchmark.

The only point of mild dissatisfaction was the padding-handling in `ls-tree`, which uses `strbuf_insert()` (with a `memmove()`) to handle field-width padding. Peff sketched two alternatives but dismissed both as over-engineering. René's follow-up confirmed that he had independently tried one of those alternatives (a struct-based formatter) and reached the same conclusion: not worth it for just two callers. He also provided the performance numbers Peff asked for: the padded case is a modest 1.2 ms improvement on the benchmark, within the noise of about 1.0 ms. The rest of René's reply is a casual technical digression about format specifier portability (`%zu` vs `%ju` vs `PRIuMAX`) and an idle thought about what an extensible formatter could do, all low-stakes.

The thread is finished with no outstanding requests, no changes to the patches, and no expectation of a v2. The series is ready for Junio to pick up.
