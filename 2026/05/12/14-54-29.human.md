This is the second patch in a three-part series by Tuomas Ahola fixing long-standing quirks in Git's "approxidate" date parser. It targets the `date_time()` function, which handles time-of-day specials like "noon" (12 PM) and "tea" (5 PM). Currently these specials wrap to the previous day if the current clock is before that time -- even when an explicit day-of-month is also supplied. So "10 May at tea", when read at 11 AM, incorrectly answers May 9. The patch fixes this by adding a guard: the day-rollover logic now only fires if `tm_mday` has not already been set to a non-negative value. It is a one-line change in `date.c` with a clear and correct-looking rationale.

The commit message gives concrete examples of the broken behavior, including one that Linus Torvalds complained about in 2006 -- good motivation. The patch also adds "today" as an alias for "now", so that "today at noon" parses correctly (previously it would have been silently rejected or misinterpreted). The test additions cover both "last Friday at noon" (the documented use case) and "January 5th noon pm" to verify that a fixed day-of-month is respected regardless of the current hour.

This is a small, focused, well-explained patch. The change is obviously correct and the test coverage seems adequate for the scope. The only thing a reviewer might flag is the addition of the "today" alias -- it is a minor user-visible change and its interaction with other specials (e.g., "today yesterday" or "today at tomorrow's noon") is not tested. But for the specific use case "today at noon", this is clearly the right thing. The patch deserves attention from anyone who has ever used or debugged approximate dates, and looks like it should be straightforward to accept.
