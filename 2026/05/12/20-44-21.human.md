This is a reply from René Scharfe to Jeff King's (Peff's) review of patch 3 of the `strbuf_add_uint()` series. The conversation was already essentially complete, and this is a quick, informal follow-up -- not a new version of the patch.

The thread had settled on an open question: Peff found the padding-handling approach in the `ls-files` patch (format the number into the strbuf, then `strbuf_insert` to add spaces on the left) inelegant because it involves a `memmove`, but he explicitly dismissed his own improvement sketches as over-engineering. René's reply here does not change that status. He confirms that the performance difference between the padded and unpadded cases was a mere 1.2 ms (within noise), so the `memmove` is not a practical concern. He then reveals that he started with a cleaner design -- a struct to hold a number string and its length -- but dropped it because it felt like too much abstraction for only two call sites. That is a reasonable design choice, and nobody is asking him to revisit it.

The last part of the email is speculation: René and Peff briefly commiserate about the general state of printf formatting in Git. René points out that C99's `%ju` and `%zu` are probably safe now (MinGW support has caught up, and examples of `%zu` already exist in the codebase), and muses about an extensible custom printf-like formatter. This is pure idle chat and has no bearing on the series.

**Bottom line: no action required.** The series is still ready for the maintainer. This reply adds a bit of implementation-history color but does not raise any issues or request any changes.
