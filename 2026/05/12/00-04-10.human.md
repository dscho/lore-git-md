This is a follow-up from Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, in the ongoing `git add -p` word-diff visualization interface design discussion.

Junio is responding to D. Ben Knoble's latest proposal. Knoble had refined his earlier idea of using a `:` prefix for longer-form commands (like `:print --word-diff`) into a concrete implementation strategy, arguing that since `:` would be a new keybinding, exempting it from single-key mode wouldn't break anyone's muscle memory.

Junio's reply is brief and measured. He acknowledges that using `:` as a prefix for rare or descriptive actions "makes sense" in principle. However, he immediately undercuts that with a significant caveat: it's not obvious to him that the complexity of building the machinery to make `:` special -- handling longhand commands with arguments -- is worth doing just for a word-diff toggle.

This is a classic Junio response: he engages thoughtfully with the design idea, but his skepticism about implementation cost is front and center. The subtext is clear -- the maintainer is not convinced this feature warrants building out a whole new command infrastructure. The thread's central open question (whether to go with a single-letter `w`, reuse `p` with options, or build the `:` system) remains fully unresolved, but this reply shifts the burden of proof back onto the proponents of the `:` approach.

For a reader tracking this thread, the key takeaway is that Junio is not blocking the idea, but he is signaling that any solution requiring significant new plumbing will need a stronger justification than "it would be nice to have." The path of least resistance -- a single-letter `w` command or reusing `p` -- still looks more likely to land, if and when the author produces a new patch version.
