This is the final piece of a well-established seven-part series from Harald Nordgren — **patch 5/5** of v7, adding an `--all-remotes` flag to the new `--forked` and `--prune-merged` commands.

The idea is straightforward: instead of naming every remote on the command line, a user can pass `--all-remotes` to operate against every configured remote at once. `--all-remotes` alone is rejected (it must be paired with `--forked` or `--prune-merged`), and explicit `<remote>` arguments still work alongside it. The implementation is clean — it adds a `for_each_remote()` call to the existing `collect_forked_set()` helper to populate the remote list, threads the new `all_remotes` flag through `list_forked_branches()` and `prune_merged_branches()`, and bails early if the flag is used in isolation. The test suite adds four cases covering the flag with `--forked`, with `--prune-merged`, validating that an explicit invalid remote is still caught, and confirming that `--all-remotes` alone errors out.

This is a natural capstone for the series — once you have per-branch opt-out (`branch.<name>.pruneMerged`) and the multi-remote pruning logic in place, the `--all-remotes` shortcut is the obvious user-facing convenience. The patch is proportional and well-tested. No outstanding objections were raised against any of the v7 patches in this round, and the thread summary indicates the series is likely ready to progress toward integration.
