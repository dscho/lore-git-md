This is a cover letter for a four-patch bugfix series from Michael Montalbo, fixing a real and demonstrable bug: negative values for `-U`/`--unified` and `--inter-hunk-context` are silently accepted by the CLI but produce garbage -- hunks with negative line counts like `@@ -503,999- +503,999- @@`, or patches that `git apply` cannot apply. The cover letter shows concrete reproduction steps with before-and-after output, making the problem vivid.

The root cause is well-diagnosed: when these options were converted from a custom `opt_arg()` parser (which used `isdigit()` and implicitly rejected negatives) to standard `OPT_INTEGER_F` / `OPT_CALLBACK`, the validation was silently lost. The series restores it by switching both options to unsigned types, adds a defensive `BUG()` in xdiff itself, and clarifies the `PARSE_OPT_NONEG` documentation to prevent future confusion (the flag only blocks `--no-*` boolean forms, not negative numeric values). This aligns CLI behavior with the config variables (`diff.context` and `diff.interHunkContext`), which already reject negatives.

There are substantive differences from v1: the commit messages in patches 1 and 4 were rewritten to stop incorrectly linking `PARSE_OPT_NONEG` to the bug, and patch 4 was trimmed to simply clarify what "negated" means, without documenting what it does not do. The range-diff shows these revisions cleanly.

The series looks technically sound and well-communicated. Each patch has a clear motivation, the fix is minimal and type-safe, and test coverage is added for the negative-value rejection. The defensive `BUG()` in xdiff is a reasonable defense-in-depth measure, placed where the invalid value would actually produce output. This is the kind of small, well-scoped fix that the project handles efficiently -- low risk of controversy, clear benefit, ready for `next`.
