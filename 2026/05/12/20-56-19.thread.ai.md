Vincent Lefevre now proposes a specific answer: recommend `--ignore-space-change` as a practical workaround. He distinguishes `--ignore-space-change` from `--ignore-all-space` (`-w`), noting that `-w` is too aggressive -- it would hide the difference between a file with one word and a file with three identical letters separated by spaces, which is a genuine structural change. `--ignore-space-change` only ignores whitespace *differences* (tabs vs. spaces, trailing whitespace) while still treating runs of whitespace as delimiters. However, Vincent honestly admits this is not a complete fix: if the only changes affect line boundaries (as in his `"foo bar"` vs `"foo\nbar"` example), `--word-diff --ignore-space-change` would still show a diff. He estimates these cases are rare in practice and recommends the guidance as a net improvement. It remains open whether this advice is sufficient or whether the patch needs broader treatment of acknowledged corner cases. The author has not yet replied to either Junio or Vincent.
