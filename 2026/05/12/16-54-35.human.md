This is a reply from **Derrick Stolee (author)** to **Matthew Cheetham's** substantive review of patch 5/7 (the `--negotiation-include` implementation) in Stolee's v3 negotiation-control series.

Stolee acknowledges and agrees with every issue Cheetham raised, and his responses are notably direct and unguarded -- he calls the COMMON-flag confusion "a great catch" and admits he violated abstractions in a way that tests also failed to catch. The key takeaways:

**On the COMMON-flag bug** (the most technically significant finding -- Cheetham observed that `find_common()` sets bit 6 but the default negotiator checks bit 2, so deduplication probably fails on v0/v1): Stolee agrees, says the test suite missed this, and proposes an architecturally cleaner fix: add a function pointer to the `fetch_negotiator` struct so the negotiator can be told "I already sent this 'have'" and manage its own deduplication. This is a bigger change than a minimal flag fix, but it respects the abstraction boundary that the current code broke.

**On resolving refs at different layers** (the design inconsistency between `--negotiation-restrict` resolving in the builtin and `--negotiation-include` resolving in the transport layer): Stolee says this is "a really key observation" and plans "a better organization" that unifies the two approaches in the next version.

**On the documentation placeholder mismatch** and the missing test for silent-ignore of unresolvable names: both acknowledged and will be fixed in v4.

What is new here is Stolee's proposed direction for the COMMON-flag fix -- extending the negotiator's public interface rather than papering over the flag confusion. The thread is waiting on that v4, which Stolee commits to here but has not yet posted. No new questions or changes in direction beyond what he already described.
