This is an author response from Derrick Stolee to Matthew Cheetham's substantive review of patch 5/7 in the negotiation-control series.

**Thread position**: This is the author's reply to Cheetham's review of the `--negotiation-include` patch. Stolee has not yet posted v4 of the series; this email indicates how he plans to address the outstanding issues.

Stolee acknowledges and accepts every issue Cheetham raised. On the most technically significant point -- the COMMON flag confusion where the patch sets bit 6 (fetch-pack.c's COMMON) but the negotiator's `get_rev()` checks bit 2 (negotiator/default.c's COMMON), meaning duplicates could occur on protocol v0/v1 -- Stolee calls this "a great catch" and says it reveals a broken abstraction: the consumer of an abstract negotiator is assuming implementation details. His planned fix goes beyond a simple bit correction: he wants to add a function pointer to the negotiator struct itself, a callback like "I chose to send this 'have'", so the negotiator can internally control what it skips without the consumer having to manipulate flags. This is a structural change to the negotiator abstraction.

On the design consistency question (why does `--negotiation-restrict` resolve refs in the builtin layer while `--negotiation-include` passes raw strings to the transport layer?), Stolee agrees that unifying these at the same layer is better and says v4 will reorganize both to resolve at the same place.

On the documentation mismatches (placeholder `<revision>` vs ref-name language), the missing test coverage for silent-ignore behavior of missing refs, and the typo -- all acknowledged and will be fixed.

This email is significant because it shows the scope of the v4 revision still to come: not just the surface fixes already mentioned in the thread summary, but also a structural rework of how `--negotiation-restrict` and `--negotiation-include` resolve refs, and a new abstraction mechanism on the negotiator to properly handle the 'have' deduplication. Cheetham's review has substantially shaped the direction of the v4 work beyond what Stolee had already planned.
