This is Junio C Hamano reviewing patch 3/5 of Harald Nordgren's `git branch --prune-merged` series, and it's a significant review -- Junio is raising fundamental objections to the core design, not just implementation details.

The review is substantive. Junio does not accept the motivating scenario the patch describes. He re-states the scenario as he understands it (fork a topic from `origin/next`, push it back, then prune when the upstream goes away) and calls it nonsense for two concrete reasons: first, `next` is something people fork from, so it is not expected to disappear. Second, if `origin/next` does get deleted, you no longer have any reference point to know whether `hn/topic` was safely integrated -- deleting based on disappearance of the remote-tracking branch is fundamentally unsafe. He also challenges the "safety check" fallback to the remote's default branch when the upstream is gone, calling it not a safety check at all.

Two other technical points: the documentation description of `--prune-merged` references "`branch_get_push` semantics", which Junio reads as a confusing code reference rather than user-facing explanation; and he questions whether the new `no_head_fallback` parameter is needed, suggesting instead that the caller could simply pass `NULL` for `head_rev` to achieve the same effect.

This is a serious setback for the series. Junio is the maintainer, and his objection is to the *logic* of the feature, not its implementation. The series has already been through seven iterations, including a design change in response to Junio's v6 concerns -- but the fundamental question of what "safe" means for this feature has not been settled to his satisfaction. The thread summary had noted the series appeared ready to progress past `seen`, but this review changes that picture entirely. Expect either a major redesign of the safety logic or significant discussion about what the feature should actually check before pruning.
