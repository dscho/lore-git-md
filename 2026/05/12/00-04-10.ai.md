This is a maintainer response from Junio C Hamano in the ongoing `git add -p` word-diff interface design thread. Junio is replying to D. Ben Knoble's detailed implementation proposals.

Junio acknowledges that Knoble's `:` prefix idea makes sense conceptually -- it provides a clear way to exempt certain commands from single-key mode, and he agrees that `:blah` could be used for rarely-used actions with descriptive names. But he then cuts straight to the practical question that has been lurking beneath the entire thread: is the machinery worth building for this one use case? He notes that word-diff is "a good candidate" but says it is "not immediately obvious" whether setting up a general `:` command infrastructure is justified.

This is a pivotal moment in the thread. Junio is effectively asking whether the interface complexity of introducing a `:` prefix mode is proportionate to the benefit of a word-diff toggle. He is not rejecting the idea outright, but he is signaling that the burden of proof is on the proponents to show that word-diff alone (or the set of foreseeable future commands) justifies the new machinery. The email does not address any of the remaining technical review items (marker parsing, line ending handling) -- this is a pure interface-design narrowing down of the remaining options.

The three proposals remain on the table (single-letter `w`, reused `p`/`P` with options, or `:` prefix), but Junio's framing here pushes the thread toward a concrete decision point: either demonstrate that the `:` machinery earns its keep, or fall back to one of the simpler alternatives. The thread is approaching its conclusion on the interface design question, but has not resolved it yet.
