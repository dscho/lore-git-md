This is a brief follow-up from **Taylor Blau** to the 10th patch of his own v3 series (co-authored with Derrick Stolee) that adds `tree:0` filter support to `git pack-objects --path-walk`.

The email consists of a single inline question. Blau points out that the patch treats `/tagged-trees` as a sentinel pseudo-path -- the same pattern already used for `/tagged-blobs` (introduced earlier in the same patch) and `/tags`. He asks why the same treatment was applied to *directly-listed trees* in the presence of `--filter=tree:0`, rather than just to tagged trees through annotated tags. He does not suggest a change, does not flag an error, and does not describe a concrete behavioral problem -- it is a genuine question about the design rationale.

This is a **surface-level** follow-up from one of the two co-authors, directed at the other co-author (the patch is authored by Blau and addressed to the list generally, though Stolee wrote the implementation as the signed-off-by chain shows). It adds no new information, does not advance the review (no other participant has engaged), and does not request or propose any action. It is best understood as Blau thinking out loud about the patch he co-authored while re-reading it.

The question is too narrowly scoped and too minor to change the overall assessment of the series: the `tree:0` patch remains clean, well-motivated, and correctly handled, including the tagged-tree edge case. Blau's curiosity about the directly-listed trees case is likely answered by the fact that directly-listed trees (objects specified on the command line with `--stdin` or `--revs`) are handled separately by the `setup_pending_objects()` function's existing direct-object routing, so they never reach the path-walk's `tree:0` suppression logic -- the sentinel path is only needed for trees discovered *during* the walk via annotated tags.
