This is the third and final patch of a three-patch bugfix series by Tuomas Ahola, completing the v2 rework of a fix for long-standing edge cases in Git's approxidate date parser.

This patch addresses a subtle inconsistency in how "today tea" and "tea today" are handled. The root problem is that the "wrap-to-yesterday" behavior of time-based specials like "noon" and "tea" (which look for the most recent occurrence in the past) interacts poorly with the day-of-month field. The author introduces a clever deferred-adjustment mechanism using negative values of `tm_mday`: setting `tm_mday` to `-1` in `date_now()` and `date_yesterday()`, and `-2` in `date_time()` when a wrap is needed. The `update_tm()` function then interprets these negative values as relative day offsets that are applied only if no explicit day-of-month is later provided. This makes "tea yesterday" produce the sensible result (tea-time of yesterday) instead of going back to the last tea-time and then another day backwards.

The change removes the `now` parameter from `date_time()` entirely, since the function no longer needs it, and marks the corresponding parameters as unused in the callers (`date_midnight`, `date_noon`, `date_tea`). The diff is contained: 31 lines changed in `date.c` and 4 new test cases added to `t/t0006-date.sh` covering "noon today" with a +36-hour offset, "noon yesterday" at -12 hours, and "tea last saturday" at both default and -12 hour offsets.

The approach is clean and well-justified in the commit message, which explains the design rationale and acknowledges that the encoding could support arbitrary negative offsets even though only -1 and -2 are used by this patch. The test additions are sensible, exercising the cross-midnight and cross-week boundary cases. As with the preceding patches in the series, this is clearly a well-crafted contribution that targets a genuine, decades-old set of quirks with minimal intrusion into the codebase. Worth a close look if you work in date parsing or have been affected by inconsistent approxidate behavior.
