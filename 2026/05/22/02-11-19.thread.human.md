This thread is an RFC for a **diff provider interface** that introduces a unified abstraction layer for consulting alternate sources of diff hunks before falling back to Git's built-in xdiff. The series (11 patches, v7) is authored by Michael Montalbo and proposes a repository-owned chain of providers, each queried in turn until one answers or fails, with the built-in xdiff as the terminal provider. The goal is to unify diff and blame integration under a single interface while preserving downstream features like word-diff, color-moved, function context, `--stat`, `git blame`, and `git log -L`. The RFC explicitly seeks design feedback on the framework direction, not a merge of the 11 patches as a single unit.

The series includes two example providers: an **in-process cache** (`diff-hunks` store) and an **external process driver** (`diff.<driver>.process`). The `diff-hunks` store is a non-authoritative cache keyed by `(old_blob, new_blob, xdl_opts)` that records hunks only when the pair's trimmed and untrimmed diffs agree, ensuring compatibility with both blame (zero context) and stat formats (any context). The external process provider is authoritative and answers by object identity alone, enabling tools to provide hunks without reading blob content. The series also introduces a warming mechanism for stat output (`--stat`, `--numstat`, `--shortstat`) to populate the cache during a warming run, and it wires both providers into `git blame` and summary formats. Patch output and `git log -L` remain built-in due to the lack of content-carrying requests in the current protocol.

Junio Hamano previously declared the series "technically complete" after addressing a merge conflict with `mm/line-log-limited-ops` in v6, and no new objections have been raised. However, Phillip Wood's recent review raises foundational questions about the proposal's motivation, terminology, and technical trade-offs. Phillip asks what the interface *unlocks* beyond caching hunk headers—whether it's primarily about performance (e.g., the 1.26–1.9× speedups demonstrated) or extensibility (e.g., enabling tighter integration with external tools like language servers or AI-assisted diffing). He also flags unclear terminology, such as "authoritative" providers and "trim-stable pairs," and raises concerns about cache keying (storing `xdl_opts` directly) and the potential for non-deterministic behavior from authoritative providers. The review is not a rejection but a request for sharper framing to justify the complexity and ensure the design aligns with Git's long-term goals.

The series is ready for design feedback, and the next steps will likely involve revising the documentation to clarify the vision, define key terms, and address the cache's design trade-offs. The feedback suggests the RFC is being taken seriously, but its success hinges on better articulating its strategic value and mitigating risks like non-determinism. If accepted, the work will return as separate series for the interface and providers.
