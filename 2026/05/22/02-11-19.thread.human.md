This thread is exploring a **diff provider interface** to let Git consult external sources for diff hunks before falling back to its built-in xdiff computation. The goal is to improve performance (e.g., caching frequently compared blobs) and extensibility (e.g., integrating language-aware diff tools or AI-assisted code review). The series, now in its seventh iteration, has evolved significantly in response to feedback.

Originally, the RFC proposed a unified "diff provider" abstraction with a repository-owned chain of authoritative and non-authoritative providers, including an in-process cache (`diff-hunks` store) and an external process driver (`diff.<driver>.process`). However, **Phillip Wood’s review** raised foundational questions about the design’s complexity, motivation, and terminology. In the latest email, **Michael Montalbo proposes a major pivot**: abandoning the "diff provider" abstraction in favor of a simpler "cousin" interface to `xdiff-interface.h`. This would focus on enabling external diff tools via pkt-line while relying on xdiff for content rendering, sidestepping the "authority" question (whether Git trusts external tools’ hunks). The `diff-hunks` store is also ejected from the RFC for now, as it complicated the design and raised cache-keying concerns.

Key participants:
- **Michael Montalbo** (author) has restructured the series multiple times, most recently reverting to the v6 framing. He now plans to re-roll the series with a tighter scope: a pkt-line external process provider (`diff.<driver>.process`) that integrates with Git’s diff/blame machinery without being authoritative.
- **Phillip Wood** (reviewer) raised substantive concerns about the RFC’s goals, terminology, and technical trade-offs. His feedback prompted the pivot to a simpler design.
- **Junio C Hamano** (maintainer) previously declared the series "technically complete" and applied a platform-compatibility fix to ensure the external process provider works across all supported platforms.

What’s been decided:
- The "diff provider" abstraction (with its chain of authoritative/non-authoritative providers) is dropped as overly complex.
- The `diff-hunks` store is deferred to a future effort.
- The external process provider will be advisory (not authoritative), with xdiff remaining the final arbiter for content rendering.
- The interface will be simpler, with no provider chain or "authority" resolution.

What’s still open:
- Whether the "cousin" interface to `xdiff-interface.h` is the right abstraction for integrating external diff tools.
- How to handle cache-keying (e.g., versioning or garbage collection) if the `diff-hunks` store returns later.
- Whether the simplified design adequately addresses the original goals (performance and extensibility).

The series is now **focused on the pkt-line external process provider**, with a clearer motivation and reduced complexity. The next iteration will likely return as a smaller, more reviewable series based on v6.
