This is a review response to Miklos Vajna's patch about `git log --follow` behavior with subtree merges. Jeff King (Peff) weighs in with substantive technical analysis after being explicitly asked for review.

Peff agrees with Junio's earlier assessment that `--follow` has fundamental limitations with merge commits, noting that even after the patch it won't do the right thing by following paths independently down both sides of history. He questions whether making the current "broken" behavior slightly more useful is actually an improvement, pointing out that choosing a parent's path arbitrarily (whether first or another parent) will always omit commits from the other side.

The review suggests exploring whether existing history-simplification flags could achieve the desired behavior through TREESAME checks, but notes uncertainty about how this interacts with `--follow`'s path limiting. Peff concludes that without evidence the patch improves more cases than it worsens, he's not convinced it's the right direction.

This is a substantive review that engages deeply with the technical tradeoffs, questioning the fundamental approach rather than just implementation details. Peff's analysis reinforces Junio's earlier concerns while offering additional technical pathways to consider. The review leaves the door open for further experimentation but makes clear the patch in its current form doesn't convincingly address the core limitations of `--follow` with merges.
