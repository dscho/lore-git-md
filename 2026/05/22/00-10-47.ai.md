This is a continuation of the architectural debate about timeout handling in Git's external notes helper mechanism, with Junio C Hamano weighing in on the discussion between Siddh Raman Pant and Johannes Sixt.

Junio acknowledges the fundamental challenge in selecting timeout values that work universally, noting that any fixed duration will inevitably be wrong for some use cases. He suggests two alternative approaches that could make the timeout mechanism more flexible:

1. Allowing the protocol itself to communicate acceptable timeout thresholds from the helper to Git ("this backend is slow, wait at least 3 seconds before timing out")
2. Having the helper explicitly signal when it cannot respond in time, rather than Git unilaterally deciding to terminate the process

Junio's comments position him as a mediator between the two existing positions - he recognizes the need for some timeout mechanism (supporting Pant's robustness argument) but suggests making it more cooperative (aligning with Sixt's protocol-focused view). His observation about protocol design being preferable to unilateral termination hints at a potential middle ground, though he doesn't explicitly endorse either side.

The maintainer's intervention suggests this debate is reaching a point where architectural guidance is needed, though Junio hasn't yet indicated a clear preference between the competing visions. His framing of the problem as fundamentally about protocol design rather than just implementation details may help steer the discussion toward a more general solution that could satisfy both robustness and protocol purity concerns.
