Performance optimization: O(1) tracking for commit-reach algorithms

This is the final patch in Kristofer Karlsson's series optimizing commit-reach.c algorithms (merge-base and ahead-behind calculations). Building on the previous deduplication patch, it introduces an O(1) solution for tracking non-stale commits in the priority queue.

The key change replaces the O(n) queue scanning with a `nonstale_queue` wrapper structure that maintains a pointer to the lowest-priority non-stale commit. This allows the algorithm to terminate early once all remaining commits are stale, reducing the per-iteration check from O(m) to O(1). The implementation carefully handles edge cases around commit priorities and maintains all invariants from the previous version.

Benchmarks on a 3.7M commit monorepo show significant improvements - merge-base calculations are 2x faster overall, with paint_down_to_common() runtime dropping from 50% to just 4% of total execution time. The changes are well-contained within commit-reach.c and maintain backward compatibility with simple cases.

The patch represents the culmination of discussion between Kristofer and Jeff King (who suggested the O(1) approach), addressing all technical concerns raised in earlier reviews. It preserves the performance gains while being mathematically cleaner than previous attempts. The series demonstrates how careful optimization can yield substantial speedups in core Git operations without compromising correctness.
