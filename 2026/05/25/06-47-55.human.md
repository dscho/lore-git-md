This is a technical review from Jeff King (Peff) offering an alternative approach to the commit-reach.c optimization series. He suggests a caching-based solution similar to what was implemented in limit_list() (commit b6e8a3b540) rather than the counter-based approach proposed in the original series.

Peff's approach would cache the most recently found non-stale commit and check it first before falling back to a linear scan. While acknowledging this is still heuristic-based (and thus not truly O(1)), he's curious how it would perform compared to the counter approach, particularly in the large monorepo case where the original series showed 2.5x speedups.

The review is substantive - it engages with the technical tradeoffs between approaches and references prior art in the codebase. Peff requests benchmark comparisons if the author can easily test this alternative implementation, while noting the counter approach may still be preferable for its guaranteed O(1) behavior.

This continues the thread's technical discussion about optimizing commit graph traversals, adding another design option to consider alongside the original counter proposal and Derrick Stolee's wrapper struct suggestion from earlier in the thread.
