This is a technical review from Jeff King (Peff) offering an alternative approach to Kristofer Karlsson's commit-reach.c optimization series. Peff suggests a caching-based solution similar to what was implemented in limit_list() (commit b6e8a3b540) rather than the counter-based approach proposed in the series.

The key idea is to cache the last-seen non-stale commit and check it first before falling back to a full queue search. This maintains the O(n) worst case but improves average case by exploiting locality - commits are often processed in order, making the cached entry likely to still be valid. Peff acknowledges this is more heuristic than the pure O(1) counter approach but requests benchmark comparisons to evaluate the tradeoffs.

The review provides a complete diff implementing this caching strategy for both paint_down_to_common() and ahead_behind(), showing how to invalidate the cache when the cached commit is popped from the queue. While technically sound, Peff notes the solution feels "a bit hacky" compared to the counter approach's conceptual cleanliness.

This represents a substantive review option beyond the two designs already under consideration (direct counter vs Derrick Stolee's wrapper struct). The thread now has three viable technical approaches to solve the O(n) scan problem, with the choice likely coming down to performance measurements and maintainability tradeoffs. Peff's request for benchmark data on the caching approach will help inform that decision.
