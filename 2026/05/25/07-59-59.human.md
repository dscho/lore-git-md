This is a performance optimization follow-up in Kristofer Karlsson's series improving commit-reach.c algorithms. The thread has evolved through several technical approaches to optimize the O(n) linear scan in merge-base and ahead-behind calculations.

Kristofer reports benchmark results comparing Jeff King's suggested caching approach against his original counter-based solution. The measurements on a large monorepo (2.4M commits) show nearly identical performance between the two methods, with both providing roughly 2x speedup over baseline. The caching solution achieves this without introducing new flag bits or counter bookkeeping, making it the cleaner approach.

The email confirms the caching strategy - which maintains an amortized O(1) check by remembering the last-seen non-stale commit - performs as well in practice as the theoretically pure O(1) counter method. Kristofer indicates he'll rewrite the patch series to use Jeff's approach, offering co-authorship given the substantial contribution.

This moves the thread toward consensus, with the caching solution now clearly favored over both the original counter approach and Derrick Stolee's wrapper struct proposal. The performance data validates Jeff's intuition about locality characteristics in commit processing order. With Junio Hamano having previously endorsed decoupling the optimization from broader flag management questions, this likely clears the path for merging the caching improvement while leaving flag infrastructure discussions for future work.
