Feature: v4 cover letter for `fetch.pruneBranches` series with default branch protection.

Harald Nordgren's cover letter for the v4 series seeks additional feedback on the local branch pruning functionality, noting successful personal use in cleaning up repositories. This follows Johannes Sixt's architectural objection to integrating branch pruning with `git fetch`, which has prompted reconsideration of the design direction.

The series remains technically sound with its core safety mechanisms intact:
- Default branch protection (new in v4)
- Reachability checks for unpushed work
- Per-branch configuration via `branch.<name>.pruneOnFetch`
- Bulk deletion optimizations

The author's real-world testing confirms the utility of the feature, having successfully cleaned up branches across multiple repositories. However, the fundamental question raised by Johannes Sixt about whether `git fetch` should modify local branches remains unresolved. This architectural concern carries weight given Sixt's role as a GUI tools maintainer and his perspective on command boundaries.

The cover letter does not propose a resolution to the design crossroads, instead inviting broader feedback. Three potential paths forward remain under consideration:
1. Proceeding with fetch integration despite boundary concerns
2. Removing fetch integration in favor of standalone `git branch` plumbing
3. Developing an entirely new automation mechanism

The series builds on solid technical foundations but now faces a higher-level design decision that will shape its final form. The author's practical experience with the feature provides valuable validation of its utility, though the architectural questions may require deeper discussion before proceeding.
