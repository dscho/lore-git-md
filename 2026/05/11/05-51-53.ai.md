This is an update on the sideband control character sanitization effort, which has been cooking in 'next' since March. Junio Hamano reports that the topic (originally from Johannes Schindelin) is now ready to graduate to 'master', but with a strategic modification to the rollout plan.

The series introduces stricter sanitization of control characters in sideband communications, allowing only ANSI color sequences by default while providing configuration options for more granular control. The key change in this update is that Junio plans to hold off on applying the final patch that would loosen these restrictions until Git 3.0. This gives users of 'master' branch a chance to experience the stricter defaults in practice before making a final decision about whether to keep them permanently or revert to a more permissive approach.

The approach represents a compromise between security (preventing potentially dangerous terminal escape codes) and usability (not breaking legitimate use cases). Junio notes the topic has already seen extended testing in 'next' without reported issues, suggesting the stricter defaults may be workable long-term. However, he leaves open the possibility of reverting to a blacklist-based approach if significant problems emerge during the 'master' testing period.

This continues the project's careful, iterative approach to security-sensitive changes - gathering real-world feedback at each stage before locking in behavior. The sideband sanitization touches remote operations like fetch/push where terminal control sequences might originate from untrusted sources, making the cautious rollout particularly appropriate.
