This is a technical review email from Jeff King (Peff) responding to Taylor Blau's proposed fix for the daemonization locking bug that can cause repository corruption during `git maintenance run --detach`.

Peff analyzes Taylor's proposed `reassign_tempfile_ownership()` approach and identifies a subtle race condition in the implementation. During the fork, there's a window where either both parent and child claim ownership of the tempfiles (potentially one deleting a lock the other process thinks it holds) or neither does (leaving a stale lock behind). Peff walks through the exact sequence of events that could lead to trouble -- a signal hitting during that window after one process has reassigned but before the other has.

While this race is theoretically concerning, Peff notes that the risk is extremely low in practice and that the existing `git gc` code actually uses an even simpler approach (just manually deleting the tempfile before daemonizing). He argues that the drop-and-reacquire strategy for locks, though racy in a different sense, is not actually wrong -- you just need some instance to eventually take the lock and run the background work, and starvation is unlikely in practice.

On the broader question of what to do with tempfiles during daemonization, Peff makes a key observation: daemonize() isn't really about spawning a child process. It's a single flow of execution that happens to change PIDs as an implementation detail. So *all* tempfiles should logically transfer ownership, not just locks. The same concern applies to other atexit handlers -- for instance, if a call to `run_processes_parallel()` were active during daemonization, the parent's atexit handler would prematurely kill those children. This just hasn't come up because nobody does that.

This is a substantive review from one of the project's most respected technical contributors: Peff identifies a real (if unlikely) edge case, provides clear reasoning, and suggests the race is acceptable rather than blocking the approach. The review advances the discussion by validating Taylor's general direction while flagging the atomicity question for consideration.
