The thread addresses a bug in `git maintenance --detach` where the maintenance lockfile could be prematurely removed due to a race condition during daemonization. Currently, when maintenance runs in detached mode, the parent process creates the lock but exits after forking, causing the lock to be released before the background maintenance completes. This could allow concurrent maintenance runs to interfere with each other.

Patrick Steinhardt has proposed a solution involving two new functions: `daemonize_without_exit()` to keep the parent alive temporarily, and `lock_file_reassign_owner()` to safely transfer lock ownership between processes. The fix ensures the lock persists through the entire maintenance operation by having the parent transfer ownership to the child before exiting. The patch includes comprehensive test coverage and has already received positive feedback from core contributors like Jeff King, Taylor Blau, and Derrick Stolee.

The changes are well-contained, touching only the maintenance subsystem's process management in `builtin/gc.c`, with supporting lockfile changes in `lockfile.c/h` and `setup.c/h`. This appears to be a solid fix for a real-world issue that was reported by a Git user.
