This is a review by Jeff King (Peff) weighing in on the implementation choices for the ongoing fix to the `git maintenance run --detach` locking bug.

Peff is responding directly to Patrick Steinhardt's counter-proposal, which suggested a narrower, caller-level fix that would add a `lock_file_reassign_owner()` function and have `git maintenance` explicitly transfer lockfile ownership when daemonizing. Peff argues against that approach, making a clear case that the fix should be baked into `daemonize()` itself rather than left to each caller.

His reasoning is that this is a general class of bug waiting to happen. He points out that `git gc --detach` nearly has the same problem and only avoids it through a fragile workaround (dropping and reacquiring the lock across the fork boundary). Peff's position is that making the fix automatic in `daemonize()` prevents future callers from introducing the same vulnerability, and keeps the function abstract enough that platform-specific daemon implementations (such as the Windows no-op) remain consistent.

This is a short email but it carries weight. Peff is one of the most respected voices on the list for this kind of low-level correctness analysis, and he is directly challenging the narrower-scope approach from Patrick. The thread now has three clear competing proposals on the table (revert, Taylor's comprehensive tempfile fix, Patrick's targeted lockfile fix), and Peff is endorsing the broader, automatic approach. The question is whether Peff's authority on the matter, combined with Jacob Keller's earlier signal that the race condition is tolerable, will shift the consensus toward Taylor's solution or whether Patrick will defend his narrower approach.
