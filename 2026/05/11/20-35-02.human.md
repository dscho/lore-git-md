This is a quick follow-up from Jeff King (Peff) in the ongoing thread about repository corruption caused by `git maintenance run --detach` and broken lock handling in the daemonized child process. Peff is responding to Jacob Keller's earlier message about the race window in Taylor Blau's proposed comprehensive fix (which would transfer ownership of **all** tempfiles from parent to child during `daemonize()`).

Peff addresses the race that he himself identified in his previous review: where a signal kills both parent and child near-simultaneously, potentially causing either double-ownership (with the bad sequence of one process deleting the other's lock) or no-ownership (leaving stale locks behind). He offers a pragmatic tweak: have the parent reassign tempfile ownership to "0" (effectively relinquishing it) *before* calling `fork()`, then reclaim ownership if `fork()` fails. This means the worst case is always "no one cleans up" — stale locks rather than corruption — which is the safer failure mode.

This is a substantive review from Peff — he is not just acknowledging the problem but proposing a concrete improvement to Taylor's implementation. The email contains no new test code or patch, just a clear architectural suggestion that moves the proposal closer to merge-ready by making the race window benign rather than merely tolerable.
