This is a technical discussion reply — Peff responds to Taylor Blau's proposed `reassign_tempfile_ownership()` fix for the daemonization locking bug, and Jacob Keller adds a brief concurrence.

Peff makes three main points. First, he pushes back on Taylor's characterization of the "drop and re-acquire" lock alternative as unworkably racy, noting that `git gc` already does exactly that (deleting the lockfile before daemonizing). The risk is starvation, which Peff argues is self-limiting — the final invoker will eventually win the race. This is worth noting not because Peff wants the alternative, but because it calibrates how bad the problem really is: the proposed fix is about design hygiene, not an absolute correctness requirement.

Second, Peff strongly endorses Taylor's instinct to reassign *all* tempfiles on fork, not just lockfiles. His reasoning is conceptually clean: `daemonize()` isn't spawning a child — there is still a single logical execution flow, and the PID change is just an implementation detail. From that perspective, every tempfile (and even other atexit-handled resources like child processes from `run_processes_parallel()`) should follow the new PID. The reason this hasn't come up before is that Git never leaves other resources running while daemonizing.

Third, Peff identifies a real race in Taylor's implementation: the parent gives up ownership while the child takes it, creating a window where both could claim ownership (risking deleting a lock out from under a third process) or neither could (leaving a stale lock). He works through the failure modes honestly: the double-claim case requires an elaborate sequence of signals and a coincident third process to cause harm, while the no-claim case is no worse than a `kill -9`. He offers no easy fix — synchronization between parent and child with pipes would be ugly — and seems to conclude the race is unlikely enough to accept.
