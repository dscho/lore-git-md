This is a technical reply from Jeff King continuing the architectural debate about how to fix the tempfile-ownership race during daemonized `git maintenance run --detach`. It is part of the longer thread about repository corruption from concurrent background maintenance, where the core question is whether tempfile ownership should be transferred comprehensively in `daemonize()` itself or handled as a narrower lockfile-only fix.

Peff picks up Jacob Keller's acknowledgment that the race between parent and child both claiming (or neither claiming) tempfile ownership is probably tolerable, and proposes a refinement to make the worst case less bad. He suggests that the parent could reassign ownership to "0" (i.e., no owner) before calling `fork()`, and then reclaim ownership if the fork fails. This means the problematic outcome would never be a double-delete; the only bad case is that nobody cleans up the lock if the child is killed before taking control, which is both unlikely and the least harmful outcome from a correctness standpoint.

This is a narrow, practical suggestion within an already-agreed direction. Peff is not reopening the design debate; he is offering a way to make the implementation simpler and safer without requiring complex synchronization between parent and child. The email is short and focused, aimed at the implementer (Taylor Blau). It reflects the kind of incremental engineering refinement that Peff is known for: take a recognized problem, propose a concrete step to reduce its severity, and move on. No new analysis, no change of position -- just a cleaner atomization of the ownership handoff.
