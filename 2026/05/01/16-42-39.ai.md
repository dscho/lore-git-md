This is a continuation of the git-gui bugfix thread about handling bare repositories and missing worktrees. Mark Levedahl responds to Johannes Sixt's (Hannes) proposed middle-ground approach with a concerning edge case he discovered.

Mark identifies a particularly problematic scenario where git-gui could inadvertently allow committing files directly into the gitdir (like `refs/heads/master` as a file) when core.bare=false but no valid worktree exists. He demonstrates this can happen with Hannes' suggested approach of falling back to parent directories when in a .git subdirectory.

The key technical insight is that git-gui's read/write mode should only run when `--is-inside-work-tree` is true. For all other cases (including bare repos and gitdirs), Mark argues git-gui should either:
1. Abort immediately with a clear error (his preferred solution), or
2. Present an interactive dialog showing available worktrees to switch to

Mark strongly opposes silently switching directories without user confirmation, as this could mask user errors and lead to unintended operations. His position aligns with the "strict correctness" approach from earlier in the thread, prioritizing safety over backward compatibility with launching from .git directories.

This reply adds weight to the correctness-focused side of the debate by demonstrating concrete data corruption risks in Hannes' more permissive proposal. The thread now has clear technical arguments on both sides, with Mark's edge case showing the potential dangers of maintaining backward compatibility in this scenario. The decision likely requires maintainer input to resolve the tradeoff between safety and compatibility.
