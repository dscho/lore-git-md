Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series, now in its 19th iteration, has reached a critical juncture. The feature promises safe automated cleanup of merged local branches with robust filtering and safety controls—per-branch opt-out, dry-run previews, and stacked-branch protection to prevent breaking dependency chains. After months of refinement, the series was poised for merging, with all prior design questions resolved and only a follow-up to extend protection to `git branch -d` remaining.

However, Junio C Hamano’s latest review has surfaced a fundamental flaw in the stacked-branch protection logic. The algorithm’s behavior hinges on the alphabetical order of branch names rather than their logical dependencies, leading to inconsistent outcomes for equivalent branch structures. For example, a chain like `a_tip→b_mid→c_lower` might yield different results than `tip→mid→lower`, even though the relationships are identical. This undermines the safety guarantees central to the feature, making it a blocking issue. The fix will likely require reworking the traversal logic to process branches in dependency order, such as via topological sorting.

Phillip Wood and Harald Nordgren had previously collaborated to finalize the "abort-and-clear" protection design—sparing branches used as upstreams for unmerged branches while clearing stale configs for kept branches. Junio endorsed this approach but now highlights that the implementation fails to deliver on its promise. The series remains technically complete, with all other features (per-branch opt-out, dry-run, repeatable upstream selectors) fully implemented and tested, but this correctness issue must be addressed before merging. The next steps will likely involve a targeted revision to the `spare_stacked_bases()` function and its interaction with the deletion pipeline.
