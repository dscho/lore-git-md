Harald Nordgren's series to automate local branch cleanup when upstream branches are deleted has reached its second version, incorporating maintainer feedback into a well-structured implementation. The core functionality is now complete, with new `git branch` plumbing commands (`--forked` and `--prune-merged`) that safely identify and remove branches whose upstreams are gone, while preserving unpushed work and checked-out branches across all worktrees. The series adds a convenient `fetch --prune-merged` integration and per-branch opt-out configuration via `branch.<name>.pruneMerged`.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk has chimed in with a minor documentation suggestion for the first patch, proposing to standardize on "remote-tracking branches" terminology rather than "remote-tracking refs" in the `--forked` command's documentation. This is purely a wording consistency fix that doesn't affect the functionality. The series appears ready for merging once this small documentation update is addressed, having already undergone substantial architectural review from Junio Hamano. The implementation provides both low-level plumbing and user-friendly porcelain while maintaining all necessary safety checks.
