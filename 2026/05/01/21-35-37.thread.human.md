Harald Nordgren's `git branch --prune-merged` series has reached its final form after 12 iterations, now including all planned safety features for automated local branch cleanup. The series introduces a new `--prune-merged` option that safely deletes branches whose changes are fully merged into their upstream, with multiple layers of protection: it warns rather than fails on unmerged branches during bulk operations, supports configurable branch exemptions via `branch.<name>.pruneMerged`, and now in the final patch adds a `--dry-run` mode to preview deletions.

The implementation preserves all existing safety checks from `git branch -d` while adding new capabilities. It compares `@{push}` and `@{upstream}` references, protects checked-out and trunk branches, and skips branches with unresolvable upstreams. The new dry-run option shows exactly what would be deleted, with output designed to be machine-parseable. Junio Hamano has already confirmed the series' technical readiness in v11, and Phillip Wood's earlier safety concerns have been addressed. With all major feedback incorporated over a year of development, this appears to be the last iteration before potential merging.
