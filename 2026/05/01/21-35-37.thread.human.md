Harald Nordgren's `git branch --delete-merged` series is now in its 23rd iteration and is effectively complete—except for two critical correctness issues raised by Phillip Wood that are blocking its merge. The series introduces a new `--delete-merged` subcommand for safe, automated cleanup of local branches, with robust filtering, safety controls, and a `--dry-run` preview mode. Junio C Hamano has already signaled the series is ready for merging once these final issues are resolved.

The core command, implemented in patch 5/7, deletes local branches merged into their upstream (specified via repeatable `--delete-merged` arguments like `--delete-merged origin/main --delete-merged origin/next`) while preserving branches used as upstreams for unmerged work. It includes multiple safety mechanisms: per-branch opt-out via `branch.<name>.deleteMerged=false`, protection for checked-out branches, and order-independent stacked-branch protection that prevents deletion of branches transitively required by unmerged work. The `--dry-run` mode (patch 7/7) provides a reliable preview without modifying refs or config.

The two remaining issues are technical and non-negotiable for safety. First, the `branch_pushes_to_upstream()` function incorrectly checks whether a branch’s *fetch* refspec maps to its upstream, rather than simulating the push operation. This risks deleting branches that should be preserved (e.g., `main` tracking `origin/main`) or keeping branches that should be deleted. Second, a test for stacked-branch protection expects the upstream config of a kept merged branch to remain intact, but the design goal is to clear stale configs for kept branches whose own upstream is being deleted. Harald acknowledges both issues and plans to fix them, though the push-detection rewrite will require re-adding some previously removed logic.

Phillip Wood’s review also prompted minor improvements to documentation and test style, which Harald has agreed to address. The rest of the series—including the flags-based API refactoring (patches 2-4/7), per-branch opt-out (patch 6/7), and `--dry-run` support—is finalized and uncontroversial. Once the two blocking issues are resolved, the series is poised to land, with a follow-up planned to extend stacked-branch protection to `git branch -d` for consistency.
