Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series, now at v19, is a long-running effort to add safe, automated local branch cleanup with fine-grained controls. The series includes the core `--delete-merged` command, a `--forked` filter for branch selection, per-branch opt-out via `branch.<name>.deleteMerged=false`, `--dry-run` preview, and stacked-branch protection to prevent breaking dependency chains. All planned features are implemented, and the series was recently unblocked by a fix for a correctness issue in the stacked-branch protection logic, which now ensures order-independent results.

The key participants are Harald Nordgren, who authored the series and addressed all feedback, and Phillip Wood, whose high-weight reviews shaped the design—particularly the stacked-branch protection mechanism (abort-and-clear) and the flags-based deletion API. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has been closely involved in the stacked-branch protection discussion. While he endorsed the design philosophy, he identified a blocking order-dependency bug in the implementation, which Harald fixed. However, Junio remains skeptical about the fix’s completeness, suggesting it may not handle all edge cases in complex dependency graphs. The series is now paused pending his further review of the fix.

The implementation is feature-complete, with all safety checks (checked-out branches, missing upstreams, push divergence, per-branch opt-out, and stacked-branch protection) and test coverage in place. The `--dry-run` mode was fixed to avoid modifying config, and the `--forked` filter now correctly distinguishes true forks from long-lived branches. Open questions are limited to the stacked-branch protection logic’s robustness; if Junio accepts the fix, the series is ready to merge. A follow-up to extend the protection to `git branch -d` for consistency has been agreed upon but won’t block the current work.
