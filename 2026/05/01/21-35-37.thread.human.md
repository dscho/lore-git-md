Harald Nordgren's `git branch --delete-merged` series, now at v19, is a step closer to landing after a critical bugfix addressed the blocking correctness issue in its stacked-branch protection logic. The series introduces safe automated local branch cleanup with granular controls—per-branch opt-out, `--dry-run` preview, and the `--forked` filter—while ensuring branches used as upstreams for unmerged work remain protected.

The latest patch fixes a flaw Junio C Hamano identified: the protection algorithm previously depended on the alphabetical order of branch names, producing inconsistent results for logically identical dependency chains (e.g., `a_tip→b_mid→c_lower` vs. `tip→mid→lower`). Harald's minimal fix ensures branches spared early in the traversal remain spared regardless of naming, resolving the order dependency. This unblocks the series, though Junio's review will confirm whether the fix fully addresses the concern. All other features—including per-branch opt-out via `branch.<name>.deleteMerged=false`, repeatable `--delete-merged` arguments, and the `--forked` filter—remain unchanged and implementation-complete.

Phillip Wood's earlier reviews shaped the series' design, particularly the refined "abort-and-clear" protection (which now fails to delete upstream-dependent branches while cleaning up stale configs) and the flags-based deletion API. His feedback on test improvements and edge cases has been incorporated, and he confirmed the series is otherwise ready for merging once the correctness issue is resolved. Junio had also requested extending this protection to `git branch -d` for consistency; Harald agreed to implement this as a follow-up after the series lands. The only remaining open question is whether the fix satisfies Junio's principle of deterministic behavior for identical branch relationships.
