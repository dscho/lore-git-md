This thread covers the final version (v25) of Harald Nordgren's `git branch --delete-merged` series, which has now been **merged to 'next'** and is undergoing integration testing. The series introduces a safe, automated way to clean up local branches with comprehensive filtering and safety controls.

The core feature is the `--delete-merged` subcommand, which deletes local branches merged into their upstream while preserving branches used as upstreams for unmerged work. Key capabilities include order-independent stacked-branch protection (preventing deletion of branches needed by others), per-branch opt-out via `branch.<name>.deleteMerged=false`, `--dry-run` preview, and the foundational `--forked` filter. The v25 update made no functional changes—it only wrapped long test command lines for readability.

The series resolved two critical correctness issues in v24: (1) a push-detection logic flaw that misidentified branches as safe to delete when `remote.pushDefault` pointed to a fork, and (2) a stacked-branch protection test discrepancy fixed by simplifying the logic to clear upstream configs for kept branches. Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood both confirmed the series is ready for merging after these fixes. Phillip Wood's reviews were particularly influential, shaping the final design and identifying the blocking issues that required resolution.

The implementation is complete, with all planned features and safety checks in place. Follow-up work includes extending stacked-branch protection to `git branch -d` for consistency (agreed by Harald but deferred until after the series lands) and a future refinement to the `--forked` filter heuristic. The series touches `builtin/branch.c`, `ref-filter.c`, documentation, and tests, and is well-tested for edge cases like self-referential upstreams and missing upstreams. Junio is expected to graduate it to `master` in the current cycle.
