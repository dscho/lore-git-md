Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series, now at v17, aims to provide safe automated cleanup of local branches with fine-grained controls like per-branch opt-out, `--dry-run` preview, and protection for branches used as upstreams. The series has been feature-complete and ready for final review—until Phillip Wood’s latest feedback uncovered a critical flaw in the stacked-branch protection mechanism.

The core issue is that v17’s upstream config clearing logic does the opposite of what was intended: it *preserves* all upstream configurations instead of clearing the upstreams of merged branches that are depended on by unmerged branches. This breaks the safety guarantee that prevents stale references. Phillip also pointed out an inefficiency in how the patch builds a separate list of upstream branches, rather than filtering candidates directly during the branch iteration. The fix will require revisiting the deletion candidate filtering and upstream-clearing logic, but the design itself—clearing rather than recursively retargeting upstreams—remains uncontested. Junio C Hamano has not objected to this approach, and Harald has already incorporated all prior feedback, including Phillip’s earlier proposal for upstream clearing. The series is otherwise mature, with all other safety mechanisms (opt-out, `--dry-run`, and push divergence checks) fully implemented and tested. A v18 addressing these issues is likely the final step before graduation to `next`.
