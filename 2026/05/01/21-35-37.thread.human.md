The `git branch --delete-merged` series, now in its 16th iteration, is nearing completion after resolving its last major design hurdle: how to handle branches that depend on others (e.g., `feature2` stacking on `feature1`). Harald Nordgren’s latest update focuses on the practicalities of *retargeting*—automatically updating the upstream of dependent branches when a merged branch is deleted. For example, if `feature1` is deleted, `feature2`’s upstream could be retargeted to `origin/master` (the branch that merged `feature1`). This approach avoids breaking workflows like `@{u}` references or implicit `git rebase` operations, which would otherwise fail if the upstream branch vanished.

The discussion has surfaced two edge cases: *recursion* (chains like `b1→b2→b3`) and *self-references* (a branch pointing to itself as its upstream). Harald suggests handling these pragmatically—warnings instead of errors, and unsetting the upstream for self-references—since a missing upstream isn’t catastrophic. The alternative, Junio’s initial preference for aborting with a clear error, remains on the table, but the consensus leans toward retargeting with safeguards. The next iteration (v17) will implement this logic, likely with a flag like `--retarget-upstreams` to control the behavior.

The series is otherwise feature-complete, with robust safety mechanisms (checked-out branches, push divergence checks, per-branch opt-out via config, and `--dry-run` previews) and thorough test coverage. Phillip Wood and Junio have both signed off on the stacked-branch protection design, leaving only the retargeting implementation as the final piece.
