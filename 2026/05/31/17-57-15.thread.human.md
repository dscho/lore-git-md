This thread is about a performance optimization for Git's priority queue implementation, specifically targeting the `prio_queue_get()` operation. Kristofer Karlsson (Spotify) proposed a two-part series: a refactoring patch to extract `sift_up()` logic, followed by a cascade-down optimization for unfused `prio_queue_get()` calls. The optimization was theoretically sound, showing a ~2% end-to-end improvement on large repositories and no regressions in exhaustive testing by René Scharfe.

However, the latest findings from René reveal a puzzling 1% slowdown on Apple M1/M5 hardware despite the optimization reducing the number of comparisons by 14–17%. Benchmarks on Git's and Linux's repositories show the cascade-down approach increases wall-clock time, even though the algorithm is working as intended. The discrepancy appears hardware-specific, with no slowdown observed on a Ryzen laptop. René's instrumentation confirms the optimization reduces comparisons (e.g., from 70M to 60M for Git's repo), but the extra struct assignments in `cascade_down()` may be causing microarchitectural penalties on Apple silicon.

The series had reached technical consensus, with maintainer Junio Hamano previously approving the analysis quality and merging the related API simplification series. The decision now hinges on whether the marginal performance gain (or loss, depending on hardware) justifies the 20-line code addition. Kristofer has acknowledged the tradeoff, leaving the final call to Junio. René, while still endorsing the patch's technical soundness, has uncovered this hardware-specific performance quirk that complicates the cost/benefit assessment.
