This thread began as a performance optimization series from Kristofer Karlsson (Spotify) aiming to speed up Git's `prio_queue_get()` operation using a cascade-down algorithm. The series consisted of two patches: a refactoring to extract `sift_up()` and the core optimization itself. While the optimization was technically sound—reducing comparison counts by 14–17% and showing a ~2% end-to-end improvement on some hardware—it introduced a puzzling 1% slowdown on Apple silicon (M1/M5), despite the algorithmic improvements. After follow-up testing by René Scharfe confirmed the regression, Kristofer withdrew the optimization patch, concluding that the marginal gains didn’t justify the hardware-specific penalty.

The discussion revealed how micro-optimizations can have counterintuitive tradeoffs across CPU architectures, even when correctness is preserved. René Scharfe, who initially endorsed the patch, confirmed the slowdown was likely due to microarchitectural factors (e.g., increased memory traffic) rather than an algorithmic flaw. The first patch (the refactoring) remains uncontroversial and may still be merged, but the optimization itself is now abandoned. No further action is expected unless future changes to the priority queue’s usage patterns (e.g., more expensive comparators) revive interest in revisiting the approach. The thread underscores the challenges of performance tuning in a cross-platform project like Git.
