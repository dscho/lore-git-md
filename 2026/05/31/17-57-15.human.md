Performance optimization for the priority queue implementation. Kristofer Karlsson proposes replacing the standard sift-down algorithm in `prio_queue_get()` with a more efficient cascade-down approach that reduces both comparisons and data movement.

The key insight is that the current implementation performs unnecessary work by first moving the last element to the root and then sifting it down (requiring 2d comparisons + 3d copies for depth d). The new approach promotes smaller children upward while letting the vacancy sink to a leaf, then does a final placement (reducing to d comparisons + d copies). Benchmarks show 9-23% speedups for common cases (ascending keys) and 1-4% improvements even in worst-case scenarios.

The patch also simplifies `prio_queue_replace()` to use get+put operations since the cascade approach no longer cleanly shares the sift-down logic. The author notes this shouldn't impact performance as replace operations aren't in hot paths. Real-world testing on a 2.5M-commit monorepo showed the sift operation's runtime share dropping from 8.2% to 0.4%.

The change is small (12 lines net) and well-tested, with no regressions observed. While the impact varies by repository size and DAG width (more noticeable in wide DAGs with deeper queues), the optimization is clean and provides measurable benefits where it matters most.
