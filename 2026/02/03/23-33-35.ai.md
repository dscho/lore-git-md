This is a follow-up discussion in the repository statistics feature thread, where Junio Hamano responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's question about why the Git project stopped using octopus merges (merges with 3+ parents). 

Junio explains that octopus merges lost their appeal because they complicate bisection and were more of a novelty when gitk was new. His response reinforces the earlier skepticism about tracking "most parents" as a useful statistic in Justin Tobler's proposed `git repo` command. The comment aligns with the emerging consensus that metrics like "most children" would be more operationally relevant than tracking octopus merges.

This exchange provides important historical perspective that further supports the likely removal of the octopus merge tracking from the feature in its next iteration. Junio's stance as maintainer carries significant weight in this design decision. The thread continues to demonstrate how the feature is being refined through community feedback, with technical considerations (bisection impact) outweighing what might seem like interesting statistics at first glance.
