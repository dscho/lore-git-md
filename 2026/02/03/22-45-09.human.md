This is Junio Hamano's review feedback on patch 2/5 of Justin Tobler's series adding repository statistics to `git repo`. The patch introduces tracking of largest individual objects by type (commit, tree, blob, tag).

Junio questions whether comparing sizes across object types is meaningful, since different types serve different purposes. He notes that while total sizes might be equal across types, the distribution matters more - a few large blobs have different implications than many small ones. He suggests the more useful insight would be identifying "spiky" distributions where a few outliers dominate a type's footprint.

The feedback doesn't reject the approach but probes the design rationale, asking whether showing largest-per-type actually helps users understand their storage profile. This continues Junio's pattern from reviewing patch 1 of focusing on how the statistics are organized and presented. The exchange remains constructive, with Junio acknowledging there may be valid use cases not immediately obvious from the patch alone.
