This thread began with a bug report from brian m. carlson about `git fsck` entering an infinite loop when verifying multiple packfiles, caused by unsafe updates to the MRU (Most Recently Used) list during iteration. The initial fix introduced a `skip_mru_updates` flag to prevent the immediate issue, which Junio approved for inclusion. However, deeper architectural concerns emerged during review, particularly from Jeff King (Peff) and Patrick Steinhardt, about the fragility of the MRU design and potential verification inaccuracies when duplicate objects exist across packs.

The discussion has now reached a turning point. Junio has weighed in to endorse Jeff King's position that the current flag-based approach is insufficient, favoring instead a more robust solution that maintains a stable list for iteration separate from the MRU. This acknowledges that the flag solution requires careful auditing of all iteration points and loses the performance benefits of MRU ordering during normal operations. Patrick Steinhardt has contributed additional patches addressing verification correctness, revealing that large objects follow different verification paths that trigger MRU updates, while small objects bypass them entirely.

The thread has evolved from a straightforward bugfix to a broader examination of pack iteration semantics, with implications for the ongoing object database (ODB) abstraction work. While the immediate infinite loop is resolved, the maintainer's position suggests the final solution will involve restructuring how `git fsck` traverses packs rather than just patching the current behavior. The discussion remains active on both the specific implementation approach (separate list vs enhanced iteration semantics) and the longer-term integration with Patrick's ODB work.
