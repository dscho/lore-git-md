This is a technical discussion about improving `git am`'s patch parsing security, specifically address the long-standing issue of accidentally applying diffs embedded in commit messages. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Jacob Keller's proposal for a new unambiguous boundary marker by suggesting alternative approaches.

Peff identifies two potential paths forward. First, he questions whether Keller's proposed marker would be too visually intrusive if made sufficiently unique to avoid false positives, suggesting instead that `git format-patch` could apply reversible quoting to problematic commit message content (similar to email's `>From` convention). Second, he proposes a simpler receiver-side solution: a `--strict` mode for `git am` that would enforce the expectation of a clean `---` separator when processing Git-generated patches.

The discussion remains focused on technical trade-offs between backwards compatibility, visual cleanliness in patch emails, and security against accidental diff application. Peff's suggestions aim to maintain existing workflows while reducing the risk surface, though he acknowledges neither approach is foolproof.
