This is a technical discussion about improving `git am`'s patch parsing security, specifically addressing the long-standing issue of accidentally applying diffs embedded in commit messages. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Jacob Keller's proposal for a new unambiguous boundary marker in patch emails.

Peff expresses concerns about the aesthetics and practicality of Keller's proposed marker, suggesting it might be too ugly if it appears in every email. He proposes an alternative approach where `format-patch` could quote problematic lines (like "---" and "diff") in commit messages when they might cause ambiguity, similar to how email clients quote "From" lines. This would maintain backward compatibility while preventing misparsing.

He also suggests a simpler receiver-side solution: a `--strict` mode for `git am` that would enforce looking for the "---" separator, which would help prevent the worst cases of embedded diffs being accidentally applied. This wouldn't be foolproof but would be easier to implement.

The discussion shows the tension between security needs and backward compatibility in Git's patch processing. Peff's suggestions aim to find a middle ground that improves security without breaking existing workflows or introducing overly complex solutions. His input carries weight given his deep knowledge of Git's internals and security considerations.
