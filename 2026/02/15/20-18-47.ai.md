This is the final patch in a 3-patch series fixing issues in `contrib/subtree`'s split operation history handling. The series addresses a problematic optimization that incorrectly excluded commits from split history, where previous fixes introduced regressions.

The key change in this patch is the complete removal of the `should_ignore_subtree_split_commit` function, which was attempting to optimize subtree splits by ignoring commits from other subtrees. The author explains this optimization was fundamentally flawed because it altered the split history in ways that violated API contracts around commit hashes. The patch demonstrates that merge commits in particular require full history processing to maintain correctness.

The removal is accompanied by expanded test coverage in t7900-subtree.sh, including a new test case that verifies proper handling of rebased subtree commits. The test suite now explicitly tracks commit counts and branch relationships to ensure history integrity. The patch builds on prior work referenced in the commit message (commits 98ba49ccc2, 83f9dad7d6, and 28a7e27cff) and fixes issues reported by George and Christian Heusel.

This appears to be a well-justified change that prioritizes correctness over optimization. The extensive test additions (68 lines added vs 50 removed) provide confidence in the fix. The patch is part of an ongoing effort to stabilize subtree operations, though as the third in a series, it would need review of the earlier patches for full context.
