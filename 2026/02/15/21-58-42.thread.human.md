This thread examines a proposal to address privacy concerns in Git's push certificate mechanism, where local filesystem paths can leak into certificates during local repository pushes. Lorenz Leutgeb originally identified this issue, noting that while URLs are sanitized, local paths remain exposed in the "pushee" header. The initial proposal suggested adding `--signed-pushee` and `--no-signed-pushee` options to control this behavior, with open questions about extending these to `git push` and potential impacts on `pre-receive` hooks.

Junio Hamano has now weighed in with critical security considerations, shifting the discussion's focus. He questions whether modifying the pushee header could undermine the certificate's core purpose of binding ref updates to specific repositories. Junio highlights two risks: replay attacks if certificates become repository-agnostic, and potential middleman scenarios if pushee substitution is allowed. While acknowledging the privacy concern, his analysis suggests the current behavior may be intentionally security-conscious. The thread now faces a deeper design question about balancing privacy against the security guarantees push certificates provide, with no clear resolution yet. Key participants are Lorenz as the proposer and Junio as the maintainer raising security objections.
