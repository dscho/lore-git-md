Bugfix for `git subtree` regression affecting squashed commits. This is a follow-up to Colin Stagner's earlier attempt to fix a regression in subtree operations (introduced in Git 2.52.0) that broke handling of squashed subtree histories. The new approach completely removes the problematic "ignore other splits" optimization that was causing incorrect behavior, rather than trying to fix it.

Colin reports that his initial fix introduced other regressions and had to be reverted. The new patch series takes a more radical approach by eliminating the optimization entirely, which not only fixes the functional issue but also provides a significant performance improvement (3x faster than the pre-regression version). The optimization was originally intended to speed up `split --rejoin` operations but appears to have been making incorrect assumptions about commit ancestry.

The patch has been tested on real-world repositories including the Athena Framework monorepo that originally surfaced the issue. Colin is seeking additional testing from George Dietrich to verify the fix works across all common subtree split scenarios. Two separate patches are mentioned - one for the main regression fix and another addressing a "recursion depth exceeded" issue on Debian/Ubuntu systems.

This appears to be a well-considered solution to a tricky regression, with the author demonstrating good judgment in abandoning an incremental fix in favor of a more robust architectural change. The performance improvements suggest the problematic optimization was not just incorrect but also a bottleneck. The patch is now in the final verification stage before potential merging.
