This thread addresses a series of fixes for Git's `contrib/subtree` component, specifically targeting issues with how the `subtree split` operation handles commit history. The problem stems from an optimization (introduced in commit 98ba49ccc2) that attempted to exclude commits from other subtrees during splits, but proved fundamentally flawed - it broke API contracts around commit hashes, particularly for merge commits.

Colin Stagner has sent a 3-patch series that takes the robust approach of completely removing the problematic optimization rather than trying to fix it. The final patch, which just arrived, eliminates the `should_ignore_subtree_split_commit` function entirely. The series includes extensive test coverage in t7900-subtree.sh, adding over 200 lines of tests that verify commit counts and branch relationships to ensure history integrity. A new test case specifically checks handling of rebased subtree commits.

The changes build on prior work (commits 98ba49ccc2, 83f9dad7d6, and 28a7e27cff) and address issues from George and Christian Heusel. While the optimization removal might impact performance, the tradeoff for correctness appears justified. The series is now complete and awaits review from subtree maintainers, particularly D. Ben Knoble, who hasn't yet commented. The thorough test additions should give reviewers assurance about the change's safety.
