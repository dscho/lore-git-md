Junio Hamano raises important security considerations regarding the proposed push certificate changes. This is a thoughtful response to Lorenz Leutgeb's privacy concerns about local filesystem paths appearing in push certificates.

The maintainer questions the fundamental purpose of push certificates, emphasizing they exist to bind a ref update to a specific repository ("pushee"). He identifies two security implications of modifying this mechanism: (1) omitting the pushee entirely could enable replay attacks by making certificates repository-agnostic, and (2) allowing pushee substitution might create a middleman scenario where certificates could be forwarded to different repositories.

Junio provides concrete examples where repository context matters - distinguishing between development and maintenance branches - to illustrate why a universal certificate (without pushee) would be problematic. He acknowledges the middleman forwarding case might be workable from a security perspective, as the certificate integrity would still be verifiable through cryptographic signatures and object chains.

The response doesn't outright reject the proposal but challenges its underlying assumptions, suggesting the current behavior may be intentionally security-conscious rather than an oversight. This moves the discussion toward deeper design considerations about what push certificates should certify, beyond just the privacy aspects. The analysis indicates any solution will need to carefully balance privacy against the certificate's security guarantees.
