This is a follow-up discussion about the `git fsck` packfile verification bugfix, focusing on the MRU list implementation details. Jeff King (Peff) responds to brian m. carlson's patch that fixed an infinite loop during pack verification by adding a `skip_mru_updates` flag.

Peff acknowledges the fix is correct for the immediate issue but raises deeper concerns about the MRU list design introduced in commit c31bad4f7d. He notes the current solution requires callers to explicitly opt out of MRU updates, which seems fragile and inefficient - particularly for cases like `for_each_packed_object()` where we want both stable iteration and MRU benefits for subsequent accesses.

The email suggests two potential improvements: either maintaining separate stable and MRU lists (reverting part of the earlier unification), or providing a dedicated stable iteration interface that doesn't require manual flag-setting. Peff highlights the tension between iteration stability and MRU efficiency, pointing out that the current approach loses optimization opportunities during pack traversal.

This moves the discussion beyond the immediate bugfix to consider broader architectural implications of the MRU handling. The thread remains technically focused, with Peff's comments reflecting his deep familiarity with Git's object storage layer and performance characteristics. No new code changes are proposed here - this is purely design feedback that may influence future work in this area.
