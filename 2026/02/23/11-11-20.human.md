This is a review response from Jeff King (Peff) on the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt's series fixing pack verification issues with large objects. The patch itself replaces `odb_read_stream_open()` with direct packfile access in `pack-check.c` to prevent MRU-triggered infinite loops during verification.

Peff praises the simplicity of the final change, noting how the preparatory refactoring in earlier patches made this fix straightforward. He then suggests improving the test case to be more specific - rather than just verifying fsck doesn't hang, it should demonstrate that corruption in one pack is properly detected when the same object exists in multiple packs. 

The key insight is that the current test might pass even without the fix due to pack ordering, whereas a better test would use two packs with the same object (one corrupt, one good) to verify fsck correctly identifies the corruption regardless of MRU ordering. This would more directly validate the fix's behavior rather than just its absence of failure.

The exchange shows Peff's characteristic attention to test quality - he's not just rubber-stamping the patch, but pushing for a test that will better protect against future regressions. His suggestion builds on Patrick's work while maintaining the collaborative tone typical of Git mailing list reviews.
