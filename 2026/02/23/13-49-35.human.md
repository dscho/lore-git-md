This is a technical discussion about arithmetic overflow safety in the context of Taylor Blau's MIDX compaction series. Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on the design of overflow-checking helpers for unsigned 32-bit arithmetic operations.

The exchange focuses on whether to use pointer-based overflow checking (like the existing st_*() helpers) or direct value returns. Peff explains his original rationale for the pointer-based st_*() interface - it made mass conversions easier during the initial adoption, though he acknowledges the design is suboptimal. He argues that for u32 operations (unlike size_t), we should design a cleaner interface since these are new helpers and we're not constrained by existing usage.

Peff notes that while size_t overflows are rare on 64-bit systems (making the clunky st_*() interface tolerable), u32 overflows could plausibly occur with very large repositories, so the helpers should be designed carefully from the start. The discussion remains technical and constructive, with Peff and Taylor considering tradeoffs between safety, ergonomics, and implementation constraints.
