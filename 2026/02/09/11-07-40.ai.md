This is a security discussion thread about `git am`'s vulnerability to embedded diffs in commit messages, with a focus on distribution workflows. Matthias Beyer responds to Phillip Wood's concerns about the scope of the issue, providing real-world context from NixOS's patch application practices.

Beyer confirms that distributions frequently apply upstream patches (NixOS alone has ~2800 instances) and notes that even experienced Git users may not realize commit message diffs can be accidentally applied. He agrees with Phillip Wood's assessment that adding warnings to `git commit` would be ineffective, as most users wouldn't enable them. The discussion highlights the tension between security hardening and practical workflow realities, particularly for distribution maintainers who often use `patch` rather than `git am`.

Key technical points: the issue affects both email and direct patch application workflows, distributions are heavy users of patch application, and even advanced users may not be aware of the diff-parsing hazard. The thread continues to balance security improvements against usability concerns, with documentation warnings (already queued by Junio) emerging as the most viable immediate solution.
