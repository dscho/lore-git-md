This is a security-focused patch discussion about handling separator lines ("---") in commit messages that could be misinterpreted by `git am`. Phillip Wood responds to concerns raised by Jeff King and Junio Hamano about legitimate uses of "---" in commit messages that would be caught by his proposed commit-msg hook.

The thread has established that some developers (including Junio and Peff) occasionally use "---" in commit messages as a workflow convenience, though this is rare. Phillip acknowledges this and considers alternatives: making the check configurable (as Kristoffer Haugsbakk suggested), adding a special comment to bypass the check, or simply dropping this part of the security hardening. He notes that unlike embedded diffs (which could apply unwanted patches), separator lines only risk truncating the message.

The discussion reveals tension between security hardening and preserving legitimate (if uncommon) workflows. Phillip seems inclined toward practicality - either finding a lightweight bypass mechanism or focusing on the more critical diff-detection parts of the security improvements. The exchange shows the project carefully weighing tradeoffs between safety and workflow flexibility, with multiple respected contributors providing real-world usage data to inform the decision.
