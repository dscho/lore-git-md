This is a security-focused discussion about `git am`'s patch parsing behavior, specifically addressing the long-standing ambiguity in distinguishing between commit message content and actual patch content when processing email streams. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King's explanation of why reverse parsing (from the end) wouldn't solve the fundamental ambiguity issue.

The key proposal is to have `git am` detect ambiguous patches (where diffs appear in commit message areas) and require explicit confirmation via a new `--accept-ambiguous-patch` flag before proceeding. This would shift from silently accepting potentially dangerous input to forcing manual review when ambiguity is detected. The solution acknowledges that perfect parsing is impossible but aims to make the risk explicit rather than hidden.

The discussion builds on prior threads (2015, 2022, 2024) that established this as a known issue affecting both Git and Unix `patch(1)`, with particular impact on distribution workflows that apply many patches. While the core technical challenge remains - legitimate patches can contain separator lines that look like commit/patch boundaries - the new approach focuses on risk communication rather than attempting to solve the unsolvable parsing problem.

Patrick's suggestion represents a pragmatic middle ground between backward compatibility and security, though as Jeff King previously noted, effectiveness may be limited by low adoption of such safety measures in practice. The thread continues to balance security hardening against real-world workflow needs, with distributions like NixOS (applying ~2800 patches) being particularly vulnerable to this class of issue.
