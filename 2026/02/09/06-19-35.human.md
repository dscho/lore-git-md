Feature proposal for `git log --graph --untangle` to simplify visualization of complex merge histories. This follow-up email from Toon Claes responds to Junio Hamano's request for concrete examples by showing before/after output from Git's own history. The key difference is that `--untangle` suppresses ancestry lines between merge commits, making the graph more compact by displaying merged branches as originating directly from their merge commits rather than their original branch points.

The example demonstrates the effect on Git's l10n (localization) merge history, where the standard `--graph` output shows a complex octopus merge with many crossing lines, while `--untangle` produces a cleaner visualization where each translation branch appears to stem directly from its merge commit. The author acknowledges remaining issues including memory leaks and naming concerns (`--ignore-merge-base` and `--disconnect-merge-base` are proposed alternatives), and seeks guidance on whether the project is open to this direction before continuing work.

Junio expresses enthusiasm for improvements to the graph visualization code while hinting at other potential areas for enhancement. The thread remains in exploratory phase, with the next steps being to address the technical issues raised while demonstrating the value of this visualization approach on more complex histories.
