This RFC thread began with a proposal to modify the experimental `git repo info` command (a GSOC project) to show all fields by default when no arguments are given, rather than the current plumbing-style approach of showing nothing. The original design by Phillip Wood intentionally followed Git's plumbing conventions, where commands output only explicitly requested data for scripting reliability.

Junio Hamano firmly opposed changing this behavior, emphasizing that plumbing commands must remain predictable - zero arguments should mean zero output. In the latest email, the contributor (SoutrikDas) acknowledges this feedback, recognizing they were unaware of Git's plumbing/porcelain distinction. This appears to settle the design question definitively in favor of maintaining strict plumbing discipline, even for new experimental commands.

While the technical change was straightforward (a conditional in `builtin/repo.c`), the more significant outcome is the maintainer's reinforcement of Git's design norms. The thread demonstrates how new contributors learn these conventions through direct engagement with the project's leadership. The command will retain its original plumbing behavior, prioritizing consistency with Git's established patterns over interactive convenience.
