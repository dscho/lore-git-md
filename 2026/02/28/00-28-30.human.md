This is a technical clarification in the ongoing fsmonitor daemon thread, specifically about the synchronization mechanism for filesystem events. Paul Tarjan explains the two-phase approach to event handling in response to Junio Hamano's question about the 1-second timeout behavior.

The key points are that the timeout only engages after initial filesystem activity is detected (when a client requests status), and only affects cases where the filesystem fails to deliver expected cookie events. On an idle system, the timeout mechanism remains dormant. When triggered, the timeout safely falls back to a full scan rather than hanging indefinitely.

This exchange confirms the design rationale behind the synchronization approach in patch 4/10 of the series, addressing Junio's concern about when and why the timeout would activate. The explanation shows the timeout is a targeted safety measure for edge cases (like problematic container filesystems) rather than a general performance tradeoff.
