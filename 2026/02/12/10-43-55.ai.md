This is a feature patch series introducing parallel hook execution, building on Adrian Ratiu's prior configurable hooks work. Phillip Wood raises two key points in response to the cover letter:

First, he suggests looking to existing hook managers for design inspiration around parallelization strategies, noting that some serialize pre-commit hooks by default (since they may modify the index) while allowing parallel execution for other cases. He emphasizes the importance of future-proofing the configuration design to support more granular control.

Second, Phillip flags a potential regression concern regarding terminal access for pre-commit hooks, referencing a past issue where hooks lost terminal access. He asks whether this series (or its dependencies) might reintroduce that problem, showing careful attention to backward compatibility implications.

The exchange highlights ongoing design considerations around the parallel hooks implementation, particularly around safety defaults and configuration flexibility. Phillip's feedback suggests the series would benefit from clearer documentation of which hooks can safely parallelize and which must remain serial, as well as explicit handling of terminal access edge cases. His tone is constructive, positioning these as refinements rather than fundamental objections to the approach.
