This is a follow-up discussion about Phillip Wood's proposed commit-msg hook improvements, part of an ongoing thread tracking multiple in-flight Git topics.

Phillip Wood is preparing a reroll of his series that enhances the sample commit-msg hook to detect problematic commit messages. The hook currently flags messages containing separator lines or diffs, which can confuse mail processing tools. In this update, Phillip notes he'll adjust the hook to safely handle subject lines starting with "diff -" (which are safe when prefixed by `git format-patch`'s "[PATCH]") and likely drop the .gitattributes patch from the series after testing revealed it wasn't needed.

Junio Hamano confirms the approach, noting that even though the "[PATCH]" prefix is optional in format-patch output, it will always appear on the "Subject:" line, making the check safe. The discussion shows this is a well-considered improvement to Git's hook infrastructure, with careful attention to real-world usage patterns. The changes appear straightforward and uncontroversial, addressing edge cases that emerged during actual use of the hook.
