This is a discussion about a proposed warning for skipped merge commits in `git format-patch`. Dominique Martinet, who had initially supported the idea, is reconsidering after realizing how rarely users would actually need to generate patches for merge commits. 

The thread reveals that specifying merge commits for patching requires complex revision syntax (`foo^- --not foo^2`), making the warning of limited practical value. Dominique also notes that the current implementation would interfere with patch counting (like `-3`) since merge commits are skipped without adjusting the count. 

The discussion originated from a user confused by `git format-patch` silently skipping merges when using Jujutsu (jj), but Dominique now believes the root issue lies with jj allowing problematic merge commits rather than with Git's behavior. 

Junio Hamano and Jeff King had previously suggested refinements to the warning system, but Dominique now concludes the change may not be worth the complexity. The patch appears unlikely to proceed unless stronger use cases emerge. The exchange highlights how Git's revision syntax and merge handling can create subtle edge cases, even for experienced contributors.
