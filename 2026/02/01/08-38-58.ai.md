Follow-up discussion in the format-patch merge commit warning thread. Dominique Martinet re-evaluates the usefulness of warning about skipped merge commits after encountering a real-world case where a user was confused by the behavior. 

The key new insight is that in normal Git usage, it's actually quite difficult to specify a range that would include only merge commits (requiring complex expressions like `foo^- --not foo^2`). The original confusion case came from a Jujutsu (jj) user who inadvertently created a merge commit with non-trivial content, but Dominique now believes the root issue may lie with jj allowing such merges rather than with Git's format-patch behavior.

Dominique also notes that the warning would interfere with patch counting (as seen in test t4014) since merge commits are skipped without decrementing the count. After this deeper analysis, Dominique concludes the warning may not provide enough benefit to justify the change and suggests dropping the idea unless new compelling use cases emerge.

This email effectively withdraws the RFC proposal after further consideration of edge cases and real-world impact. The thread appears to be concluding with consensus that while the warning might help some users, the implementation tradeoffs and limited practical benefit make it not worth pursuing further.
