This is a continuation of the RFC discussion about introducing AI-assisted workflows for Git's localization (l10n) process, specifically focusing on quality assurance and reviewer responsibilities.

Jiang Xin, the Chinese translation maintainer, responds to concerns raised by Michal Suchánek about over-reliance on AI for translation reviews. The core tension is between the practical challenges of reviewing translations in multiple languages versus maintaining human oversight for quality and cultural sensitivity.

Jiang Xin emphasizes that Git's l10n effort covers 19 languages (14 actively maintained), making comprehensive human review impractical for a single coordinator. He argues that AI should serve as an optional aid - particularly for detecting obvious issues like ads or political content - while still relying on human judgment from language team leads. The response references Jiang Xin's own positive experience using AI tools during the Git 2.53 Chinese translation cycle as evidence of their potential value.

The exchange highlights an ongoing philosophical divide: Michal Suchánek sees AI assistance as a slippery slope toward reduced human accountability, while Jiang Xin positions it as a pragmatic tool to scale review efforts across many languages. The thread remains unresolved, with fundamental questions about trust, quality control, and the role of automation in open source translation work.
