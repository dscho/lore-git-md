This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about AI-assisted localization (l10n) workflows for Git's translation process. Jiang Xin, the Chinese translation maintainer, responds to concerns raised by Michal Suchanek and Peter Krefting about the risks of AI-generated translations.

The email clarifies that the proposed AI tooling remains strictly optional, with no automation overriding human judgment. Jiang Xin shares a positive experience from handling the Chinese translation in Git 2.53's l10n cycle, where AI-assisted translation proved both fast and high-quality for their needs. The primary motivation is to help l10n coordinators with quality control during reviews, as existing tools like git-po-helper only catch technical errors, not semantic issues or inappropriate content.

Key technical details include the Chinese team's practice of maintaining a glossary in po/zh_CN.po to ensure consistency across both human and AI-assisted translations. The thread context shows this remains contentious, with Peter Krefting strongly opposed for Swedish translations and Michal Suchanek suggesting limited use cases like abandoned translations. New legal concerns from brian m. carlson about copyright and DCO compliance add another layer of complexity to the discussion.

The patch itself is not changing any code - it's a documentation update in po/README.md presenting AI tools as optional aids. The debate now spans technical quality concerns, cultural preferences across language teams, and emerging legal questions about AI-generated content in open source. Jiang Xin's position remains pragmatic - encouraging experimentation while respecting each team's autonomy.
