This is a continuation of the discussion about AI-assisted localization (l10n) workflows in Git, specifically addressing concerns about quality control and human oversight in translation reviews. The exchange highlights a tension between two perspectives: Michal's concern that AI tools might lead to complacency in human review (if reviewers don't look closely because they assume AI caught problems), and Jiang Xin's pragmatic need for scalable quality control across many languages. Jiang Xin cites his positive experience using AI for Chinese translations during the Git 2.53 cycle as evidence that the approach can work when applied judiciously.

This reply doesn't introduce new technical elements but reinforces the core disagreement about tooling philosophy - whether optional AI assistance inevitably shapes expectations and practices in ways that could degrade quality, versus whether it can remain truly optional while providing value in specific scenarios. The thread remains unresolved on fundamental questions about accountability, quality assurance, and the psychology of tool adoption in volunteer translation work.
