This is a discussion thread about AI-assisted translation workflows in Git's localization (l10n) process, specifically addressing concerns about quality control and human oversight.

Michal Suchánek responds to Jiang Xin's proposal for using AI tools in translation reviews, expressing skepticism about the effectiveness of AI for semantic-level quality checks. The core concern is that if the l10n coordinator (Jiang Xin) doesn't understand a language, they can't properly verify AI-generated reviews of translations in that language - neither for false positives nor false negatives. Michal argues this creates a situation where AI is effectively "in charge" of quality control without proper human oversight.

The exchange highlights a tension in the l10n workflow: while AI tools could theoretically help review translations across many languages, they may not be reliable enough to replace human judgment, especially for nuanced semantic checks. Jiang Xin had previously noted the practical challenge of reviewing 19 active languages, but Michal counters that without language proficiency, the coordinator can't validate the AI's work anyway.

This discussion follows Jiang Xin's RFC series introducing AI-assisted workflows, showing that while the technical implementation may be sound, philosophical concerns about quality control and human oversight remain unresolved. The thread doesn't propose solutions but surfaces important questions about the limits of automation in translation work.
