This is a discussion about using AI tools in Git's localization (l10n) workflow, specifically for translating and reviewing `.po` files. Jiang Xin, the Chinese translation maintainer, is responding to concerns raised by Peter Krefting and Michal Suchánek about the quality and appropriateness of AI-assisted translations.

The key points are:
- Jiang Xin clarifies that AI tools are optional aids, not requirements, and should not override human judgment in the translation process
- He shares a positive experience using AI to handle Chinese translations during a recent l10n cycle when the usual lead was unavailable
- The proposal focuses on using AI to assist with quality review (flagging irrelevant content or ads) rather than replacing human translators
- A glossary system is in place for Chinese translations to maintain consistency, regardless of whether AI or humans do the work

The thread reveals ongoing tensions between those who see AI as a helpful tool (Jiang Xin) and those who view it as producing unreliable "gibberish" (Peter Krefting). Michal Suchánek's middle-ground position suggests AI might be appropriate only for abandoned translations with sufficient training data. The discussion remains unresolved, with quality concerns and legal questions (raised earlier by brian m. carlson) still unaddressed.
