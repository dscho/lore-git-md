This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about introducing AI-assisted workflows for Git's localization (l10n) process. brian m. carlson raises several concerns about the quality and legal implications of AI-generated translations.

The email strongly opposes AI-assisted translations on three grounds: quality, legal, and philosophical. First, they share personal experiences showing AI translations often produce incorrect results, even for widely spoken languages like Spanish and French, and notes the particular challenges with regional variants like European vs. Brazilian Portuguese. Second, they highlight potential legal issues with the Developer's Certificate of Origin (DCO) requirements, as copyright law may make AI-generated translations problematic to contribute. Finally, they argue philosophically that Git should maintain high-quality human-reviewed translations as a matter of respect for non-English speaking users.

The response comes from a position of valuing human translation quality over automation efficiency, and suggests the project's current stance in SubmittingPatches (which explicitly rejects AI-generated content) should apply to translations as well. While not blocking the RFC outright, this presents a significant counterpoint to Jiang Xin's proposal of optional AI assistance, particularly around the legal and policy implications that hadn't been fully addressed in previous discussion. The tone is firm but not hostile, grounded in practical concerns rather than ideological opposition.
