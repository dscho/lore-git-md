This is a security-focused discussion about the `commit-msg` hook's handling of separator lines in commit messages, part of the ongoing effort to prevent accidental patch application from embedded diffs.

Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on Phillip Wood's reconsideration of whether the sample `commit-msg` hook should detect and warn about separator lines ("---") in commit messages. Peff acknowledges his own occasional use of separator lines in commit messages, though he notes he doesn't use the sample hook himself. He suggests the sample hook might reasonably err on the side of caution even if it catches some legitimate uses, since users can always modify or disable it.

The exchange reveals a tension between security hardening and workflow flexibility. While separator lines can legitimately appear in commit messages (as both Peff and Wood confirm they use them), they also pose a security risk when `git am` misinterprets them as patch boundaries. Peff's stance is pragmatic - the sample hook can be opinionated since it's just an example, and users with different needs can adapt it.

This discussion occurs alongside Kristoffer Haugsbakk's queued documentation warnings and Patrick Steinhardt's proposed `--accept-ambiguous-patch` flag, showing the project's multi-pronged approach to this long-standing security issue. The thread highlights how even security-conscious changes must account for real-world Git usage patterns, with maintainers like Peff demonstrating legitimate cases that might be caught by overzealous validation.
