Patrick Steinhardt has completed an 8-patch series that changes Git's default maintenance strategy from traditional garbage collection ("gc") to geometric repacking. This significant change better handles modern repository sizes by maintaining a geometric progression of packfiles, reducing the need for expensive full repacks. The series followed a careful progression - first hardening test infrastructure across seven patches to handle the strategy change, then making the actual behavior change in this final patch.

The test infrastructure work (patches 1-7) systematically updated test scripts to either disable auto-maintenance or explicitly request the "gc" strategy where tests depended on specific behavior. This included changes to rebase, send-pack, fetch, gc, and maintenance tests. The final patch now switches the default strategy to "geometric" for manual maintenance while keeping "incremental" as the default for scheduled maintenance, with accompanying documentation updates.

Derrick Stolee, who designed the geometric repacking approach, has approved the direction. The series has shown steady progress with each patch addressing specific test interactions before the final behavior change. While geometric repacking isn't a perfect solution for all cases (extremely large repositories may still need "incremental"), this change better serves most modern repository scales while maintaining backward compatibility through configurable strategies.
