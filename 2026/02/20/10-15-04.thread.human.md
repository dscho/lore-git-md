Patrick Steinhardt's 8-patch series to make geometric repacking the default `git maintenance` strategy has been thoroughly reviewed and approved by key domain experts (Derrick Stolee, Justin Tobler, Toon Claes), with all prior feedback addressed. The series prepares test infrastructure (patches 1-7) before switching the default maintenance strategy from "gc" to "geometric" for manual operations in patch 8, while keeping "incremental" as the default for scheduled maintenance. The change is a significant optimization for large repositories, with careful test isolation to preserve legacy behavior.

However, a critical bug report from Stefan Haller has surfaced, revealing two serious issues with the geometric repacking feature introduced in Git 2.54.0. First, the `geometric-repack.auto` condition triggers far too aggressively—256 times more sensitive than documented—causing unnecessary background repacks even in tiny repositories. Second, these repacks race with concurrent Git commands, producing intermittent "invalid object" errors, temporary file failures, and commit failures at a rate of about 1 in 1,100 commits during stress testing. The root cause lies in `prune_packed_objects()` removing fanout directories (`objects/XX/`) while concurrent writers attempt to create temporary files, bypassing Git's single-retry mitigation. Stefan's report includes minimal reproducers and demonstrates real-world impact, such as failures in the lazygit CI suite.

While the geometric repacking series itself remains technically sound and ready for merging, this newly discovered bug—unrelated to the strategy change—demands urgent attention. The issue is now being treated as a high-priority bug in the maintenance subsystem, with Patrick Steinhardt acknowledging its severity. A fix will likely involve either adjusting the auto-condition logic or hardening the repack/prune machinery against concurrent access. The series may proceed independently, but the bug's data corruption risk during routine operations makes it a critical concern for Git's usability.
