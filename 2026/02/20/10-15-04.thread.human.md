Patrick Steinhardt’s patch series to make geometric repacking the default `git maintenance` strategy is ready for merging, with all prior feedback addressed and approvals from key reviewers. The series prepares test infrastructure and switches the default strategy from "gc" to "geometric" for manual operations while keeping "incremental" as the default for scheduled maintenance.

However, two critical issues have emerged. First, Stefan Haller identified a bug where `geometric-repack.auto` triggers repacks 256× more aggressively than intended due to a scaling bug in `too_many_loose_objects()`. Patrick proposed raising the default threshold from 100 to 6700 (matching `gc.auto`) to mitigate this, but Stefan has declined to test the fix, leaving its real-world effectiveness unconfirmed. Second, Stefan reported concurrency failures—"invalid object" errors and temporary file issues—during concurrent operations, caused by `prune_packed_objects()` removing fanout directories while writers attempt to create files. This race condition predates the geometric repacking series but is now surfacing more frequently due to the more aggressive repacking introduced in Git 2.54.0.

The series itself remains technically sound and approved, but these bugs demand attention. The discussion has shifted to whether the threshold adjustment alone is sufficient to mask the concurrency issue in practice, or if further hardening of the repack/prune machinery is needed. No new solutions have been proposed yet, and the underlying race condition remains unresolved.
