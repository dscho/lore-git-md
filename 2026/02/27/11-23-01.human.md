Performance optimization patch from Patrick Steinhardt addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. This is the second patch in a two-part series, following a cleanup of debug output.

The patch reduces write syscall overhead when streaming packfiles by buffering more data before writing to the client. In production systems handling hundreds of concurrent upload-pack processes at multi-gigabit speeds, profiling showed 80% of time spent in kernel-level pipe write contention. The solution increases the buffer fill threshold to 2/3 capacity before flushing, reducing write syscalls from ~400,000 to ~130,000 when cloning the Linux repository.

The change is surgical (7 lines added to upload-pack.c) and builds on existing buffering infrastructure. Patrick notes that while further buffering could reduce writes to ~100,000, the additional read overhead makes the current 2/3 threshold the sweet spot. The patch includes performance measurements and acknowledges help from Matt Smiley at GitLab.

This appears to be a well-motivated optimization targeting a real production bottleneck, with clear before/after metrics. The change is low-risk as it works within existing buffering logic rather than introducing new mechanisms.
