This is a performance optimization patch targeting `git-upload-pack` lock contention during high-concurrency packfile transfers. It's the second in a two-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt, following a cleanup of debug output.

The patch addresses a specific bottleneck observed in production where hundreds of concurrent `git-upload-pack` processes were contending on kernel-level locks during pipe writes, despite having sufficient CPU, memory, and I/O capacity. The root cause was excessive `write()` syscalls - each packfile data read was triggering one or two writes (more with sideband enabled).

The solution modifies the buffering strategy in `upload-pack.c` to accumulate data before writing - specifically waiting until the buffer is at least 2/3 full. This reduces write syscalls from ~400,000 to ~130,000 when cloning the Linux repository. The change is surgically implemented with just 7 lines added, leveraging existing buffering infrastructure.

The patch includes production-tested performance data and rationale for the 2/3 threshold (further optimization to full buffers showed diminishing returns). It's a focused, evidence-based optimization from an experienced contributor in server performance, with clear before/after metrics showing significant syscall reduction. The change maintains the existing behavior while improving throughput under high concurrency.
