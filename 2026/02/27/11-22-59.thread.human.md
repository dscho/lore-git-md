Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series to reduce lock contention in `git-upload-pack` is now complete and ready for merging. The ten-patch series implements `writev()`-based buffering across multiple subsystems to dramatically reduce write syscalls during high-concurrency operations like cloning large repositories. When cloning the Linux repository, the changes reduce write syscalls from about 400k to just 44k.

The final patch in the series focuses on `git pack-objects --stdout`, increasing the buffer size from 8KB to 64KB when writing packfiles with progress reporting. While the performance improvement is modest on idle systems, the real benefit comes from reduced kernel lock contention under heavy load. Jeff King reviewed and suggested this approach, and Johannes Sixt has validated the platform compatibility.

Junio Hamano has approved the technical implementation in its v4 state, which includes refinements like keepalive prioritization and accurate transmission tracking. The series touches several subsystems (`upload-pack`, `pack-objects`, `sideband`, `csum-file`, `wrapper`) with careful attention to cross-platform compatibility (POSIX, Windows, NonStop) and behavioral edge cases. All open questions have been resolved, and the maintainer is expected to merge the series in the next integration window.
