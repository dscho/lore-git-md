Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series for `git-upload-pack` has been approved for merging in its v4 state. The ten-patch series significantly reduces lock contention during high-concurrency operations by implementing `writev()`-based buffering across multiple subsystems. When cloning large repositories like Linux, this cuts write syscalls from about 400,000 to just 44,000.

The latest patch in the series, reviewed and approved along with the rest of v4, refines the keepalive handling when buffered data is present. Based on Jeff King's suggestion, it now prioritizes flushing actual data over sending empty keepalive packets when the timeout is reached. This maintains the connection while avoiding unnecessary network traffic, though the author notes the impact will be minor in most cases since keepalives typically occur before pack data starts flowing.

Junio Hamano has approved the technical implementation after thorough review, and Johannes Sixt has validated the platform-specific edge case handling. The series is now considered complete with all open questions resolved, and is expected to be merged in the next integration window. The optimizations touch multiple subsystems (`upload-pack`, `pack-objects`, `sideband`, `csum-file`, `wrapper`) while maintaining protocol semantics and cross-platform compatibility.
