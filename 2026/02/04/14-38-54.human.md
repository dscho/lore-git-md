This is a discussion about AI-assisted translation workflows in Git's localization (l10n) process, specifically regarding quality concerns with machine-generated translations. The email comes from Lilydjwg (依云), a Chinese translator, responding to earlier concerns raised by Peter Krefting (Swedish) and Michal Suchánek about AI-generated translations.

The thread has revealed strong opposition from some maintainers, with Peter Krefting outright rejecting AI tools for Swedish translations due to poor quality output. Michal Suchánek provided a more nuanced view, citing Mozilla's Japanese SUMO community as a cautionary example where AI translations caused problems.

Lilydjwg adds another data point from their experience with Chinese translations, mentioning specific cases (fish shell and Python docs) where machine translations resulted in nonsense output. They express concern about being burdened with reviewing low-quality AI-generated translations, though they take a more neutral stance on whether others should use the tools.

The discussion highlights a growing divide in the l10n community between those who see AI as a potential efficiency tool and those who view it as a threat to translation quality, particularly for technical terminology. The Git project now faces the challenge of balancing these perspectives while maintaining translation quality standards.
