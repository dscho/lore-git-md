Jiang Xin, Git's Chinese translation maintainer, has proposed introducing optional AI tools to assist with localization (l10n) workflows, particularly for reviewing .po file translations. The discussion has revealed deep divisions between those who see AI as a pragmatic solution for scaling translation quality control and those concerned about legal, quality, and cultural risks.

The proposal currently exists as experimental changes in the git-po repository's next branch, offering AI-assisted guidance as optional suggestions rather than requirements. Jiang Xin points to successful use in Chinese translations during the Git 2.53 cycle as evidence the approach can work. However, opponents like Peter Krefting (Swedish translations) and Michal Suchanek remain skeptical, arguing that even optional tools might lead to complacency in human review and could miss culturally sensitive content. brian m. carlson has raised additional legal concerns about copyright and DCO compliance with AI-generated content.

The latest exchange highlights Jiang Xin's practical challenge as l10n coordinator overseeing 19 languages (14 actively maintained), where manual semantic review of all translations is impossible. He argues AI tools could help catch extreme cases in languages he doesn't know, Michal counters that this might create a false sense of security. The fundamental disagreement remains unresolved - whether optional AI assistance can remain truly optional while shaping expectations and review practices in volunteer translation work.
