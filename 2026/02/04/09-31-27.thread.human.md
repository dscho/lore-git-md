Jiang Xin, the Chinese translation maintainer, has sparked discussion with an RFC proposing AI-assisted workflows for Git's localization process, particularly for handling .po file translations and reviews. The proposal includes initial tooling already committed to the git-po repository's next branch, with standard prompts for AI-assisted translation updates and reviews.

Peter Krefting, the Swedish translation maintainer, strongly opposes the idea, citing quality concerns and requesting Swedish translations be excluded from any AI integration. Michal Suchánek has now entered the discussion with a more balanced perspective, drawing parallels to Mozilla's Japanese SUMO community where AI application to existing translations caused issues. Michal suggests AI might help with abandoned or missing translations for general topics, but notes Git's specialized terminology may not translate well automatically, and warns of potential quality control challenges if AI tools lead to an influx of low-quality submissions.

The thread remains open with no clear consensus yet from the broader l10n team, as the discussion now encompasses both technical implementation questions and fundamental debates about whether AI should be involved in translation work at all. Jiang Xin's initial proposal of AI as a helper tool now faces scrutiny from multiple perspectives about where and how - or if - such automation should be applied.
