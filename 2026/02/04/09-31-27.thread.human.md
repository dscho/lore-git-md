This RFC thread explores the contentious proposal to introduce AI-assisted workflows into Git's localization (l10n) process, led by Chinese translation maintainer Jiang Xin. The discussion has revealed deep divisions between those who see AI as a pragmatic tool and those who view it as a threat to translation quality and legal compliance.

Jiang Xin has demonstrated successful use of AI tools during the Git 2.53 Chinese translation cycle, positioning them as optional aids that helped maintain progress during a maintainer absence. He emphasizes these are merely suggestions that don't override human judgment. However, Michal Suchánek's latest response expresses skepticism, interpreting this as an attempt to offload review responsibilities to AI. He argues that human reviewers should catch issues like inappropriate ads or political content if properly engaged, and worries AI tools could become de facto gatekeepers even if nominally optional.

The debate spans multiple dimensions: Peter Krefting maintains strong opposition for Swedish translations based on quality concerns, while brian m. carlson has introduced legal objections citing copyright and DCO compliance issues. Technical discussions contrast git-po-helper's existing mechanical checks with AI's potential semantic review capabilities, with unresolved questions about handling culturally sensitive content and technical terminology.

At its core, this remains a polarized discussion between pragmatic adoption (Jiang Xin, with cautious support from Michal for limited use cases) and principled opposition (Peter and brian). The Chinese translation example shows successful application, but broader concerns about quality control, legal risks, and the slippery slope toward reduced human oversight remain unresolved. With experimental changes already in git-po's next branch, the thread appears headed toward an uneasy compromise where AI tools remain optional but face significant skepticism from parts of the l10n community.
