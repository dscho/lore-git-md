This is a mentoring email from Git maintainer Junio C Hamano to a Google Summer of Code student, redirecting their initial technical proposal toward Git's required microproject onboarding process. The student had proposed adding worktree path detection functionality via a new `git repo info` command, but Junio advises starting with simpler pre-approved microprojects first.

Junio clarifies that the purpose of microprojects is to practice the patch submission and review cycle, not necessarily to produce mergeable code. He emphasizes that demonstrating the ability to work with reviewers is more important than tackling complex problems at this stage. The email includes a review of the current microproject suggestions, noting that some may be outdated or too involved for new contributors.

The thread captures Git's structured approach for onboarding new contributors, particularly through GSoC, where students must first demonstrate basic competency with the project's workflow before moving to more substantial work. While the original `git repo info` proposal involved interesting technical questions about worktree detection architecture, those discussions are being deferred until after the student completes a microproject.
