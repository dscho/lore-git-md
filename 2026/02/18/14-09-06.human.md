This is the third patch in a series fixing strbuf memory handling issues, specifically addressing the `strbuf_attach()` function. The patch changes the function to explicitly enforce its documented requirement that the `alloc` parameter must be larger than `len` (to leave room for the NUL terminator), rather than silently reallocating when this isn't true.

The change replaces a `strbuf_grow()` call that was quietly fixing undersized allocations with a `BUG()` check that will fail loudly if the contract is violated. This makes the behavior match the function's documentation and catches programming errors earlier. The patch also moves the NUL termination to happen immediately after the buffer is attached, rather than relying on the removed `strbuf_grow()` call to do it.

This continues the series that has already fixed an off-by-one in `strbuf_reencode()` and updated callers of `strbuf_attach()`. The change is straightforward and appears correct - it tightens the function's invariants in a way that matches its documented behavior. The patch is small and focused, touching only `strbuf.c`. Given that the first two patches in the series haven't drawn objections, this one seems likely to be uncontroversial as well.
