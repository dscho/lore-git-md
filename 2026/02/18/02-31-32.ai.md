This is a bugfix patch targeting the `git subtree` contrib command, specifically addressing incorrect commit exclusion during subtree splits. The patch is the third in a series (v2 3/3) and follows up on reports from George and Christian Heusel about split history regressions.

The core issue stems from `should_ignore_subtree_split_commit` logic that was meant to optimize splits by ignoring commits from other subtrees, but incorrectly altered the split history in ways that violated the API contract (commit hashes should remain stable). The patch removes this problematic optimization entirely rather than trying to fix it, as the current implementation actually slows down many operations while being incorrect.

Key technical details:
- The removed function checked commit trailers to identify "foreign" subtree commits
- Merge commits were particularly affected as their ancestry checks depend on complete split history
- The fix ensures split operations maintain consistent history even with rebased/cherry-picked subtree commits
- New test cases verify correct behavior with multiple subtrees and rebased commits

The patch appears well-justified with clear commit message tracing the history of the bug through three prior attempts (98ba49c, 83f9dad, 28a7e27) and linking to the original bug report. Test coverage is thorough, including a new case that simulates rebased subtree commits with trailers. The change is focused - removing the broken optimization without introducing new complexity.
