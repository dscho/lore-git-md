[PATCH 0/5] Remove `the_repository` from merge-ort and replay
Author: Elijah Newren
Thread type: Patch series (v1 -> completed)
Subsystems: merge-ort, replay
Related effort: `the_repository` removal (ongoing project)

This series systematically eliminates direct uses of `the_repository` global variable and `the_hash_algo` from merge-ort.c and replay.c, replacing them with repository-specific parameters available via `opt->repo`. The changes are part of the broader project-wide cleanup to remove implicit repository dependencies.

Patches 1-3 converted `write_tree()` and other functions in merge-ort.c to take explicit `struct repository *` parameters, replacing most `the_repository` and `the_hash_algo` uses with `opt->repo` equivalents. Patch 3 specifically addressed `the_hash_algo` references, converting them to `opt->repo->hash_algo` with Patrick Steinhardt suggesting and Elijah Newren agreeing to add a one-line commit message explanation documenting this mechanical change, reinforcing Git's commit message conventions.

Patch 4 introduced compile-time enforcement against `the_repository` usage in merge-ort.c via macro redefinition (with temporary exceptions for `prefetch_for_content_merges()` and `DEFAULT_ABBREV`). Patrick Steinhardt suggested adding a comment explaining the `DO_NOT_USE_THE_REPOSITORY` macro definition. Kristoffer Haugsbakk noted a minor inconsistency in macro casing (all-caps vs lowercase) which Elijah committed to fixing for consistency. Elijah now acknowledges uncertainty about why `prefetch_for_content_merges()` still needs `the_repository`, having copied this pattern from `diffcore_std()` without understanding its purpose, and invites Jonathan Tan to explain the underlying rationale. Elijah agrees to document the macro's purpose and temporary exceptions per Patrick's suggestion.

The final patch (5/5) applied the same compile-time prevention mechanism to replay.c, with Patrick Steinhardt again recommending adding explanatory comments about the macro's purpose. He characterized the series as "a welcome cleanup" with no substantive technical concerns, indicating readiness for merging. The remaining discussion points are minor documentation improvements around temporary exceptions and macro style consistency.

All changes are mechanical, behavior-preserving replacements that maintain correct merge-ort functionality while advancing the subsystem's alignment with the project's parameterization goals. Elijah's authorship of merge-ort ensures technical soundness throughout. The series achieved its original scope without expansion or controversy, following established practices for global variable elimination. With all five patches submitted and core technical work completed, the series appears ready for merging pending final documentation polish and possible clarification from Jonathan Tan about the prefetching behavior.
