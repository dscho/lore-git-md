This thread explores a proposed shorthand syntax (`@{primary}` or `@{default}`) for referencing a repository's primary branch, now in its fourth iteration. The original idea aimed to provide a convenient way to switch between branches across multiple repositories without per-repo configuration. Harald Nordgren's implementation currently resolves to the remote-tracking branch pointed by `origin/HEAD`, though the design has evolved from its initial local-configuration-based approach.

Junio Hamano, the maintainer, has raised fundamental concerns about the feature's conceptual foundation. He questions whether the current remote-tracking behavior aligns with the "default" naming, as it conflicts with existing mechanisms like `init.defaultBranch` and `git var GIT_DEFAULT_BRANCH`. Junio suggests the name might need changing (perhaps to something like `@{dumbo}`) to avoid implying a relationship with local configuration. He also revisits whether the original motivation—using `init.defaultBranch`—might have been a better starting point, as it handles unconfigured repositories more gracefully.

While the technical implementation appears sound with growing test coverage, the discussion has shifted from refinement to re-examining first principles. Key unresolved questions remain about naming semantics, the relationship to existing branch-naming mechanisms, and whether the remote-tracking approach adequately serves the original use case. Junio's persistent concerns suggest the feature may need stronger conceptual justification or a design pivot before proceeding further. The thread currently sits at an impasse between practical utility and precise terminology alignment.
