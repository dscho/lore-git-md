This is a discussion about the proposed `@{default}` shorthand for referencing a repository's primary branch, now in its fourth iteration. Junio Hamano raises several key concerns about the current implementation and its conceptual foundation.

The core tension revolves around whether the shorthand should resolve to the remote-tracking branch (via `origin/HEAD`) or the local `init.defaultBranch` configuration. Junio argues that for users who clone repositories and follow upstream naming conventions, `origin/HEAD` is more relevant than `init.defaultBranch`. However, he questions the utility of automatically mapping this to a local branch name, as the typical workflow doesn't involve working directly on the primary branch.

Junio sketches a realistic topic-branch workflow where `@{upstream}` and `@{push}` are useful, but finds the proposed `@{default}` less clearly valuable. He notes that `origin` already serves as a shorthand for `refs/remotes/origin/HEAD` and suggests the local branch mapping might not align with common practices.

The discussion has shifted from implementation details to first principles - whether the feature solves a real problem given its pivot from local config to remote-tracking resolution. Junio's persistent questioning indicates the proposal needs stronger justification of its use cases and clearer definition of "primary branch" semantics, particularly for repositories without clear upstreams. The naming (`@{default}` vs `@{primary}`) remains unresolved, with Junio half-jokingly suggesting `@{dumbo}` to avoid premature commitment to terminology.
