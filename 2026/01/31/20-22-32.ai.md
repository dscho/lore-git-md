This is a feature proposal for a `@{default}` shorthand to reference a repository's primary branch, now in its fourth iteration. Harald Nordgren responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about the naming and behavior of the proposed shorthand, providing concrete workflow examples from their experience across multiple repositories.

The email highlights real-world use cases where the shorthand would simplify common operations like creating feature branches from the primary branch, particularly in environments where branch names vary (main/master/trunk) across different projects. Nordgren shares an extensive list of repositories they work with, demonstrating the diversity of primary branch naming conventions in practice.

Key points in this response:
- Defends the utility of the shorthand by showing actual shell commands it would simplify
- Acknowledges Junio's suggestion to explore `repo_default_branch_name()` as an alternative implementation
- Provides empirical data on primary branch naming variations across 30+ real projects
- Maintains focus on the original motivation - reducing friction when switching between repositories with different conventions

The discussion remains unresolved around whether the shorthand should resolve to remote-tracking branches (current implementation) or local configuration (Junio's suggestion), and whether "default" is the right term given the remote-centric behavior. Nordgren indicates they'll experiment with Junio's proposed approach, suggesting the thread may see another iteration.
