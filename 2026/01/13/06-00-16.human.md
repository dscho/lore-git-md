This is a technical discussion about the `git-history` command's replay infrastructure, specifically focusing on the `replay_revisions()` interface and conflict handling design.

Elijah Newren follows up on Patrick Steinhardt's explanation of the dual error reporting mechanism in the replay machinery (both return codes and a `replay_result` struct). He probes deeper into the practical implications of the proposed conflict reporting approach, questioning how GitLab's use case for surfacing conflict information would work without first-class conflict handling. Newren raises several specific concerns about the assumptions behind tracking conflicted commits, including whether the implementation can accurately identify which commits would conflict in non-linear history.

The exchange reveals an important design question about whether the replay machinery should report just the first conflict encountered or attempt to track all potential conflicts. Newren's questions suggest the current approach may be oversimplifying the problem space, particularly for non-linear histories. This discussion represents one of the final technical refinements needed before the `git-history` series could be considered ready for merging.
