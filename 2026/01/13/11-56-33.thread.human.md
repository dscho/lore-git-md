This thread addresses several technical issues in Git's hook subsystem, focusing on stdout/stderr separation in pre-push hooks and stdin feeding performance/deadlock risks. The discussion was initiated by Chris Darroch and has drawn participation from key contributors including Jeff King (Peff), Adrian Ratiu, and Patrick Steinhardt.

The core problems involve three areas: a regression in stdout/stderr separation for pre-push hooks, performance bottlenecks in stdin feeding, and newly identified deadlock scenarios. The fixes are being split into three commits for v3, with the stdout/stderr separation already validated and ready. The stdin feeding solution has evolved significantly based on Peff's analysis, which demonstrated concrete deadlock scenarios and quantified a 100ms performance penalty when processing more than 500 lines.

Adrian Ratiu is implementing the fixes, now incorporating Peff's findings. For server-side hooks, he'll implement a proper poll loop to address both deadlock and performance issues, while maintaining existing behavior for single non-parallel hooks. The client-side fixes for stdout/stderr separation will proceed in v3. The discussion has revealed important architectural tradeoffs around real-time output versus buffering, with Peff's test cases providing concrete data to guide the solution.

Key open questions remain about whether to require `ungroup=1` or implement poll() handling, the tradeoffs of stderr buffering, and final verification of backward compatibility. The thread has become a valuable case study in hook execution internals, with participants working toward solutions that balance performance, safety, and backward compatibility.
