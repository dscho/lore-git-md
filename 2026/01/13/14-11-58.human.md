This is a bugfix addressing a regression in Git's hook subsystem, specifically affecting the pre-push hook's output handling. The patch comes from Adrian Ratiu with input from Junio Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt.

The issue stems from a recent change that made all hooks redirect stdout to stderr by default, which broke the pre-push hook's special behavior of keeping the streams separate. Through code archaeology, Junio confirmed that pre-push was historically the only hook that didn't follow this redirection pattern.

The solution introduces a `stdout_to_stderr` flag in the hook options structure (defaulting to 1 for backward compatibility with most hooks) while explicitly setting it to 0 for pre-push hooks. This maintains the expected behavior where pre-push hook output goes to stdout while other hooks continue sending output to stderr.

The patch is well-motivated and addresses a regression, though it reveals gaps in test coverage for hook output behavior that will be addressed in follow-up work. Junio has approved the approach and suggested a minor tweak to the patch title for better documentation. This appears ready for merging once the additional tests are included.
