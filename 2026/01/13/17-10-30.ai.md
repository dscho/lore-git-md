This is a technical discussion about rebase conflict handling in the context of a user's difficulties moving Linux kernel patches between branches.

Jeff King (Peff) responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's suggestion to use `git merge-tree` as a rebase dry-run alternative, pointing out a subtle but important distinction. While `merge-tree` can show conflicts between branch endpoints, it doesn't fully replicate the incremental conflict detection of a rebase operation where each commit is applied sequentially. Peff explains that a true rebase dry-run is fundamentally impossible because conflicts in later patches depend on how earlier conflicts are resolved.

The email highlights two key technical points: 1) rebase operations must process commits sequentially and cannot predict all potential conflicts upfront, and 2) tools like `git-imerge` (referenced in the footnote) exist to help manage complex rebase/merge operations through incremental conflict resolution. This discussion serves as both a response to the immediate thread and a broader educational note about Git's rebase semantics. The tone is helpful and explanatory rather than critical, typical of Peff's technical responses.
