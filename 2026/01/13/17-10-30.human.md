This is a technical discussion about rebase behavior and conflict detection in Git, specifically addressing a user's difficulty with rebasing Linux kernel patches across divergent branches.

Jeff King (Peff) responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's suggestion to use `git merge-tree` as a way to preview rebase conflicts. While acknowledging it's a useful approximation, Peff explains why it's not equivalent to a true rebase dry-run: merge-tree only shows conflicts between the endpoints, whereas rebase applies patches sequentially and conflicts can depend on earlier patch resolutions. He notes this inherent limitation makes a proper rebase dry-run impossible since conflicts in later patches depend on how earlier ones are resolved.

The exchange highlights an important distinction between merge and rebase conflict detection, with Peff pointing to Michael Haggerty's imerge tool as a more sophisticated approach for handling complex rebase/merge scenarios. The discussion remains technical and constructive, offering practical insights while clarifying the limitations of proposed solutions.
