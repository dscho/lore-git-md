This is a performance discussion thread about `git pack-refs --all` slowness on large repositories with NFS storage. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Martin Fick's flame graph analysis showing that nearly 42% of the runtime is spent on page faults, with significant time also in SSE-optimized memcmp (29.8%) and object header unpacking (30%). The packed-refs storage creation itself only accounts for 8.7% of runtime.

Patrick expresses surprise at both the high the page fault overhead is and that `packed_refs_store_create()` takes as much time as it does. He suggests that Martin's NFS mount options (which show 32KB rsize/wsize) might benefit from being increased to 1MB, as was historically recommended for NFS performance. The thread remains at an impasse between short-term NFS optimizations and Patrick's consistent advocacy for adopting the reftable backend as the architectural solution.

The flame graph data confirms the mmap/NFS interaction as the primary bottleneck but doesn't provide clear direction on whether to pursue immediate mmap-specific optimizations or focus on the reftable transition. Patrick's response indicates he still sees the reftable path as the more promising solution despite the new performance data.
