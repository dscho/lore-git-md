Bugfix discussion for Git's stdin/stderr handling in hooks. Jeff King (Peff) identifies and demonstrates two concrete performance issues in the current hook execution code, building on earlier discussion about stdout/stderr separation.

The first issue is a deadlock risk when feeding stdin to hooks in 500-line batches - if the pipe fills up, Git could block waiting to write while also waiting to read stderr. The second issue shows a 100ms delay when processing exactly 501 refs due to an unnecessary poll timeout between batches.

Peff provides a clear reproduction case showing how a push with 501 refs takes 100ms longer than one with 500 refs, traced to the poll() timeout. He suggests two potential solutions: either require `ungroup=1` mode (which avoids polling) or implement proper poll() handling for both stdin and stderr.

This email advances the thread by moving from theoretical concerns to measurable performance impacts, with concrete test cases that could be added to the test suite. The discussion remains focused on the right technical solution while maintaining backward compatibility. Peff's analysis carries weight given his deep knowledge of Git's internals and performance characteristics.
