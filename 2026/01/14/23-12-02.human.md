This is a continuation of the ongoing discussion about enhancing `git status` to show divergence from both upstream and push tracking branches. Harald Nordgren responds to Junio Hamano's earlier proposal for a configurable `status.compareBranches` setting that would allow specifying which branches to compare against.

Harald acknowledges the technical challenges of implementing a flexible configuration while maintaining the ability to show accurate push/pull advice. He argues that since `git push` and `git pull` are Git's two fundamental remote operations, their associated branches (`@{push}` and `@{upstream}`) should remain special cases rather than being generalized into a fully configurable system.

The email reflects a shift in the discussion from implementation details to design philosophy, with Harald advocating for keeping the UI focused on these two primary workflows. This comes after extensive technical discussion about how `@{push}` behaves in different workflow configurations (particularly triangular workflows) and whether push tracking should be shown by default. The thread appears to be moving toward consensus on making push tracking visible by default while maintaining a clean separation between the two fundamental branch relationships.
