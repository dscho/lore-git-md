This is a bugfix for gitk's color configuration, addressing inconsistencies in how ref and tag colors are rendered. The patch corrects two specific issues: `tagfgcolor` was being ignored (with all labels using `headfgcolor` instead), and head outlines were hardcoded to black rather than respecting the `headoutlinecolor` setting.

Johannes Sixt (Hannes) provides a thorough review, noting that while the patch itself is technically sound, the commit message could be clearer about the current behavior and implications of the change. He suggests restructuring the explanation to better document gitk's ref styling system and explicitly call out what remains hardcoded versus what becomes configurable. The review also raises an important question about backward compatibility - since these color settings aren't exposed in gitk's UI, users may be unaware they exist, making the behavioral change potentially surprising.

The fix is narrowly scoped to the `drawtags` procedure and maintains existing hardcoded colors for non-head refs (black outline with #ddddff fill) while making head/tag colors properly respect their configuration settings. This came up while working on VSCode theme export functionality. The patch appears ready to merge once the commit message is improved to address Hannes' feedback about documenting the current styling system and change implications.
