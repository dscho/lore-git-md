This is a lighthearted technical contribution to the ongoing SHA-256 hash coincidence thread, maintaining the playful tone established by previous participants. Klaus Sembritzki builds on Jeff King's brute-force search program for finding strings whose SHA-256 hashes contain specific byte patterns (like "deadbeef").

The patch modifies Jeff's original program to increment the most significant bit first rather than the least significant bit, theorizing this might improve performance due to potential autocorrelation in hash outputs. Klaus provides a bit-reversal implementation and shows sample runs with different counter widths, finding different solutions when terminating early. He later identifies and fixes a bug in his bit-reversal logic that only worked correctly for even numbers.

The results demonstrate that SHA-256 remains effectively random despite the optimization attempt - the speedup is not dramatic, confirming the hash's cryptographic properties.
