This is a lighthearted technical follow-up in the SHA-256 hash coincidence thread, where participants have been finding amusing patterns in hash outputs. Klaus Sembritzki contributes to Jeff King's brute-force search program by experimenting with bit-reversal optimizations.

Klaus first modified Jeff's program to increment the most significant bit first rather than the least significant bit, theorizing this might improve performance due to autocorrelation in hash outputs. He provided a bit-reversal implementation and sample runs with different counter widths (32, 31, 30, 29 bits). Later, he identified and fixed a bug in his bit-reversal logic that only worked correctly for even numbers.

The results show SHA-256 remains effectively random despite these optimization attempts - the speedup is minimal, confirming the hash's cryptographic properties. The thread remains purely for entertainment, with no technical implications for Git.
