This is a lighthearted technical follow-up in the SHA-256 hash coincidence thread, where participants are exploring amusing patterns in hash outputs. Klaus Sembritzki continues his optimization attempts of Jeff King's brute-force search program for finding SHA-256 outputs containing specific byte sequences like "deadbeef".

Klaus first modified Jeff's program to increment the most significant bit first rather than the least significant bit, theorizing this might improve performance due to potential autocorrelation in hash outputs. He provided a bit-reversal implementation and showed sample runs with different counter widths (32, 31, 30, 29 bits). The results showed modest variations in when solutions were found, confirming SHA-256's cryptographic randomness properties.

In this follow-up, Klaus identifies and fixes a bug in his bit-reversal logic that only worked correctly for even numbers. The corrected version shows more consistent behavior across different counter widths, though the performance impact remains minimal.
