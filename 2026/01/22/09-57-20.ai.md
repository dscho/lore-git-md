This is part 10 of a 12-part series refactoring Git's hook subsystem to support parallel execution. The patch addresses I/O performance issues in the parallel execution infrastructure, specifically fixing a throughput bottleneck reported by Kristoffer Haugsbakk where child processes could experience artificial 100ms delays due to polling timeouts.

The core change modifies `run-command.c` to poll both child input and output file descriptors simultaneously, eliminating potential deadlocks where parent and child processes could block waiting for each other (a scenario Jeff King identified). The solution maintains the existing behavior for sequential execution while optimizing parallel hook execution by ensuring data is fed to child processes as quickly as possible.

Key technical elements include:
- Bidirectional I/O polling replacing the previous output-only approach
- Preservation of the ungroup=1 behavior for sequential execution
- Explicit handling of the pre-push hook's separate stdout/stderr streams
- The `pp_buffer_io()` helper managing the polled I/O operations

The patch follows up on earlier v7 discussion where Kristoffer noted the throughput impact and Jeff King raised the deadlock concern. Adrian confirms he'll correct a minor terminology issue ("stdout" vs "stderr" in the commit title) in the upcoming v8 revision. With this change, the parallel execution infrastructure now appears technically complete, having addressed all major performance and correctness concerns raised during review.
