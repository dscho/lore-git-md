Bugfix discussion regarding `git reset`'s reflog behavior. Jeff King (Peff) concurs with Junio C Hamano's concerns about changing how no-op resets handle reflog entries, reinforcing the potential for breaking existing scripts and losing useful human-readable context.

The thread centers on whether to skip reflog updates when `git reset` doesn't actually change HEAD (a "no-op" reset). While the original patch aimed to reduce reflog pollution, both Junio and Jeff highlight important downsides: scripts using `@{1}` to reference the pre-reset state would break, and the reflog's message field (which records the target commit name) provides valuable forensic information even for no-op resets.

Jeff's reply adds weight to Junio's earlier objection by noting the human-readable aspect of reflog messages, suggesting the current behavior may be intentionally useful rather than merely redundant. The discussion remains at an impasse between optimization and backward compatibility, with both maintainers expressing reservations about changing the status quo. The patch appears unlikely to progress without addressing these concerns, either through a compatibility-preserving approach or stronger justification for the behavioral change.
