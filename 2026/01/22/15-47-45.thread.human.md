Junio Hamano has raised concerns about a proposed change to `git reset`'s reflog behavior. The original patch sought to optimize no-op resets (where the target commit matches HEAD) by skipping reflog entries, but Junio points out this could break existing scripts that rely on the current behavior. He gives a concrete example where a script uses `git reset --hard` followed by `git diff --stat @{1}` to compare against the pre-reset state - if the reset were a no-op and skipped the reflog entry, `@{1}` would unexpectedly refer to an older state. 

While the optimization goal is reasonable, Junio's response suggests the change would need to either preserve compatibility for such use cases or be accompanied by a broader discussion about whether the optimization justifies potentially breaking existing workflows. His "mildly negative" reaction indicates the patch in its current form is unlikely to be accepted without addressing these concerns. The thread now awaits either a revised approach from the original author or further discussion about how to balance optimization with backward compatibility.
