This is a technical analysis in the ongoing thread about a `git subtree` regression affecting squashed operations. The discussion has progressed from bug report to root cause analysis, with Colin Stagner now proposing a solution.

The key insight is that the current `should_ignore_subtree_split_commit()` heuristic fails when dealing with rebased or cherry-picked squash commits, as it assumes squash commits will either have no parents or only parents that are also squash commits. The proposed solution is to remove this heuristic entirely, as it was primarily an optimization for `split --rejoin` operations and may not be worth maintaining given its complexity and performance impact.

Colin also notes that Git 2.51.0 added GPG signing support to subtree operations, which may explain why some commits appear verified differently than expected. The discussion remains focused on the core issue - whether to keep the problematic heuristic or remove it - with Colin leaning toward removal as the simpler, more robust solution.
