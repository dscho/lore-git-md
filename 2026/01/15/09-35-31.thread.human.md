Patrick Steinhardt's series to standardize commit list function names (`commit_list_copy()`, `commit_list_reverse()`, `commit_list_free()`) has sparked a broader discussion about Git's coding guidelines. While the mechanical changes improve consistency by following the `struct S_<verb>()` pattern, Junio Hamano has raised concerns about the long-term maintenance implications. 

The maintainer notes that keeping compatibility wrappers indefinitely—rather than removing them after Git 2.53 as originally planned—would create ongoing burden, since these functions are used across both maint and master branches. Junio suggests the project might need to revisit its naming rules, potentially grandfathering in well-established functions that don't conform to current standards rather than forcing widespread mechanical changes to stable interfaces.

This exchange highlights a recurring tension in the project between enforcing consistency and preserving backward compatibility. Junio's intervention signals that while the technical merits of standardization are clear, the practical costs may warrant adjusting either the timeline for dropping wrappers or the scope of the naming guidelines themselves. The thread now sits at a decision point about how to balance these competing concerns.
