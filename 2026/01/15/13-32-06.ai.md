This is a refactoring discussion about Git's coding guidelines and backward compatibility, specifically regarding the context of Patrick Steinhardt's recent series to standardize commit list function names.

Junio C Hamano raises concerns about the long-term maintenance burden of keeping compatibility wrappers for the renamed commit list functions (`copy_commit_list`, `reverse_commit_list`, `free_commit_list`). He notes that because these functions exist in both maint and master with different callers, the wrappers would need to remain indefinitely rather than being removable after Git 2.53 as Patrick suggested. 

Junio questions whether the project's naming rules should be revised to exempt well-established functions that don't follow the current `struct S_<verb>()` pattern, suggesting the guidelines could focus only on new types while grandfathering in existing names. This would avoid forcing widespread mechanical changes to stable interfaces. The comment reflects Junio's maintainer perspective on balancing consistency against the practical costs of breaking changes, even when they're technically improvements.

The discussion touches on a recurring tension in large codebases: when to enforce new standards retroactively versus accepting historical inconsistencies. Junio's response indicates this rename series may need reconsideration of either the timeline for dropping wrappers or the scope of the naming rules themselves. The thread sits at a decision point about whether to proceed with the standardization or adjust the project's guidelines to accommodate legacy patterns.
