This is a security-related discussion about ANSI escape sequence handling in Git's sideband channel, continuing from earlier patches that introduced granular control via `sideband.allowControlCharacters` configuration.

Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on the debate between strict security defaults (blocking potentially dangerous sequences) versus practical compatibility (allowing sequences used by real-world tools). He responds to brian m. carlson's examples of pre-receive hooks running build tools like Cargo that generate cursor and erase sequences, particularly in self-hosted environments and push-to-deploy workflows.

Peff proposes a middle path: keep the new security controls but make them opt-in rather than enabled by default. This would preserve backward compatibility while still giving security-conscious users the ability to lock down their configuration. The suggestion aims to resolve the tension between brian's operational concerns and Johannes Schindelin's security-focused implementation.

The technical implementation remains stable (using bitmask flags for color, cursor, and erase sequences), but this email focuses on the policy question of defaults rather than implementation details. Peff's position reflects Git's general philosophy of providing flexible configuration while avoiding breaking changes.
