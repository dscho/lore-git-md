This is a discussion about triangular workflows in Git, specifically focusing on how `@{push}` behaves under different `push.default` configurations. The email from D. Ben Knoble follows up on Jeff King's explanation of when `@{push`} works in triangular workflows (where the fetch and push remotes differ).

Key technical points:
- `@{push}` works with `push.default=current` and `push.default=matching`, resolving to the expected remote ref in triangular setups
- It does not work with `push.default=simple` (the default), which enforces that upstream and push destinations must match
- The discussion clarifies that `@{push`} is most useful in triangular workflows where the push remote differs from the fetch remote

The exchange serves as a practical note about configuration requirements for triangular workflows, with both participants confirming they use `push.default=current` globally.
