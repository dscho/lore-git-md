This is a security-related discussion about ANSI escape sequence handling in Git's sideband channel, continuing the thread about CVE-2024-32002 and CVE-2024-52005. Johannes Schindelin pushes back against the emerging consensus for permissive defaults in the new `sideband.allowControlCharacters` configuration, arguing that the proposed opt-in model undermines security.

Schindelin questions why existing use cases (which he characterizes as rare and poorly designed) should dictate default behavior when they could be implemented more securely through Git's own highlighting mechanisms. He points to precedent in other tools like GNU tar and cURL that sanitize terminal output, and reminds participants that he has already compromised by allowing color sequences by default.

The email reveals a tension between security-first and compatibility-first approaches. Schindelin's position is that allowing any control sequences by default is a "well-known worst practice," while acknowledging he has already accommodated the most common case (color) in his implementation. His frustration surfaces in questioning how much more permissiveness reviewers expect, given that no demonstrated use cases require more than color sequences.

This appears to be the last substantive objection to the permissive-default approach that Junio and Jeff King have endorsed. While the technical implementation is settled, this email captures the philosophical disagreement about where to strike the balance between security hardening and backward compatibility. The thread seems headed toward resolution with permissive defaults, but Schindelin's concerns about setting a poor security precedent remain on the record.
