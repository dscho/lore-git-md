This is a user help request about `git rebase` behavior in the Linux kernel development context. Alan Mackenzie reports successfully resolving his rebase issues with private help, though the solution requires manual conflict resolution of his 8 commits against a large diff (3400 lines).

The core issue stemmed from attempting to rebase from linux-6.13.y to master in the Linux Stable repository, where the branches had significantly diverged (2012 commits difference). The key insight was identifying the specific commit (733381204a86) marking where his changes began, allowing targeted rebasing of just his 8 commits rather than everything since linux-6.13.y.

While the immediate problem is solved, the thread highlights two broader pain points: the lack of a `--dry-run` option for `git rebase` to preview conflicts, and the non-obvious meaning of branch status messages showing large commit counts that don't reflect local work. The resolution demonstrates the value of understanding commit ranges in complex rebase scenarios, though the manual conflict resolution remains labor-intensive.
