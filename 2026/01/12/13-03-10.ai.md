This is a discussion about the future design direction for the `git-history` command, specifically around conflict handling for potential future subcommands. The thread context shows this is part of a v9 series introducing `git-history` with `reword` and `split` subcommands, building on Git's replay machinery.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to SZEDER Gábor's concern that the series shouldn't be merged without clear plans for handling conflicts in future subcommands. Patrick makes several key points: 

1. The current subcommands (`reword` and `split`) are conflict-free by design, so conflict handling decisions shouldn't block their implementation. He argues these operations are self-contained enough that future changes to conflict handling approach wouldn't impact them.

2. He acknowledges the need for separate discussions about:
   - General conflict handling in replay-based commands
   - Supporting stacked branch workflows with conflicts
   - Potentially moving toward first-class conflict support in Git

3. He suggests getting the current infrastructure merged to gather user feedback, which could inform future design decisions. The series has already incorporated significant technical refinements (including detached HEAD support and interface simplifications) from Elijah Newren's review.

The exchange highlights a tension between wanting to avoid design lock-in (SZEDER's concern) and the practical desire to ship useful functionality (Patrick's position). Patrick positions the conflict handling discussion as orthogonal to the current patch series, while acknowledging it's an important future consideration. The technical implementation appears ready, with only higher-level design questions remaining open.
