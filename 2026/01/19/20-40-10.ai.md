This is a technical discussion about pointer representation and platform compatibility in the context of the xdiff refactoring series, which is part of the broader Rustification effort.

Jeff King (Peff) responds to Ezekiel Newren's concerns about pointer consistency between C and Rust, particularly regarding Harvard architecture compatibility. Peff confirms that while C allows different pointer sizes between types, a union can enforce consistent size and alignment when needed. He notes that reading from a different union member than was written to remains undefined behavior, which may not solve the Rust interop problem.

The email raises two key practical considerations: (1) whether undefined behavior could cause issues even on common platforms, and (2) how modern platforms like CHERI Arm (with their "fat pointers") might affect the implementation. Peff suggests that while Git likely doesn't need to support the most esoteric architectures mentioned in C FAQs, there may be relevant modern platforms to consider.

This discussion now forms a critical decision point for the series - whether to explicitly drop support for architectures with inconsistent pointer behavior to enable Rust interoperability, or to find a solution that maintains broader compatibility. The technical details about union usage and platform considerations will inform both the immediate xdiff refactoring and set precedent for future Rust-C boundary work in the codebase.
