Jeff King (Peff) has completed his series addressing memory management issues in Git's push tracking reference handling. The four-patch set cleans up const-correctness and ownership semantics in remote.c and branch.c, focusing on the `branch.push_tracking_ref` field. 

The series began with three patches fixing specific leaks and const-correctness issues, including a fix for branch_get_push_1() when encountering invalid configurations. The final patch, just submitted, completes the work by making `push_tracking_ref` always allocated (changing from `const char*` to `char*`), eliminating a subtle trap where the branch might hold either an owned or borrowed string. This ensures `branch_release()` can safely free the field while maintaining the same external behavior.

Peff's changes demonstrate his characteristic thorough approach to memory management, addressing what he described as a "hacky memory management scheme" from 2015. The series modifies 33 lines total across remote.c, branch.c, and test files, adding test coverage while making no user-visible behavior changes beyond leak fixes. Though not urgent (the issues were longstanding), the changes provide important groundwork for future push tracking reference improvements.
