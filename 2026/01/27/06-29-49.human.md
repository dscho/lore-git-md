This is a follow-up discussion in the ODB abstraction effort, specifically about type safety for object info flags. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano and René Scharfe's earlier conversation about compiler warnings for enum mismatches.

Patrick confirms that GCC's `-Wenum-conversion` can catch some incorrect enum usage (like in `builtin/backfill.c`), but fails to detect problems when passing integer constants or OR'ed flags. He explores two potential solutions: 

1. A more aggressive approach using structs with bitfields, which would provide full type safety at the cost of less ergonomic syntax (requiring designated initializers for each call).

2. Accepting the current partial protection, acknowledging its limitations.

The discussion shows the trade-offs between type safety and usability in the ODB abstraction work. While the immediate flag cleanup has been merged, this follow-up surfaces deeper design considerations for future refactoring. Patrick leaves the decision to Junio on whether to pursue the more type-safe struct approach, offering to reroll the patches if desired. The exchange demonstrates the project's careful attention to API design and compiler-enforced correctness, even after initial changes are complete.
