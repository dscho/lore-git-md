This is part 10 of a refactoring series for Git's xdiff subsystem, with Rust interoperability considerations. Phillip Wood provides a review summary of Ezekiel Newren's 10-patch series that restructures xdiff internals to enable future optimizations while establishing Rust-compatible infrastructure.

The review focuses on several key points: The IVec implementation in patch 1 needs work to avoid undefined behavior. Patch 3 introduces an extra pass over input records without clear justification. Patch 4's change to exclude common lines from diff algorithms changes output behavior, which may be good but needs analysis. The proposed conversion from dstart/dend to delta_start/delta_end in patches 5-8 seems less convenient than the current indices. The final patches moving cleanup functions don't fully demonstrate IVec's capabilities and may not actually save time as claimed.

Phillip questions how the various cleanups connect to the IVec introduction and suggests the series needs more work to justify its changes and demonstrate benefits. The review maintains a constructive tone while identifying substantive technical concerns about implementation choices and performance claims. This feedback will likely require significant revision before the series can progress.
