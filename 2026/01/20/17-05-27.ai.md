Junio C Hamano weighs in on the ongoing sideband security discussion, proposing a nuanced approach to the default filtering behavior. He agrees with brian m. carlson that strict filtering should not be universally enabled by default, but suggests differentiating between trusted everyday remotes and unknown ones. 

The maintainer envisions a two-tiered model where new clones would apply strict filtering by default for safety, while established repositories could relax these restrictions for their configured remotes. This attempts to balance security against usability impact, particularly for workflows that rely on colored output from trusted hooks. 

Junio's intervention doesn't resolve the fundamental disagreement between the security-first and compatibility-first camps, but shifts the discussion toward implementation details of how defaults might vary by context rather than whether filtering should exist at all. His suggestion implies the configuration system may need to track remote trust levels, though he leaves the specifics open for further discussion. This maintains the series' technical soundness while acknowledging the social dimension of the debate.
