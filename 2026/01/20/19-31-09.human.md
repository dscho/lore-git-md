This is a security discussion about terminal control sequence filtering in Git's sideband channel, building on the ongoing thread about CVE-2024-32002 and CVE-2024-52005. Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on Junio Hamano's proposal for differentiated default behavior between new clones and established repositories.

Peff cautiously suggests repurposing the existing `GIT_PROTOCOL_FROM_USER` mechanism (used for file:// protocol restrictions) as a way to distinguish trusted vs untrusted remotes for sideband filtering. He notes this would allow command-line-specified remotes to be more lenient while still protecting against malicious .gitmodules redirects. However, he raises two significant concerns: the scheme makes heuristic trust assumptions that may be too convoluted, and the trust boundaries don't perfectly align between protocol safety and terminal control safety.

The email reflects the thread's broader tension between security and usability, with Peff offering a technical suggestion while clearly flagging its limitations. His intervention maintains the thread's thoughtful tone while moving the discussion toward concrete implementation options for Junio's proposed two-tiered trust model. The technical soundness of the filtering implementation itself remains uncontested, with the debate now focused on policy decisions around defaults and trust signals.
