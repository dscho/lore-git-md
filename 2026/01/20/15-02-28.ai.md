This is a review comment on patch 3/10 in Ezekiel Newren's xdiff refactoring series, which aims to prepare the xdiff subsystem for future optimizations while maintaining Rust interoperability. Phillip Wood raises two technical concerns about the current implementation.

First, he questions the performance tradeoff of eliminating the line-count estimation function `xdl_guess_lines()` in favor of processing lines twice (once to split and once to classify), noting it's unclear whether this actually improves efficiency. Second, he points out that error handling for `xdl_classify_record()` appears to have been lost in the refactoring.

The patch removes `xdl_guess_lines()` entirely and restructures the classification to happen in separate loops after line splitting. Phillip's review suggests the author should either justify the performance characteristics of this change or reconsider the approach, while also restoring proper error handling. This feedback continues the thread's pattern of detailed implementation scrutiny, particularly around performance-sensitive xdiff internals.
