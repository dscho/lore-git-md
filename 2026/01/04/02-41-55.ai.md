This is a discussion about version control workflows, specifically comparing rebase-based linear history versus merge-based development. Nico Williams strongly advocates for Sun Microsystems' former approach of maintaining strictly linear history through rebasing, arguing it provides clearer historical understanding than merge-based workflows that preserve intermediate development states.

The email responds to Junio Hamano's comment about not needing to see the "drunken-man's walk" of development iterations in the final history. Nico expands on this by describing Sun's historical workflow that prohibited merges entirely, requiring teams to maintain linear history through rebasing. He characterizes merge workflows as "toxic and obnoxious" and sees no value in preserving development iterations, only wanting to see clean final states.

This perspective contrasts with Git's default merge-based workflow, though the discussion remains theoretical rather than proposing any concrete changes to Git itself. The exchange highlights differing philosophies about what constitutes valuable historical record in version control - whether to preserve all intermediate states (merge workflow) or only final polished states (rebase workflow). Nico's position aligns with the latter, valuing clarity of final intent over preservation of development process.
