This is a discussion about version control workflows, not a patch submission. Nico Williams advocates for strictly linear history (rebasing rather than merging) based on their experience at Sun Microsystems, where they used a rebase-only workflow for decades. They argue that merge workflows are "toxic and obnoxious" because they preserve the "drunken-man's walk" of development history rather than showing a clean, linear progression. 

The email is a reply to Junio C Hamano's comment about how final patch submissions should present a clean narrative rather than showing the roundabout development process. Nico strongly agrees with this perspective, stating that linear history makes it much easier to understand code evolution. They go so far as to say the only merges they would tolerate are fast-forwards used to document push boundaries, though even those are not ideal in their view.

This is a philosophical position rather than a technical proposal - Nico is not suggesting any changes to Git itself, but rather weighing in on the broader discussion about how developers should structure their commit histories. The perspective comes from extensive experience with Sun's Teamware system, which enforced linear history through policy rather than technical constraints.
