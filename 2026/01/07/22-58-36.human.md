Performance investigation update on `git pack-refs` slowness with NFS and high ref counts. Martin Fick shares `perf` results from a production Gerrit server (3M refs, NFS storage) that confirm the mmap/NFS interaction is indeed the bottleneck, with about two-thirds of the time spent in memory operations (`_memcmp_sse4_1`, `unpack_object_header_buffer`) and page faults. The data shows the issue is not in packed-refs handling itself but in how Git accesses the packfile contents when processing refs.

This aligns with Patrick Steinhardt's earlier diagnosis and his advocacy for moving to the reftable backend as the long-term solution. The `perf` data, while somewhat opaque to Martin, provides concrete evidence that the mmap-heavy approach doesn't play well with NFS at this scale. The thread continues to show a clear paths forward: either short-term workarounds like NO_MMAP builds or the more architectural shift to reftable. Martin's production testing continues to be valuable in grounding this discussion in real-world data.
