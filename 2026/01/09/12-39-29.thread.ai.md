Patrick Steinhardt's 17-patch series refactors Git's ref backend validation to centralize consistency checks for both files and reftable backends. The series has progressed through three phases: files backend simplification (1-8), shared verification infrastructure (9-14), and fsck check migration (15-17). 

Karthik Nayak provided thorough technical review, confirming correctness of key changes including: files backend simplification (patch 1), elimination of `files_fsck_refs()` (patch 4), and proper worktree handling in reftable validation (patch 14) where he as original author approved the fix. The shared validation infrastructure introduced `refs_fsck_symref()` (patch 9) and `refs_fsck_ref()` (patch 10) for cross-backend checks, with patch 10 establishing the concept of non-symbolic refs after naming discussion.

Junio Hamano decisively settled the final open question by rejecting "direct refs" terminology in favor of "non-symbolic refs", calling the former "not understandable". This maintains Git's convention of preferring clear, established phrasing over novel terms. With this resolved and all technical concerns addressed by Karthik's review, the series appears ready for a final iteration incorporating Junio's naming preference before merging.

The work is part of the ongoing ref backend abstraction effort to share logic between files and reftable implementations. Key technical changes include proper worktree handling via `backend_for_worktree()` to avoid N^2 checks, migration of fsck checks for zero-OID refs and HEAD symref to the shared infrastructure, and removal of now-redundant functions like `fsck_head_link()`. The series has followed Git's typical review pattern - substantive technical review from domain experts followed by maintainer input on naming and conventions as the final gate before merging.
