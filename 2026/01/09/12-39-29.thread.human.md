Patrick Steinhardt's series to refactor Git's ref backend validation has reached its final stages after thorough technical review from Karthik Nayak, the original author of much of the reftable backend code. The 17-patch series simplifies the files backend, introduces shared verification infrastructure, and migrates fsck checks to work consistently across both storage formats. Karthik has signed off on all the substantive changes, including fixes to worktree handling and the elimination of redundant checks.

The only remaining question is a naming issue raised by Junio Hamano regarding patch 10's introduction of "direct refs" to mean non-symbolic references. While Karthik had accepted this novel terminology during review, Junio has now weighed in with a clear preference for the more conventional "non-symbolic refs" phrasing, calling the proposed term "not understandable." This appears to be the final hurdle before the series can be merged, as the technical approach itself has been vetted and approved. The exchange highlights Git's cultural preference for clarity and established conventions over novel terminology, even when the new phrasing might be more concise. Patrick will likely need to send one more version adjusting this naming before the series can proceed to integration.
