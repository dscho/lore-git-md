This is a refactoring patch series that continues the discussion about standardizing function names in Git's commit list API. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio C Hamano's concerns about the long-term maintenance burden of compatibility wrappers for renamed functions.

Patrick acknowledges Junio's point about the wrappers needing to remain indefinitely due to differences between maint and master branches. However, he argues for gradually converging on consistent naming, citing his personal frustration with the current mix of `commit_list_*` and legacy names (`copy_commit_list`, `reverse_commit_list`, `free_commit_list`). He notes that while wholesale renaming crusades should be avoided, targeted changes to particularly confusing cases (like these three functions that stand out from the rest of the API) can improve developer experience.

The exchange highlights Patrick's characteristic approach to large-scale refactoring through incremental changes, contrasted with Junio's maintainer perspective that weighs the costs of permanent compatibility layers. Patrick leaves the decision open, stating he'll drop the series if the project considers it too invasive, while maintaining there is value in addressing naming inconsistencies that actively cause confusion during development. The thread remains at an impasse between consistency goals and practical maintenance concerns.
