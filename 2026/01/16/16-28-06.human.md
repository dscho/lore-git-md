This is a review comment on the final patch in the approved `git-history` series, focusing on the `reword` subcommand's behavior. SZEDER Gábor raises concerns about two aspects of the implementation that they feel were not adequately addressed in the commit message or design.

First, they criticize the commit message for presenting only the advantages of the new `reword` approach (in-memory operation, no checkout) while omitting its drawbacks compared to `git rebase --reword`. They request more objective documentation of the trade-offs.

Second, and more substantively, they challenge the default behavior of rewriting all local branches containing the commit being reworded. They argue this violates Git's typical porcelain convention where commands operate only on the current branch unless explicitly told otherwise (via `--all` or similar). They consider this default dangerous because users might accidentally rewrite multiple branches without realizing it, potentially leading to data loss if the old commits are garbage-collected before the mistake is noticed.

The comment comes after maintainer approval (Junio Hamano) and technical sign-off (Elijah Newren), suggesting these concerns were either not strongly enough voiced earlier or were considered and overruled during the 11-iteration review process. The reviewer is effectively registering a last-minute objection to what they see as problematic defaults, though the patch appears headed for integration regardless.
