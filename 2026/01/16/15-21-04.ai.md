This is a security-related discussion about ANSI escape sequence handling in Git's sideband channel, continuing the debate over default behavior for the `sideband.allowControlCharacters` configuration.

Junio C Hamano responds to Ondrej Pohorelsky's report that Fedora/CentOS/RHEL have successfully deployed Johannes Schindelin's restrictive ANSI sequence filtering patches as opt-in for six months. While acknowledging the value of the filtering mechanism itself, Hamano firmly rejects making it opt-out by default, arguing that breaking existing (if rare) working setups would be irresponsible regardless of their design merits. He emphasizes consensus that the feature is valuable when opt-in, but maintains the core disagreement with Schindelin/Steinhardt's security-first approach.

The email crystallizes the philosophical divide: downstream evidence now shows restrictive defaults work in practice (Pohorelsky), but Hamano and Jeff King's compatibility-first position holds that working setups - even if architecturally questionable - must not be broken. The technical implementation appears settled (bitmask flags, sequence validation), but the policy debate continues with Hamano explicitly framing the disagreement as about responsibility to users rather than technical feasibility. This positions the thread for either documentation of both approaches or potential stalemate, with Windows remaining a special case due to its stricter historical defaults.
