This is a post-approval critique of the `git-history` command series, specifically addressing documentation and safety concerns. SZEDER Gábor challenges Junio Hamano's acceptance of Patrick Steinhardt's v11 patch series, arguing that the implementation still has significant issues despite Elijah Newren's technical sign-off.

The critique focuses on two main problems: incomplete documentation regarding the tradeoffs between in-memory operations and traditional rebase behavior, and what SZEDER sees as an unsafe default behavior for branch references. The series currently rewrites all local branches by default during history editing operations, which SZEDER argues violates Git's porcelain conventions where destructive operations should be explicit. He suggests making multi-branch updates opt-in rather than the current opt-out model.

While the technical implementation has been thoroughly reviewed and approved, this late-stage feedback highlights interface design concerns that weren't fully addressed during the 11-iteration review process. The critique doesn't challenge the core functionality but rather its user-facing safety characteristics and documentation completeness. These appear to be polish items that could potentially be addressed in follow-up patches rather than blocking the series' integration.

The exchange demonstrates how even after extensive review and maintainer approval, significant design questions can still surface in Git's mailing list workflow. SZEDER's intervention at this late stage suggests the command's innovative approach to history editing may need further refinement to align with Git's established conventions.
