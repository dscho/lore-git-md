This is a critical review of the `git-history reword` subcommand's design choices in the final patch of an approved series. SZEDER Gábor raises concerns about two aspects of the implementation that they feel were inadequately addressed in the commit message and design.

First, they criticize the commit message for presenting only the advantages of the in-memory operation approach while omitting known drawbacks compared to `git rebase --reword`. The reviewer expects a more balanced technical comparison in the documentation.

Second and more substantively, they challenge the default behavior of rewriting all local branches containing the modified commit. They argue this violates Git's established porcelain conventions where commands typically operate only on the current branch unless explicitly told otherwise (via `--all` or similar). The reviewer considers this particularly dangerous for future conflict-producing operations, as users might accidentally rewrite multiple branches and lose commits to garbage collection. They strongly advocate making multi-branch rewriting an opt-in behavior rather than the default.

The critique comes after maintainer approval (Junio Hamano) and technical sign-off (Elijah Newren), suggesting these concerns were either not raised earlier or were not deemed blocking issues. The thread context shows this is the final patch in an 11-iteration series that has already addressed numerous other technical points, including moving core logic to libgit.a and standardizing conflict reporting. The reviewer's focus on documentation completeness and safety defaults suggests these are seen as important but non-blocking polish items for a feature that has otherwise met the project's technical standards.
