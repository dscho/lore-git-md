Security patch discussion regarding ANSI escape sequence handling in Git's sideband channel. Ondrej Pohorelsky from Red Hat reports positive experience with Johannes Schindelin's security patches, which have been carried in Fedora/CentOS/RHEL for six months with no reported issues. The patches make the stricter ANSI sequence filtering opt-in by default in Red Hat distributions.

Pohorelsky supports merging the security improvements even as opt-in, arguing that providing hardening options shouldn't be blocked by debates over defaults. This comes in response to Patrick Steinhardt and Johannes Schindelin advocating for restrictive defaults due to terminal emulator vulnerabilities, while Junio C Hamano and Jeff King prefer maintaining compatibility through opt-in configuration.

The email highlights the real-world deployment perspective, showing the patches work in production without breaking workflows. As a downstream maintainer, Pohorelsky's practical experience adds weight to the argument that the security measures are viable, though the core debate about default behavior remains unresolved between security-conscious and compatibility-focused camps.
