[Security] Downstream maintainer reports positive experience with ANSI escape sequence restrictions

Ondrej Pohorelsky from Red Hat reports that Fedora, CentOS, and RHEL have been carrying Johannes Schindelin's security patches for ANSI escape sequence handling in Git's sideband channel for at least six months. The distribution modified the default to be opt-in (more restrictive) and provided release notes explaining the change. Pohorelsky notes no reported issues from users, though acknowledges uncertainty about adoption breadth.

This email reinforces the security-focused position in the ongoing debate, providing real-world deployment evidence that restrictive defaults have not caused significant problems in major Linux distributions. The maintainer advocates for merging the patches even with opt-in defaults as an improvement over the status quo, suggesting the policy debate about ideal defaults should not block the technical implementation.

The message adds weight to Patrick Steinhardt and Johannes Schindelin's security-first arguments by demonstrating that restrictive handling works in practice at scale. It also highlights the practical reality that many users may never discover or enable security features if they are opt-in rather than default. The thread continues to reflect tension between security hardening and backward compatibility, with this contribution providing empirical data favoring the security position.
