This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the `git-history` command proposal, specifically addressing the scope of branch rewrites during history editing operations.

Patrick Steinhardt acknowledges Elijah Newren's clarification about limiting branch rewrites to local branches (refs/heads/*) rather than including remote-tracking refs or tags. Newren's rationale includes:
- Future "immutable branches" concept that would protect shared history
- The inherent immutability of tags
- The problematic nature of rewriting remote-tracking refs
- Local branches representing the user's explicit areas of interest

Steinhardt accepts this refinement to the branch selection strategy and indicates he will incorporate it into the next revision of the patch series. This represents a significant narrowing of the original proposal's scope, addressing both technical concerns about scaling and philosophical concerns about rewriting shared references.

The exchange shows the proposal evolving toward a more conservative approach that:
1. Respects Git's existing immutability boundaries
2. Focuses on the user's explicit working context
3. Avoids potentially disruptive automatic rewrites of shared history

This resolution addresses one of the key open questions in the thread while leaving other aspects (like stacked branch handling and performance implications) for future discussion. The implementation will now need to adjust its branch walking logic to honor these new constraints.
