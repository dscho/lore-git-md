This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Patrick Steinhardt's proposed `git-history` command, specifically addressing performance concerns around branch rewriting scope.

The email shows Patrick responding to Phillip Wood's analysis of the branch selection logic for history rewriting operations. Phillip had raised two key points in the previous message:
1. The performance impact of walking all branches may be less severe than feared, since most refs in large repositories are tags or remote-tracking branches rather than local branches
2. The proposed implicit range-based approach (similar to `git rebase --update-refs`) would miss some descendant branches that users would expect to be rewritten

Patrick confirms he's incorporating these insights into the next version of the patch series, indicating he'll now rewrite all branches descended from the target commit rather than using the implicit range approach. This suggests the implementation will be more comprehensive in tracking branch relationships, though potentially at some performance cost.

The exchange demonstrates the careful balancing act between correctness and performance in history rewriting tools, with Patrick showing willingness to feedback that prioritizes user expectations over initial implementation concerns. The thread continues to refine the command's behavior while maintaining its goal of providing Jujutsu-like history editing capabilities within Git.
