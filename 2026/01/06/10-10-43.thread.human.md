Jeff King (Peff) posted a two-patch series fixing issues in Git's performance test infrastructure. The first patch corrected where test output files were written by fixing `TEST_OUTPUT_DIRECTORY` assignment timing, while the second preserved `GIT_PERF_*` environment variables that were being clobbered during test runs. Both changes maintained compatibility with various build configurations.

Ramsay Jones responded, agreeing with Jeff's specific fixes but raising a broader concern about how `GIT-BUILD-OPTIONS` handles environment variable overrides. He shared an abandoned prototype from the meson integration work that attempted to allow selective overriding of build options, though he acknowledged it was an incomplete solution. Ramsay suggested that properly addressing this would require restructuring `GIT-BUILD-OPTIONS` to distinguish between overrideable and non-overrideable settings, opening a deeper design discussion about test infrastructure's flexibility that remains unresolved. While Jeff's immediate fixes stand on their own, Ramsay's response points to a larger architectural question that emerged during the meson transition.
