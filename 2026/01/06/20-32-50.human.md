Bugfix discussion for UTF-16LE `.gitignore` files on Windows. This is a follow-up to Matthieu Beauchamp-Boulay's initial patch that aimed to handle non-UTF-8 encoded exclude files, particularly addressing the common case where Windows PowerShell creates UTF-16LE files with BOMs.

The thread has evolved from implementation details to deeper design questions after brian m. carlson pointed out Git's historical behavior of treating exclude patterns as arbitrary byte sequences that must match the filesystem's encoding. Torsten Bögershausen suggests a narrower technical approach focused specifically on detecting and converting UTF-16LE files with BOMs, rather than attempting comprehensive Unicode handling.

Matthieu acknowledges the complexity of the original proposal and is reconsidering the approach. Key points in this email:
- Removes the claim about accidental commits being part of the problem
- Agrees warnings may be ignored but argues against hard failures for encoding issues
- Considers simpler null-byte detection as an alternative approach
- Recognizes the working-tree encoding may not be relevant for exclude files
- Notes the special case of Git's "UTF-16-LE-BOM" handling versus standard iconv behavior

The discussion remains unresolved between three potential solutions: comprehensive Unicode handling, a Windows-specific BOM fix, or maintaining the status quo with better user education. Matthieu appears to be moving toward a middle ground that would handle the Windows UTF-16LE case while being more cautious about broader encoding changes.
