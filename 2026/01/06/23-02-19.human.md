Performance investigation of `git pack-refs` slowness on NFS continues, with Martin Fick responding to Patrick Steinhardt's suggestions. This is part of an ongoing thread analyzing why `git pack-refs --all` takes 5 minutes on a Gerrit server with ~3M refs while JGit completes in 20s.

Martin pushes back on the idea of abandoning NFS entirely, noting its unique advantages for their use case (instant replication to hundreds of mirrors) despite known performance issues. He suggests the problem may be Git-specific since JGit performs well in the same environment, and proposes architectural improvements to reduce lock contention - specifically reading most data before acquiring the packed-refs.lock and only verifying critical sections afterward.

The discussion has shifted from initial I/O concerns to memory access patterns, particularly around mmap performance over NFS. Martin plans to try Patrick's flame graph profiling suggestion but notes it may be difficult on their production server. The thread remains focused on understanding and potentially optimizing Git's behavior in high-refcount NFS environments, with Martin advocating for changes that would benefit Gerrit deployments specifically.
