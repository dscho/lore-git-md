This is a technical discussion in the ongoing xdiff refactoring series, specifically addressing the growth strategy for the new `ivec` data structure. The exchange focuses on patch 1/10 which introduces the C implementation of `ivec`, a vector-like container designed to eventually interoperate with Rust's `Vec`.

René Scharfe raised a concern that the growth strategy in `ivec_reserve()` appears to use constant growth steps (linear growth), which can lead to quadratic complexity. He references Mozilla's recommendation for exponential growth (factor 1.5) as implemented in Git's `ALLOC_GROW` macro.

Ezekiel Newren clarifies that the growth is actually exponential, not linear, and explains the design choice to mirror Rust's `Vec` behavior. The goal is twofold: (1) to make the behavior familiar to C programmers who will eventually work with Rust's `Vec`, and (2) to simplify future conversion from `IVec` to `VecVec` when Rust is introduced. He acknowledges the lack of proper generics in C as a fundamental constraint shaping this implementation.

The discussion highlights the careful balancing act between Git's established patterns (`ALLOC_GROW`) and the Rustification effort's forward-looking requirements. This exchange occurs against the backdrop of the confirmed standards violation in the `ivec` implementation (pointer casting UB) and the ongoing evaluation of Phillip Wood's proposed type-safe alternative. The growth strategy discussion remains open, with performance characteristics and Rust compatibility both in play.
