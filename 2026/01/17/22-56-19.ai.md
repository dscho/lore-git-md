This is a discussion about the default behavior and documentation of the `git history reword` subcommand, part of Patrick Steinhardt's ongoing series to introduce history editing capabilities. The exchange follows Junio Hamano's approval of the series, with SZEDER Gábor raising concerns about safety and documentation.

Elijah Newren responds to SZEDER Gábor's critique that the commit message presents an unbalanced comparison with `git rebase --reword` and that the default behavior of rewriting all local branches containing the commit is unsafe. Newren argues the differences from rebase` are intentional design choices, not oversights, and defends the all-branches default as solving real workflow problems with dependent branches. He notes this approach aligns with other tools like Jujutsu and GitButler, and was supported by multiple commenters in the thread.

The discussion reveals a tension between Git's traditional single-branch porcelain conventions and newer tools' multi-branch awareness. Newren proposes a configurable compromise via a `history.scope` setting that could accommodate both workflows while maintaining the all-branches default. The exchange highlights that while the technical implementation has been approved, the user-facing design decisions around safety and defaults remain contentious enough that they may require follow-up patches before final integration.
