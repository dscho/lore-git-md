The xdiff refactoring series from Ezekiel Newren, which introduces infrastructure for future optimizations and Rust interoperability, has been on hold since February 2026 due to conflicts with Phillip Wood's smaller xdiff cleanups that were merged at that time. Maintainer Junio Hamano has now checked in, noting that since those conflicting changes have landed, the series could be rebased on current master to resume progress.

Phillip Wood's earlier consolidated review raised several substantive concerns that remain unresolved: questions about the IVec implementation's safety (particularly around growth patterns and undefined behavior), skepticism about the efficiency claims of extra data passes, and unclear benefits of the structural changes for Rust goals. The series proposes significant xdiff subsystem changes including a new IVec type, restructuring internals, and moving delta indices - all with an eye toward Rust compatibility, though the specific benefits for that goal have been questioned.

With the merge conflicts now resolved, the path forward depends on Ezekiel addressing Phillip's technical concerns in a rebased version. Junio's nudge suggests the maintainer is open to reviving this work if the author can satisfactorily resolve the outstanding issues around memory management safety and demonstrated benefits. The ball is now in Ezekiel's court to either revise the approach or decide whether to continue the effort given the feedback.
