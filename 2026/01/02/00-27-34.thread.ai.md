Performance optimization and modernization of Git's byte-swapping functions in `compat/bswap.h` (initial patch from Rostislav Krasny). The series replaces manual bit-twiddling implementations with `memcpy`-based type punning for better performance and maintainability while avoiding undefined behavior. Key technical scope: complete network byte order API including new 16-bit variants (`bswap16`, `ntohs`, `htons`, `put_be16`), renamed functions for consistency (`default_swab32` -> `default_bswap32`), and optimized implementations of `get_be32/64`/`put_be32/64` using compiler intrinsics (GCC/Clang `__builtin_bswap*`, MSVC `_byteswap_*`) with portable fallbacks.

Benchmark results show significant speedups: 4x faster at -O0 and 45% improvement with Clang at -O2 on x86-64, with comparable or better performance at higher optimization levels. Jeff King (Peff) has responded to the first iteration, acknowledging the performance improvements (noting the 1.8x speedup with Clang at -O2) but questioning whether maintainability is the primary motivation given these functions are modified. He suggests focusing the justification on the performance gains and indicates he will provide more detailed technical feedback about benchmarking methodology and implementation choices.

Current status: initial submission with thorough benchmarking on x86-64 (GCC 15.2.1, Clang 21.1.7) awaiting more comprehensive review. Open questions/needs: verification of portability across additional architectures (ARM, PPC, RISC-V) and compilers, review of type-punning safety in edge cases, and potential refinement of the performance-focused rationale based on Peff's feedback. The implementation appears carefully crafted but touches critical data integrity paths requiring careful scrutiny from multiple reviewers.
