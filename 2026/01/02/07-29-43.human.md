Performance optimization discussion about byte-swapping functions in Git's `compat/bswap.h`. Jeff King (Peff) provides a detailed technical analysis of Rostislav Krasny's proposed changes, which aim to modernize and simplify the byte-swapping implementation using `memcpy`-based type punning.

Peff replicates the original benchmarking setup but finds the performance claims may be test-specific artifacts rather than general improvements. His analysis shows:
- The dramatic 4x speedup at -O0 is irrelevant for production use
- At -O2, GCC shows no meaningful difference between old/new implementations
- Clang shows a 45% improvement in the original benchmark, but Peff demonstrates this disappears when testing more realistic aligned access patterns
- Both implementations ultimately compile to similar bswap instructions in optimized builds

While not opposed to the change for readability/maintainability benefits, Peff expresses skepticism about the performance claims being broadly applicable. The discussion remains technical and constructive, with Peff providing detailed assembly analysis and alternative test scenarios. This appears to be a thorough review from one of Git's most experienced contributors, suggesting the performance rationale may need refinement while leaving the door open for the change on other merits.
