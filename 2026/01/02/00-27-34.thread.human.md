Rostislav Krasny proposed modernizing Git's byte-swapping functions in `compat/bswap.h`, replacing manual bit-twiddling with `memcpy`-based type punning for better performance and maintainability. Initial benchmarks showed promising speedups (4x at -O0, 45% with Clang at -O2), though Jeff King (Peff) questioned whether performance was the primary motivation given these functions are rarely modified.

Peff has now conducted a deeper analysis of the benchmarks, examining assembly output and testing more realistic access patterns. While he confirms some speedups in specific cases (1.8x with Clang at -O2), he finds the most dramatic improvements may be artifacts of test conditions rather than fundamental gains. For aligned 8-byte reads - more representative of real usage - the performance difference vanishes. Peff remains open to the changes for maintainability but suggests tempering the performance claims. The discussion remains focused on careful evaluation of these low-level operations that are critical for Git's data integrity. Rostislav will likely need to address these findings in a revised submission.
