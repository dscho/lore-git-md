Rostislav Krasny proposed optimizing Git's byte-swapping functions in `compat/bswap.h`, replacing manual implementations with `memcpy`-based type punning for better performance and maintainability. Initial benchmarks showed significant speedups (4x faster at -O0, 45% improvement with Clang at -O2 on x86-64) while maintaining identical external behavior.

Jeff King (Peff) has now weighed in, acknowledging the performance gains but questioning whether maintainability is the right focus for these rarely-modified functions. He suggests emphasizing the demonstrated speed improvements instead. Peff's response indicates he'll provide more detailed technical feedback soon, likely covering benchmarking methodology and implementation specifics. As one of Git's most experienced contributors, his engagement signals the patch is being taken seriously but may need refinement in its justification and possibly additional benchmarking across more compiler/architecture combinations. The core technical approach appears sound, but the discussion is just getting started.
