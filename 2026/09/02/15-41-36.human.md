This is the latest review in a bugfix series that extends Git's protection against unsafe commit operations during conflict resolution. The series, now in its fourth iteration, blocks `git commit --amend` and `git commit <paths>` during `git am`, `git revert`, and all forms of `git rebase` when conflicts are present, preventing users from accidentally corrupting repository state.

Phillip Wood's review here is brief but positive: he approves the changes from v3 to v4, describing them as "good," and expresses satisfaction with all patches except the second one, where he has "mixed feelings." The second patch removes the restriction on `git commit <paths>` during conflict resolution for empty commits, a change introduced in response to Junio C Hamano's observation that the result would still be an empty commit and thus harmless. Phillip's hesitation stems from a broader design question he raised earlier: why allow partial commits during rebase for empty commits but block them during cherry-pick, given that both operations write `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` and `.git/MERGE_MSG`? This inconsistency remains unresolved, but Phillip does not block the series on it, calling the rest of the work "pretty sound" and a "useful improvement to our UI."

The only change in v4 is a mechanical rename to clarify intent: `FROM_REBASE_EMPTY` becomes `FROM_REBASE_NOW_EMPTY`, `is_from_rebase_empty()` becomes `is_from_rebase_now_empty()`, and `ONGOING_REBASE_EMPTY` becomes `ONGOING_REBASE_NOW_EMPTY`. This addresses Junio's stylistic feedback about whether the original names adequately conveyed that the commit *became* empty during rebase. The series is technically complete, with all substantive feedback incorporated, and is likely to proceed to `next` soon. The unresolved UI consistency question may warrant a follow-up patch or documentation update, but it does not affect the correctness or readiness of the current work.
