This is a follow-up discussion from Jeff King (Peff) in the thread addressing a memory leak in the submodule error path. The original two-patch series (already summarized) proposed making `repo_clear()` idempotent and fixing the leak, but Peff noted in the commit message that the `parsed_object_pool` API’s asymmetry—allocating via `new` but lacking a corresponding `free`—was a potential future cleanup.

Here, Peff explores that cleanup in more detail, reporting that it quickly becomes messy due to circular header dependencies. The core issue is that `repository.h` and `object.h` (which defines `parsed_object_pool`) cannot include each other without causing a loop, and `hash.h` also depends on `repository.h` when `USE_THE_REPOSITORY_VARIABLE` is set. He outlines two potential solutions: splitting `the_repository` into a forward-declaration header (which would require ~9 files to include `repository.h` explicitly) or decoupling `the_hash_algo` from `the_repository` by making it a standalone pointer. Both approaches reveal hidden dependencies where code relies on `hash.h` to implicitly provide `the_repository`.

Peff concludes that the complexity outweighs the benefit of removing the pointer indirection, at least for the scope of this bugfix series. The discussion is purely exploratory—no patches are proposed—and serves as a technical note for future refactoring efforts. It highlights the fragility of Git’s header structure and the trade-offs involved in cleaning up long-standing design quirks. No objections or alternative proposals have been raised yet.
