This is a follow-up exploration from Jeff King (Peff) in the ongoing thread about fixing a memory leak in the submodule error path. The original two-patch series—making `repo_clear()` idempotent and plugging the leak—is already under review and uncontroversial. Here, Peff digs deeper into a related but tangential issue: the `parsed_object_pool` API’s asymmetry, where the pool holds a redundant pointer back to its containing repository.

Peff observes that the `parsed_object_pool`’s `repo` field is never actually used for anything except circular access to `repo->parsed_objects`. The entire object lookup and allocation machinery only needs the pool, not the repository. To demonstrate, he provides a 60-line patch that removes the `repo` pointer from `parsed_object_pool` and introduces `_via_pool` variants of core functions like `lookup_commit()` and `lookup_object()`. These variants operate directly on the pool, while the existing functions remain as thin wrappers for compatibility.

The patch is technically sound—it compiles and correctly represents the data dependencies—but Peff concludes it isn’t worth pursuing. The churn is significant (six files touched) for minimal clarity gain, and the circular header dependencies between `repository.h`, `object.h`, and `hash.h` make any deeper refactoring messy. He notes that splitting `the_repository` into a forward-declaration header or decoupling `the_hash_algo` would require even more invasive changes, likely affecting around nine files.

This email is purely exploratory; no new patches are proposed, and the discussion remains a solo technical note. It highlights the fragility of Git’s header structure and the trade-offs involved in cleaning up long-standing design quirks, but it doesn’t alter the status of the original bugfix series. The memory leak fix itself remains straightforward and uncontroversial, with no objections raised so far.
