Subject: [PATCH] rerere: fix race condition between rebase and background maintenance
Series version: single patch (v1 posted; v2 planned as two-patch series)
Originating author: Thomas Bachem
Thread type: bugfix patch

Core problem or goal
Fix a deadlock or crash in the `rerere` subsystem introduced in Git 2.54 where background maintenance (`git rerere gc`)—triggered by the geometric maintenance strategy after every commit—races with an in-progress rebase to acquire the `MERGE_RR.lock`, causing the rebase to fail when the lock is held. The thread has evolved from a timeout-based mitigation to a two-part solution: (1) disable background maintenance during rebase via config overrides, and (2) fix error handling in `rerere_setup()` to propagate errors instead of dying.

Current status
Under review; v1 introduced a `rerere.lockTimeout` config and `RERERE_SKIP_LOCKED` flag, but this approach was rejected as insufficient. The planned v2 will disable `gc.auto` and `maintenance.auto` during rebase via `-c gc.auto=false -c maintenance.auto=false` for `git commit`, `git merge`, and exec commands, and will propagate `rerere_setup()` errors to the sequencer. No merge status reported; Junio and Patrick Steinhardt’s input pending.

Key technical details
- Files touched: `rerere.c` (error propagation), `sequencer.c` (config overrides), `t/t4200-rerere.sh`, `t/t7900-maintenance.sh`
- New behavior: Sequencer passes `-c gc.auto=false -c maintenance.auto=false` to all `git commit`/`merge` invocations and via `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` for exec commands; `rerere_setup()` returns errors instead of dying
- Lock acquisition: No longer uses `hold_lock_file_for_update_timeout()`; background maintenance is disabled entirely during rebase
- Test case: 20,000-entry `rr-cache` to trigger the race; focus shifts to verifying maintenance is disabled
- Architectural context: The geometric maintenance strategy’s aggressive triggering of `rerere gc` (after every commit with even a single `rr-cache` entry) exacerbates the problem, but this is not addressed in the series

Open questions and loose ends

2. Is the fork-based background maintenance design inherently flawed for interactive operations? (Phillip Wood’s critique remains unresolved beyond this fix.)
3. Will Junio or Patrick Steinhardt weigh in on the revised approach?

Participant positions

- **Phillip Wood**: Substantive review; opposed the timeout-based fix as papering over the root cause. Advocated disabling background maintenance during rebase via config overrides and fixing the sequencer’s error handling. His critique shaped the revised approach.

Related work
