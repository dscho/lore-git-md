This is a substantive review from Derrick Stolee responding to Elijah Newren’s patch series on improving `git push` performance from shallow clones. Stolee acknowledges missing earlier parts of the discussion but re-engages to address the trade-offs between Elijah’s approach (modifying pack generation in `send-pack`) and Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to enable `push.negotiate` by default.

Stolee agrees with Elijah that the scenarios where `push.negotiate` fails—such as shallow clones of rebased pull requests or pushes to unrelated repositories—are real and problematic. However, he challenges the assumptions underlying Elijah’s patch, particularly the idea that objects not advertised by the server might still exist due to delayed garbage collection or shared object databases. Stolee argues these assumptions are not reasonable defaults and that users should have a clear, recoverable way to handle cases where the assumption fails (e.g., retrying without the config setting).

The most critical point in Stolee’s review is his concern about the multi-ref push case, which he previously identified as a flaw in Elijah’s patch. He reiterates that the patch risks corrupting the receiver’s repository by over-excluding objects needed by other refs in a multi-ref push when one ref is rejected for shallow-update reasons. While HTTP servers are protected from this issue, Stolee warns that corruption could still occur over the `file://` protocol, making this a serious blocker for merging.

Stolee’s tone is constructive, but his feedback underscores the need for a safer alternative—likely his earlier proposal to repurpose `push.negotiate` for shallow repos—before the patch can proceed. The discussion remains focused on correctness and safety, with Stolee’s review shifting the emphasis toward minimizing risk rather than optimizing performance.
