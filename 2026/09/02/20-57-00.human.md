This is a **performance-focused bugfix** targeting the `git push` command when used from shallow clones. Elijah Newren responds to Derrick Stolee’s critique of his patch, which aims to eliminate redundant tree transfers (gigabytes in large repos) during pushes from shallow clones. The core debate hinges on whether the patch’s assumptions about server-side object availability are reasonable and whether it introduces correctness risks.

Elijah pushes back against Stolee’s concerns about the patch’s default assumptions, arguing that shallow pushes already rely on implicit assumptions (e.g., that the server has the parents of shallow grafts). He presents a matrix of scenarios showing that his approach—assuming the server has the shallow graft—yields better outcomes (faster success or failure) in all but one edge case (where the server lacks the graft but has its parents). He dismisses this edge case as rare and acceptable, emphasizing that the patch prevents absurd workflows (e.g., downloading gigabytes of blobs just to re-upload them to the same server).

On the **critical over-exclusion flaw** Stolee identified (where the patch could omit objects needed by accepted refs in multi-ref pushes), Elijah clarifies that `receive-pack`’s connectivity checks prevent corruption, regardless of protocol. The flaw would only cause unnecessary ref failures, not repository corruption. He also highlights a comical inefficiency in current behavior: shallow clones with `--filter=blob:none` may download huge blobs from a server only to re-upload them moments later.

The email advances the thread by:
1. **Defending the patch’s assumptions** as reasonable and beneficial in most cases.
2. **Downplaying the over-exclusion flaw** as a non-corruption issue (though still a bug).
3. **Reiterating the patch’s necessity** even if `push.negotiate` is enabled by default, due to race conditions (e.g., concurrent repacks) and protocol limitations (e.g., SSH without `GIT_PROTOCOL=version=2`).

The discussion remains **technically substantive**, with Elijah standing by his approach while acknowledging the need to address the over-exclusion flaw. The next steps will likely involve a v3 patch to fix the flaw or further debate over the trade-offs between Elijah’s config-based solution and Stolee’s `push.negotiate`-based alternative. The patch touches `send-pack.c` and introduces the `push.shallowExcludeBoundary` config (default: `true`).
