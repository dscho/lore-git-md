This is a follow-up in the `rerere` lock race thread, now shifting toward a revised solution. Thomas Bachem responds to Phillip Wood’s substantive review with a plan for a two-patch v2 series.

The first patch will make `rerere_setup()` return an error (instead of dying) when the `MERGE_RR.lock` is held, allowing the sequencer to fail gracefully and continue the rebase without `rerere` assistance at that stop. The second patch will disable background maintenance (`gc.auto` and `maintenance.auto`) for the sequencer’s `git commit`, `git merge`, and `exec` commands, preventing the race at its source. Thomas notes that this matches the behavior of in-process commits (`try_to_commit()`), which already skip auto maintenance, and that the merge backend never runs maintenance at all—even at the end of the rebase—because it doesn’t go through `finish_rebase()`.

The discussion also surfaces a secondary issue: since Git 2.54’s geometric maintenance strategy, every `maintenance run --auto` triggers `rerere gc` as soon as the `rr-cache` has even a single entry, regardless of staleness. Thomas flags this as a potential refinement for later, but won’t address it in this series.

Phillip’s preference for disabling maintenance during rebase is now the agreed direction; the open question is whether to add a single maintenance run at the end of the sequence for the merge backend. Thomas leans toward keeping the change minimal for now. The next step is to wait for Junio Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt to weigh in before rerolling.
