This thread is a two-patch bugfix series from Jeff King (Peff) that plugs a memory leak in Git’s submodule error path. The leak, flagged by Coverity after nearby code changes made it newly visible, occurs when `repo_submodule_init()` fails and a URL string allocated during repository setup isn’t freed. Peff also explored a deeper cleanup of the `parsed_object_pool` API—specifically, its asymmetric allocation (via `new` without a corresponding `free`) and a redundant `repo` pointer—but concluded both issues are too invasive to address in this series due to circular header dependencies and the risk of exposing hidden assumptions in the codebase.

The series itself remains under review and unchanged. The first patch makes `repo_clear()` idempotent (a defensive measure), and the second adds the missing `free()` call in `submodule--helper` along with a test in `t/t7426-submodule-get-default-remote.sh` to verify the leak is plugged. Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the series and suggested a minor robustness improvement (initializing `ret` at declaration) but explicitly noted it’s non-blocking. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, hasn’t weighed in on the bugfix but did respond to the exploratory discussion, reaffirming that the parsed object pool’s dependency on the repository’s hash algorithm is intentional and not easily decoupled.

The broader architectural discussion about reducing coupling between the parsed object pool and the repository remains open but theoretical. Patrick Steinhardt recently revisited the idea of passing only the necessary pieces (e.g., the hash algorithm) to subsystems rather than the full repository structure, but he ultimately abandoned the approach, likely due to complexity. No actionable changes or objections have emerged from this discussion, and the bugfix itself is uncontroversial and ready for merging once the minor suggestion is addressed (or declined). The thread highlights the project’s balancing act between subsystem isolation and preserving existing architectural commitments.
