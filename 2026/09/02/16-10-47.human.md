This is the sixth and final patch in Christian Couder’s six-part series introducing a new `parse-options` sub-API for early argument scanning. It converts `git fast-import` to use the new `early_scan_options()` function, fixing a long-standing bug where `--allow-unsafe-features` was silently ignored if it appeared after an option whose value was given as a separate argument (e.g., `--depth 5 --allow-unsafe-features`).

The patch replaces a hand-rolled early scan that stopped at the first non-option argument with a call to `early_scan_options_from_options()`, which builds the scan rules directly from the existing `struct option` array. This ensures the early scan and the full `parse_options()` call agree on which options take values, eliminating the misparsing. The patch also updates the documentation to clarify that `--allow-unsafe-features` must be spelled in full (abbreviations like `--allow-unsafe` are still accepted by `parse_options()` but not by the early scan, erring on the safe side).

The implementation is clean and idiomatic: a new `early_wanted` array lists the options the early scan cares about, and a trivial callback (`option_parse_early_allow_unsafe`) sets the flag. The test coverage is thorough, adding two new tests to `t/t9300-fast-import.sh` that verify the bug is fixed and the full-spelling requirement is enforced.

This is a **well-motivated, low-risk bugfix** that fits neatly into the series’ overall goal. The patch touches only `fast-import.c` and its documentation, and the changes are confined to the early-scanning logic. The NEEDSWORK comment is preserved but updated to reflect the remaining limitation (abbreviations), making it clear this is not a regression. The patch is **ready for review** and should attract little controversy.
