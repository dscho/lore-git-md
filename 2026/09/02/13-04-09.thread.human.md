This thread is a three-patch bugfix series from Yannik Tausch that fixes a long-standing edge case in Git's pathspec handling. The issue occurs when an exclude pathspec (like `:!unrelated/path`) appears first in the list, causing `common_prefix_len()` to incorrectly return zero as the common prefix length even when later positive pathspecs (e.g., `foo/bar` and `foo/baz`) share a directory. The series also addresses a memory-safety bug where negative pathspecs shorter than the common prefix derived from positive pathspecs trigger a heap-buffer-overflow in `match_pathspec_with_flags()`.

The series is now ready for merging, structured as three patches: Junio C Hamano's preparatory refactoring (making `match` and `original` fields in `struct pathspec_item` `const char *`), a memory-safety fix (this email), and the core fix for `common_prefix_len()`. The output-parameter design (`common_prefix_len(pathspec, &matched_prefix)`) is the agreed-upon solution, and no further technical changes are expected. The patch in this email specifically fixes the memory-safety issue by ensuring negative pathspecs are matched with a zero prefix, preventing out-of-bounds reads. It includes a regression test that verifies the fix under AddressSanitizer.

Key participants are Yannik Tausch (author) and Junio C Hamano (maintainer). Junio reviewed the patch substantively, proposed the output-parameter design, and contributed the preparatory refactoring. Yannik identified the bug, provided test coverage, and adopted Junio's approach. The series is narrowly scoped, addressing both the memory-safety issue and the common prefix edge case without introducing behavioral changes beyond the fixes. A minor unresolved detail about the output parameter type (`char **` vs. `const char **`) remains but is not a blocker.
