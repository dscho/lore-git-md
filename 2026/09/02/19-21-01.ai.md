This is a maintainer rejection of the `hooks.allowNoVerify` feature patch. Junio C Hamano declines the contribution, citing two main concerns: the potential for a "slippery slope" of ever-more-complex configuration layers to prevent CLI overrides, and the narrow scope of the feature (limiting it to hooks rather than addressing the broader problem of preventing accidental bypass of *any* verification step, whether implemented as hooks or built-in logic).

The email does not engage with the patch's implementation, test coverage, or documentation; it focuses solely on the conceptual design. Junio suggests that if the goal is to enforce discipline around verification steps, the mechanism should be general enough to cover built-in checks (e.g., pre-commit format validation) as well as hooks, and warns against introducing a new `hooks.` configuration namespace for a feature he views as too narrowly scoped. No alternative approach is proposed, and the tone implies the discussion is closed unless a fundamentally different design is presented. The patch is unlikely to proceed without a major rethink.
