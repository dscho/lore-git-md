This is a technical exploration by Jeff King (Peff) into cleaning up a long-standing API asymmetry in Git’s `parsed_object_pool` structure, where the pool holds a redundant pointer back to its containing repository. The discussion is a follow-up to his earlier two-patch series fixing a memory leak in the submodule error path, where he noted this asymmetry as a potential future cleanup.

Peff observes that the `parsed_object_pool`’s `repo` pointer is never actually used for anything except circular access to `repo->parsed_objects`, which is always the same as the pool itself. He proposes a refactoring that would eliminate the pointer entirely, replacing repository-based lookup functions (e.g., `lookup_commit()`) with pool-based variants (e.g., `lookup_commit_via_pool()`) and maintaining thin wrappers for backward compatibility. The included patch demonstrates the change, touching six files (`alloc.c`, `alloc.h`, `commit.c`, `commit.h`, `object.c`, `object.h`) and introducing about 60 lines of churn for what Peff describes as "a minor amount of clarity."

The key insight is that the object pool’s operations do not depend on the repository itself—only on the pool’s internal state—so passing the pool directly would more accurately reflect the actual data flow. However, Peff concludes that the refactoring is not worth pursuing at this time due to the amount of code churn required and the risk of exposing hidden dependencies (e.g., code that relies on `hash.h` to implicitly provide `the_repository`). The discussion remains exploratory, with no patches proposed for merging, and serves primarily as a technical note for future refactoring efforts.

No objections or alternative proposals have been raised yet, and the thread remains focused on the original bugfix series. The header dependency issues Peff previously outlined (circular includes between `repository.h`, `object.h`, and `hash.h`) remain the primary obstacle to any deeper cleanup. This exploration does not affect the behavior or on-disk format of Git, nor does it introduce new symbols or tests. It is purely an internal plumbing discussion.
