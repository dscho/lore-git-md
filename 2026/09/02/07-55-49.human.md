This is a CI/build system patch in the follow-up series to modernize GitLab CI's Asciidoctor version handling. Patrick Steinhardt replies to Jeff King's second patch, which replaces the manually installed Asciidoctor 1.5.8 with the system-provided version (2.0.20 in Ubuntu). The patch itself is uncontroversial and ready for merging, but Patrick raises two tangential concerns that do not block progress.

First, he notes a potential risk: by testing only a modern Asciidoctor version, the CI might miss compatibility issues with older versions that users still rely on. He muses about whether Asciidoctor offers a compatibility mode to flag features unsupported in earlier releases, but acknowledges this may be overthinking. The observation is speculative and not a call for action—just a thought experiment about long-term maintenance.

Second, he questions why the `concurrent-ruby` gem is still installed, as the patch removes the explicit Asciidoctor pin but leaves this dependency untouched. The gem was introduced in a 2024 documentation patch to support synopsis typesetting, but the commit message and mailing list discussion (as Patrick notes) do not explain its necessity. This is a curiosity rather than a critique; the gem’s presence does not affect the patch’s correctness or goals.

The patch remains a straightforward, low-risk maintenance update. It simplifies CI configuration by eliminating an outdated version pin and aligns the tested Asciidoctor version with what users are likely to have installed. Patrick’s comments are exploratory, not blocking, and the series as a whole is ready for Junio to pick up.
