Here’s the human-readable summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread fixes a race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem where background maintenance—triggered aggressively by the geometric maintenance strategy after every commit—can deadlock or crash an in-progress rebase by holding the `MERGE_RR.lock`. The solution is a two-patch series: the first makes `rerere_setup()` return errors instead of dying when the lock is held, while the second disables background maintenance during rebase by overriding `gc.auto` and `maintenance.auto` for all rebase operations. Phillip Wood and Thomas Bachem have now aligned on the approach, with Phillip emphasizing that the merge backend must behave identically to the apply backend by running maintenance at the end of the rebase to avoid leaving it disabled indefinitely.

The locking fix uses a 1-second timeout for non-maintenance callers (like `git commit`) and skips the lock entirely for background maintenance, preserving existing behavior while preventing crashes. If `rerere_setup()` fails, the rebase stops at the conflict but allows `--continue` to proceed normally. The deeper issue—the geometric maintenance strategy’s aggressiveness—will be addressed separately by Patrick Steinhardt, who plans to adjust `maintenance.rerere-gc.auto` to consider garbage-collection eligibility rather than just counting entries. Phillip still argues that garbage collection is useless mid-rebase, as all objects and `rr-cache` entries created during the rebase are too fresh to be collected, but the immediate fix focuses on disabling maintenance during rebase as a pragmatic stopgap. The v2 patch is expected soon, with the locking changes settled and the sequencer config overrides scoped to ensure consistency between rebase backends.
