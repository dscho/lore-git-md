Here’s the human-readable summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread fixes a race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem that can crash or deadlock rebases when background maintenance—particularly the geometric maintenance strategy introduced in Git 2.54—runs concurrently. The issue surfaces when `git rerere gc` (triggered after every commit with even a single rerere entry) prunes the `rr-cache` while another command is recording conflicts, leading to lock contention on the shared `MERGE_RR` lock. Thomas Bachem’s two-part series addresses this with a locking patch (now at v3) and a pending sequencer patch to disable maintenance during rebase.

The v3 locking patch, which has incorporated all review feedback from Junio, Phillip Wood, and Patrick Steinhardt, introduces a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1 second) for non-maintenance callers like `git commit` or `git rebase`, while background maintenance skips the lock entirely. Explicit `git rerere` commands now wait for the timeout before failing, and the patch includes safeguards against incompatible flag combinations. Thomas clarified that the race isn’t just about geometric maintenance’s aggressiveness but about any background `rerere gc` interfering with foreground operations, and that the lock is per-worktree, so concurrent writers in different worktrees don’t directly contend for it.

Phillip Wood has been the most vocal reviewer, initially opposing the timeout-based fix as a band-aid but now accepting it as a pragmatic stopgap. He insists on consistency between the merge and apply backends in the sequencer patch, requiring the merge backend to run maintenance at the end of a rebase to avoid leaving it disabled indefinitely. Phillip also argues that garbage collection is useless mid-rebase (since all objects and rerere entries are too fresh to prune) and raises usability concerns about background-task warnings mixing with foreground output, though Thomas notes that daemonized maintenance runs suppress their output.

Patrick Steinhardt identified the geometric maintenance strategy’s defaults as a contributing factor and plans a separate series to adjust `maintenance.rerere-gc.auto` to consider garbage-collection eligibility. Junio’s feedback has been fully addressed in v3, and the locking patch is now ready for final review. The sequencer patch, which will disable maintenance during rebase via config overrides, is still pending. Open questions include whether rebases should run maintenance at all, the sufficiency of the 1-second timeout, and Phillip’s broader critique of the fork-based background maintenance design. The series is under active review with no objections to the current approach.
