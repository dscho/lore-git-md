This thread addresses a race condition in Git's `rerere` subsystem, where background maintenance (like `git rerere gc`) prunes conflict-resolution cache entries while a foreground operation (such as a rebase) is actively recording conflicts. Thomas Bachem proposed a two-part fix: (1) a locking patch with configurable timeouts and unified error handling, now in its third iteration, and (2) a pending patch to disable background maintenance during rebase via config overrides.

The locking patch, which touches `rerere.c`, `builtin/rebase.c`, and related files, introduces a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1000ms) for `rerere_setup()`, replacing the previous hardcoded 1-second wait. Background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) now skips the lock entirely (`RERERE_NOWAIT`), while explicit commands like `git rerere forget` wait for the full timeout before failing. Junio Hamano recently weighed in, clarifying that commands should fail after waiting to avoid silent data corruption, and endorsing Phillip Wood’s concern about background-task warnings cluttering foreground output. The patch has incorporated all prior feedback from Junio, Phillip, and Patrick Steinhardt, and is now ready for final review.

The open questions revolve around deeper design issues: whether the fork-based background maintenance model is inherently flawed for interactive operations, and whether garbage collection is even useful mid-rebase (Phillip argues it isn’t). Thomas plans to address the latter in the sequencer patch, which will disable maintenance during rebase and ensure consistency between the merge and apply backends. Patrick Steinhardt’s separate effort to adjust the geometric maintenance strategy’s defaults may reduce the need for rebase-specific workarounds. For now, the locking patch is a pragmatic stopgap, but the thread’s unresolved critiques suggest longer-term changes may be needed.
