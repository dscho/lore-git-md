This is the second round of discussion on Jeff King’s CI/build system series (`peff/ci-asciidoctor-bump`), specifically the second patch that replaces the pinned Asciidoctor 1.5.8 installation with the system-provided version. The patch itself is uncontroversial and ready for merging, but Patrick Steinhardt raises two tangential concerns that do not block the change but could inform future CI policy.

Patrick first worries about forward compatibility: by switching to a modern Asciidoctor version (2.0.20), the CI build might inadvertently start relying on features unavailable in older versions, silently breaking builds for users who still use 1.5.8. He muses whether Asciidoctor has a compatibility mode that could warn about such regressions, but acknowledges this may be overthinking. The concern is noted but not pursued further in this thread.

He then notices that the patch still installs the `concurrent-ruby` gem, even though Ruby itself is no longer needed for Asciidoctor. The gem was introduced in September 2024 for Jean-Noël Avila’s synopsis-style documentation conversion, but the commit message and mailing list discussion do not explain why it is required. Patrick’s question about its purpose remains unanswered in this email; it is a loose end that does not affect the current patch but may warrant follow-up if the gem’s necessity is unclear.

The patch under discussion remains a straightforward, low-risk maintenance update: it replaces an outdated version pin with the system package, simplifying the CI script and aligning the tested version with what users are likely to have installed. The concerns Patrick raises are valid but orthogonal to the immediate change and do not require action before merging.
