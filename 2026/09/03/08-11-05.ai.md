This is a follow-up in the ongoing `rerere` lock race discussion, specifically Thomas Bachem’s reply to Patrick Steinhardt’s substantive review. The email clarifies the planned v2 approach for the two-patch series: one patch for `rerere.c` (locking and error handling) and one for `sequencer.c` (config overrides to disable background maintenance during rebase).

Thomas agrees with Patrick’s proposal to adjust `maintenance.rerere-gc.auto` to consider garbage-collection eligibility, but notes this is orthogonal to the rebase-specific fix. He also accepts Patrick’s locking improvements: using `LOCK_REPORT_ON_ERROR` for better error messages, unifying the `RERERE_SKIP_LOCKED` and timeout branches, and keeping a bounded wait (up to `rerere.lockTimeout`) for non-maintenance callers. The sequencer patch (disabling maintenance via config overrides) remains unchanged, as it addresses a separate layer of the problem.

The key technical details for v2 are now settled: `rerere_setup()` will wait up to 1 second (configurable via `rerere.lockTimeout`) before warning and returning `-1`, allowing callers like `git commit` to proceed without `rerere` rather than dying. Background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) will pass a timeout of 0, skipping the lock entirely. Error messages will be more descriptive, and the code will be simplified by merging the skip and timeout logic. Thomas plans to reroll once Phillip Wood has reviewed the updated approach. No new open questions are introduced; the focus is on implementation details and coordination.
