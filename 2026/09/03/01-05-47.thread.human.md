This thread is about a bugfix for the `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism in `git push`. Aleksei Sviridkin identified and fixed an uninitialized variable bug in the files backend that could cause the reflog walk to terminate prematurely when the remote-tracking ref has no reflog, such as in fresh clones. This could lead to false positives in the "remote ref updated since checkout" check, potentially allowing unsafe force-pushes.

The fix is minimal—initializing `timestamp_t date` to `0` in `remote.c`—and includes a new test that reproduces the bug. The patch has evolved through three versions, addressing all review feedback, including the removal of an `Assisted-by: LLM` trailer and relocating test cleanup. Junio Hamano, the maintainer, confirmed the fix is correct but requested clarification on the fallback value rationale, which Aleksei has now provided: `0` forces a full reflog scan for safety, while alternatives like "now" or a fixed age could risk premature termination. The patch is ready for integration, with no remaining technical objections.

The bug, introduced in 2020, is platform-dependent and was reliably triggered on macOS/arm64, though it could remain latent on other platforms due to uninitialized stack behavior. The reftable backend is unaffected. The thread also clarified Git's policy on AI-assisted contributions: the project does not accept AI-generated content in patches, even when labeled, preferring concise, human-written explanations. This is a well-scoped, well-tested fix for a security-sensitive edge case.
