This thread is about a bugfix for Git's `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism in `git push`. Aleksei Sviridkin identified and fixed an uninitialized variable bug in the files ref backend that caused the reflog walk to terminate prematurely when the remote-tracking ref had no reflog entries. This edge case—common for remote-tracking refs created by `git clone`—could lead to false positives in the "remote ref updated since checkout" check, potentially allowing unsafe force-pushes.

The fix is minimal (a single line in `remote.c`) and includes a new test in `t/t5533-push-cas.sh` to reproduce the issue. The bug was introduced in 2020 (commit 99a1f9ae10) and affects the security-sensitive `--force-if-includes` code path, though its impact varies across platforms due to the uninitialized stack value. The patch is well-documented and appears to have no side effects, making it a straightforward candidate for merging. No review feedback or maintainer decisions have been posted yet.
