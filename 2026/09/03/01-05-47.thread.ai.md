Subject: [PATCH] push: fix uninitialized variable in `--force-if-includes` reflog walk
Series version: standalone patch (v2 posted)
Originating author: Aleksei Sviridkin
Thread type: bugfix

Core problem or goal
The `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism in `git push` contains an uninitialized variable bug in the files backend that causes the reflog walk to terminate prematurely when the remote-tracking ref has no reflog (e.g., in fresh clones). This leads to false positives in the "remote ref updated since checkout" check, potentially allowing unsafe force-pushes.

Current status
Under review; v2 posted addressing Junio's review requests. The patch remains a minimal, targeted fix (one line in `remote.c`) that initializes the timestamp variable to zero, ensuring the reflog walk covers the entire local branch history when the remote-tracking ref lacks reflog entries. Junio's requested changes—removal of the `Assisted-by: LLM` trailer and earlier placement of `test_when_finished` in the new test—have been incorporated. The code change itself (`timestamp_t date = 0`) is confirmed correct and unchanged from v1. Junio additionally clarified Git's policy on AI-assisted contributions: while `Assisted-by:` trailers are not prohibited, the attribution "LLM" is considered uninformative and unwelcome. The policy (detailed in `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`) rejects content that appears AI-generated, sounds overly formal, or cannot be explained by the sender, emphasizing AI as a step-by-step guide or debugging aid rather than a source of copy-pasted solutions.
