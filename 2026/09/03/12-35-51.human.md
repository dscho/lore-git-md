This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about whether Git should reject branch names consisting solely of `@`. The thread remains a design debate rather than a patch review.

Bence Csókás pushes back against Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s citation of Junio C Hamano’s 2024 stance advocating deprecation of bare `@` as a synonym for `HEAD`. Bence argues that `@` is a useful shorthand, especially in complex expressions like `git range-diff long-branch-name{^..otherbranch,..@}`, and that removing it would impose a real ergonomic cost. The reply does not engage with the specific inconsistency in porcelain branch-name validation (where `@` is accepted as a branch name but cannot be checked out via porcelain commands) or with the proposed fix to tighten `check-ref-format --branch`. Instead, it frames the issue as a broader design question about Git’s syntax and user experience.

The thread now has two clear positions: one favoring stricter validation to prevent usability traps (supported by Peff and D. Ben Knoble, and reinforced by Junio’s 2024 remarks), and another defending `@` as a convenient shorthand. No patch has been proposed yet, and the discussion remains open-ended. The tone is civil but the disagreement is substantive, suggesting that any eventual patch may need to address both the validation inconsistency and the broader syntax question.
