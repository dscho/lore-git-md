This is a bugfix patch addressing a race condition in Git's `rerere` subsystem, where background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) can deadlock or crash an in-progress rebase. The discussion here is a follow-up to Patrick Steinhardt's substantive review, where Thomas Bachem outlines the planned v2 approach.

The revised solution combines two key changes: first, the sequencer (which drives rebase) will disable background maintenance entirely during rebase via config overrides (`-c gc.auto=false -c maintenance.auto=false`), addressing the root cause by preventing the race from occurring. Second, `rerere_setup()` will be modified to propagate errors instead of dying when it fails to acquire the `MERGE_RR.lock`, allowing the rebase to continue without `rerere` assistance for that step. The locking logic will use `LOCK_REPORT_ON_ERROR` for better error reporting and unify the timeout and "skip locked" branches by setting a timeout of 0 for the garbage collection case.

Thomas acknowledges Patrick's suggestion to adjust the geometric maintenance strategy's aggressiveness (via `maintenance.rerere-gc.auto`) but notes that this is a separate issue and won't eliminate the need for the rebase-specific fixes. The v2 series will retain a bounded wait (up to `rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1 second) for non-gc callers, balancing the cost of retrying against the risk of re-resolving conflicts. Phillip Wood's earlier critique of the timeout approach is addressed by making the wait optional for the gc case and ensuring the sequencer no longer triggers maintenance during rebase.

This looks like a well-motivated and technically sound fix for a real problem introduced in Git 2.54. The v2 approach is cleaner than the initial timeout-based patch, and the discussion suggests the authors are converging on a solution that both fixes the immediate issue and aligns with broader maintenance strategy improvements. The patch touches `rerere.c` and `sequencer.c` and includes test updates, making it a focused but important change for users of interactive rebase.
