This is a **bugfix patch discussion** in the ongoing thread about a race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem, where background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) triggered by the geometric maintenance strategy races with an in-progress rebase to acquire the `MERGE_RR.lock`, causing the rebase to fail. The email is a **substantive review reply** from Phillip Wood, clarifying and refining the agreed-upon solution for the upcoming v2 patch series.

Phillip confirms Thomas Bachem’s plan to implement two patches: one to make `rerere_setup()` return errors instead of dying on lock contention, and another to disable background maintenance (`gc.auto` and `maintenance.auto`) during rebase by passing `-c gc.auto=false -c maintenance.auto=false` to `git commit`, `git merge`, and exec commands. The key new detail here is **consistency between the merge and apply backends**: Phillip insists that the merge backend should also call `run_auto_maintenance()` at the end of a rebase, just as the apply backend does in `finish_rebase()`. This ensures both backends behave identically and avoids leaving maintenance disabled indefinitely.

The discussion also clarifies the error-handling fix: if `rerere_setup()` fails due to a held lock, the rebase will stop at the conflict (without rerere recording or replay) and allow `--continue` to proceed normally. This is a cleaner failure mode than the current crash, and Phillip agrees it doesn’t require rescheduling the pick. The focus remains on **disabling maintenance during rebase** as the primary fix, with the error-handling improvement as a secondary safeguard.

The email leaves one open question: whether to add a single maintenance run at the end of the rebase sequence. Thomas leans toward keeping it minimal, but Phillip’s insistence on consistency suggests this will likely be included in v2. The patch series is now well-scoped, with clear technical goals and alignment between the author and reviewer. This is worth tracking for anyone interested in the sequencer, maintenance subsystem, or `rerere` internals, as it addresses an architectural flaw rather than just its symptoms.
