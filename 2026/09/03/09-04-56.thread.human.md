This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s patch series that replaces the hard-coded 60-day cutoff in `git maintenance`’s `rerere gc` task with a dynamic heuristic. The goal is to reduce unnecessary invocations, lower overhead, and fix a race condition reported in GitGitGadget PR 2214. The series is currently under review in its first version.

The key change introduces a configurable threshold (`maintenance.rerere-gc.auto`, defaulting to 512) that triggers the `rerere gc` task only when the estimated number of stale entries meets or exceeds it. The heuristic samples the `rr-cache` directory, using a "17" prefix heuristic and scaling each sampled entry by 256 to estimate stale entries. The series touches `rerere.c`, `rerere.h`, `builtin/gc.c`, and maintenance configuration documentation, with new functions like `rerere_entry_is_stale()` and `rerere_gc_estimate()`.

Thomas Bachem provided a tested review confirming the series works as intended, validating the heuristic’s behavior with both synthetic and real-world `rerere` entries. Derrick Stolee’s recent review of the first patch engaged with the code’s structure, suggesting an alternative refactoring path for `prune_one()` but ultimately accepting the current approach as clean and necessary for the series. No blocking concerns or edge cases were raised, and the patch remains ready for integration. The series is self-contained with test coverage in `t/t7900-maintenance.sh`, and no open questions or loose ends have been identified yet.
