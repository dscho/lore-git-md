This thread is a well-documented bug report about a memory leak in `git history reword`, discovered by Kristoffer Haugsbakk. The leak, which surfaces under AddressSanitizer (ASan), was introduced by the merge of Patrick Steinhardt’s `ps/receive-pack-shallow-optim` branch (commit `13763ecf`, March 2026) and is present in the `master`, `next`, and `seen` branches as of September 2026. The issue occurs in the object database (ODB) layer, specifically in `unpack_compressed_entry()` in `packfile.c`, where 283 bytes are leaked during a `--dry-run` operation. The leak is tied to commit buffer handling and appears to require a "real repo" with packfiles or a commit graph to reproduce, suggesting it’s not a trivial or flaky issue.

Kristoffer’s bisect confirms the regression was introduced by the `ps/receive-pack-shallow-optim` merge, though the exact culprit within that branch isn’t pinpointed. The stack trace points to `repo_get_commit_buffer()` -> `odb_read_object()` -> `packfile_store_read_object_info()`, indicating the leak is triggered when Git reads and decodes commit objects. The report is actionable, with clear reproduction steps and a bisect log, but no fix has been proposed yet. The leak’s impact is currently limited to `git history reword`, but its presence in the ODB layer could hint at broader implications for other commands that interact with commit buffers or packfiles. Developers familiar with the ODB or packfile subsystems are likely best positioned to investigate and resolve this.
