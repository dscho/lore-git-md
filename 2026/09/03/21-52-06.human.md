This is a review of the final patch in Siddharth Shrimali’s `git repack --drop-filtered` series, now in its fifth iteration. The series as a whole is technically complete, has addressed all prior feedback, and is proposed for the `next` integration branch. Samuel Bronson’s email reports real-world testing of the feature and proposes a behavioral change to the safety guard introduced in patch 6/6.

Bronson’s feedback is substantive and tested. He successfully used `--drop-filtered` on a real `llvm-project` clone (not originally a partial clone) and found the feature effective at reclaiming space. However, he objects to the current behavior of **dying when a blob referenced by the index would be dropped**. His patch (attached) changes the guard from a fatal error to a warning, **skipping only the index-referenced blobs** while dropping all others. This makes the command more resilient in non-bare repositories with a populated worktree.

The proposed change is small but consequential: it shifts the safety guard from a hard failure to a soft warning, allowing the operation to proceed while still protecting index-referenced blobs. Bronson’s rationale is pragmatic: the current behavior is disruptive, especially after the slow `enumerate_promisor_blobs()` step (which lacks progress output). His patch also suggests future extensions: allowing `--filter=blob:none` and adjusting the test suite to match the new behavior.

This feedback does not question the series’ core design or safety model. The series remains ready for integration, but Bronson’s suggestion may prompt a v6 if the author or maintainer agrees the warning-based approach is preferable. The discussion is now focused on **user experience trade-offs** (hard failure vs. soft warning) rather than correctness.
