This thread is about a targeted bugfix to the `git-contacts` contrib script, authored by Aleksei Sviridkin. The script, which suggests reviewers for patches by analyzing `git blame` output, has long misclassified "boundary" commits—where `git blame` stops its traversal—as legitimate contributors. These boundary commits often didn’t touch the file at all, leading to false positives in reviewer suggestions. The fix explicitly ignores boundary commits and adds the `--root` flag to `git blame` to avoid mislabeling the initial commit or shallow clone cutoffs. Testing shows the change works: in 100 recent commits, 27 lists lost false contacts, 9 correctly became empty, and 3 revealed previously obscured names due to denominator shrinkage.

The patch is minimal, focusing only on the blame parsing logic and its documentation, and applies cleanly to the `maint` branch. It’s a standalone fix with no dependencies or controversy, addressing an issue present since 2013. The commit message and notes provide clear motivation, including quantitative analysis, and the author deliberately avoided unrelated cleanups (e.g., SHA-256 support) to keep the change focused. The updated man page now documents the boundary commit exception. No objections or further discussion are expected, as the fix aligns with the script’s intended purpose.
