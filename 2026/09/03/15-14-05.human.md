This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about whether Git should reject bare `@` as a branch name in porcelain commands. D. Ben Knoble weighs in briefly to support Bence Csókás’s position, reinforcing the argument that `@` is a useful shorthand for `HEAD` in complex expressions.

Knoble’s reply is surface-level but adds weight to the convenience argument. He acknowledges Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s citation of Junio C Hamano’s 2024 stance on deprecating bare `@` but frames it as a reference point for future patch proposals rather than a decisive factor. The tone is pragmatic, aligning with Csókás’s real-world usage examples and suggesting that any tightening of validation rules will need to justify itself against the ergonomic benefits of `@`.

The thread remains split between two clear positions: stricter validation (to prevent accidental branch creation) and preserving `@` as a shorthand (for usability). Knoble’s endorsement of the latter does not introduce new technical details or open questions, but it signals that the convenience argument has support beyond a single voice. The next step is still likely to be a patch proposal, which will need to address both the usability trap (inability to check out `@` via porcelain commands) and the convenience trade-off.
