This is a follow-up in the `rerere` lock race thread, specifically Patrick Steinhardt’s reply to Thomas Bachem’s v2 plan. The email confirms the technical direction for the two-patch series: the locking changes in `rerere.c` will retain a bounded wait (up to `rerere.lockTimeout`, default 1 second) for non-maintenance callers like `git commit`, while background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) will skip the lock entirely (timeout=0). This balances responsiveness with correctness, avoiding the rebase crash while preserving `rerere`’s conflict-resolution benefits.

Patrick endorses the unified skip/timeout logic and `LOCK_REPORT_ON_ERROR` improvements he previously suggested, emphasizing that the wait is a preexisting issue (only exposed by the geometric maintenance strategy’s aggressiveness) and that users would prefer a brief delay over Git dying or silently skipping `rerere`. He reiterates that the maintenance strategy’s heuristic (planned for a separate series) and the sequencer’s config overrides (disabling maintenance during rebase) are complementary but orthogonal fixes. The locking changes are now settled; the next step is Phillip Wood’s review of the v2 implementation.

Key technical details for the future AI summarizer: the `rerere_setup()` function will wait up to 1 second before warning and returning `-1`, allowing callers to proceed without `rerere` rather than dying. Background maintenance will bypass the lock entirely. Error messages will use `LOCK_REPORT_ON_ERROR` for clarity. The sequencer patch (config overrides) remains unchanged. The geometric maintenance strategy’s default (`maintenance.rerere-gc.auto`) will be addressed separately by Patrick, focusing on garbage-collection eligibility rather than entry count.
