This is a follow-up in the ongoing trace2 hardening series, where Jeff King (Peff) and Derrick Stolee continue to explore the architectural implications of eliminating `die()`-triggering functions from the trace2 subsystem. The exchange focuses on the limitations of the current approach and the feasibility of more comprehensive solutions.

Peff expands on his earlier critique, emphasizing that the `banned-die.h` mechanism—while a pragmatic first step—only addresses direct calls to banned functions (e.g., `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`) and misses indirect calls via helpers like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`. He suggests that a true "no-`die()`" trace2 would likely require a ground-up rewrite, as even a "safe strbuf" library would eventually need to surface allocation or truncation failures to callers, effectively creating a new string interface. His tone is measured but skeptical, framing the problem as one of dependency pruning: by the time you’ve made all dependencies safe, you’ve essentially rewritten the subsystem.

Derrick acknowledges the challenge but remains pragmatic, noting that updating dependencies to be "safe" is no easier than a rewrite. He shares a CodeQL query (vibe-coded, not production-ready) that detects transitive `die()` calls in trace2, underscoring the pervasiveness of the issue. Peff agrees that static analysis could solve the "whack-a-mole" problem of identifying indirect calls, but cautions that rewriting the code to avoid those dependencies would still be a significant effort. He speculates that trace2’s role as a passive data consumer (rather than an active participant in Git’s logic) might make it easier to isolate, but this remains untested.

The discussion leaves open whether the current series (v3) is sufficient as a first step or if it should be held for a more comprehensive solution. No objections are raised to the series itself, but the exchange clarifies that the hardening effort is far from complete. The CodeQL query and the idea of a "safe strbuf" or ground-up rewrite are now on the table as potential future work, though neither is tied to a specific timeline or owner. For now, the series remains under review, with the understanding that it provides incremental progress rather than a full fix.
