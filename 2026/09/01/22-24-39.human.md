This is the third patch in Elijah Newren’s five-patch bugfix series that tightens Git’s safety net during conflict resolution. It rewords the error message shown when a user tries to `git commit --amend` after an empty commit was dropped during rebase. The old message—“You are in the middle of a rebase—cannot amend.”—was generic and could mislead users into thinking amending is never allowed during rebase stops. The new phrasing—“The now-empty commit has been dropped—cannot amend.”—is specific: it tells the user exactly why HEAD no longer points to the commit they expected, so amending would affect the wrong commit. The change is tiny (two lines in `builtin/commit.c` and the matching test update in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh`), uncontroversial, and already received Junio’s explicit approval (“Much less awkward than the previous round”). It’s a pure usability improvement that makes the error message clearer without touching any logic.
