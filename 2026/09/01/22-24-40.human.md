This is the fourth patch in Elijah Newren’s five-patch bugfix series that extends Git’s existing protection against unsafe commit operations during conflict resolution. This patch specifically blocks `git commit --amend` when the user is in the middle of resolving conflicts for `git am`, `git revert`, or any form of `git rebase`.

The change is straightforward: it adds a new `sequencer_ongoing_operation()` helper that detects which operation is in progress, then uses a `switch` statement in `builtin/commit.c` to refuse `--amend` with a clear error message tailored to the operation (`"You are in the middle of a revert -- cannot amend."`, `"You are in the middle of an am session -- cannot amend."`, or `"You are resolving conflicts during a rebase -- cannot amend."`). The rebase merge backend is handled carefully to avoid blocking legitimate interactive rebase directives (`squash`, `reword`, `edit`, `break`): it only refuses `--amend` when `rebase-merge/stopped-sha` exists and `rebase-merge/amend` does not, which marks a genuine conflict stop.

The patch touches `builtin/commit.c`, `sequencer.c`, and `sequencer.h`, and adds thorough test coverage in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh`, `t3507-cherry-pick-conflict.sh`, and `t4151-am-abort.sh`. All substantive feedback from earlier iterations has been addressed, and Junio C Hamano has explicitly approved the series’ structural improvements. This patch is ready for integration and is uncontroversial.
