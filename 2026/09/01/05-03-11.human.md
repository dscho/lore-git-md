This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing trace2 hardening thread. Jeff King (Peff) clarifies his stance on the `banned-die.h` mechanism introduced in Derrick Stolee’s patch series. While he previously argued that the series only addresses direct calls to `die()`-prone functions and misses indirect calls via dependencies like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`, he now explicitly states that he is *not opposed* to the header as an interim solution. His tone is pragmatic: he acknowledges that the whack-a-mole approach (blocking individual functions) is not something he would prioritize, but he does not object to Derrick’s efforts to use it as a stepping stone toward hardening trace2. This does not resolve the broader architectural debate—whether a ground-up rewrite of trace2 is needed to fully eliminate unsafe dependencies—but it signals that Jeff sees value in incremental progress, even if he remains skeptical that it can fully solve the problem. The exchange leaves the door open for Derrick’s series to proceed while the long-term solution is explored.
