This is the final review for the `--linearize` option in `git replay`, a feature that flattens merge commits into a linear history. Elijah Newren, the project's foremost expert on merge machinery, has given his **Reviewed-by** to the v9 iteration, signaling the series is technically complete and ready for integration.

The v9 changes are purely cosmetic: replacing "multiple revision ranges" with "multiple branches" in error messages and documentation, and simplifying the rationale for the single-branch restriction. These tweaks address feedback from Justin Tobler and Elijah himself in v8, making the UX clearer without altering the underlying implementation. The core logic—dropping merge commits and reparenting subsequent commits onto the last non-merge commit—remains unchanged since v8, which resolved the blocking "multi-branch ambiguity" by restricting `--linearize` (and now also `--revert` and `--advance`) to single-branch usage.

The series is a three-patch refactoring + feature addition:
1. A helper function to track replayed commits (pure refactoring).
2. Moving replay base resolution out of `pick_regular_commit()` (pure refactoring).
3. Implementing `--linearize` with the single-branch restriction and updated documentation.

Key details:
- **Restrictions**: `--linearize` cannot be used with multiple branches or `--contained`.
- **Behavior**: Differs from `git rebase --no-rebase-merges` (documented and tested).
- **Compatibility**: Can be combined with `--revert` but not with `--advance`.
- **Test coverage**: Thorough, including edge cases like replaying to root and verifying error messages.
