This is a **substantive review** from Jeff King (Peff) on the final patch of D. Ben Knoble’s `dk/use-nsec-runtime` series, which graduated to `next` on 2024-07-10. Peff questions whether the `#ifndef NO_NSEC` guard around the `core.usenanosec` config handler in `environment.c` is necessary. His concern centers on a hypothetical edge case: if a user builds two versions of Git (one with `NO_NSEC` and one without) on a system that *does* support nanosecond timestamps, an index generated by the non-`NO_NSEC` build could store real nanosecond values, which the `NO_NSEC` build would then misinterpret as zero, leading to false "racy" comparisons. He acknowledges this scenario is unlikely in practice and that removing the guard wouldn’t eliminate the `NO_NSEC` build knob, so the current approach is reasonable. However, his comment surfaces a subtle correctness risk that wasn’t explicitly addressed in prior discussion.

The review is narrowly scoped to this single hunk and does not challenge the series’ overall design or readiness. The series remains technically complete and in `next`, with all prior feedback (performance, cross-platform correctness, architectural constraints) resolved. Peff’s observation is more of a "what-if" than a blocker, but it adds a new dimension to the platform-compatibility discussion. The author may choose to document this edge case in `Documentation/technical/racy-git.adoc` or leave it as a "don’t do that" footnote. No code changes are required unless the author or maintainer decides the risk warrants mitigation. The thread’s prior consensus (no runtime warning for `NO_NSEC` platforms, unconditional `use_nanosec` field) remains intact.
