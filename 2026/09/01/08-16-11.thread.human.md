This thread began with a quick fix to restore Ruby availability in GitLab CI after a dependency change broke the documentation build, but it quickly evolved into a small but meaningful modernization of the project’s CI configuration. Patrick Steinhardt’s original patch (`ps/ci-depends-on-ruby`) solved the immediate problem by ensuring Ruby was installed for `gem install asciidoctor`, and it’s already merged. The follow-up work, led by Jeff King, took a broader view: his two-patch series (`peff/ci-asciidoctor-bump`) removed an outdated version pin on Asciidoctor (1.5.8, from 2018) and switched to the system-provided package (2.0.20 in Ubuntu), simplifying the CI setup and better reflecting what users actually have installed.

The discussion wrapped up cleanly. Patrick Steinhardt, who started the thread, reviewed and endorsed Jeff’s patches, agreeing that testing compatibility with ancient Asciidoctor versions isn’t worth the effort—any issues can be handled reactively. Junio C Hamano also weighed in early, supporting the move away from the old pin. The only loose end is the `concurrent-ruby` gem, which was introduced in 2024 for Jean-Noël Avila’s documentation work but whose necessity remains unexplained in the thread. That’s a minor curiosity, not a blocker, and the series is now ready for Junio to merge. The changes touch only the CI dependency script and carry no risk of breaking user-facing behavior.
