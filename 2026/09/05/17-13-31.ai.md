This is the test patch in Aleksei Sviridkin’s two-patch v3 series, part of the ongoing effort to document the long-standing but undocumented edge case in `git cherry-pick --no-commit` where `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` is *not* created when the operation fails due to conflicts. The patch adds a single-line check (`test_ref_missing CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`) to the existing "advice from failed cherry-pick --no-commit" test in `t3507-cherry-pick-conflict.sh`, directly addressing Phillip Wood’s argument that the absence of `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` in this scenario is not currently verified by the test suite.

The test is narrowly scoped and uncontroversial in its implementation—it simply asserts that `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` is missing after a conflicting `--no-commit` cherry-pick, which aligns with the intentional behavior introduced in 2011 (commit d7e5c0cbfb). However, its inclusion remains the primary point of contention in the series. Phillip Wood argues the test is necessary to guard against regressions in the "tricky" logic of `do_pick_commit()` (where the `!opts->no_commit` guard determines whether `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` is written), while Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt oppose it on grounds of test suite overhead, framing it as a "random test" without sufficient justification. The author revived the test in v3 after Phillip’s latest feedback, but the broader policy debate—whether the test’s value outweighs its maintenance cost—is still unresolved.

The patch touches only `t/t3507-cherry-pick-conflict.sh` and introduces no new symbols, CLI options, or behavioral changes. It complements the documentation patch (part 2/2), which clarifies the asymmetry with `git revert --no-commit` (which *does* write `REVERT_HEAD`) and reframes the absence of `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` as a deliberate design choice. The test’s fate will likely hinge on whether the author can tie it to a larger effort (e.g., test modernization) or demonstrate a concrete risk of regression, rather than relying solely on completeness. For now, the series remains under review, with the test patch’s inclusion still an open question.
