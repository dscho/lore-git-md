This is a reply in the `rerere` race-condition bugfix thread. Thomas Bachem concedes Junio’s point about the trade-off between losing `rerere` recording and allowing a rebase to proceed. In v4 of the patch, he will remove the special-case behavior that let foreground commands (like `git rebase`) proceed without `rerere` after a timeout. Instead, all commands—including `git commit` and `git merge`—will wait the full configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, default 1000 ms) and then fail if the lock is still held, just as they do today. The sole exception remains `git rerere gc`, which will continue to give up immediately (`RERERE_NOWAIT`) because it is a best-effort cleanup.

The change simplifies the patch by dropping the `RERERE_LOCK_OR_DIE` flag and the corresponding caller-side hunks. It also aligns the behavior with Junio’s preference: a held lock during rebase will now stop the rebase at the conflict (without recording or replay) rather than silently proceeding and risking the loss of the user’s conflict resolution. The rebase can still be resumed with `git rebase --continue` once the lock is released. Thomas notes that this scenario should be rare, as the patch series already ensures that background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) gives way when it loses the lock race and the upcoming sequencer patch will disable auto-maintenance during rebase.

The default timeout remains finite (1000 ms) to avoid hangs from stale locks, but users who prefer to wait indefinitely can set `rerere.lockTimeout` to -1. Thomas also floats the idea of writing the rebase stop state *before* invoking `rerere` to allow `--continue` even if the lock is held, but he plans to explore that separately. The v4 patch will focus on the locking and error-handling changes already agreed upon.
