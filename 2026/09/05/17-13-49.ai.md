The author retracts their earlier withdrawal of the test (part 1/2 of the series) after realizing the existing test coverage does *not* verify the conflict case. The justification centers on the "tricky" logic in `do_pick_commit()`, where the decision to write `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` depends on three interdependent conditions: the command type, whether the merge started, and the `--no-commit` flag. The author argues this complexity warrants a dedicated test to guard against regressions, even though the behavior (introduced in 2011) is confirmed as intentional. The revived patch directly addresses Phillip Wood’s earlier advocacy for test coverage while still facing opposition from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt on test-suite overhead grounds.

Key technical details remain unchanged from the thread summary: the test targets the sequencer subsystem, references the `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` ref and `--no-commit` option, and touches only `t/t3507-cherry-pick-conflict.sh`. The unresolved debate hinges on whether the test’s value (protecting a subtle edge case) outweighs its cost (runtime and maintenance overhead). The author’s shift in position—from withdrawal to revival—adds weight to Phillip Wood’s argument but does not resolve the broader policy question. The documentation patch (part 2/2) remains uncontroversial and is likely to proceed regardless of the test’s fate.
