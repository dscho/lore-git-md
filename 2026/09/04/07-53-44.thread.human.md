This thread is about a small but important behavioral consistency fix for Git’s auto maintenance feature during sequencer-driven operations (rebase, cherry-pick, revert). Thomas Bachem sent a two-patch series (v2) that defers auto maintenance until the entire sequence completes, matching how the apply backend already works. The change prevents maintenance from running during intermediate commits or merges, which could interfere with the operation or create inconsistent behavior.

The series is technically complete and addresses all prior feedback. Phillip Wood reviewed the first version, suggested extending the deferral to cherry-pick and revert for consistency, and raised minor readability concerns—all of which were addressed in v2. The implementation touches the sequencer, config, and auto maintenance subsystems, adding a reusable helper (`git_config_append_parameter()`) to build and cache the `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` string that disables maintenance during the sequence. Tests verify that maintenance runs exactly once at the end, not during intermediate steps.

There are no open technical objections, and the series is ready for integration. The only loose end is a hypothetical edge case about very long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might be beneficial, but Phillip Wood considers this a watch item rather than a blocker. The patch is clean, well-tested, and maintains backward compatibility while ensuring consistent behavior across all sequencer commands.
