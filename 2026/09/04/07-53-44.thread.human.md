This thread is about a patch series by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when Git's auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations like `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git revert`. The goal is to defer maintenance until the sequence completes, matching the behavior of the apply backend and avoiding interference with intermediate steps.

The series is now in its second version (v2), consisting of three patches, and addresses all prior feedback from reviewers Phillip Wood and Patrick Steinhardt. The key change is that auto maintenance is now disabled during individual commands (like `git commit` or `git merge`) within a sequencer operation and runs exactly once at the end. The implementation includes a new helper function for building config strings and caches the config in the replay context to avoid repeated allocations. Test coverage verifies that maintenance runs only once at sequence completion.

Phillip Wood, who provided substantive review, has no remaining objections, and the series is considered technically complete. The only open item is a hypothetical edge case about very long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might improve performance, but this is noted as a watch item rather than a blocker. The patches touch the sequencer, auto maintenance, and config subsystems and appear ready to proceed.
