This is the latest update in Thomas Bachem’s two-patch series fixing a race condition between `rerere` background maintenance and foreground operations like rebase. The thread is now focused on finalizing the locking patch (v3) before the sequencer config-override patch is posted.

Thomas has trimmed the commit message significantly in v3, addressing Phillip Wood’s feedback about conciseness. He also corrects a behavioral oversight in v3: `rerere_clear()` (called by `git rebase --abort` and `--skip`) will now fail if the lock is held, matching the behavior of `git rerere clear` and preventing stale `MERGE_RR` entries from being incorrectly recorded. This change removes the need for special flags in `rerere_clear()` and `rerere_forget()`, simplifying the patch and leaving `am.c` and `rebase.c` untouched.

On locking semantics, Thomas clarifies the distinction between `git rerere gc` and other commands: `gc` is the only one that silently skips when the lock is held (since it’s a best-effort cleanup), while `git rerere`, `git rerere forget`, and `git rerere clear` fail after waiting for the timeout. Foreground operations like merge or commit warn and proceed without rerere if the lock is held, avoiding disruption. This aligns with Junio’s review, which emphasized that a held lock signals active modification of the `rr-cache` and should not be ignored by explicit user commands.

Junio and Phillip raised concerns about background-task warnings mixing with foreground output, but Thomas explains this isn’t possible: background maintenance runs are daemonized (closing standard descriptors and redirecting to `/dev/null`), and on Windows (where daemonization isn’t possible), the triggering command waits for maintenance to complete. The only warning users see comes from the foreground command after it gives up waiting for the lock.

The patch is now technically settled, with all review feedback incorporated. The remaining work is the sequencer config-override patch (disabling maintenance during rebase), which Thomas plans to post after addressing Phillip’s feedback on consistency between the merge and apply backends. The series is under review, with no objections to the revised approach, and looks ready for merging once the sequencer patch is finalized. The fix is well-motivated, thoroughly tested, and addresses a real usability issue introduced by the geometric maintenance strategy.
