This is a substantive review of the test patch in Aleksei Sviridkin’s two-part series documenting and testing the behavior of `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` during conflicting `git cherry-pick --no-commit` operations. The series aims to clarify a long-standing but undocumented edge case where `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` is *not* created when a cherry-pick fails due to conflicts, contrasting with `git revert --no-commit` (which *does* write `REVERT_HEAD`).

Phillip Wood’s review engages directly with the test’s design, not its motivation. He proposes a more robust alternative: instead of adding a new test case, the check for `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` absence should be folded into an *existing* test that already verifies the advice printed during conflicts. His suggestion uses `git show-ref --verify` (which fails cleanly if the ref is missing) and ties the check to a test that already confirms the cherry-pick failed *due to conflicts*, not some other error. This avoids the fragility of `test_must_fail` without an error-message check, which could mask unrelated failures. The review is constructive and technically precise, offering a concrete improvement rather than a rejection. It does not challenge the documented behavior or the series’ goal, only the implementation of the test.
