This is Junio C Hamano's **substantive review** of the v2 locking patch in Thomas Bachem's `rerere` race-condition series, responding to Phillip Wood's feedback. Junio engages with two key points: the behavior of `git rerere` commands under lock contention and the user experience of background-task warnings.

On locking behavior, Junio clarifies the intent of the timeout mechanism: after waiting, commands should fail rather than proceed, because a held lock signals that another process is actively modifying the `rr-cache` and expects exclusive access. He justifies the distinction between `git rerere gc` (which can silently skip) and `git rerere forget`/`git rerere clear` (which should fail loudly) by their user-visible semantics—GC is a best-effort cleanup, while `forget`/`clear` are explicit user commands that should not silently ignore failure. His framing reinforces Phillip’s earlier critique that the current documentation (`git rerere gc does not retry`) is misleading; all commands respect the timeout, but `gc` is the only one that proceeds without error.

On user experience, Junio endorses Phillip’s concern about background-task warnings mixing with foreground output, calling it "a very good point." This aligns with the thread’s broader discussion about whether background maintenance should suppress or redirect its output to avoid confusing users during interactive operations like rebase.

The review is concise but substantive, focusing on design intent and usability rather than implementation details. Junio’s input suggests the patch may need adjustments to error handling and documentation clarity, particularly around the behavior of `git rerere gc` and the visibility of background-task messages. His agreement with Phillip’s critiques adds weight to unresolved questions about the fork-based maintenance model’s suitability for interactive workflows.
