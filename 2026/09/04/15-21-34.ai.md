This is the **substantive review** of Thomas Bachem’s v2 locking patch in the `rerere` race-condition series. Phillip Wood’s feedback focuses on three main areas: commit message clarity, locking behavior, and user experience.

The review starts by critiquing the commit message’s motivation. Phillip argues that the race’s root cause is better framed as concurrent writers in different worktrees rather than the geometric maintenance strategy’s aggressiveness (which Patrick Steinhardt’s patches have already addressed). He suggests distilling the message to remove unrelated details and focus on the cross-worktree scenario, which makes the timeout-based solution more intuitive—conflict resolution is expected to be fast, while garbage collection can be deferred.

On locking behavior, Phillip questions why `git rerere`, `git rerere forget`, and `git rerere clear` fail immediately instead of waiting. He also suggests adding a `BUG()` check in `setup_rerere()` to catch incompatible flag combinations (e.g., `RERERE_NOWAIT` with `RERERE_LOCK_OR_DIE`), which would prevent subtle bugs in caller logic.

The most pointed critique concerns user experience: background `rerere gc` printing warnings to the terminal during foreground operations (like rebase) is confusing, as the messages mix with the user’s primary task output. Phillip implies this could be mitigated by suppressing background-task warnings or routing them to a log file.

The review is constructive and aligns with the series’ goal of balancing robustness with usability. While Phillip accepts the timeout-based approach as a pragmatic stopgap, his feedback highlights lingering design questions—particularly around error visibility and the fork-based maintenance model’s suitability for interactive workflows. These points remain unresolved beyond this patch and may resurface in follow-up discussions.
