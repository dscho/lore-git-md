This is a **tooling discussion** in the tail end of the `git checkout -m` autostash conflict handling series. The series itself is already approved and queued for `next`; this exchange is about Git’s style enforcement infrastructure.

Phillip Wood clarifies why Git’s GitHub CI workflow (`.github/workflows/check-style.yml`) runs `git clang-format` but does not fail the build even when the output deviates from the project’s style guide. The job was intentionally made non-blocking because `clang-format`’s output is not always stable across versions and does not perfectly match Git’s coding conventions. As a result, the CI job serves mainly as a suggestion engine rather than an enforcement gate, and few (if any) contributors actually review its output. Phillip suggests that the project could simplify contributor experience by fully adopting `clang-format`’s style decisions, but notes that this would depend on how closely its output aligns with Git’s current preferences. The discussion remains purely about tooling and has no impact on the readiness or integration of the autostash series.
