This is Junio C Hamano's reply to Thomas Bachem's v3 locking patch in the `rerere` race-condition series. The patch itself is a well-tested bugfix that adds configurable locking timeouts and unified error handling to prevent background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) from disrupting foreground operations like rebase.

Junio’s concern is narrow but sharp: the commit message currently says that when the lock is held, the rebase should “warn and go on without rerere” because “a lost recording or replay is nothing next to a rebase that cannot continue.” Junio disagrees with that trade-off. He argues that during a rebase, every chance to reuse a previous conflict resolution is valuable, and silently skipping `rerere` recording risks wasting the user’s time if they later discover their resolution was not saved. He would prefer that the rebase fail immediately if the lock is held, rather than proceeding without `rerere` and potentially losing the user’s work.

The code itself may already align with Junio’s preference—it fails after waiting for the timeout—but the commit message wording is misleading. This is a documentation-level issue, not a code change. Thomas will likely tweak the commit message to clarify that the rebase stops at the conflict (without recording or replay) and allows `--continue` to proceed normally, rather than silently skipping `rerere`. No rescheduling of the pick is needed, and the user’s resolution is not lost because the rebase remains paused.
