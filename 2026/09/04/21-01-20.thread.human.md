Tyler Cipriani’s two-patch series fixes a long-standing bug in `git push --force-if-includes`, where the safety check incorrectly verified the reflog of the *local* branch matching the remote destination (e.g., `main`) instead of the branch actually being pushed (e.g., `src` when pushing `src:main`). This could lead to false rejections or unintended data loss, particularly when pushing from a differently named branch or a detached HEAD. The series is now under review, with both patches submitted as v1.

The first patch corrects the core logic in `remote.c` to check the reflog of the pushed ref (`ref->peer_ref->name`), adds special handling for `HEAD`-based pushes, and rejects detached HEAD pushes by default (with an escape hatch for deletions). The second patch refines the advice messages for detached HEAD scenarios, introducing a new `advice.forceIfIncludesDetachedHead` config knob and ensuring consistent behavior across all transport layers. Tests cover edge cases like mismatched branch names, `HEAD`-based pushes, and detached HEAD states.

D. Ben Knoble’s review of the first patch is substantive and supportive. He initially questioned whether the false rejection scenario might be intentional but concluded that the patch’s approach—requiring an explicit bypass for unintended data loss—aligns with the mechanism’s purpose. He confirmed the fix preserves existing behavior for detached HEAD pushes and resolved his only nit (commit message clarity) by acknowledging the series context. The core change—consulting the reflog of the pushed branch—remains uncontested, and no open questions linger. The series is now awaiting further review or maintainer feedback.
