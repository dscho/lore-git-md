This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series from Tyler Cipriani that corrects a flaw in Git’s `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism. The issue causes `git push` to check the wrong reflog—it looks at the local branch matching the *remote* destination name (e.g., `main` when pushing to `origin/main`) instead of the branch actually being pushed (e.g., `src` when pushing `src:main`). This can lead to false rejections or unintended data loss, particularly when pushing from a differently named local branch or a detached HEAD.

The series is under review and consists of two patches. The first fixes the core logic in `remote.c` to consult the reflog of the pushed ref, adds special handling for `HEAD`-based pushes and detached HEAD states (rejecting them by default), and explicitly allows deletions. The second patch adjusts the advice message for detached HEAD scenarios, introducing a new config knob (`advice.forceIfIncludesDetachedHead`) and a `ref->unverifiable` flag to clarify why the push was rejected. The advice now guides users to either specify the expected remote tip with `--force-with-lease` or bypass the check entirely.

D. Ben Knoble, who previously reported the issue, has reviewed the series and confirmed the fix aligns with his understanding of the root cause. He initially questioned whether detached HEAD pushes should be allowed but accepted the series’ decision to reject them by default, noting that HEAD’s reflog is too generic to be useful. His review was substantive, reinforcing the design and leaving no open technical concerns. The only minor feedback was a small nit on the first patch, which the author can address or ignore. The thread has no remaining loose ends, and the implementation direction is now settled.
