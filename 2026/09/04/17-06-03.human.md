This is a **substantive review** from Junio C Hamano on Thomas Bachem’s `rerere` locking patch (part of the two-patch series fixing a race condition between rebase and background maintenance). Junio weighs in on two key design questions raised by Phillip Wood: the behavior of `git rerere` commands under lock contention, and the user experience of background-task warnings.

Junio agrees with Phillip that the commit message should be distilled to focus on the core issue—concurrent writers in different worktrees—rather than the geometric maintenance strategy’s aggressiveness, which he considers a separate (and already addressed) concern. He clarifies the intent of the locking logic: `rerere.lockTimeout` is about *waiting*, not retrying. After the timeout expires, commands should fail rather than proceed, because a held lock signals that another process is actively modifying the `rr-cache` and expects exclusive access. This aligns with the principle that locks are not just advisory; they enforce mutual exclusion.

On the specific behavior of `git rerere`, `git rerere forget`, and `git rerere clear`, Junio argues these commands should fail loudly if the lock is held, rather than waiting or proceeding. His rationale is user intent: these commands target specific entries (e.g., "this resolution is wrong, remove it now"), and failing immediately makes it clear the operation did not succeed. In contrast, `git rerere gc` can safely skip its work if the lock is held, since its goal is opportunistic cleanup ("discard old cruft if possible"). Junio’s distinction here is pragmatic—GC is best-effort, while explicit user commands demand precision.

Junio also endorses Phillip’s usability concern about background-task warnings mixing with foreground output. He calls it a "very good point," reinforcing that background processes (like `rerere gc`) should not pollute the terminal with messages that could confuse users during interactive operations like rebase. This suggests a need for either suppressing such warnings or routing them to a log file, though Junio does not propose a specific solution in this reply.

The review is measured but firm. Junio’s feedback does not reject the patch outright but signals that the locking behavior and error visibility need refinement before merging. His comments carry weight as the maintainer, and his alignment with Phillip’s critiques suggests the patch will likely require another iteration to address these points. The unresolved questions—how to handle lock timeouts for user-initiated commands, and how to manage background-task output—remain open for Thomas to address in v4.
