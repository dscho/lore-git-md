This is the sixth and final patch in Elijah Newren’s v3 series optimizing `git push` performance from shallow clones. It adds user-facing advice to help recover from the false-positive failure mode introduced by the core optimization (patches 4 and 5).

When a multi-ref push fails because a shallow boundary excluded an object needed by another ref, Git now suggests pushing the refs one at a time. The advice is gated behind the new `advice.pushShallowBoundary` config (default: enabled), and only appears when the push includes multiple refs and the optimization was active. The patch updates `send-pack.c` to track whether a shallow boundary was excluded and whether multiple refs contributed to the pack, then emits the advice if both conditions are met. The test coverage verifies that the hint appears in the expected scenario and that it is actionable (splitting the push avoids the interaction).

This is a usability follow-up to the earlier patches, addressing Derrick Stolee’s concern about the false-positive failure mode. The advice is clear and practical, and the implementation is careful to avoid noise (e.g., the hint only appears when the failure is likely caused by the optimization). The patch is small, focused, and well-motivated; it does not change the core behavior but makes the trade-off more palatable. If the series lands, this will be a welcome improvement for users who encounter the edge case.
