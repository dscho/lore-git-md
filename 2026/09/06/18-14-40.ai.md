This is a meta-discussion reply in the ongoing thread about Git's version numbering after 2.56. Brian M. Carlson weighs in on the readiness for a major version bump to Git 3.0, offering a substantive perspective on what remains to be addressed before such a transition.

Carlson identifies three key areas that should factor into the decision: (1) **SHA-256 support on major forges** (he mentions an upcoming update at Git Merge), (2) **libgit2's SHA-256 and reftable support**, and (3) the **lowercase-only object IDs series**, a breaking change he plans to re-roll soon and suggests soaking for one or two releases. He dismisses other SHA-256 or reftable work as unlikely to be ready in time, noting that JGit and Gitoxide were informed of the impending 3.0 transition over a year ago. He also explicitly excludes Rust platform support as a blocker, citing the lack of progress on platforms without existing Rust toolchains.

His conclusion leans toward option (3) from Junio’s original email — a cautious delay with a version like 2.97 or even 2.95, followed by a jump to 3.0 when readiness is clearer. This reflects a preference for a deliberate, staged transition rather than an abrupt version bump. The tone is pragmatic, focusing on what is realistically achievable in the near term while acknowledging that some foundational work (like the lowercase-only object IDs series) may need time to stabilize. No new technical details about code or subsystems are introduced; the discussion remains at the policy level.
