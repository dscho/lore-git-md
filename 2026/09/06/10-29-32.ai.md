This is a follow-up in the `rerere` race-condition bugfix thread, specifically addressing Junio C Hamano’s feedback on the v3 patch’s error-handling approach. Thomas Bachem responds to Junio’s concern that silently allowing a rebase to proceed after a lock timeout risks losing the user’s conflict resolution effort. Thomas argues that the conflict preimage can always be recovered by manually running `git rerere` at the rebase stop point, and that failing the rebase outright is unnecessarily disruptive—especially given Git’s existing limitations when restarting interrupted rebases (e.g., lost commit messages or author metadata). He proposes retaining v3’s behavior for conflict stops (warning + proceeding) while keeping the lock-timeout failure for post-resolution recording, `git rerere` commands, and `git am --continue`. The trade-off is now explicit: a warning at conflict stops preserves rebase continuity, while failures elsewhere prevent silent corruption of the `MERGE_RR` file. This reply advances the discussion toward a consensus on error-handling granularity, though Junio’s preference for loud failures may still prevail. Key technical details: the conflict preimage remains recoverable via manual `git rerere`; rebase restarts lose commit metadata regardless of this patch; and the patch’s current design already distinguishes between conflict stops (warn) and other operations (fail).
