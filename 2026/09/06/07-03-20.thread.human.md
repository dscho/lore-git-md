The Git mailing list is discussing what version number to use for the next major release after 2.56, scheduled for late September 2026. Junio C Hamano kicked off the thread by outlining four options—3.0, 2.99, 2.98/2.97, or 2.57—and asked whether the project is ready for a major version bump, particularly in light of the `BreakingChanges` document.

Brian M. Carlson has weighed in with a pragmatic take, arguing that a cautious delay (option 3: 2.97 or even 2.95) would be the most realistic path forward. He highlights three key areas that should influence the decision: SHA-256 support on major forges (with an update expected at Git Merge), libgit2’s readiness for SHA-256 and reftable, and his own lowercase-only object IDs series, which he plans to resubmit soon and suggests soaking for one or two releases. He dismisses other SHA-256 or reftable work as unlikely to be ready in time and explicitly rules out Rust platform support as a blocker, noting that platforms without existing Rust toolchains have made no progress. His position reflects a preference for a deliberate, staged transition rather than an abrupt jump to 3.0.

No decision has been made yet, and the thread remains open for further input. The discussion is still at the policy level, with no code changes or technical details under review—just a question of timing and signaling.
