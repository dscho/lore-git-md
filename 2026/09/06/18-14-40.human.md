This is a meta-discussion reply about Git’s next version number after 2.56. Brian M. Carlson weighs in on Junio’s four options, advocating for a cautious approach—either **Git 2.97** or **2.95**—to allow time for remaining breaking changes to settle before a 3.0 release.

Carlson identifies three unfinished items that could justify delaying 3.0: (1) SHA-256 support on major forges (he plans an update at Git Merge), (2) libgit2’s SHA-256 and reftable readiness, and (3) his own lowercase-only object IDs series, which he’ll re-roll soon and suggests soaking for one or two releases. He dismisses Rust platform support as a non-blocker, noting no serious progress on unsupported platforms, and argues that JGit and Gitoxide have had ample notice about SHA-256’s arrival in 3.0.

His preference for **option 3** (2.97 or 2.95) reflects a desire to avoid rushing 3.0 while still signaling an impending major version bump. The tone is pragmatic, focusing on ecosystem readiness rather than project-internal milestones. No code or patches are involved; this is purely a policy and timing discussion.
