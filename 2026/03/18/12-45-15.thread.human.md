This thread explores a performance optimization attempt for Git's commit-reach.c that initially appeared promising but turned out to introduce a regression. René Scharfe, known for his clean refactoring work, originally proposed replacing the priority-queue-based bitmap cleanup in the `ahead_behind()` function with a simpler bulk-free approach using `deep_clear_bit_arrays()`. The change seemed elegant, removing unnecessary heap operations while maintaining identical functionality.

However, benchmark results revealed the optimization actually made the operation about 10% slower. The bulk approach, while theoretically cleaner, ended up visiting more commit entries (including those with NULL bitmaps) than necessary. The original priority queue method, despite its heap operations, proved more efficient by only processing the few remaining unvisited commits. René promptly reported these findings and suggested alternative approaches - either reverting to the original method or trying an array-order traversal - though initial testing showed equivalent performance between these options. The thread serves as a valuable case study where theoretical cleanliness didn't translate to actual performance gains, demonstrating the importance of empirical validation even for seemingly straightforward optimizations.
