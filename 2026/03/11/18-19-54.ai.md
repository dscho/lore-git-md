This is a bugfix addressing zombie processes left behind when Git exits abnormally during network operations. The patch adds atexit handlers to ensure child processes (like git-remote-https and ssh) are properly reaped even when transport errors trigger immediate exits.

Andrew Au provides a concrete use case in response to Junio Hamano's question about real-world impact: a long-running containerized service that continuously spawns Git processes to monitor repositories. In this scenario, zombies accumulate over time rather than being cleaned up at container exit, with the author observing over 6,500 zombie processes before identifying the issue.

The technical solution (unchanged from v1) introduces symmetric atexit handlers in transport-helper.c and connect.c that:
- Track child processes via conn_to_reap and helper_to_reap variables
- Register cleanup handlers when children are spawned
- Clear handlers during normal cleanup to avoid double-waiting
- Ensure cleanup occurs even on abnormal exit paths

The patch appears technically sound and now has a clear justification for its practical impact. The author's response shifts the discussion from implementation details to demonstrating real-world necessity, showing this isn't just a theoretical concern but affects actual containerized deployments where Git runs as a subordinate process under PID 1. The fix maintains existing behavior for normal exits while plugging the zombie leak during error conditions.
