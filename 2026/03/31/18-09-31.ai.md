Maintainer feedback on reftable fsync abstraction. Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's patch that removed direct `fsync()` calls from the reftable stack implementation. While acknowledging the change is technically safe (since Git's reftable backend always provides an fsync callback), Junio questions whether silently succeeding when no callback is provided is the right approach. He suggests an alternative that would fail explicitly when no fsync mechanism is available, proposing either a hard error (via `BUG()`) or returning -1 to signal the missing callback.

This feedback introduces a design question about failure modes in the reftable library - whether to fail loudly when required functionality is missing or silently succeed. The discussion touches on the library's contract with its callers and what behavior is least surprising. Junio's comment indicates the patch may need revision before merging, though the core portability goal remains uncontested. The exchange shows the maintainer engaging with the series at a design level while the technical foundation (abstracting system calls) remains sound.
