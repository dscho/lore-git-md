This is a follow-up in the `git checkout -m` autostash feature series, where the author Harald Nordgren responds to maintainer Junio Hamano's positive feedback with some hesitation about the implementation's robustness. 

While Junio had praised the approach of using the real operation's failure as a trigger for the stash/merge/unstash fallback (an optimization suggested by reviewer Phillip Wood), Harald reports encountering some cases where `git stash pop` worked unexpectedly after conflicts. This suggests there may still be edge cases to address before the feature is production-ready, despite the maintainer's approval of the core approach.

The exchange highlights the careful balance between technical approval and real-world testing - while the architectural decisions have been validated, practical experience reveals scenarios that may need additional handling. Given Junio's earlier strong endorsement of the unified `-m` behavior approach, this is likely to be addressed through iteration rather than a fundamental redesign.
