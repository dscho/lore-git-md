This is a follow-up in the `git checkout -m` autostash feature series, where the author Harald Nordgren expresses some doubts about the implementation's robustness despite maintainer approval. 

Junio Hamano had just praised the current approach (particularly the removal of redundant dry-run checks) and indicated the implementation looked ready to merge after final polish. However, Nordgren now reports encountering cases where `git stash pop` resolved conflicts automatically after the checkout, suggesting the behavior may not be fully production-ready yet.

The exchange reveals an interesting dynamic - while the maintainer sees the technical approach as sound based on his typical usage patterns, the original author (who presumably has done more extensive testing) is observing edge cases where the conflict resolution doesn't behave as intended. This suggests the implementation may need more real-world testing before finalizing, despite the architectural approval.

From a technical perspective, the cases where `stash pop` resolves conflicts automatically could indicate either:
1. The conflicts weren't actually conflicts (false positives in the initial detection)
2. The stash application is being too aggressive in its conflict resolution
3. Some interaction between the checkout merge and stash application that needs tighter coordination

The thread appears to be moving toward acceptance, but this latest note suggests the author wants to investigate these edge cases further before considering the work complete.
