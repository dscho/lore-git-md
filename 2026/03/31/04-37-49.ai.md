This is a follow-up in the l10n (localization) thread about AI-assisted translation workflows, specifically reporting on real-world testing results. Jiang Xin, the Chinese translation maintainer, shares empirical data from using the proposed AI tooling to translate `po/zh_CN.po`.

The email provides concrete metrics from an AI-assisted translation session covering 177 entries, achieving a 98/100 review score (170 perfect translations, 7 with issues). It identifies a specific pattern of unnecessary positional syntax (%n$) in placeholders that required manual correction, with examples shown in the diff. Jiang Xin notes this will be addressed in an upcoming update to `po/AGENTS.md`.

Key technical details include:
- The test was run against current master (post-patch 1 merge)
- Review used the same AI-assisted workflow documented in the series
- Found 1 critical, 3 major, and 3 minor issues in the 7 imperfect translations
- Placeholder ordering was the primary source of false positives
- The workflow produced both human-readable (`review-output.po`) and machine-readable (`review-result.json`) outputs

The results strongly support the efficiency claims from earlier in the thread, though they also show the continued need for human oversight - particularly for stylistic choices like placeholder syntax. This reinforces the hybrid human/AI workflow design proposed in the series. The email doesn't directly address the outstanding legal/DCO concerns but provides valuable empirical validation of the technical approach.
