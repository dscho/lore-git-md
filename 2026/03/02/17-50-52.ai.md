This is a performance optimization discussion in the ongoing investigation of reftable backend regressions. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt's benchmarking setup with a technical analysis of the `receive-pack` slowdown.

Peff identifies that the performance regression stems from unnecessary sideband muxer setup/teardown for each `update` hook invocation, even when no hook exists. His testing shows the slowdown disappears when disabling sideband processing or adding an early exit when no hook is found. He proposes a preliminary fix that skips hook processing entirely when no `update` hook exists, noting this could be generalized to other hooks.

The discussion remains focused on the technical root cause rather than broader implications. Peff's analysis builds on Patrick's earlier benchmarking work, providing a specific code-level explanation for the 2.66x slowdown observed with the reftable backend. The proposed solution is presented as a starting point that may need refinement to properly integrate with Git's hook infrastructure.
