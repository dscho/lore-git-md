This is the fourth patch in a series implementing responsive design improvements for kernel.org, focusing on layout adjustments for the extras and footer sections. The patch comes from Rito Rhymes, who has been leading this cross-project effort to make kernel.org more mobile-friendly, building on their prior work with GitGitGadget and gitweb.

The patch specifically targets overflow issues that occur below 848px viewport width, where fixed-width elements in the extras and footer sections were causing horizontal scrolling. The solution introduces responsive CSS techniques:
- Converting the extras section to a wrapping flexbox
- Stacking blogroll and social blocks to full width
- Making donor logos wrap naturally by removing manual line breaks
- Adding progressive refinements at 550px and 400px breakpoints to adjust column counts

The changes are surgical - focused on fixing layout issues while preserving the desktop experience. The implementation follows Rito's established approach of targeted, incremental improvements validated through prior work. The patch is part of a carefully sequenced series where each change builds on the foundation established by earlier patches (viewport meta tag and basic CSS adjustments).

Technical details include new flexbox layouts, media queries for breakpoint-specific adjustments, and careful handling of donor logos to maintain their visibility while allowing natural reflow. The patch demonstrates Rito's expertise in responsive design patterns that maintain content structure across device sizes.
