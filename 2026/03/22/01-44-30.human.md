This is a performance optimization follow-up in the strbuf_getwholeline() discussion.

Jeff King (Peff) responds to René Scharfe's concerns about behavioral consistency between the getdelim() and non-getdelim() implementations of strbuf_getwholeline(). The core issue revolves around how partial reads should be handled when errors occur - whether to preserve the partial data (current non-getdelim behavior) or discard it (getdelim behavior).

Peff notes that getdelim() implementations likely return partial read counts before indicating errors, though he acknowledges this may vary across platforms. He makes the pragmatic observation that once a FILE handle errors, the partial data becomes somewhat irrelevant since retries aren't realistically possible. This suggests the behavioral difference may not be as critical in practice as the theoretical discussion implies.

The exchange represents the winding down of a technical discussion that has already achieved its main goals - removing unnecessary NULL checks and improving performance. What remains are minor philosophical questions about error handling consistency that don't fundamentally impact the function's safety or performance benefits. Both contributors seem to recognize this as the natural conclusion point for the thread.
