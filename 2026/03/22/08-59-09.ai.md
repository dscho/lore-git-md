Eric Sunshine responds to Mateo Patino's RFC about `STRBUF_INIT_CONST`, providing important historical context about why previous attempts at this optimization failed. This is a review response in the ongoing discussion about whether to pursue this strbuf optimization as a GSoC project.

Sunshine clarifies that past submissions weren't rejected arbitrarily - they stalled because authors didn't address reviewer feedback. He highlights Jeff King's (Peff) fundamental objection from 2020: the strbuf API guarantees its buffer is always writable, while `STRBUF_INIT_CONST` would violate this by pointing to read-only string literals. This creates an API contract violation that would require auditing all strbuf consumers, making the change impractical.

The response suggests this isn't just a matter of implementation approach - there's a deep architectural incompatibility with how strbuf is designed to work. Unless Mateo can propose a fundamentally different solution that preserves strbuf's writability guarantee, Sunshine implies this effort may remain a dead end. The email serves as a reality check about the technical hurdles, while still leaving room for innovative approaches that could overcome them.
