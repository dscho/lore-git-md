This is a performance optimization series from Patrick Steinhardt addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. The series has evolved through three versions, with v3 focusing on refinements to the `writev()`-based approach for reducing write syscalls.

The core optimization reduces write syscalls from ~400k to ~44k when cloning the Linux repository. The series introduces several key improvements:
- A new patch (3/10) that prefers flushing buffered data over sending keepalive packets
- Fixed handling of `iov_len` overflows in the `writev()` wrapper
- Continued use of `writev()` for pktline batching after considering brian m. carlson's empirical data showing its benefits
- Integration of Jeff King's (Peff) feedback on buffer fill thresholds and keepalive timing

The changes span multiple subsystems (`upload-pack`, `pack-objects`, `sideband`, `csum-file`, `wrapper`) with careful attention to cross-platform compatibility (POSIX, Windows, NonStop). The series maintains all safety and portability considerations while preserving the substantial performance improvements demonstrated in earlier versions.

Notable technical aspects include:
- The `hashfd_ext()` interface for hashfile operations
- Platform-compatible `writev()` wrappers with integer overflow protection
- Buffer management strategies in `upload-pack` and `pack-objects`
- Sideband pktline batching via `writev()`

The discussion has narrowed to implementation refinements, with the main remaining point being Peff's question about whether the `upload-pack` buffering layer is necessary given upstream buffering. Patrick maintains it helps pack-objects hook users without regressing the unbuffered case.
