This is a performance optimization patch in Patrick Steinhardt's ongoing series to reduce lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. The specific patch addresses keepalive timing adjustments when buffering data, responding to Jeff King's (Peff) review feedback about edge cases.

The key change modifies how `upload-pack` calculates keepalive timeouts when buffering data. Instead of using fixed intervals, it now tracks time since the last data transmission and adjusts the polling timeout accordingly. This prevents extended periods without data transmission that could trigger client timeouts, particularly important as subsequent patches will make buffering more aggressive.

Peff raised two theoretical concerns: clock reset edge cases (where time going backwards could cause extended waits) and partial line writes in packfile URIs. Patrick acknowledges these are unlikely in practice, noting the use of monotonic clocks on most platforms (via `getnanotime()`) mitigates the clock issue. The discussion reflects the careful balancing act between theoretical correctness and practical performance gains that characterizes this optimization series.

The patch maintains the series' focus on measurable performance improvements while addressing reviewer concerns about edge cases. It's part of the broader effort to reduce write syscalls from ~400k to ~44k when cloning the Linux repository, with three competing implementation approaches still under discussion in the thread (`writev()`-based, Peff's buffer management, and Johannes Sixt's simpler writes).
