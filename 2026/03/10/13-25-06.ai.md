This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series targeting lock contention in `git-upload-pack`. The patch focuses on reducing write syscalls when writing packfile data in `git pack-objects --stdout`.

The key change increases the buffer size from 8KB to ~64KB (LARGE_PACKET_DATA_MAX - 1) when writing packfiles with progress reporting. This is motivated by several factors: packfiles are typically large (megabytes/gigabytes), modern networks can handle higher bandwidths, and the progress updates remain frequent enough at this size. The specific size was chosen to align with pktline chunking used in git-upload-pack.

The patch demonstrates significant syscall reduction (355k to 44k when packing Linux repo) while maintaining progress visibility. Though the performance gain is modest on idle systems, it substantially reduces kernel lock contention in high-concurrency scenarios. The change builds on earlier work in the series that introduced the `hashfd_ext()` interface for buffer management.

This completes the series' technical implementation, with all subsystems (`upload-pack`, `pack-objects`, `sideband`, `csum-file`) now using optimized `writev()`-based buffering. The remaining discussion point (from Peff) about whether `upload-pack` needs its own buffering layer remains unresolved but doesn't block this change.
