This is part 3/10 of Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series targeting lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. The patch focuses on improving keepalive handling when buffering data.

The change modifies `upload-pack.c` to prioritize flushing buffered data over sending empty keepalive packets when both are possible. When a keepalive timeout occurs but there's pending data in the buffer (and the packfile transmission has started), the patch now flushes that data instead of sending a protocol-mandated empty pktline. This maintains the keepalive functionality while reducing protocol overhead.

The implementation tracks whether packfile data has started (`packfile_started`) and checks the buffer fill level (`output_state->used > 1`) to determine when to flush. The buffer management preserves the last byte (used for pktline framing) when flushing partial contents. The change preserves all existing behavior when no data is buffered or when operating without a sideband channel.

This is a logical optimization within the series' broader goal of reducing write syscalls, though as Patrick notes in the commit message, the practical impact may be limited since keepalives typically occur before pack data starts flowing. The patch incorporates feedback from Jeff King (Peff) and maintains the series' focus on measurable performance improvements while handling edge cases correctly.
