This is a follow-up discussion about signature handling in `git fast-import`, specifically addressing naming and behavior of the recently merged `re-sign-if-invalid` mode.

Junio C Hamano responds to Justin Tobler's consideration of the `re-sign-if-invalid` naming, agreeing that the "re-" prefix may be redundant since the operation creates a fresh signature regardless of the previous state. Junio suggests the simpler `sign-if-invalid` would be more accurate, as the "if-invalid" qualifier already implies the signature was present but invalid.

The discussion expands beyond naming to consider broader use cases. Junio notes the current implementation's limitation - it only handles re-signing commits that were previously signed (even if invalid), not unsigned commits. He suggests it might be more flexible to separate the concerns: using `--signed-commits=strip-if-invalid` to remove bad signatures while adding a separate `--sign-commits` option to sign previously unsigned commits during history rewriting.

This builds on the recently merged three-patch series that added signature handling modes to `git fast-import`, with Justin Tobler implementing Christian Couder's original proposal. The core functionality remains stable, but this exchange highlights potential future directions for the feature, particularly around handling mixed signed/unsigned histories during bulk operations. The naming question appears likely to be resolved in favor of `sign-if-invalid`, though no immediate patch is promised.
