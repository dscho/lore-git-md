Performance optimization series addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. This is the third iteration of Patrick Steinhardt's work to reduce write syscalls in the packfile writing path, now expanded to 10 patches.

The series focuses on reducing lock contention when multiple `git-upload-pack` processes write data simultaneously. Key improvements in v3 include:
- Added overflow protection in the `writev()` wrapper
- New patch preferring data flushing over keepalive packets
- Continued refinements to buffer management and keepalive logic

The changes span multiple subsystems (`upload-pack`, `pack-objects`, `sideband`, `csum-file`) and introduce a new `hashfd_ext()` interface. Performance gains remain substantial - reducing write syscalls from ~400k to ~44k when cloning the Linux repository.

The technical discussion has converged on the `writev()` approach after considering platform compatibility and performance data. Remaining points under discussion include whether the `upload-pack` buffering layer is necessary given upstream buffering, with Patrick maintaining it helps pack-objects hook users without regressing unbuffered cases.

This is a well-motivated series with clear performance improvements, though some reviewers question whether all the complexity is justified. The patches show careful attention to cross-platform safety and edge cases.
