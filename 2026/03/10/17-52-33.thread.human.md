Patrick Steinhardt continues his work on improving Git's build system with an 8-part series that does two main things: reorganizes build-related tooling into a new "tools/" directory, and adds precompiled header (PCH) support to speed up compilation. The changes are now in their second iteration (v2) with all technical feedback addressed, showing promising 40% build speed improvements in testing.

The series has seen thorough review from several key developers. Phillip Wood provided detailed feedback on the PCH implementation, particularly around compatibility with the reftable backend, leading to additional documentation in the commit messages. Junio Hamano has expressed some concern about how moving Coccinelle scripts might affect workflows, but hasn't raised blocking objections. The latest patch makes a mechanical change to git-compat-util.h to ensure warning suppression works correctly with PCHs - a pragmatic solution that avoids more disruptive changes.

With the technical aspects now settled, the series appears ready for merging. The remaining open questions are minor - monitoring real-world PCH behavior across platforms and any follow-up needed for Coccinelle users. This continues Patrick's pattern of careful, incremental infrastructure improvements that maintain compatibility while delivering measurable performance gains.
