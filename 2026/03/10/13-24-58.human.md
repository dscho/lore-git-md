This is part 2/10 of Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack`. The patch focuses on improving keepalive packet timing when buffering pack data.

The change modifies how `upload-pack` calculates when to send keepalive packets during packfile transmission. Previously, keepalives were sent based purely on poll() timeouts, which could lead to unnecessarily long gaps in communication when data was being buffered. The new approach tracks the last time data was actually sent to the client and adjusts keepalive timing accordingly.

Key technical changes:
- Adds tracking of last data transmission time via `last_sent_ms`
- Modifies poll() timeout calculation to account for buffering delays
- Passes a `did_send_data` flag through the data relay pipeline
- Maintains the same keepalive semantics but with more accurate timing

The patch is part of a larger effort to reduce write syscall contention in high-concurrency scenarios. While the keepalive change itself is relatively small, it's an important piece of the overall optimization strategy that has already shown significant improvements (reducing write syscalls from ~400k to ~44k when cloning the Linux repo). The implementation appears clean and well-motivated, with clear commit message explaining both the current behavior and the improvement.
