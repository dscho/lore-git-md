This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series targeting lock contention in `git-upload-pack`. The patch focuses on reducing write syscalls when writing packfile data through `git pack-objects --stdout`.

The key change increases the buffer size from 8KB to ~64KB (LARGE_PACKET_DATA_MAX - 1) when writing packfiles with progress reporting. This provides a significant reduction in write syscalls (from 355,000 to 44,000 when packing the Linux repository) while maintaining reasonable progress updates. The buffer size was chosen to align with pktline chunking used in git-upload-pack.

The patch builds on earlier work in the series that introduced `hashfd_ext()` as a more flexible interface for buffer management. While the performance improvement is modest on idle systems, the main benefit comes from reducing kernel lock contention on busy servers handling many concurrent clones/fetches.

This appears to be a well-considered optimization that balances progress reporting frequency with syscall reduction, particularly for the common case where pack-objects is used by upload-pack. The change is small and focused, with clear justification for the chosen buffer size.
