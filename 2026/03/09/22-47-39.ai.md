Test modernization discussion about assertion verbosity in prereq checks. Jeff King weighs in on a thread where Junio C Hamano had questioned whether converting `test -e` to `test_path_exists` in lazy prereq definitions was appropriate, since the latter fails loudly when the path doesn't exist.

Jeff argues that the verbose failure from `test_path_exists` could actually be helpful for debugging when a prereq isn't met, since it makes the reason for skipping clearer. He shows example output demonstrating how the two approaches differ in verbosity, noting that with `-v` the failure reason is more explicit. While Junio had expressed concern about the loud failure being inappropriate for a prereq check, Jeff suggests the extra information could be valuable for diagnosing why tests are being skipped.

This is part of the ongoing test modernization effort, highlighting a subtle distinction between quiet environment probing versus explicit assertions in test infrastructure. The discussion touches on tradeoffs between silent skipping and explicit failure reporting in test prerequisites.
