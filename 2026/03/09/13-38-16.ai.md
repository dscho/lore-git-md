This is a follow-up in the RFC series adding remote group support to `git push`, responding to design questions about `push.default=simple` behavior and force-with-lease semantics. Maintainer Junio Hamano provides clear guidance on both points, establishing a principle that group pushes should behave identically to running individual pushes sequentially.

Junio's key technical position is that `git push G` (for group G containing remotes r1..rN) should be semantically equivalent to running `git push r1; git push r2; ...` separately. This means:

1. For `push.default=simple`, each remote in the group follows its own upstream rules - some may succeed while others fail, just as they would individually. No special group handling is needed or desired.

2. For `--force-with-lease`, the same commit expectation (if specified) applies uniformly across all remotes. When unspecified (and thus guessed from remote-tracking branches), each remote may get a different expectation based on its tracking state.

The response implicitly rejects two alternative approaches the author had floated: automatically switching to `push.default=current` for groups, and introducing per-remote lease tracking. Junio's principle keeps the group push feature mechanically simple by deferring semantic consistency to the user's configuration of individual remotes.

This email resolves the two main open design questions from the RFC, providing the architectural direction needed to finalize the implementation. The author now has clear maintainer guidance to proceed without special group semantics for these cases.
