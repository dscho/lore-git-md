The thread explores design improvements for the experimental `git replay` command, which provides a lower-level alternative to `git rebase` that works with bare repositories. Toon Claes initially suggested refactoring the interface from mutually-exclusive options (`--onto`, `--advance`, `--revert`) to clearer subcommands (`rebase`, `pick`, `revert`), while introducing a consistent `--ref` option for specifying reference updates. This RFC sparked discussion about interface clarity and backward compatibility.

Justin Tobler raised concerns about the proposed design, noting that requiring `--onto` with the `rebase` subcommand felt redundant and that the subcommand approach might not be the most intuitive solution. In response, Toon provided detailed examples showing why the current asymmetric behavior exists - where `--onto` implicitly determines the ref update differently than `--advance`/`--revert`. He's considering alternatives like making both `--onto` and `--ref` required (accepting some redundancy for plumbing commands) or combining operations under fewer options.

The discussion remains constructive, with Toon showing flexibility to iterate on the design based on feedback. Key unresolved questions include finding the right balance between interface consistency and backward compatibility, and whether positional arguments might improve usability. The command remains marked experimental in the documentation, giving flexibility to refine the design before stabilization.
