This is a status update on an ongoing refactoring effort in the xdiff subsystem, part of the broader Rustification initiative.

Ezekiel Newren confirms he is actively working on a revised version of his 10-part xdiff refactoring series, which aims to prepare the diff engine for future optimizations while making it more Rust-compatible. The new iteration will be "quite different" from the original and will rebase on top of master, incorporating Phillip Wood's now-merged xdiff cleanup work that had previously caused conflicts.

Maintainer Junio Hamano acknowledges the update with a simple "Thanks", indicating he is tracking this work but waiting to see the concrete changes in v2 submission. The thread shows this is part of the larger project conversation about introducing Rust into Git's codebase while maintaining performance and safety in critical subsystems like xdiff.

The original series had raised questions about memory safety (particularly around the IVec implementation) and efficiency impacts, which the author now has an opportunity to address in the revision. This remains a technically sensitive area given xdiff's performance-critical role in Git's diff machinery.
