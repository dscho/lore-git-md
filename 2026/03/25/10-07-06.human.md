This is a review comment on the final patch of Pablo Sabater's `--graph-lane-limit` feature series. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) raises concerns about the visual interpretation of lane truncation in the graph output.

Hannes questions whether the current implementation's use of '.' markers for truncated lanes matches user expectations. He expected truncation to mean vertical lines would be interrupted and later resumed (with color being the only connection), rather than having excess lanes simply replaced with dots. The test cases show 3 lanes when the limit is 2, and 4 when the limit is 3, which Hannes finds confusing at first glance.

This appears to be a fundamental design question about how graph truncation should visually represent hidden lanes. The current approach prioritizes preserving commit markers ('*') while showing truncation marks ('.') for excess lanes, but Hannes suggests this may not provide enough visual continuity for users tracking connections between commits.

The patch itself had appeared ready for merging, but this review introduces new considerations about the user experience of truncated graphs. Hannes indicates he needs to reconsider his position after realizing how the truncation behaves in practice. This may delay final approval until the visual design question is resolved.
