This is part of Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack`. The series has expanded from 2 to 10 patches in v2, with this patch specifically adapting the keepalive mechanism to work better with buffered writes.

The change modifies how `upload-pack` handles keepalive packets during packfile transfers. Previously, keepalives were sent based purely on poll() timeouts, which could lead to unnecessarily long delays when data was being buffered. The new logic tracks time since the last data transmission and adjusts keepalive timing accordingly, preventing extended silent periods during buffering.

Key technical changes:
- Adding a `did_send_data` flag to track successful transmissions
- Introducing `last_sent_ms` timestamp tracking
- Dynamically calculating poll timeout based on time since last send
- Updating timestamps whenever data is sent (packfile lines, progress messages, or keepalives)

This addresses feedback from Jeff King about the interaction between buffering and keepalives, while maintaining the series' goal of reducing write syscall contention. The patch is well-contained and follows the established pattern of the larger optimization effort.
