This is a performance optimization discussion in the ongoing hook subsystem improvements, specifically addressing a regression in `git-receive-pack`'s sideband hook handling.

Jeff King (Peff) responds to Adrian Ratiu's patch that restores an optimization where sideband hooks would early-exit if no hook exists, avoiding unnecessary async thread creation. While approving the change, Peff notes some deeper inefficiencies in the hook lookup process that remain - particularly around repeated `access()` calls for traditional hooks - but considers these pre-existing issues not worth blocking the current fix.

The discussion reveals that while the immediate performance regression is solved (with a 2.7x speedup confirmed), there's room for further optimization in how hooks are looked up and cached. Peff sketches an alternative approach using `HOOK_INIT` that could eliminate redundant lookups, though he acknowledges this may be premature optimization given the current performance characteristics.

This email doesn't introduce new changes but provides valuable context about potential future directions for the hook subsystem's performance characteristics, coming from one of Git's most experienced contributors. The tone is constructive and forward-looking, treating the current patch as an improvement while identifying areas that might benefit from deeper architectural work.
