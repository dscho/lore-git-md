This is part 3 of Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. The patch specifically targets write syscall reduction by improving buffering in the packfile data relay path.

The change modifies `upload-pack.c` to buffer more data before writing to the network, waiting until the output buffer is at least 2/3 full before flushing. This reduces write syscalls from ~400,000 to ~130,000 when cloning the Linux repository. The 2/3 threshold was chosen as a balance - while filling the buffer completely could reduce writes further to ~100,000, testing showed diminishing returns that didn't justify the potential increase in read syscalls.

The patch acknowledges that similar improvements could be made in `git-pack-objects`, but deliberately focuses on `upload-pack` since it's the common path that must handle both direct pack-objects output and custom hooks via `uploadpack.packObjectsHook`. A follow-up patch will address pack-objects separately.

Technical implementation adds a simple conditional to check buffer fill level before writing, leveraging existing buffering infrastructure. The change is relatively contained (7 lines) but has significant impact on high-concurrency scenarios where many upload-pack processes contend for write locks. This builds on earlier feedback from Jeff King about buffer synchronization and represents one piece of the comprehensive solution being developed across multiple subsystems.
