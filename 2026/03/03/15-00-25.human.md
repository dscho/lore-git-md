This is part 10 of Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series targeting lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. The patch focuses on reducing write syscalls when writing packfile data via `git pack-objects --stdout`.

The change increases the buffer size from 8KB to ~64KB (LARGE_PACKET_DATA_MAX - 1) when writing packfiles to stdout. This provides a significant reduction in write syscalls (from 355,000 to 44,000 when packing the Linux repo) while maintaining reasonable progress meter updates. The new buffer size aligns with pktline chunking used in git-upload-pack and accounts for the sideband byte.

The patch builds on earlier work that introduced configurable buffer sizes via `hashfd_ext()`, addressing a case that was overlooked in a previous optimization. While the performance improvement is modest on idle systems, the reduced lock contention will be more noticeable in high-concurrency scenarios. The change has been reviewed by Jeff King (Peff) and fits into the broader series' goal of optimizing I/O patterns in the pack-writing pipeline.
