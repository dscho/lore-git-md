Performance optimization series addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack` during high-concurrency scenarios. This is version 2 of Patrick Steinhardt's patch series, now expanded from 2 to 10 patches to incorporate feedback from Jeff King (Peff) and address additional edge cases.

The core optimization remains reducing lock contention when writing packfile data by buffering writes until the buffer is 2/3 full (reducing write syscalls from ~400k to ~130k when cloning Linux). New in v2 are several complementary improvements:

1. Buffer size adjustments in `git-pack-objects` to further reduce syscalls
2. Introduction of `writev()` for pktline writes, halving syscalls for packet data
3. Improved keepalive handling to account for buffering delays
4. Platform compatibility wrappers for `writev()`
5. Cleanup of the csum-file interface

The series now addresses Peff's concerns about buffer synchronization and custom hooks (via `uploadpack.packObjectsHook`), while maintaining the substantial performance gains. Junio C Hamano had already expressed enthusiasm for the approach in v1, suggesting this will likely be merged once these refinements are settled.

The changes are well-motivated by production-scale problems (hundreds of concurrent upload-packs at multi-gigabit speeds) and backed by concrete measurements. The implementation remains surgical while expanding to handle edge cases, showing the kind of iterative refinement the project values.
