This is a technical discussion in Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series about reducing write syscalls in `git-upload-pack`. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Junio C Hamano's concerns about integer overflow handling in the `writev()` compatibility wrapper, while also proposing an alternative approach to buffer management.

Peff acknowledges Junio's point about the potential for size_t wraparound in the `writev()` implementation, noting that Coverity also flagged this issue. He suggests that rather than trying to handle wraparound after the fact, the code should pre-check the total size before attempting writes, matching POSIX's `writev()` behavior of returning EINVAL for oversized requests.

More significantly, Peff proposes an alternative optimization strategy that avoids `writev()` entirely for the common case of pkt-line writes in `upload-pack`. His prototype patch shows how contiguous header+data writes could be combined into single `write()` calls by carefully managing buffer layout. This approach would work universally across platforms while still achieving the desired syscall reduction.

The discussion highlights two viable paths forward for the optimization series: either refine the `writev()` implementation with proper overflow checking as Junio suggested, or adopt Peff's buffer management approach that sidesteps the portability concerns entirely. Both approaches would maintain the substantial performance improvements (reducing writes from ~400k to ~44k for Linux repo clones) while addressing the safety and compatibility issues raised.
