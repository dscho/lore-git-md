This is a process-related follow-up in the fsmonitor Linux implementation series. Patrick Steinhardt raises two concerns about the patch submission cadence and potential AI involvement.

The email notes that three versions of the patch series were sent in quick succession without allowing time for review between iterations, making it difficult for reviewers to know which version to focus on. Patrick requests that future iterations allow more time (days rather than hours) between versions to properly incorporate feedback.

He also asks for clarification about whether AI was involved in writing the patch series, and if so, which parts. This reflects growing awareness in the Git community about the need to understand AI's role in contributions, though the project has not yet established formal policies on AI-generated code.

The tone is constructive but firm, with Patrick (a major contributor and team lead at GitLab) setting expectations about contribution norms. This comes in the final stages of a technically complex 12-patch series that has already addressed all substantive technical feedback, suggesting the process concerns are the last remaining items before potential integration.
