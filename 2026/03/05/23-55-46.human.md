This is a performance and compatibility patch series for `contrib/subtree`, specifically addressing recursion depth issues in `git subtree split` on systems with POSIX shell recursion limits (notably Debian/Ubuntu). The series is now in its second version, rebased on current master (628a66ccf6).

The core issue stems from how `git subtree split` processes deep history graphs - it can hit the 1000-call recursion limit in default Debian/Ubuntu POSIX shells. Colin Stagner's three-patch series restructures the split operation to reduce recursion depth through several coordinated changes: eliminating function side effects, functionalizing the traversal logic, and most significantly in patch 3, replacing recursive processing with bulk commit range handling.

The changes are focused entirely in `contrib/subtree/git-subtree.sh` with 88 lines added and 7 removed across the series. While the cover letter mentions testing on a large repository, there are still no concrete performance measurements provided to quantify the improvement. The series appears technically sound but awaits review feedback - no responses to either v1 or this v2 iteration have been recorded yet.

For users on affected platforms, this could resolve frustrating recursion limit failures during subtree operations. The changes are contained to the subtree implementation and don't interact with other in-flight topics. The lack of performance data makes it hard to assess the real-world impact, but the approach looks reasonable for reducing recursion depth in the history traversal.
