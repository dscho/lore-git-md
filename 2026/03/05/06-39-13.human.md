This is a technical discussion about platform compatibility in Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series for `git-upload-pack`. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) weighs in on the ongoing debate between using `writev()` versus alternative buffering approaches to reduce write syscalls.

Hannes strongly opposes using `writev()` in the compatibility layer, arguing that a compat implementation cannot provide the same guarantees as a kernel-native one. He specifically warns against relying on POSIX-specified error behavior that may not be replicable in the compat wrapper. His position is that simple `write()` calls are preferable, and he expresses skepticism that `writev()` would yield noticeable performance gains in practice.

This email adds a platform expert's voice to the architectural debate, introducing new concerns about the validity of emulating `writev()` behavior across different systems. Hannes' Windows-focused perspective carries weight given his long history maintaining Git's Windows compatibility. His intervention may shift the discussion toward simpler buffering strategies that avoid `writev()` entirely, though the performance impact remains to be seen.
