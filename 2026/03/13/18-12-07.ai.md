This is a follow-up discussion in the `the_repository` removal thread about the `mktree` refactoring, focusing on the design philosophy behind Git's command-line flag handling. Tian Yuchen seeks clarification from Junio Hamano about why the `-flag` handling is implemented through `parse_options()` rather than at a higher level.

The exchange reveals an interesting design choice in Git's architecture - that help text handling (`-h`) is intentionally treated the same as other command-specific flags (`-z`), rather than being special-cased at a global level. Junio maintains that all flags are equally part of a command's interface, rejecting any notion that help text is somehow less "business logic" than other options.

This discussion doesn't affect the technical soundness of the original patch (which remains ready for inclusion), but provides insight into Git's CLI design philosophy. The question touches on historical decisions that may not be immediately obvious to new contributors, highlighting how even straightforward refactorings can surface deeper architectural questions. The thread serves as a good example of the kind of implicit knowledge that maintainers possess about the codebase's design patterns.
