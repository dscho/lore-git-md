Bugfix for promisor-remote subsystem addressing recursive lazy-fetch issue, with discussion of server-side behavior.

Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on the ongoing discussion about recursive lazy fetches in promisor-remotes, providing technical context about when REF_DELTAs might legitimately appear in packfiles. He outlines two scenarios where a server might send REF_DELTAs even when OFS_DELTAs would be preferred: if the client doesn't advertise ofs-delta capability (unlikely with modern Git), or during pack-reuse where ofs-deltas get converted to ref-deltas. 

The key insight is that these cases might trigger unnecessary backfill fetches in index-pack even when the base object is actually present in the pack. Peff suggests the current patch (which propagates GIT_NO_LAZY_FETCH=1 to child processes) would handle this correctly by preventing the eager backfill while still allowing the object to be resolved normally during pack processing.

The email highlights the value of having packet traces to confirm the actual negotiation behavior, but acknowledges the client-side hardening is likely correct regardless of the server's behavior. This moves the discussion forward by providing expert perspective on the edge cases while maintaining support for the proposed fix. The patch appears technically sound, with any remaining questions being about diagnostic confirmation rather than the solution itself.
