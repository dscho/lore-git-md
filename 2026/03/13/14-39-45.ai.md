This is a feature patch series introducing a `pre-add` hook mechanism, now facing fundamental design reconsideration despite technical completion. The discussion has shifted from implementation details to questioning the core value proposition of the hook's limited scope.

Phillip Wood raises several key concerns in this response:
1. The `--dry-run` behavior (skipping hooks) inherited from `pre-commit` may be suboptimal, contrasting with `pre-push` which runs hooks during dry runs. He suggests avoiding replicating what he sees as a design flaw in `pre-commit`.
2. The hook's exclusion of interactive/patch modes creates a confusing user experience where some `git add` operations bypass validation. Phillip argues this significantly reduces the hook's utility.
3. He questions whether the hook should also validate changes staged by `git commit`, seeing the current limited scope as potentially not worth the complexity.
4. The read-only nature of the index files passed to the hook remains unresolved - Phillip argues for either fully committing to read-only or explicitly supporting modifications.

The author Chandra Kethi-Reddy acknowledges these concerns but considers broader integration (like commit-time validation) out of scope for this initial implementation. They also note the rapid v4-to-v5 revision cycle was due to addressing straightforward technical feedback rather than ignoring deeper design questions.

This exchange reveals the series is at a crossroads - while the implementation is technically sound, reviewers are questioning whether the limited-scope design provides enough value to justify inclusion. The maintainer's earlier skepticism suggests the series may need significant redesign or reconsideration of its core premise before proceeding.
