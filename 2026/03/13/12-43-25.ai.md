Bugfix for promisor-remote subsystem addressing recursive lazy-fetch issue, now with packet traces and deterministic reproduction.

Paul Tarjan provides critical new evidence in this ongoing discussion about runaway recursive lazy-fetches in blob:none partial clones. His packet trace from GitHub confirms the server is correctly using noop negotiation, while his local reproduction scripts demonstrate exactly how REF_DELTAs against filtered blobs can trigger infinite recursion. The key findings are:

1. The server-side behavior is protocol-compliant - the trace shows proper noop negotiation and thin-pack response, with the client-side `--filter=blob:none` causing the problematic REF_DELTA situation when pack-reuse converts OFS_DELTAs to REF_DELTAs against filtered-out blob bases.

2. Two reproduction scripts conclusively demonstrate the issue:
   - `repro-depth1.sh` shows a minimal case where a single REF_DELTA against a promised-but-absent blob causes one level of recursion
   - `repro-unbounded.sh` simulates the production scenario by forcing infinite recursion (killed at depth 50) that matches the observed behavior of accumulating 1.4GB temp files

3. The proposed `GIT_NO_LAZY_FETCH=1` solution works correctly in all cases - preventing recursion while still allowing proper delta resolution when the base objects are available through normal means.

This email solidly confirms the technical soundness of the fix while providing concrete evidence of the failure mode. The reproduction scripts in particular will be valuable for future regression testing. The discussion has now moved from theoretical edge cases to demonstrated behavior, with consensus clearly favoring merging the client-side hardening while leaving potential server-side improvements for future work.
