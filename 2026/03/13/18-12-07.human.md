This is a follow-up discussion in the `the_repository` removal thread about the `mktree` command refactoring, specifically addressing design philosophy around command-line flag handling. Tian Yuchen seeks clarification from Junio Hamano about why the `-h` help flag is handled by `parse_options()` rather than at a higher level in Git's architecture.

The exchange reveals an interesting design choice in Git's command processing - all flags, including help text, are treated as first-class parts of each command's interface rather than being handled by a global mechanism. Junio maintains this is intentional, emphasizing that `-h` is not special compared to other flags like `-z`. 

While this discussion doesn't affect the technical soundness of the `mktree` refactoring patch (which remains ready for inclusion), it provides valuable insight into Git's design philosophy. The thread shows how even straightforward refactorings can surface deeper architectural questions that help contributors understand the codebase's implicit patterns.
