This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing thread about unifying conflict resolution in `git checkout` and `git switch` through autostash behavior. Nordgren notes he personally uses the stash/checkout/unstash workflow frequently (similar to `git pull --rebase`'s autostash) but doesn't use `checkout -m`. He questions whether maintaining this as a separate autostash feature might be preferable to the current direction of overhauling `checkout -m`'s conflict handling.

This email highlights a tension between two approaches:
1. The maintainer-approved path of unifying conflict resolution via `checkout -m` improvements
2. The author's original vision of a simpler autostash option mirroring existing rebase behavior

The patch appears to be at a crossroads where architectural decisions about Git's conflict resolution model may need explicit discussion. Nordgren's comment suggests the current implementation direction, while technically sound, may not fully address his original use case of quick branch switching with automatic stashing. The thread now faces a design question about whether these should remain distinct features or continue merging into a unified approach.
