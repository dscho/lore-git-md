This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt's performance optimization series addressing lock contention in `git-upload-pack`. The patch focuses on reducing write syscalls when writing packfile data through `git pack-objects --stdout`.

The change increases the buffer size from 8KB to ~64KB (LARGE_PACKET_DATA_MAX - 1) when writing packfiles with progress reporting. This reduces write syscalls from 355k to 44k when packing the Linux repository, though the performance improvement is noted as mostly negligible on idle systems. The real benefit comes from reduced kernel lock contention in high-concurrency scenarios.

The buffer size was chosen to align with pktline chunking used by `git-upload-pack`, while still maintaining reasonable progress update frequency (64KB/s being a conservative bandwidth assumption). The patch removes the last vestige of the old `hashfd_throughput()` API by moving its progress reporting configuration into the pack-objects caller.

This completes the series' technical implementation, with all subsystems now optimized for `writev()` batching. The change has been reviewed by Jeff King (who suggested the approach) and validated by Johannes Sixt for platform compatibility. With this final piece in place, the series is ready for merging by Junio Hamano.
