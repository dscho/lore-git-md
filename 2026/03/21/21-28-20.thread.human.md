Lorenzo Pegorari has responded to Junio Hamano's detailed review of the test implementation in his v3 patch series for promisor file handling during repacks. The series, part of Lorenzo's GSoC work under Eric Sunshine's mentorship, is now in the final stages of review.

In his response, Lorenzo accepts all of Junio's feedback on improving the reliability and maintainability of the tests in patch 4/5. He agrees to make several concrete improvements: properly quoting shell variables, using direct file operations instead of`cat`, checking `.promisor` files directly rather than deriving them from packfiles, and simplifying the test setup by directly assigning known values. He also accepts the suggestion to streamline the `sed` pipeline that was previously using an unnecessary `head` command.

The most significant remaining issue is the flaky behavior in the multiple-pack test case, where close timestamps could cause unreliable test results. While Lorenzo hasn't yet proposed a specific solution in this response, he acknowledges the problem and commits to addressing it in the next version. This follows earlier discussions where he had suggested using `repack -a -f` to force single-pack output, though that approach still needs verification.

The series continues to progress well through review, with all major architectural questions (like the switch to Unix epoch timestamps and string-list usage) already resolved. Junio has provided a SQUASH??? commit with suggested changes that Lorenzo will examine, and Eric Sunshine is expected to review the revised implementation. With these test improvements addressed, the series appears to be nearing readiness for inclusion.
