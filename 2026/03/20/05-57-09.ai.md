Performance-focused discussion about strbuf_getwholeline() buffer allocation strategy.

Jeff King (Peff) pushes back on Junio Hamano's proposal to simplify strbuf_getwholeline() by always allocating a fresh buffer for getdelim() operations. While acknowledging the appeal of Junio's approach for eliminating edge cases, Peff argues the performance cost would be too high in practice. His key concern is that the proposed change would force a new allocation on every call rather than reusing the existing strbuf buffer, which he notes is a common optimization pattern throughout Git's codebase.

The exchange highlights a fundamental tension in this utility function's design: Junio's patch prioritizes correctness and maintainability by making error handling trivial, while Peff emphasizes preserving the performance characteristics that make strbuf reuse valuable in practice. Peff doesn't provide specific benchmarks but references measurable improvements from the current buffer reuse pattern, suggesting this isn't just theoretical.

This reply keeps the technical discussion grounded in real-world usage patterns, where strbuf_getwholeline() often appears in loops processing input line-by-line. The thread remains focused on evaluating whether the simplicity gains justify breaking established optimization patterns, with both maintainers bringing their deep experience with Git's performance characteristics to bear.
