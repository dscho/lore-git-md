This is a platform compatibility discussion continuing the thread about const-correctness changes for glibc 2.43 and C23 standards. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Toon Claes' proposal about using C11 generic selection macros to handle const-correct string operations, while also addressing broader compilation issues.

Peff confirms that conditionally compiling with C11 generic selections would be acceptable for Git if limited to compilers that support it, though he notes a simple macro approach might suffice for the immediate case. He shares his own quick fixes in a personal branch but hasn't sent them upstream to avoid duplicating Collin Funk's work. The email highlights a particularly messy interaction between glibc's preprocessor conditionals and older C standards that can produce confusing warnings when building historical Git versions.

The discussion remains technical, with Peff identifying practical workarounds (like `-Wno-discarded-qualifiers` for GCC users) while acknowledging the deeper systemic issue. He points out that clang users face worse problems due to incompatible warning flags and glibc header interactions, suggesting this may need resolution at the glibc/clang level rather than in Git. The email maintains the thread's focus on finding solutions that balance standards compliance with Git's conservative approach to compiler features.
