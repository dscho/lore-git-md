This is a clarification on the scope of Google Summer of Code (GSoC) microprojects for Git. Karthik Nayak responds to Trieu Huynh's question about how many patches or files can be changed in a single microproject submission.

The context is Trieu's 16-patch series modernizing test scripts by replacing command piping with temporary files to properly capture Git's exit codes. After Junio Hamano pointed to the "only one quality-focused microproject per applicant" guideline, Trieu sought clarification on what constitutes an acceptable scope.

Karthik's response makes it clear that microprojects are intended as an onboarding exercise and should be limited to a single file change. This directly addresses Trieu's question while implicitly indicating their 16-file series exceeds the expected scope. The reply is concise and authoritative, referencing the official guidelines without engaging in technical discussion about the patches themselves.

This email effectively closes the administrative question about GSoC scope, though the technical merits of Trieu's patches remain open for review. The exchange highlights how the project balances mentorship (through constrained microprojects) with maintaining contribution quality standards.
