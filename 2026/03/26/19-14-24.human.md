This is the final patch in Jeff King's (Peff) series addressing const-correctness issues in Git's revision parsing code, specifically focusing on parent mark handling in `builtin/rev-parse.c`. The patch completes the structural approach of replacing in-place string modifications with proper allocation patterns.

The change refactors how `git rev-parse` handles parent shorthand marks like `^!`, `^@`, and `^-` by:
1. Renaming the misleading `dotdot` variable to `mark` (aligning with a 2017 change in revision.c)
2. Using `xmemdupz()` to create modifiable copies of const strings when needed
3. Adding proper cleanup with `free()` calls in error paths

The patch follows the same allocation-based solution established in the series, avoiding the const-cast workarounds from earlier discussions. Peff notes this may behave slightly differently than revision.c for complex mark combinations, but considers that out of scope for this fix.

The changes are focused and mechanical - about 25 lines modified with clear variable renaming and memory management. This continues the thread's progression from Michael Gruber's initial const-cast fixes toward more architecturally sound solutions. Given Peff's expertise and the prior consensus around this approach, this appears to be a solid conclusion to the series.
