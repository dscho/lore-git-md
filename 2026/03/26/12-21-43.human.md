This is a discussion about the URL-based promisor remote auto-configuration feature in Christian Couder's series. Patrick Steinhardt raises two key points about the design:

First, he suggests adding a `git clone --accept-promisors` command-line option as a one-shot alternative to persistent configuration, noting that many users may prefer not to maintain ongoing promisor configurations.

Second, he proposes separating the concerns of accepting, storing, and updating promisor remotes into distinct configuration mechanisms. He argues the current design conflates these different use cases under `promisor.acceptFromServerUrl`, making it impossible to express common workflows like "store at clone time and update automatically" without also enabling runtime acceptance.

The feedback continues Patrick's pattern of constructive technical critique focused on improving the feature's design and usability. While not blocking progress, it suggests significant architectural reconsideration may be needed before finalizing the series. The proposal to split configuration into separate "accept/store/update" knobs would require reworking several patches if adopted.
