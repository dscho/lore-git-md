This is a review comment on the first patch in Christian Couder's series introducing URL-based whitelisting for promisor remotes. Patrick Steinhardt agrees with the core idea of prioritizing accepted promisor remotes over non-accepted ones, but raises three specific suggestions:

1. He asks whether the importance of remote ordering is documented anywhere, implying this behavior should be clearly specified.

2. He questions the future-looking paragraph about auto-configuring remotes, seeking clarification about whether the plan is to avoid storing announced promisors in config entirely.

3. He offers two concrete code improvements: simplifying the boolean logic in the new helper function (`if (!accepted_only != !r->accepted)`) and suggests adding a test to verify the new ordering behavior.

The review is constructive and focused - Patrick supports the direction while identifying areas that need clarification or improvement. His questions about documentation and testing are particularly relevant given this is foundational work for a larger security-sensitive feature. The exchange shows the careful scrutiny applied even to preparatory refactoring when it underpins functionality involving remote trust decisions.
