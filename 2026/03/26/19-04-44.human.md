This is the first patch in Jeff King's (Peff) series to improve const-correctness in Git's revision parsing code, specifically focusing on how ".." range operators are handled. The patch refactors the dotdot parsing interface to avoid modifying const strings, which was causing compiler warnings with ISOC23 and could potentially lead to segfaults.

The key change replaces the previous approach of temporarily modifying the input string (by overwriting dots with NULs) with a cleaner allocation-based solution. Now `handle_dotdot()` makes a temporary copy of the left-hand side of the range operator using `xmemdupz()`, avoiding any const violations. The patch also simplifies the interface by passing the parsed components (left/right sides, symmetric flag, and original string) explicitly rather than reconstructing them from a modified buffer.

This is part of a broader effort to move away from type-system workarounds toward proper const-correct solutions in Git's core. The change touches about 40 lines in `revision.c` and maintains all existing functionality while being more robust. Given Peff's expertise and the prior discussion trajectory, this structural improvement is likely to be well-received.
