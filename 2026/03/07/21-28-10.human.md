This is a performance investigation thread about slow `git fetch` operations over a slow network connection (1Mbps down/10Mbps up). The original poster R. Diez follows up on brian m. carlson's earlier analysis, providing more details from their testing with `GIT_TRACE2` and `GIT_TRACE_PACKET`.

Key findings from the new data:
- Most of the 24-second fetch time (23.7s) is spent in the `git-upload-pack` child process, despite this being a download operation (a known naming quirk in Git)
- Packet tracing shows 13 seconds spent on a mysterious "0000" packet, 2 seconds on HEAD/master ref resolution, and 7 seconds fetching individual tags
- The poster remains hesitant to try the experimental reftable backend despite its potential benefits
- They question whether Git could parallelize tag downloads even for a single remote, suggesting the current serial implementation may be suboptimal

The thread remains technically focused, with the poster now armed with concrete timing data but still seeking to understand whether the observed behavior is expected given their network constraints or indicates room for Git optimization. The discussion is likely to continue with deeper analysis of the trace data and potential workarounds.
