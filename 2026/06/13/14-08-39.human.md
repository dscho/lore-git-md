Performance optimization for merge-base calculations in large repositories, particularly monorepos with imported histories. Kristofer Karlsson provides a technical clarification about how the STALE bit propagates in the commit graph during merge-base walks, correcting his earlier description.

The key insight is that in monorepos with imported repositories (represented as separate commit histories with generation number 0), the current algorithm forces a full traversal of the main graph even after finding the merge-base. This happens because the walk crosses an import above the merge-base, keeping the traversal alive via max_nonstale until the entire graph is drained. Karlsson's measurements show this leads to 2.3 million unnecessary steps in a real-world case where HEAD and HEAD~1000 share a clear merge-base.

This email builds on the earlier discussion with Derrick Stolee about optimizing the `paint_down_to_common` function, now with concrete evidence of the performance impact in Spotify's monorepo. The clarification about STALE propagation helps explain why the current implementation behaves suboptimally in these cases, reinforcing the case for Karlsson's proposed early-termination optimization when one side of the comparison is exhausted.
