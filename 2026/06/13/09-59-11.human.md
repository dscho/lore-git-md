This is a review email from Johannes Sixt (Hannes) responding to Harald Nordgren's patch series about `git commit --amend --no-op` behavior. The series proposes preserving commit hashes when amending without changes, but Hannes raises several thoughtful questions about the implications.

Hannes questions whether the proposed behavior is actually desirable, noting that `--amend` is fundamentally a request to create a new commit. He probes edge cases: should this only apply to `--no-edit` or also unchanged messages? Should it be considered a failure when no new commit is created? He also asks about reflog behavior and challenges the special handling of signed commits, suggesting the signature shouldn't need updating if nothing changes.

These are substantive technical questions that go to the heart of the proposal's design philosophy rather than surface-level nits. Hannes isn't rejecting the idea outright but is testing its boundaries and consistency with Git's existing behavior patterns. The review suggests the patch may need to either broaden its scope (to handle edited-but-unchanged messages) or better justify why `--no-edit` is special. The signature handling rationale in particular appears to need clarification.

This review moves the discussion forward by identifying aspects that need either implementation changes or stronger justification in the commit message. It's the kind of feedback that often leads to a v2 patch with refined behavior or documentation.
