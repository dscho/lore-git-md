This thread explored a proposed optimization to `git commit --amend --no-edit` that would avoid creating new commit objects when no substantive changes were made. The original author, Harald Nordgren, provided a technically sound implementation with thorough test coverage, but maintainer Junio C Hamano rejected the approach as too abrupt, citing specific merge/revert workflows that depend on the current always-create-a-new-commit behavior. Johannes Sixt raised conceptual concerns about whether suppressing commit creation aligns with Git's model, while Ben Knoble noted the current behavior's utility for forcing CI re-runs.

In his follow-up, Harald acknowledged Junio's rejection as a showstopper and indicated he'll likely drop the effort. He shared that his own motivation came from years of using `--amend --no-edit --date="now"` to force CI re-runs, mistakenly believing the hash would stay unchanged - an ironic twist that highlights how the current behavior, while perhaps unintentional, serves some real-world needs. The thread concludes with the optimization shelved for now, though Junio had suggested a potential multi-step transition path if the change is pursued in the future through a more gradual approach.
