This thread is about improving Git’s contributor documentation to make patch review etiquette clearer for newcomers. Weijie Yuan’s v2 patch series updates `MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `SubmittingPatches` to codify norms like discussing feedback before rerolling and setting a default cadence of no more than one reroll per day. While most of `SubmittingPatches` has strong consensus, Junio C Hamano raised substantive objections to the proposed exceptions for "substantial rework" and "nearly accepted" patches, arguing they risk encouraging rushed updates.

The latest exchange shows progress: Weijie accepted Junio’s alternative of signaling invalidated parts instead of the "substantial rework" exception, resolving one contested point. On the "nearly accepted" scenario, Weijie clarified his intent—this was meant for cases where a series is almost ready (e.g., has "Looks good" from Junio) and only a minor issue remains, not as a loophole for premature "final" versions. He proposed adding a reminder to double-check for unresolved feedback before sending updates. The `MyFirstContribution.adoc` changes remain unresolved due to redundancy concerns, but the focus has shifted to refining the cadence guidance in `SubmittingPatches`.

Key participants are aligned on the core goal—respecting reviewer bandwidth while maintaining patch quality—but differ on how to document exceptions. Junio emphasizes deliberate updates and existing practices like marking "final candidate" iterations, while Weijie seeks to balance flexibility with clarity. The next version will likely incorporate Junio’s signaling suggestion and either drop or reword the "nearly accepted" guidance. The thread itself models the collaborative practices it documents, with responsive engagement and proper attribution.
