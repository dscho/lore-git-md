This thread is about improving Git's contributor documentation to make implicit patch review norms explicit for newcomers. Weijie Yuan's two-patch RFC series (now at v2) proposes updates to `MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `SubmittingPatches` to clarify when and how contributors should send updated patch versions after review.

The core of the series—changes to `SubmittingPatches`—has strong consensus, with Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt both supporting the addition of concrete guidance on reroll cadence (e.g., a default of no more than one reroll per day) and the principle of discussing feedback before sending updates. Junio’s latest email, however, questions two specific recommendations: allowing faster rerolls for "substantial rework" and when a topic is "close to acceptance." He argues that substantial changes inherently take time to polish and that marking a version as a "final candidate" (a practice he’s observed in Patrick’s patches) already serves the purpose of encouraging last-minute feedback. This feedback may lead to revisions in the patch’s wording or removal of the contested guidance.

The `MyFirstContribution.adoc` updates remain unresolved, as Patrick noted redundancy with existing text and the author has not yet revised them. The thread itself models the collaborative practices it documents, with proper attribution, responsive engagement, and substantive discussion. The next steps will likely involve Weijie addressing Junio’s feedback on the cadence exceptions and deciding whether to retain or drop the `MyFirstContribution` changes. The series is close to transitioning from RFC to a regular patch submission, pending these final adjustments.
