Patrick Steinhardt's ref backend modernization series has reached an advanced stage, with all nine patches reviewed and all documentation issues resolved. The series transitions Git's reference backends from relative to absolute path handling, eliminating the complex chdir_notify_reparent() mechanism. Karthik Nayak provided thorough reviews on key patches, while Junio Hamano and Jeff King weighed in on repository initialization and performance considerations.

Jeff King's benchmarks revealed a 10x slowdown in edge cases involving deep directory hierarchies with many loose refs, though both he and Patrick agree this is an acceptable tradeoff given that modern ref storage (packed-refs and reftable) mitigates the impact. In a recent exchange, Patrick expressed some hesitation about the performance regression and suggested possibly deferring the final patch that removes chdir_notify entirely. He also revisited the idea of using openat() as a more robust solution that could address both performance and TOCTOU concerns, though noted Windows compatibility challenges. Jeff agreed this could be worth exploring with a proper abstraction layer.

The series remains in good shape for integration, with Patrick demonstrating thoughtful consideration of the performance implications while keeping the architectural improvements on track. The discussion reflects the project's careful balance between modernization and maintaining performance, with both contributors maintaining constructive engagement on potential future directions for filesystem access patterns.
