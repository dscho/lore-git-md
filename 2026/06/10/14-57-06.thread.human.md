This is the sixth iteration of Patrick Steinhardt’s 11-patch series to overhaul Git’s reference backend, fixing long-standing initialization issues that could cause recursive failures when "onbranch" configuration was in use. The series has now reached a polished state, with the latest changes addressing feedback from Justin Tobler and Jeff King (Peff) and incorporating preparatory cleanups.

The core problem—recursive ref store initialization triggered by early config reads—has been solved by deferring write-critical configuration (like `core.logAllRefUpdates`) until the first write operation. This lazy-loading design abandons the earlier `ignore_refs` workaround and instead decouples read-critical and write-critical config parsing. The series touches nearly every layer of the ref backend, from memory leak fixes in the `chdir_notify` subsystem to structural changes in the reftable backend’s write option handling.

Key participants have weighed in: Peff endorsed the lazy-loading approach as the correct fix, while Justin provided detailed reviews of the backend-specific changes, including the patch that moves `core.logAllRefUpdates` parsing back into the ref backends. The series is now functionally complete, with no major objections remaining. Open questions center on performance overhead in write-heavy workloads and edge cases like concurrent writes, but these are mitigated by the design’s use of uncached config paths and hash consistency checks. The next step is final review, with Junio and Karthik Nayak likely to scrutinize the reftable backend’s dynamic write option passing before the series graduates to "next."
