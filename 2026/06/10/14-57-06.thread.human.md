Patrick Steinhardt’s refactoring series to modernize Git’s reference backend is now at a crossroads over patch 7/8, which addresses a recursive initialization issue during ref store setup. The series began as a performance-driven overhaul but pivoted to incremental improvements after early versions revealed a 10x slowdown. Patches 1–6 and 8 remain uncontroversial, covering memory leaks, initialization hygiene, and path computation optimizations, but patch 7/8’s design has reignited debate.

Jeff King (Peff) reopened the discussion by arguing that the current workaround—temporarily setting `ref_storage_format` to `UNKNOWN` to block recursive config parsing—treats a symptom rather than the root cause. He proposed two alternatives: deferring backend-critical config reads until after ref store initialization or reading them via `read_repository_format()`, which ignores all includes. Patrick countered that Peff’s solutions are either too narrow (ignoring valid includes like `includeif.gitdir`) or too cumbersome (lazy-loading config). Instead, he suggested extending `struct config_options` with an `ignore_refdb` flag to selectively disable `includeif.onbranch` conditions during ref backend initialization, preserving other includes while avoiding recursion. He plans to prototype this approach, which could lead to a v4 of the patch.

The outcome will shape how Git handles ref backend config parsing, with implications for both current and future backends. Karthik Nayak, Junio Hamano, and Justin Tobler had previously accepted the current design as pragmatic, but Peff’s critique has shifted the focus back to architectural trade-offs. The series remains incremental, but this debate highlights the tension between immediate fixes and long-term maintainability in Git’s ref backend modernization.
