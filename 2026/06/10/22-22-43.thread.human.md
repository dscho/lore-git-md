Kristoffer Haugsbakk's series documenting Git's commit trailer conventions in the SubmittingPatches guidelines continues to receive careful review from Junio C Hamano. The six-part series, which aims to consolidate and clarify trailer usage norms, has been generally well-received but requires some refinements.

Junio's latest review focuses on the documentation of the `Based-on-patch-by` trailer, raising an important concern about the potential for the current wording to inadvertently encourage including patches without proper Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) sign-off. He emphasizes that the documentation should actively encourage obtaining sign-off from original authors for non-trivial contributions, not just describe the trailer's usage. This maintains Junio's role as the project's standards-keeper, ensuring the guidelines align with Git's legal requirements.

Earlier in the thread, Junio provided both substantive and surface-level feedback on other parts of the series - suggesting clarifications to the trailer ordering explanation and catching minor issues like typos and formatting. The author will need to address these points in a v2 of the series, particularly strengthening the legal considerations around patch attribution while maintaining the comprehensive documentation of existing trailer conventions.

The discussion shows the careful balance required when formalizing project practices - capturing current usage while upholding Git's standards for contributor rights and responsibilities. With Junio's guidance, the series appears on track to become an authoritative reference for trailer usage in Git commit messages.
