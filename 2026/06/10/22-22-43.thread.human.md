This thread updates Git’s `SubmittingPatches` documentation to clarify how commit-message trailers should be used and ordered. Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s six-part series has now addressed all feedback except one lingering question: whether to document non-identity trailers like `Closes:` at all, given their rarity in Git’s history.

The series is nearly ready. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have reviewed it thoroughly, with Junio focusing on legal compliance (DCO sign-offs for `Based-on-patch-by`) and Patrick requesting concrete examples for non-identity trailers. Kristoffer’s data shows only `Change-Id`, `Message-ID`, and `Fixes` appear frequently in Git’s commit history, so he proposes either omitting non-identity trailers or providing a single example. The rest of the series—covering trailer principles, Linux-specific trailer avoidance, and markup consistency—has been agreed upon, including a recent refinement to explain trailer ordering as chronological rather than type-based.

The only remaining open question is whether to include non-identity trailers in the documentation. Otherwise, the series is complete and uncontroversial, offering clear guidance for contributors while maintaining Git’s legal and practical requirements.
