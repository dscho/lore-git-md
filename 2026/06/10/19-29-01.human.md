This is a follow-up discussion in the thread about consolidating reachability functions in Git's commit-reach.c. Derrick Stolee, the original author of both functions being discussed, weighs in with technical context about the algorithmic differences between them.

Stolee acknowledges the redundancy between `get_reachable_subset()` (2018) and `tips_reachable_from_bases()` (2023), noting they were written five years apart when commit-graph availability expectations differed. He explains the newer function's DFS approach performs better with commit-graphs by leveraging generation numbers to minimize traversal, while the older BFS approach had optimizations for repositories without commit-graphs.

The key insight is that modern Git can reasonably assume commit-graph availability, making the DFS approach generally preferable. Stolee confirms both functions share the same termination condition (stopping when all targets are found) and generation number optimizations, but the DFS variant can more aggressively increase the minimum generation threshold during traversal.

This response addresses Junio's earlier question about performance implications by clarifying the historical context and current assumptions. The discussion remains technical and constructive, with Stolee supporting the consolidation while providing the missing rationale for the algorithm choice. The thread appears headed toward consensus on merging the functions, with the only remaining consideration being whether to maintain BFS fallback behavior for commit-graph-less cases.
