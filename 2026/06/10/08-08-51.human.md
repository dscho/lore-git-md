**Performance optimization discussion on commit message style**

Patrick Steinhardt weighs in on the ongoing discussion about AI-generated commit messages, sparked by Jeff King's review of Tamir Duberstein's `git describe` performance patch. While the technical aspects of the patch itself are settled (it provides a 17x speedup by limiting ref iteration to tags by default), the conversation has shifted to broader concerns about commit message quality in the AI era.

Steinhardt identifies two key issues with the AI-generated message in this case: excessive verbosity (including irrelevant details like machine specs) and a lack of coherent narrative flow. He argues that contributors should be expected to refine AI output before submission, and suggests the project may need to reject patches where the author appears to be merely passing through unedited AI content without proper review.

This exchange reflects growing pains as AI tools become more prevalent in development workflows. While not blocking the current patch (which has already addressed its technical concerns), the discussion signals that the project may need to establish clearer norms around AI-assisted contributions to maintain review efficiency. The thread serves as a case study in balancing automation benefits with the need for human judgment in technical communication.
