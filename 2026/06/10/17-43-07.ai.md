This is a policy discussion about AI-assisted contributions to Git, sparked by Tamir Duberstein's `git describe` optimization patch (which is technically ready to merge). The thread has evolved from reviewing a specific performance improvement to establishing project norms for AI-generated content.

Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's concerns about unedited AI output by proposing to document the project's stance in `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`. The suggested addition would explicitly reserve the right to reject patches where the contributor appears to be merely passing through AI-generated content without sufficient understanding or refinement.

The proposed policy language avoids banning AI tools outright but makes clear that contributors must engage meaningfully with their output. This positions Git similarly to other open source projects grappling with how to maintain quality standards amid increasing automation, while stopping short of Patrick's more stringent suggestion to actively push back against all unedited AI content.

The discussion remains constructive, with Junio signaling agreement on the need for some guardrails while leaving room for case-by-case judgment. Notably, this policy consideration does not affect the underlying `git describe` optimization, which has already cleared technical review.
