The `git history squash` feature, proposed by Harald Nordgren, is now complete in its sixth iteration and ready for Junio C Hamano’s final review. The tool efficiently collapses a range of commits—including those with merge commits—into a single commit while preserving descendant history, avoiding the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based approach. All prior feedback has been addressed, including input validation, merge commit handling, and the `--reedit-message` template format, which now omits `fixup!`/`amend!` noise and retains only essential context (a numbered list of commit subjects and `squash!` bodies) for usability.

The series has converged on a design that rejects operations with refs pointing to commits inside the squashed range by default, advising users to explicitly retarget branches with `--update-refs=head`. A key usability question remains: whether `--reedit-message` (or `--edit`) should be the default to encourage commit hygiene, as suggested by Phillip Wood and Matt Hunter. Meanwhile, Matt Hunter and Phillip Wood have highlighted a broader recoverability concern—`git reset --hard` is insufficient to undo operations where `--update-refs` moves multiple branches, as Git’s reflog lacks visibility into which refs were affected. Junio has clarified that the reflog was never designed for undo, reinforcing the need for either improved documentation (e.g., `--dry-run --verbose`) or a future oplog system (as proposed by Patrick Steinhardt) to enable atomic reversal of multi-ref operations. The series itself is technically complete, with no further design changes expected, though minor usability tweaks may follow based on feedback.
