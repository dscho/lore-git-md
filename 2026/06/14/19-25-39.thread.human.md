The `git history squash` command, introduced by Harald Nordgren, is now in its 11th iteration and effectively complete. This tool collapses a commit range—including those with merge commits—into a single commit while preserving descendant history, offering a faster alternative to the conflict-prone rebase workflow. After extensive review, all technical concerns have been resolved, including input validation, merge handling, and the usability debate over default message editing, which now opens an editor with an autosquash-style template matching `git rebase -i --autosquash`.

The series has received strong support from key participants. Phillip Wood’s substantive reviews shaped the final design, particularly the decision to make message editing the default to encourage commit hygiene. Junio C Hamano signaled intent to queue the series with his "Will replace" comment on v7, and Matt Hunter’s explicit sign-off on v10 confirmed all prior concerns were addressed. The implementation is now technically sound, with refactoring patches (like the recent extraction of sequencer helpers) ensuring maintainability and consistency across Git’s history-editing commands.

The command’s default behavior rejects operations where local branches point to commits inside the squashed range, guiding users to explicitly retarget branches with `--update-refs=head`. Interior merges are allowed as long as the range has a single base, and the tool handles `fixup!`/`squash!`/`amend!` commits by grouping them under their targets in the template. The series is ready for integration, with Junio’s endorsement positioning it as a foundational piece of a larger effort to modernize Git’s history-editing infrastructure—specifically, as a faster backend for `git rebase --autosquash` when combined with `git replay`.
