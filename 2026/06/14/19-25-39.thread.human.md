The `git history squash` feature has landed in Junio’s `next` branch as a four-patch series by Harald Nordgren. It lets you collapse any linear or merge-commit range into its oldest commit with a single three-way merge, avoiding the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based workflow. The command is `git history squash <range>`, and the `--reedit-message` flag gives you an editor seeded with every folded-in commit message (oldest first), matching the behavior of `git rebase -i`.

All prior objections—efficiency, message-editing experience, and where the feature should live—were addressed in the final design. Phillip Wood’s concerns about performance and user experience are resolved by the single-merge implementation and the complete message template; Patrick Steinhardt’s architectural preference for a `git history` subcommand is satisfied; and D. Ben Knoble’s alternative proposal was adopted. Junio applied the series to `next` after two trivial adjustments: renumbering the test script to avoid a collision and replacing four raw `grep` calls with `test_grep` to comply with a new test-lint rule.

The only follow-up was a low-weight note from D. Ben Knoble pointing out that the test-lint rule change had actually allowed bare `grep` when used as a filter, but Junio’s replacements stood. The thread is closed, and the feature is now queued for the next release.
