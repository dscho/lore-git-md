The `git history squash` feature, proposed by Harald Nordgren, is now preparing its sixth iteration to collapse a range of commits into a single commit while preserving descendant history. The core functionality—a three-way merge of the range against the oldest commit’s parent—is complete, but v6 will address stricter input validation, edge cases like `fixup!`/`squash!` commits, and clearer documentation before Junio C Hamano’s final review.

Key participants have shaped the design: Phillip Wood’s thorough reviews identified usability gaps (e.g., single-commit ranges, merge commits with external parents), while Junio and Patrick Steinhardt finalized the ref-handling approach—rejecting operations with interior refs by default, with advice to use `--update-refs=head`. Harald has pushed back on input syntax changes, confirming `<base>..<tip>` as the only viable form due to Git’s revision parser limitations, though Junio noted this restricts complex workflows (e.g., excluding branches). The latest update signals Harald’s intent to explore multi-argument support in the future, but v6 will focus on resolving open questions and expanding test coverage. The series remains on track, with no major design changes expected in this iteration.
