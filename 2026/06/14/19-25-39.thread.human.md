The `git history squash` patch series by Harald Nordgren is functionally complete in its 13th iteration and ready for integration, but its progress is currently stalled by procedural expectations rather than technical concerns. The command aims to efficiently collapse a commit range into its oldest commit while preserving descendant history, offering a faster alternative to the conflict-prone rebase-based approach.

All substantive technical feedback has been addressed in v13, including Phillip Wood’s fixup patches for range validation, merge-parent preservation, ref-handling, and autosquash message rules. The series now implements default message editing with an autosquash-style template, stricter input validation, and protection for local branches descended from the squashed range. Junio C Hamano has signaled intent to queue the series, and D. Ben Knoble’s vision of using `git history squash` as a backend for `git rebase --autosquash` has been endorsed as a project-level aspiration.

However, Phillip Wood’s latest email shifts focus to process, demanding explicit replies to unresolved questions (notably a ref-filter display bug in patch 3.5), clearer documentation of behavioral changes, and slower iterations to avoid fragmenting discussion. Harald has not yet responded to these expectations, and Phillip’s upcoming absence will further delay resolution. Until Harald engages with these procedural demands—either by addressing the feedback or explicitly declining to do so—the series remains technically complete but procedurally blocked. A final v14 may be required to resolve these lingering items.
