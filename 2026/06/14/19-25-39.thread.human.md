Here’s the human-mode summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

The `git history squash` feature, proposed by Harald Nordgren, is now in its sixth iteration and ready for Junio’s final review. The tool collapses a range of commits into the oldest one while preserving descendant history, avoiding the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based approach. Key design decisions are settled: it rejects merges with external parents (but allows fully contained merges), detects and blocks operations that would leave refs pointing to now-vanished commits, and offers `--update-refs=head` to retarget the current branch. The `--reedit-message` flag lets users edit the combined commit message, and its template has been refined to reduce noise—omitting `fixup!`/`amend!` subjects and showing only the oldest commit’s full message plus a numbered list of subjects for the rest.

Recent discussion has focused on the template’s final polish. Phillip Wood argued that `git rebase -i`’s current format forces users to scroll past irrelevant commented-out lines, and Junio has now proposed a cleaner alternative: a minimal summary of commit subjects followed by the editable message body. This keeps the context visible without clutter. Meanwhile, Matt Hunter and Phillip have raised broader usability questions—whether `--reedit-message` should be the default (to encourage commit hygiene) and how users can recover if `--update-refs` moves branches unexpectedly. The latter highlights a gap in Git’s tooling: reflogs don’t clearly show which refs were affected, making undo difficult. These concerns may lead to minor tweaks or documentation updates, but the core implementation is complete and awaiting Junio’s sign-off.
