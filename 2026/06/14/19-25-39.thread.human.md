The `git rebase --squash` feature proposal remains in limbo, awaiting resolution of key architectural and workflow concerns. Harald Nordgren's patch series introduces a `--squash` option to fold commit ranges while preserving original messages, but faces decisive challenges from Phillip Wood and others.

The core debate pits workflow integration against architectural fit. Harald argues the rebase implementation offers superior usability by automatically inferring commit ranges from upstream relationships, eliminating the need for manual range specification. However, Phillip Wood has raised serious efficiency concerns: the current approach risks repeated conflict resolution if the rebase base changes mid-operation, as it processes commits one-by-one rather than computing a single three-way merge of the entire range. He also criticizes the lack of interactive message editing, particularly when folded commits introduce new context.

An alternative approach has emerged from D. Ben Knoble and Patrick Steinhardt, who propose implementing this functionality as a `git history squash` subcommand instead. This would provide more flexible range selection through rev-list and pattern-based mechanisms, aligning with Patrick's broader `git history` roadmap.

The thread is currently stalled on three fronts: Phillip's unresolved efficiency critique (the primary technical blocker), the workflow-integration vs. architectural-fit debate, and the absence of Junio Hamano's final position on feature acceptance. Harald's latest responses have focused on user experience arguments but haven't addressed the technical concerns raised by Phillip. The recent procedural clarification about cover letter conventions, while helpful for future review clarity, doesn't advance the substantive discussion. The next move likely rests with Harald to either revise the implementation to address Phillip's efficiency concerns or engage more deeply with the `git history` alternative.
