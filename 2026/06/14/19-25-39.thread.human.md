The `git history squash` command, proposed by Harald Nordgren, is now in its eighth and likely final version, functionally complete and ready for integration. The series introduces a new `git history squash` subcommand that efficiently collapses a commit range—including merge commits, provided all parents are within the range—into its oldest commit while preserving descendant history. This avoids the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based approach.

Key features include strict input validation (rejecting single-commit ranges, empty ranges, and ranges with multiple bases), autosquash alignment for `fixup!`/`squash!`/`amend!` commits (grouping them under their targets in the `--reedit-message` template, just like `git rebase -i --autosquash`), and robust ref-handling (rejecting operations with interior refs by default, with an advice message guiding users to `--update-refs=head` to explicitly retarget the current branch). The command also sanitizes all `rev-list` options to prevent silently incorrect results.

All prior technical concerns have been addressed, including merge commit handling, fixup/squash/amend logic, and template consistency. The series has received substantive review from Phillip Wood, Junio C Hamano, and Patrick Steinhardt, with Junio signaling intent to queue it for the next release ("Will replace" in v7). The only remaining open questions are minor: whether `--reedit-message` should be the default (a commit hygiene debate left unresolved) and whether the default squashed commit message should incorporate `amend!` messages targeting the first folded commit. Harald has now acknowledged Matt Hunter’s feedback on the latter, noting it as a small but worthwhile consistency improvement that may be addressed before final integration. The series is technically sound and awaits Junio’s formal queuing.
