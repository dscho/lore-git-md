The `git history squash` command, proposed by Harald Nordgren, is now in its ninth iteration and functionally complete. This tool efficiently collapses a range of commits—including those with merge commits—into a single commit while preserving descendant history, avoiding the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based approach. The latest version, v9, addresses all prior feedback, including a critical `rev-list` option validation gap, an edge case in `amend!` message handling raised by Matt Hunter, and test suite readability improvements.

Key participants include Harald Nordgren, who has driven the series to completion; Phillip Wood, who provided substantive technical reviews and usability suggestions; and Junio C Hamano, who signaled intent to queue the series with a "Will replace" note in v7. The command now aligns with `git rebase -i --autosquash` in its `--reedit-message` template, groups `fixup!`/`squash!`/`amend!` commits under their targets, and incorporates `amend!` messages into the default squashed commit message. Junio has also endorsed the broader vision of using `git history squash` and `git replay` as a faster, non-interactive backend for `git rebase --autosquash`, positioning this as a foundational piece of Git’s future history-editing infrastructure.

The series is ready for integration, with only minor documentation and reflog message nits remaining—uncontroversial issues that Harald has acknowledged and will address in a future update. The unresolved debate over whether `--reedit-message` should be the default remains a minor usability tweak rather than a blocker. Junio’s sign-off suggests the series will likely appear in the next release.
