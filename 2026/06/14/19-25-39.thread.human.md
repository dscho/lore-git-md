This thread is about the `git history squash` command, a new standalone subcommand proposed by Harald Nordgren that efficiently collapses a range of commits into a single commit while preserving descendant history. The series is now in its tenth iteration (v10) and is functionally complete, addressing all prior feedback.

Harald has implemented the feature to fold commit ranges—including those with merge commits—into their oldest commit, avoiding the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based approach. Key improvements in v10 include accurate reflog messages that preserve the full revision expression, proper sanitization of `rev-list` options to maintain predictable behavior, and clearer documentation for `amend!` and `--reedit-message` usage. The command now groups `fixup!`, `squash!`, and `amend!` commits under their targets in the `--reedit-message` template, matching the behavior of `git rebase -i --autosquash`.

The series has received substantive review from Phillip Wood, who raised usability concerns that have since been resolved, and Junio C Hamano has signaled intent to queue it for the next release with a "Will replace" note on v7. The only remaining open question is a minor usability debate over whether `--reedit-message` (or `--edit`) should be the default, but this is not a blocker. Junio and D. Ben Knoble have also endorsed the broader vision of using `git history squash` as a faster, non-interactive backend for `git rebase --autosquash` when combined with `git replay`, positioning this command as a foundational piece of Git’s history-editing infrastructure.

The implementation touches `builtin/history.c` and refactors sequencer helpers in `sequencer.c`/`sequencer.h` to ensure template consistency. The series is ready for integration, with all technical concerns resolved and Junio’s endorsement in place.
