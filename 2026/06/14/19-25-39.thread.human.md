This thread is about the final stages of `git history squash`, a new command that collapses a range of commits into their oldest ancestor while preserving descendant history. Harald Nordgren’s v14 patch series is now functionally complete and technically ready for integration, with all prior feedback incorporated.

The command is designed to avoid the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based approach. It handles linear and merge-commit ranges, protects descendant branches by default, and supports autosquash-style message editing. The latest iteration, v14, addresses the last procedural and technical hurdles: Phillip Wood’s `Signed-off-by` trailer is now included, test robustness has been improved by replacing a fragile pipeline with `git rev-parse --short`, and the series structure remains decomposed for incremental review.

Junio Hamano has signaled intent to queue the series with “Will replace” in an earlier version, and all substantive reviews—particularly Phillip Wood’s detailed edge-case feedback—have been resolved. The series is currently cooking in `seen` and appears on track for the next release. Future work may integrate `git history squash` as a backend for `git rebase --autosquash`, but this is not a blocker for merging.
