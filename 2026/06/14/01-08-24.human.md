This is a substantive review raising concerns about the proposed `GIT_CLONE_DEPTH` environment variable feature. brian m. carlson questions whether shallow clones are appropriate for packaging workflows at all, arguing that Debian's approach of downloading sources before building (for reproducibility and privacy) would make the environment variable unnecessary.

The review identifies two potential problems with the feature: (1) it could break scripts that assume full history when `git describe` is called, and (2) shallow fetches are expensive for servers to serve, potentially leading to throttling. The reviewer acknowledges the author's use case but suggests the change might create more problems than it solves by making clone behavior depend on environment state.

The concerns focus on broader ecosystem impact rather than implementation details, suggesting this feature might need more discussion about whether the tradeoffs are worthwhile. The review doesn't reject the idea outright but presents counterarguments the author will need to address in any revision.
