This is a discussion about introducing AI-assisted code review to the Git project's workflow, sparked by the ongoing conversation about review bandwidth challenges. Christian Couder proposes formalizing AI review as a potential solution to reduce trivial review burden, suggesting it could catch issues like style violations, memory problems, and commit structure before human review.

The email explores several open questions about implementation: whether to use existing corporate AI systems (like those at GitLab/GitHub) versus a project-managed solution, timing of AI feedback (immediate or fallback), and how to handle substantive AI suggestions that might conflict with project policies. Christian references Sashiko, an AI review tool used by Linux kernel developers, as a potential model.

The proposal comes in the context of Patrick Steinhardt's earlier acknowledgment of limited review bandwidth from corporate contributors and Junio Hamano's suggestion about balancing patch submission with review obligations. Christian positions AI review as a way to maintain quality while addressing the human review bottleneck, though notes human oversight would still be required for architectural decisions and direction alignment.

Key technical considerations mentioned include:
- Potential integration with existing CI requirements
- Configuration to reference Git's CodingGuidelines
- Handling of different LLM outputs across platforms
- Prevention of problematic AI suggestions
- Authentication/abuse prevention mechanisms

The discussion represents a significant potential shift in how Git development might operate, though it remains at the exploratory stage with many open questions about implementation and policy.
