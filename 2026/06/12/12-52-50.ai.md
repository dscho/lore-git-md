This is a substantive technical review from Derrick Stolee responding to Kristofer Karlsson's RFC about optimizing merge-base calculations in `paint_down_to_common`. Stolee raises important concerns about the proposed early termination condition when finding all merge bases in complex DAG topologies.

Stolee provides a concrete counterexample where the optimization would fail - a graph where commits B and X both reach C through different paths, with B having a much higher generation number than C. In this case, the optimization would terminate too early before discovering that C should be marked stale. He notes this particularly affects cases where we need the full set of independent merge bases, not just a single merge base.

The review suggests alternative approaches, including investigating DFS-based methods for the stale-checking phase after both sides are exhausted. Stolee acknowledges the optimization could work for simpler cases (single merge base) but cautions about correctness in more complex scenarios. He encourages further investigation into DFS approaches that could maintain correctness while still providing performance benefits.

This is a deep technical review from someone with expertise in Git's graph algorithms, providing both theoretical counterexamples and practical suggestions for alternative approaches. The discussion remains focused on the technical merits while acknowledging the value of the optimization goal.
