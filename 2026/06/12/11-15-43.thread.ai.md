Thread identity: Performance optimization proposal for commit reachability algorithms in `paint_down_to_common` function, affecting merge-base calculations. Authored by Kristofer Karlsson as RFC, now with substantive review from Derrick Stolee.

Core problem: Current merge-base computations may perform unnecessary traversal after one side of the comparison is fully processed. The proposal introduces an early termination condition when one branch's commits are fully traversed, but this may not preserve correctness for all DAG topologies.

Current status: RFC stage with theoretical proposal showing promising speedups (300x-1000x) in simple cases, but Derrick Stolee has identified cases where the optimization would fail to find all merge bases. No implementation yet.

Key technical details: Targets `paint_down_to_common` in commit-reach.c. The optimization leverages two-phase traversal (infinite-generation then finite-generation commits). Stolee's counterexample shows the early termination could miss merge bases in complex DAGs where commits are reached through multiple paths with different generation numbers. Alternative DFS-based approaches for the stale-checking phase are suggested.

Open questions: The optimization needs revision to handle complex DAG cases while preserving correctness. DFS-based approaches may offer a path forward. Need to verify behavior with octopus merges and other edge cases. The scope may need to be narrowed to single-merge-base cases where the optimization is provably correct.

Participant positions: Kristofer Karlsson proposed the optimization with initial correctness proof. Derrick Stolee provided substantive technical review identifying a class of cases where the optimization fails, suggesting alternative approaches while acknowledging the value of the performance goal.

Related work: Builds on author's previous optimizations for single-merge-base cases. Relevant to ongoing performance work in large monorepos and commit-graph optimizations. Connects to Stolee's work on generation numbers and reachability algorithms.
