Thread identity: Performance optimization proposal for commit reachability algorithms in `paint_down_to_common` function, affecting merge-base calculations. Authored by Kristofer Karlsson as RFC, now with substantive review from Derrick Stolee.

Core problem: Current merge-base computations may perform unnecessary traversal after one side of the comparison is fully processed. The proposal introduces an early termination condition when one branch's commits are fully traversed, with ongoing refinement to handle complex DAG topologies where multiple paths exist between commits.

Current status: RFC stage with theoretical proposal showing promising speedups (300x-1000x) in simple cases. Derrick Stolee has confirmed that Karlsson's refined termination condition (requiring both no non-stale P1|P2 candidates in the queue AND exhaustion of one side) correctly handles the initial counterexample graph, producing the proper single merge base [B] rather than the incorrect [B, C]. This aligns with how `queue_has_nonstale()` already terminates the loop.

The refined optimization considers both commit exhaustion and STALE bit propagation through the intersection of commit histories. Stolee has introduced a new technical consideration - potentially switching to depth-first search (DFS) when all queued commits have both P1 and P2 bits set, though he cautions this would add significant implementation complexity. The discussion is now moving into deeper algorithmic considerations beyond the initial proposal.
