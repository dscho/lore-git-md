Kristofer Karlsson has proposed a performance optimization for Git's commit reachability algorithms, specifically targeting the `paint_down_to_common` function used in merge-base calculations. The key insight is that the traversal can terminate early once one side of the merge-base comparison is fully processed, rather than continuing to exhaust all paths. This could yield dramatic speedups of 300x-1000x for merge operations in large repositories, based on preliminary testing.

The proposal includes a detailed correctness argument leveraging the two-phase traversal structure (infinite-generation commits first, then finite-generation ones) and topological ordering guarantees. As an RFC, the author is seeking feedback on the theoretical soundness before implementation. Given the potential impact on merge performance in large monorepos, this being a follow-up to the author's previous optimizations, and the careful technical presentation, this thread is likely to attract scrutiny from Git's algorithms experts. The optimization would benefit all callers of `paint_down_to_common`, making it broadly applicable beyond just single-merge-base cases.
