Kristofer Karlsson proposed an optimization for merge-base calculations in `paint_down_to_common` that could dramatically speed up operations in large repositories by terminating early when one branch is fully traversed. The RFC included test results showing potential 300x-1000x improvements and a theoretical correctness argument.

Derrick Stolee, an expert in Git's graph algorithms, has raised significant concerns about the proposal's correctness in complex cases. He provided a concrete counterexample where the optimization would fail to identify all necessary merge bases in certain DAG topologies, particularly when multiple independent merge bases exist. While acknowledging the potential performance benefits for simpler cases, Stolee cautioned against adopting the optimization as-is for general use. He suggested exploring alternative approaches that might achieve similar speedups without sacrificing correctness, such as using depth-first search during the stale-checking phase. This substantive review shifts the discussion from theoretical possibility to practical implementation challenges that must be addressed before the optimization could be considered safe.
