This is a substantive technical review of a proposed performance optimization for Git's merge-base calculation algorithm. Derrick Stolee, a Git contributor with deep expertise in graph algorithms and performance optimizations, responds to Kristofer Karlsson's RFC proposing an early termination condition in `paint_down_to_common`.

Stolee identifies a potential correctness issue with the proposed optimization when finding *all* merge bases (not just the first one). He provides a concrete example where the optimization would incorrectly terminate early, missing some merge bases. The key insight is that the STALE bit propagation - which marks commits that cannot be merge bases - requires walking certain paths even after one "side" appears exhausted.

While acknowledging the optimization's potential (especially for the common case of single merge bases), Stolee suggests investigating a depth-first search (DFS) approach for the post-exhaustion phase as an alternative optimization path. This maintains correctness while potentially still providing performance benefits in the multi-merge-base case.
