This thread reports a bug in Git's notes handling during rebase operations. The issue occurs when a commit's changes are already present in the target branch (but not from the same patch) - the commit disappears during rebase, but its notes incorrectly persist and get attached to the rebased parent commit. 

The author has created a test case in `t/t3322-notes-rebase.sh` that demonstrates the problem: when rebasing commits where one becomes empty (because its changes already exist in the target branch), the notes from that disappearing commit incorrectly transfer to the rebased parent commit. This has been causing tracking problems in the author's workflow where they use notes to track Message-Ids of sent patches.

The test shows a clear case where notes should not persist when commits disappear during rebase due to content being already present. This appears to be a legitimate bug that's been present since at least 2023, though no other contributors have chimed in yet to confirm whether they've observed similar behavior.
