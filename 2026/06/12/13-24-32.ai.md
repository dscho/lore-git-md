This is a substantive review of Taylor Blau's performance optimization patch that integrates `--delta-islands` with `--path-walk` in `git pack-objects`. Derrick Stolee raises important questions about whether the patch fully demonstrates the intended benefits of these two features working together.

Stolee focuses on two key aspects: the ability to write bitmaps with `--path-walk` (which wasn't previously possible) and whether the combined features actually achieve meaningful pack size reductions. While the patch clearly shows performance improvements from bitmap reuse (point 1), Stolee notes the test results don't convincingly demonstrate the path-walk's pack size benefits (point 2). He suggests the test repository may not adequately exercise the path-walk optimization cases where it should shine.

The review identifies several areas needing clarification: whether the simple implementation truly integrates the features' behaviors rather than just making them coexist, whether the test coverage includes non-trivial interactions between path-walk and bitmaps, and whether the documentation claims about pack size reductions are adjusted to reflect actual observed behavior. Stolee's technical analysis of the object ordering implications shows deep engagement with the patch's mechanics while remaining constructive in tone.

This review moves the discussion forward by shifting focus from correctness (which the tests verify) to the harder question of whether the patch achieves its full optimization potential. The feedback suggests additional test scenarios may be needed to validate the interaction between path-walk's delta compression benefits and bitmap reuse before the series can be considered complete.
