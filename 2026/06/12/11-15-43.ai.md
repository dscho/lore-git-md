This is a performance optimization proposal for Git's commit reachability algorithms, specifically targeting the `paint_down_to_common` function used in merge-base calculations. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, suggests a new termination condition that could dramatically speed up merge-base computations in large monorepos.

The key insight is that once one "side" of the merge-base calculation (e.g., one branch being compared) is fully traversed, no new merge-base candidates can be found. The proposal would stop the traversal at this point rather than continuing until all possible paths are exhausted. The author provides a detailed correctness argument based on the two-phase nature of the traversal (infinite-generation commits first, then finite-generation ones) and the topological ordering guarantees.

The optimization shows promising results in preliminary testing, with reported speedups of 300x-1000x for merge-base and merge-tree operations in large repositories. The change would benefit all callers of `paint_down_to_common`, not just single-merge-base cases as with the author's previous optimization.
