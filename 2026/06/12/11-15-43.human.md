This is a performance optimization proposal for Git's merge-base calculation, targeting slow performance in large monorepos with complex commit graphs. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, builds on their previous work optimizing the single merge-base case, now aiming to accelerate finding all merge-bases.

The key insight is that the current algorithm in `paint_down_to_common` continues processing commits longer than necessary. The proposed optimization would terminate the walk as soon as one "side" of the commit ancestry being compared is exhausted from the traversal queue, rather than waiting for all possible paths to be stale. The author provides a detailed correctness argument explaining why this is safe, particularly in the context of commit-graph generation numbers.

The potential performance impact is substantial - the author reports observed speedups of 300x to 1000x in large monorepo scenarios. Importantly, this would benefit all callers of `paint_down_to_common`, not just single merge-base cases. The email is structured as an RFC to validate the correctness argument before implementing the change.

This appears to be a well-reasoned optimization from an author who has previously contributed in this area. The technical explanation is thorough and the performance claims are compelling if validated. The change would affect core commit-walking logic, so careful review of the edge cases will be important, but the approach seems promising for addressing a known pain point in large repositories.
