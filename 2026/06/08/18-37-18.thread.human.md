This thread introduces the `--linearize` option for `git replay`, a feature designed to flatten merge commits into linear history by walking only first parents, effectively providing server-side functionality equivalent to `git rebase --no-rebase-merges`. The series, now at v4 with three patches from Toon Claes, has incorporated nearly all feedback from reviewers including Junio Hamano, Elijah Newren, Justin Tobler, and Johannes Schindelin. Junio has indicated readiness to merge the series to 'next', and all patches carry Dscho's Signed-off-by.

The implementation is technically complete, with comprehensive test coverage for edge cases like root commits and option combinations, clear documentation, and no unresolved technical concerns—except one significant architectural debate. Patrick Steinhardt has raised concerns about the long-term extensibility of both the boolean `reverse` flag introduced in Patch 1/3 and the `--linearize` option itself in Patch 3/3. He notes that `git rebase` supports three modes for handling merges (`--no-rebase-merges`, `--rebase-merges`, and `--rebase-merges=rebase-cousins`), while `--linearize` hardcodes only the first. This, he argues, could limit future flexibility and obscure the meaning of callsites. His review does not reject the patch but invites discussion on whether the interface should mirror `git rebase`'s full flexibility now to avoid future refactoring.

The series has otherwise received strong support. Elijah Newren and Justin Tobler approved the technical approach after addressing documentation and test coverage gaps, while Johannes Schindelin remains supportive. Toon has volunteered to reconcile the series with Dscho's merge-replay approach if needed. The outstanding question is whether the current implementation is sufficient as an MVP or if it should be expanded to support all `git rebase` modes before merging. Junio's final approval may hinge on resolving this debate, potentially delaying the series' progression to 'next'.
