The `git replay --linearize` feature, introduced in v5 of Toon Claes's patch series and merged to `master` on July 15, 2026, has hit a critical post-merge snag. While the series aimed to provide server-side linearization of history—flattening merge commits by walking only first parents—it inadvertently introduced a **silent commit-dropping regression** in single-branch replay scenarios. Johannes Schindelin identified the issue: when replaying a single branch (e.g., `master~2..master`), only the tip commit is preserved, while intermediate commits (including merges) vanish. The root cause is the removal of the `replayed_base` logic in `pick_regular_commit()`, which previously ensured linearized history was preserved for single branches.

The regression isn't the only concern. The thread has also become a **philosophical debate** about CLI design. Patrick Steinhardt initially argued for consistency with `git rebase`'s `--rebase-merges` syntax, but after conceding the UX clarity argument, he now demands **explicit justification in commit messages** for any divergence from established patterns. Johannes Schindelin, meanwhile, rejects `git rebase` as a usability anti-pattern and advocates for clearer option names like `--replay-merges=<mode>`. Toon Claes has since weighed in, defending the current `--linearize` option name by clarifying its intended behavior: it should produce a **single linear sequence** regardless of input branches, making the output predictable. He suggests users who want to preserve branch independence can replay branches separately, though this may require manual `COMMITTER_DATE` adjustments. The discussion now centers on **documenting this behavior prominently** in the man page to avoid user surprises.

Despite the merged status, the series remains critically flawed. The **silent commit-dropping regression** is the highest priority, requiring an urgent follow-up patch to restore the `replayed_base` logic or equivalent. The **CLI design debate** has evolved into a process-level discussion about how to document design decisions, with Patrick Steinhardt framing it as a principle for project-wide consistency. A secondary concern—how the feature handles divergent merge commits—remains unresolved but is overshadowed by the regression. The thread now reflects a clash of design principles (UX clarity vs. consistency) and a new expectation for **documented rationale** in commit messages. For now, the feature is merged but broken, and the debate over its interface is far from settled.
