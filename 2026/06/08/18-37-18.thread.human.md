Toon Claes and Johannes Schindelin (Dscho) have completed their three-patch series adding a `--linearize` option to `git replay`. This new feature flattens merge commits into linear history, similar to `git rebase --no-rebase-merges`, providing an alternative to Dscho's separate merge-replay approach. 

The series began with preparatory refactoring - converting an enum to a boolean flag and introducing symmetrical commit mapping helpers. The final patch, just submitted, implements the core linearization logic by skipping merge commits and mapping them to their first parent. The implementation includes comprehensive test coverage and clear documentation, while explicitly prohibiting use with `--revert` for safety.

The work represents a successful collaboration between Toon and Dscho, offering users a choice between this linearization approach and Dscho's merge-preserving method. While the technical implementation appears sound, some coordination may still be needed with Kristoffer Haugsbakk's documentation work and Patrick Steinhardt's related `git history drop` series to ensure consistent terminology and avoid overlap. The series is now complete and ready for maintainer consideration.
