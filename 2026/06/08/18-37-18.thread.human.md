The `git replay --linearize` series by Toon Claes has been fully merged to Junio Hamano's 'seen' branch, but a subtle behavioral difference with traditional flattening rebase has now surfaced. While the series was approved and merged after addressing all prior feedback, Junio's latest review highlights that `git replay --linearize` behaves differently from `git rebase --no-rebase-merges` when replaying merge commits that merge two divergent branches.

Specifically, when replaying a merge commit (Z) that merges branches X and Y, `git replay --linearize` grafts only one branch (e.g., X) onto the new history, silently discarding the other branch (Y). In contrast, a flattening rebase would replay both branches, producing a linear history that includes both X and Y (as X' and Y'). Junio suggests this may surprise users, as it effectively drops half of the merged history without warning. He proposes adding a test to document this behavior and questions whether users should have control over which branch survives.

The review includes a concrete test case to expose the issue, along with a ready-to-apply patch that would serve as both a regression test and documentation of the current behavior. This is a substantive technical point that could lead to a follow-up patch to either adjust the behavior or clarify its implications in the documentation. The series itself remains merged, but this feedback may prompt further refinement.
