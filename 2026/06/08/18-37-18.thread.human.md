### Human mode summary

This thread is about adding a `--linearize` option to `git replay` that flattens merge commits into a linear history. The feature, now at version 7, is technically complete and addresses all prior feedback, including a regression fix that ensures all replayed commits are properly linearized. The series is ready for review, with no unresolved technical objections, but Junio C Hamano has raised a new concern about combining `--linearize` with `--revert`.

The key participants are Toon Claes (the author), Johannes Schindelin (who contributed preparatory work and supports the current design), and Junio (who has focused on edge cases and interface clarity). The series introduces `--linearize` as a standalone flag, diverging from `git rebase`'s `--rebase-merges` syntax, and this design choice has been justified in the commit messages. A behavioral difference from `git rebase --no-rebase-merges` is documented and tested.

The latest development is Junio’s substantive review of v7, where he questions whether the replay machinery can safely handle partial merge ranges when `--linearize` and `--revert` are combined. Specifically, he asks what happens if the replay range includes a merge commit and one side of the merge but excludes the other side and the merge base. This edge case was not covered in earlier versions, and its resolution will determine whether the mutual exclusivity restriction is retained or removed. The series remains under review, with no evidence of graduation to `next` or `master`.
