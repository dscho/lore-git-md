This is the second and final patch in a performance optimization series for Git's priority queue implementation. The patch generalizes the "lazy queue" pattern (which defers removals to allow subsequent puts to become simple replaces) into the core `prio_queue` implementation. This eliminates duplicate optimizations in several callers while maintaining measured speedups of 1.7-2.7% on traversal-heavy operations like merge-base and revision walking.

The key technical change fuses get+put operations (a dominant pattern in commit traversals) into single replace operations, avoiding full remove-rebalance-insert cycles. The patch removes the now-redundant `prio_queue_replace()` function since the optimization is handled automatically by the data structure. Three callers that previously implemented their own get+put fusion (builtin/describe.c, commit.c, and builtin/show-branch.c) are simplified to use the standard interface.

Performance benchmarks on a 1.8M-commit monorepo show consistent speedups with no regressions. The changes have been thoroughly reviewed across four iterations, with all substantive feedback addressed. The first patch in the series (already summarized) handled interface hardening and naming conventions; this second patch completes the optimization work.

The patch is well-tested and ready for merging, representing the culmination of an effort to eliminate duplicate lazy_queue implementations while maintaining performance gains across Git's commit traversal operations.
