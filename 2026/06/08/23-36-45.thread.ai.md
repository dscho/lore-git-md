Thread summary:

Performance investigation in worktree diff operations initiated by D. Ben Knoble, with key analysis from Jeff King. The thread began with Knoble reporting a 10x slowdown in `git diff` operations in secondary worktrees (223.3ms vs 3.4ms in main worktree), profiling showing excessive time in `ie_match_stat` and `ce_modified_check_fs`. Jeff King recontextualized this as a racy-git problem where files sharing modification times force full content checks, suggesting USE_NSEC build configuration for nanosecond-precision timestamps as a solution.

Knoble's follow-up confirms `git update-index --refresh` resolves the slowdown, validating King's racy-git diagnosis. The discussion now explores two solution paths: 1) whether `git diff` should auto-refresh the index (seen as a band-aid) and 2) whether new worktrees should refresh their index on creation. Knoble notes some operations like `git restore` may already do this implicitly.

Technical investigation reveals Meson lacks explicit USE_NSEC support despite handling some timestamp configurations. Knoble proposes adding this and testing its effectiveness, with potential patches for both the build system change and worktree creation behavior. The thread has evolved from initial observations to considering both immediate workarounds (index refresh) and systemic solutions (USE_NSEC support in Meson, worktree initialization changes). Key open questions remain about the tradeoffs between automatic refreshes versus precision timestamp solutions, and whether worktree-specific factors beyond racy-git may still play a role.
