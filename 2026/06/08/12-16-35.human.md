This is a substantive review from Junio C Hamano on Pablo Sabater's RFC patch to change `git history reword` behavior. Junio challenges the core rationale for making `history reword` abort when commit messages match, arguing that both the current behavior of `commit --amend`/`rebase -i` and the proposed `history reword` behavior are conceptually flawed.

Junio makes several key points: (1) Committer timestamp updates are a legitimate reason to recreate commits even when messages match, (2) capability differences between commands don't justify behavioral inconsistencies, and (3) the ideal design would default to no-op when messages match (with a `--force` override) - though he acknowledges this ship may have sailed for established commands. He also notes `rebase -i`'s multi-commit nature complicates any force-rewrite mechanism.

The review shifts the discussion from implementation details to broader design philosophy, suggesting the proposed change might be solving the wrong problem. Junio's position implies that if `history reword` is to diverge from amend/rebase behavior, it should be toward a more principled design rather than just optimizing for implementation constraints. This raises the bar for the RFC, requiring the author to either defend the pragmatic approach more strongly or reconsider the underlying design.
