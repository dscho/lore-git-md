Performance optimization for `git ls-files` with pathspec filtering. This is a follow-up to Tamir Duberstein's patch that speeds up `ls-files` by filtering pathspecs before expensive `lstat` operations, showing dramatic improvements (60.7s->1.06s) in large repositories when most entries don't match the pathspec.

In response to Junio C Hamano's review, Tamir provides additional data showing the optimization adds only 14-42ms overhead in the worst-case scenario where all entries match the pathspec. He also clarifies that the change doesn't affect `--recurse-submodules` behavior since those options are already mutually exclusive with `--deleted`/`--modified` modes where the optimization applies.

The thread has now addressed both of Junio's technical concerns: (1) the performance tradeoff is quantified as minimal, and (2) the submodule behavior remains unchanged. The optimization appears ready for consideration, having demonstrated significant wins for selective pathspecs with negligible cost for all-matching cases.
