Performance regression in worktree diff operations. D. Ben Knoble reports that `git diff` runs about 10x slower in secondary worktrees compared to the main worktree, with benchmark data showing 3.4ms vs 223.3ms mean times. The slowdown appears in basic diff operations (without `--cached`) and significantly impacts shell prompt responsiveness when checking dirty status.

Profiling reveals the worktree spends more time in `ie_match_stat`, `ce_modified_check_fs`, and data conversion paths compared to the main worktree's faster `preload_thread` and symlink checking. The worktree also shows 150x more CPU instructions executed and twice as many page faults. The issue reproduces back to at least Git v2.50.0. Knoble seeks guidance on whether this is a known issue and offers to investigate further, noting potential areas of interest in the worktree and object-file subsystems where Patrick Steinhardt and Jeff King have previously contributed.
