This is a review of a performance optimization for `git describe` that would limit ref iteration to tags by default. The original patch showed significant speedup (3.1x) by avoiding iteration over non-tag refs when they would be filtered out anyway.

Patrick Steinhardt raises several technical points in his review:

1. He questions why the benchmark results weren't presented in raw hyperfine format, suggesting the original presentation might obscure some details.

2. He notes the optimization makes sense but its impact depends on repository structure (ratio of tags to other refs).

3. He suggests another potential optimization (wiring up exclude patterns) but acknowledges it's out of scope for this change.

4. He objects to declaring the `ref_count` variable at file scope in the test script, preferring test-local declarations.

5. He questions why the performance test is limited to the "files" ref backend, as the optimization should work for all backends.

The review is substantive - it engages with both the technical merits of the change and its test coverage. Patrick doesn't challenge the core optimization but identifies several areas where the implementation could be improved, particularly around test hygiene and completeness. His comments suggest the patch needs revision before it would be ready for merging.
