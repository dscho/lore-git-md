Compiler compatibility follow-up on version check implementations.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Dominik Loidolt's research into the history of GCC's bit-shift version check implementation in `compat/posix.h`. Having reviewed Loidolt's findings that traced the current approach to glibc and demonstrated NetBSD's successful use of a more readable comparison form, Steinhardt confirms there's no compelling reason to retain the bit-shift style. He notes the theoretical runtime evaluation difference would be irrelevant since these checks are compile-time evaluated in Git's usage.

The email leaves the decision to Loidolt on whether to submit a v3 patch series that would modernize both the Clang and GCC version check implementations to use the clearer comparison style. This represents a potential expansion of scope from the original Clang-focused warning fix, now that the historical context and technical equivalence of both approaches have been established. The exchange demonstrates Git's characteristic attention to historical context even for seemingly small implementation details, while showing openness to modernization when justified by research.
