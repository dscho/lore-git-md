This is a follow-up discussion in the RFC thread about adding feedback output to `git history reword`. Pablo Sabater responds to Patrick Steinhardt's substantive review of their proposed change, defending the need for some form of success output while engaging with the technical and philosophical concerns raised.

The key points in this response:
1. Pablo argues that unlike core Git commands which do provide feedback (like `git commit --amend`), the current silent behavior of `git history reword` makes it hard to verify the operation succeeded without additional commands.
2. They explain their specific choice to show the rewritten commit hash - because the original hash becomes invalid after reword, forcing users to manually search for the new hash in the history.
3. Pablo acknowledges Patrick's point about also showing rewritten branches and HEAD changes, admitting they hadn't considered this aspect but seeing its value.
4. On the consistency question (why treat `reword` differently from other `history` subcommands), Pablo concedes the feedback mechanism could be generalized, but maintains that some basic output should exist even in non-verbose mode.
5. While receptive to Patrick's verbose mode suggestion, Pablo pushes back slightly on the "rule of silence" principle, suggesting porcelain commands may warrant different feedback standards than plumbing.

The exchange shows Pablo thoughtfully engaging with the review while standing by the core motivation for the change. The discussion remains open on several fronts: whether to scope the feedback more broadly (to HEAD/branches), whether to generalize it across subcommands, and how to balance Git's traditional silence with porcelain usability needs. Patrick's earlier suggestion of a verbose mode appears to be gaining traction as a potential compromise path forward.
