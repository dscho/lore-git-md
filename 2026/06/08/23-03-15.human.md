This is a performance discussion about Tamir Duberstein's `git ls-files` optimization that filters pathspecs early to avoid unnecessary `lstat` calls. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Junio C Hamano's review by confirming the theoretical performance tradeoff - while the optimization helps when most entries don't match the pathspec, it could slow down cases where most entries do match.

Peff provides a concrete example of a potentially slower case: running `git ls-files` with all tracked files as pathspecs would result in quadratic matching overhead. However, he notes this is already a slow operation in practice and hasn't been a priority for optimization. He shares historical context about an abandoned pathspec trie optimization effort, suggesting the current approach may be good enough despite the theoretical edge case.

The exchange shows the optimization is technically sound but acknowledges a tradeoff that's unlikely to matter in real-world usage. With both the performance benefits (already demonstrated) and limitations (now clearly understood) documented, the patch appears ready for maintainer consideration.
