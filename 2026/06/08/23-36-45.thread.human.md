The thread continues to dissect why `git diff` is painfully slow in secondary worktrees—220ms versus 3ms in the main worktree—and has now homed in on the exact mechanism. The root cause is racy-git entries: files whose timestamps match the index timestamp but whose content may have changed. These entries slip past the existing counter-based guard in `diffcore_skip_stat_unmatch()`, preventing the index from being refreshed and persisted, so every `git diff` re-checks the same files from scratch.

Jeff King (Peff) just posted a patch that forces racy entries to be treated as stat-dirty by passing a new flag (`DIFF_RACY_IS_MODIFIED`) to `ie_match_stat()`. The patch works: after the first diff (which is now ~3.6× slower), subsequent diffs drop from ~500 ms to ~30 ms. The trade-off is clear—pay once to refresh the index, then reap the benefit on every later invocation. Peff suspects the first-diff slowdown comes from redundant content checks between `diff_filespec_check_stat_unmatch()` and `ce_modified_check_fs()`, but he’s not yet sure whether chasing that optimization is worth the complexity.

The patch gives the thread a concrete baseline: it proves that counting racy entries as dirty is a viable fix, and it surfaces a new performance wrinkle that may shape the final solution. Junio and others are now weighing whether the first-diff penalty is acceptable, or if the guard logic should be reworked more fundamentally. Meanwhile, the runtime-configuration camp (led by brian m. carlson) is still in play, but Peff’s patch has shifted the immediate focus to the diffcore subsystem itself.
