The worktree diff performance thread has homed in on a clear diagnosis and competing solutions. D. Ben Knoble initially reported that `git diff` in secondary worktrees was 65 times slower than in the main worktree (223 ms vs 3.4 ms), tracing the issue to racy-git timestamp precision. The root cause is second-granularity timestamps forcing full content checks via `ie_match_stat` and `ce_modified_check_fs`.

Jeff King (Peff) has proposed two fixes. His first patch added a `DIFF_RACY_IS_MODIFIED` flag to force index refreshes, which eliminated the slowdown for subsequent diffs but introduced a first-diff performance regression (1800 ms vs 500 ms). His latest patch takes a more fundamental approach: removing the `#ifdef USE_NSEC` guard in `read-cache.c` to enable nanosecond-aware timestamp comparisons unconditionally. This minimal change (8 lines removed) eliminates racy entries at the source, resolving the performance issue without tradeoffs. Testing shows the problem "goes away" with this approach.

Junio C Hamano has proposed an alternative design: adding a field to the `istate` structure to track racy entries that would benefit from auto-refreshing. This would avoid propagating flags through the diff machinery while achieving the same goal. Peff has cautiously agreed this is "fairly clean" but continues to prefer his nanosecond-precision patch as the simpler, more fundamental solution. The discussion has also touched on the role of `refresh_index_quietly()` in `cmd_diff()` and the counter-based guard in `builtin/diff.c`, though these are now secondary considerations.

The thread is now focused on validating Peff’s latest patch and evaluating Junio’s `istate` proposal as a fallback. No major objections have been raised, and the remaining open question is whether Peff’s nanosecond patch might introduce hidden edge cases. The counter-based guard’s architectural merit remains a side discussion. Participants appear aligned on the goal—eliminating racy-git entries—with the primary debate being whether to address it at the source (Peff’s patch) or through architectural refinements (Junio’s proposal).
