This is Junio Hamano's review of Miklos Vajna's patch to improve `git log --follow` behavior with non-linear histories and renames. Junio raises two main points:

First, he critiques the commit message's use of past tense ("used to store") when describing the current behavior, noting this could be confusing. He suggests using present tense for clarity and consistency with Git's documentation style.

Second, he questions whether the proposed solution (a commit-to-path map) fully handles merge cases where different parents may require following different paths. Using a concrete example of a merge commit M with parents B and C (each needing different paths path1 and path2), he asks how the slab would handle their common ancestor A - suggesting the single-path-per-commit model may not suffice for complex merge scenarios.

The review is substantive, engaging with the technical approach rather than surface-level issues. Junio doesn't reject the patch outright but identifies a potential gap in the design that needs addressing. His example demonstrates careful consideration of the edge cases that could arise in Git's commit graph traversal.
