This is a feature patch series introducing a new `--linearize` option to `git replay` that converts merge commits into linear history, similar to `git rebase --no-rebase-merges`. The series comes from Toon Claes with contributions from Johannes Schindelin (Dscho).

The first two patches refactor the replay internals to prepare for the new functionality, while the third adds the actual `--linearize` option. The motivation is to provide an alternative to Dscho's earlier work on replaying merges, giving users more control over how merge commits are handled during replay.

The implementation appears clean and well-structured, with test coverage added in t3650. The cover letter thoughtfully considers potential conflicts with other in-flight series (Kristoffer's documentation changes and Patrick's `git history drop` work) and offers to resolve them. 

This seems like a useful addition that aligns with existing rebase functionality while keeping the replay command's focus on history manipulation. The patch quality looks solid, with clear commit messages and appropriate test coverage. The option is deliberately not added to `git history` as the authors feel it doesn't fit that command's use case.
