This is a follow-up discussion in Derrick Stolee's series about disabling config includes, focusing on the environment variable mechanism. The exchange reveals some important nuances about the proposed feature.

Patrick Steinhardt had raised concerns in his review about whether a binary on/off switch provides enough granularity, suggesting an allow-list approach might better serve real-world use cases where some includes are desirable while others should be blocked. In this response, Stolee acknowledges the validity of Patrick's point about tools potentially needing project-relative config access.

Stolee concedes that recommending `GIT_CONFIG_INCLUDES=0` universally may be too strong, and agrees there's room for more nuanced control in the future. However, he maintains that having a complete disable option remains valuable for certain tools that need strict sandboxing, even if it's not a one-size-fits-all solution.

The discussion highlights the tension between security/sandboxing needs and workflow flexibility - a classic tradeoff in configuration systems. While the current implementation offers a simple binary switch, both participants recognize this might evolve toward more sophisticated controls if real-world usage reveals the need. The tone remains constructive, with Stolee open to future enhancements while defending the immediate utility of the basic mechanism.
