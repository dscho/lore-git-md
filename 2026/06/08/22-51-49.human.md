This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) on Derrick Stolee's config includes disablement series. Peff raises important concerns about the broader implications of the proposed changes.

The review makes two key technical points:
1. Disabling includes only addresses one vector of config-based sandbox escapes - other config options (like core.attributesFile or external diff commands) can still break sandboxing
2. The global nature of `--no-includes` could unintentionally break legitimate workflows that rely on conditional includes (like work vs personal email addresses)

Peff suggests the current approach may be too broad a solution for the stated problem, potentially creating new issues while only partially solving the sandboxing concern. The review doesn't reject the concept outright but encourages more careful consideration of the tradeoffs and potential unintended consequences.

The feedback is technically deep, citing specific config examples and real-world scenarios that would be affected. It demonstrates Peff's characteristic thorough understanding of Git's config system and its edge cases. This moves the discussion forward by highlighting aspects the original proposal hadn't fully addressed.
