This is a **substantive review** of the fourth patch in Tian Yuchen’s six-patch series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. Derrick Stolee raises two actionable points about the test placement and potential enhancements, both aimed at strengthening the series’ empirical validation.

Stolee’s first concern is **test placement**: the patch adds edge-case tests *after* the optimization (patch 3) rather than before. This ordering risks obscuring whether the tests pass due to the new logic or would have passed under the old behavior. He suggests reordering the series so the tests appear before the code change, allowing them to serve as regression checks. This is a structural suggestion, not a critique of the tests themselves, and aligns with the project’s preference for test-driven development.

His second point is **performance instrumentation**: he proposes augmenting the tests with a "commits walked" counter in the merge-base code, which would empirically demonstrate the optimization’s impact. This would address his earlier request (in the thread summary) for benchmarks validating the speedup from patch 3. The challenge here is that `t6600` tests three reachability backends (full graph, half graph, no graph), each with different traversal characteristics, but Stolee sees this as an opportunity to highlight the optimization’s consistency across backends.

The review is narrowly scoped to the test patch but carries implications for the entire series. Neither point questions the correctness of the edge-case coverage—Elijah Newren’s tests are praised for their thoroughness—but rather focuses on how to best *demonstrate* that correctness and performance. Stolee’s feedback is constructive and specific, offering clear paths to address both concerns without requiring redesign of the core logic. The ball is now in the author’s court to decide whether to reorder the series or add instrumentation.
