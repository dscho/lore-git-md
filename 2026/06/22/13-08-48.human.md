This is Junio C Hamano’s reply to the second and final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC v2 series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving all `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. Junio acknowledges the goal of reducing root directory clutter (from ~550 to ~80 files) but expresses skepticism about whether the change will meaningfully improve navigability. His primary concern is the disruption to in-flight and upcoming patches, which will require updates to accommodate the new directory structure, complicating backports and fixes for both the current and future codebase.

The tone is measured but cautious. Junio does not outright reject the proposal—he notes his willingness to accept changes he doesn’t personally favor—but his reservations about the practical costs (rebase pain, merge conflicts, and maintenance overhead) are clear. This email sets up a potential discussion about whether the benefits of the reorganization outweigh the transitional friction, particularly for downstream forks like GitHub’s and Microsoft’s, which were previously flagged as needing coordination.

For a busy reader, the key takeaway is that the series is technically ready (all feedback addressed, CI passing) but faces a maintainer-level question about timing and disruption. If you’re tracking Git’s internals or downstream integrations, this is worth monitoring; if you’re a casual observer, the outcome may not affect you directly. No new technical objections are raised here—just a logistical one.
