This is a **follow-up in the ongoing performance optimization series** for `paint_down_to_common()`, specifically addressing Derrick Stolee’s review of patch 2/6. The series aims to optimize Git’s merge-base calculation by terminating early when one side of a history is exhausted, a common bottleneck in repositories with asymmetric histories (e.g., import grafts or shallow clones).

Kristofer Karlsson, the patch’s author, responds to Stolee’s **surface-level and structural feedback** on the `paint_queue` struct introduced in patch 2. He agrees to adopt Git’s coding style (e.g., reformatting `switch` statements to split `case` blocks onto separate lines) and to rename the `pq` field to avoid ambiguity (e.g., `queue` instead of `prio_queue`). On the `case 0` logic, he clarifies that it’s intentional—used as a marker for "not in the queue"—and plans to improve documentation or add inline comments to make this clearer.

The most substantive discussion revolves around **where to place the early-exit condition**. Stolee suggests moving it into `paint_queue_get()` to simplify the loop, while Kristofer prefers keeping it external to avoid tightly coupling the queue logic with its usage. He also agrees to rephrase the termination check from `queue.p1_count + queue.p2_count + queue.pending_merge_bases == 0` to `(!queue.p1_count || !queue.p2_count) && !queue.pending_merge_bases` for clarity, though he notes this mirrors the logic already introduced in patch 3. Both agree to keep patches 2 and 3 separate to isolate the performance impact of the new counters.

The exchange confirms that **all technical objections to the series are resolved**, and the remaining work is stylistic or organizational. The author’s willingness to adapt to Stolee’s suggestions—particularly around refactoring order and code clarity—signals the series is nearing readiness for v2. For readers tracking this effort, the key takeaway is that the core optimization (patch 3) remains uncontroversial, and the focus has shifted to polishing implementation details.
