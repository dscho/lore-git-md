This is the third patch in the v2 series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. The email itself is a brief technical follow-up from Kristofer Karlsson, co-author of the core optimization patch, responding to Derrick Stolee’s suggestion to use **hyperfine** for performance benchmarking.

Kristofer acknowledges the tool and agrees to use it for measuring the impact of the changes. He expresses mild concern that the overhead of operations like `repo_parse_commit()` might overshadow the performance gains from the new `paint_queue` struct, making it hard to isolate the effect of the optimization. However, he accepts that **hyperfine** is the right starting point and plans to use it to demonstrate at least that there is no measurable regression. If needed, he may later introduce a custom binary to spread out the cost of commit parsing across multiple internal runs, but for now, the plan is to proceed with **hyperfine** as the agreed method for empirical validation.

The exchange is purely procedural and confirms the performance instrumentation approach for the series. No new technical concerns are raised, and the core optimization remains unchanged.
