This thread is Patrick Steinhardt’s third iteration of a four-patch series refactoring Git’s connectivity checks to remove hardcoded packfile assumptions, a blocker for his larger ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to generalize promisor object handling so that connectivity checks work with any backend—packfiles, reftable, or future pluggable storage—without sacrificing correctness or performance.

The series is now complete at v3, addressing all substantive feedback from earlier rounds. The key fix was a logical inversion in Patch 4, where the connectivity check incorrectly fell back to a full rev-list scan when a promisor object *was* found; this is now resolved with a cleaner `found` boolean flag in the iteration callback. Other improvements include a performance optimization in Patch 2 (reordering prefix matching to avoid unnecessary pack preparation) and better error propagation for pack open failures. Patch 3 refactored promisor-specific logic into a dedicated function, improving maintainability, while Patch 1 laid groundwork by consolidating pack-exclusion checks. A new test in `t/t5616-partial-clone.sh` verifies the optimized promisor check is used during partial fetches and that the fallback to rev-list occurs when expected.

Junio C Hamano and Christian Couder provided the most substantive reviews, with Junio catching the logical inversion in v2 and Christian suggesting the performance optimization and control-flow improvements. Patrick addressed all feedback, and the series is now uncontroversial and ready for final review. The changes are confined to `connected.c` and the test suite, with no user-facing behavior changes or on-disk format modifications. The next likely step is Junio queuing the series for `next` once ODB-focused reviewers (e.g., Justin Tobler, Taylor Blau) sign off. No open questions remain, and the series is a critical enabler for the ODB abstraction effort.
