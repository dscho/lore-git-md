This is Junio C Hamano weighing in on the stacked-branch protection design for Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series. The email confirms the final design choice: **when a branch scheduled for deletion is still the upstream of another branch, the operation will fail rather than delete it**. This aligns with the "abort-and-notify" approach Phillip Wood endorsed in the parent email and resolves the last major design ambiguity in the series.

Junio emphasizes that `--delete-merged` (or `--prune-merged` in earlier versions) should simply be a convenience wrapper around `git branch -d`, applying the same safety checks. This means the stacked-branch protection will apply uniformly to both commands, ensuring consistency. The decision avoids the complexity and potential pitfalls of automatic retargeting (e.g., updating dependent branches to point to `origin/master`), which could break `git rebase --fork-point` by introducing stale commits.

The patch series is now technically complete in terms of design, with all planned features implemented (per-branch opt-out, `--dry-run`, and stacked-branch protection). However, the current implementation of stacked-branch protection in v17 contains a critical bug: it preserves all upstream configurations instead of selectively clearing them for unmerged dependent branches. Harald Nordgren has acknowledged this and will revise the patch in the next iteration. Once fixed, the series should be ready for final review.
