This is the third patch in Tian Yuchen’s six-patch series optimizing Git’s merge-base calculation for one-sided histories. The patch implements the core performance win: an early-exit condition in `paint_down_to_common()` that terminates the walk once one side’s commit queue is exhausted, provided the walk has entered the finite-generation region. The change is minimal (13 lines in `commit-reach.c`) and builds directly on the per-side counters introduced in patch 2.

Derrick Stolee’s review is brief but positive. He endorses the early-exit logic and suggests moving the check into the dequeueing method for cleaner code organization, though he acknowledges the current placement is functionally correct. His main request is for performance benchmarks showing that patch 2 (the refactoring) adds no overhead and that this patch delivers the expected speedup. The review is substantive but uncontroversial—it focuses on code structure and measurable impact rather than correctness.

The patch itself is well-motivated and clearly explained. It targets a known bottleneck in repositories with asymmetric histories (e.g., PR branches against large mainlines) and cites 100–1000x speedups in benchmarks. The finite-generation guard ensures the optimization only activates when topological ordering is reliable, avoiding regressions in edge cases like grafts or shallow histories. Kristofer Karlsson is listed as the author, with help from Stolee and Elijah Newren, reflecting prior feedback on the halt condition.

While the patch is uncontested, the series as a whole faces a structural dispute over the refactoring approach in patches 1–2. Stolee has objected to patch 1’s degradation of code clarity and proposed an alternative ordering, but this patch (3/6) is unaffected by that debate. The remaining patches (4–6) add test coverage and documentation, which have not yet been reviewed.

For readers tracking the series, this patch is the payoff: a simple, effective optimization with clear performance benefits. The open question is whether the preparatory refactoring will be reworked to address Stolee’s concerns, but the core logic here looks solid.
