This is a **surface-level review** from Derrick Stolee on the second patch in Tian Yuchen’s six-patch series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. The series aims to speed up merge-base calculations by terminating early when one side of the history is exhausted, and this patch introduces a new `paint_queue` struct with per-side counters (`p1_count`, `p2_count`, `pending_merge_bases`) to replace the old `nonstale_queue` abstraction.

Stolee’s feedback is purely stylistic and mechanical: he points out that the `switch` statements in `paint_count_transition()` violate Git’s coding guidelines by cramming multiple cases onto single lines, and suggests a more conventional formatting. He also questions whether `case 0` should ever occur (it shouldn’t for `new_paint`, but is valid for `old_paint`), though he doesn’t propose removing it. Beyond style, he raises two minor naming concerns: the abbreviation `pq` in the struct could be ambiguous, and the loop’s termination condition (`queue.p1_count + queue.p2_count + queue.pending_merge_bases == 0`) would be clearer as `(!queue.p1_count && !queue.p2_count && !queue.pending_merge_bases)`.

The review is **not substantive**—it does not engage with the patch’s correctness, performance implications, or the broader refactoring strategy. Stolee’s earlier objection to the series’ structure (specifically, that patch 1’s refactoring of `ahead_behind()` is unnecessary if the goal is to replace `nonstale_queue`) remains unresolved, but this email does not revisit that point. The feedback here is unlikely to block progress, but the author may need to address the style nits before the series advances.
