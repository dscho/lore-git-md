This is a **maintainer's response** to Patrick Steinhardt's architectural concern about the boolean refactoring in Toon Claes's `git replay --linearize` series (Patch 1/3). Junio C Hamano weighs in on the trade-offs between code simplicity and future extensibility, siding with Patrick's caution.

The email centers on two key points: readability at the callsite and the risk of painting the code into a corner if a third replay mode is ever needed. Junio echoes Patrick's preference for enums over booleans when the parameter's meaning isn't immediately obvious from context, noting that even a well-named boolean variable (`use_revert_mode`) loses clarity when passed as a raw `true`/`false` argument. He suggests that verbose comments or sticking with the enum would be preferable to obscuring the intent.

This isn't a rejection of the patch, but it does signal that the refactoring may need to be revisited before the series can merge. The discussion is now squarely about balancing immediate code clarity against long-term flexibility—especially relevant given that Patch 3/3 introduces `--linearize`, a third mode in all but name. Junio's tone is measured, but his alignment with Patrick's concerns suggests the boolean approach may not survive another iteration without stronger justification. The series remains technically sound and otherwise approved, but this feedback could force a redesign of the foundational refactoring.
