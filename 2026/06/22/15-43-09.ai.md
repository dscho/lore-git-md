This is a **maintainer's follow-up** in the `git replay --linearize` series, specifically addressing the architectural debate over Patch 1/3’s boolean refactoring. Junio C Hamano weighs in on Patrick Steinhardt’s concerns about callsite clarity and future extensibility, siding with Patrick’s critique.

Junio reinforces the argument that boolean parameters obscure intent at the callsite—even when well-named variables are used—by contrasting C’s positional arguments with languages that support named parameters. He illustrates the problem with `my_function(true, false)` versus `my_function(use_revert_mode=true, verbose=false)`, noting that the former forces readers to consult the function signature to understand what `true` means. While C allows verbose comments like `my_function(true /* use_revert_mode */, false)`, Junio suggests that enums (e.g., `REPLAY_MODE_REVERT`) are often clearer and more future-proof when more than two modes are possible.

The message does not reject the patch outright but signals that the boolean approach may not be an improvement. It leaves the door open for Toon Claes to either revert to the enum or propose an alternative that balances readability and extensibility. This exchange is now the primary blocker for Patch 1/3, and likely for the entire series, given Junio’s "Will merge to 'next'" approval was contingent on resolving Patrick’s concerns. The discussion remains constructive and focused on trade-offs rather than correctness.
