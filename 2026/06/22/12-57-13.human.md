This is a **documentation patch** updating `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to codify how contributors should handle design critiques in patch resubmissions. The patch is now **effectively final** and ready for merging after receiving explicit approval ("LGTM") from Patrick Steinhardt, a high-weight reviewer and prolific contributor.

The update adds about 20 lines to two sections of `SubmittingPatches`, instructing authors to (1) defend design decisions on the mailing list *before* revising code, (2) document resolutions in cover letters and commit messages, and (3) preserve a "justification trail" for reviewers. It explicitly states that topics with unresolved fundamental critiques will not be merged. The v4 text refines earlier phrasing to clarify the sequential workflow ("defend first *and* work with reviewers") and adds concrete examples of design critiques (e.g., questioning whether a feature is worth implementing).

Junio, the maintainer, positions this as a best-practice clarification to reduce frustration for reviewers and improve transparency in the project’s decision-making. The patch does not introduce new policy but formalizes existing expectations. While earlier versions sparked discussion about tension with Git’s "show me the code" norm—particularly from Kristoffer Haugsbakk, who argued it could burden contributors without C experience—the v4 text softens prescriptive language and avoids implying that contributors who cannot implement ideas are unable to defend them. These broader cultural questions remain unresolved but are outside the patch’s scope.

With Junio as the author and Patrick’s unqualified approval, this patch is likely to be merged without further revisions. The broader debate about how to balance design justification with code-first contributions may resurface in future threads, but this update solidifies the project’s current stance. No code, build system, or test changes are involved.
