This is a **substantive architectural discussion** in the ongoing `git log --graph` cascading indentation series, specifically about the safety and design of the revision queue abstraction. Kristofer Karlsson responds to Jeff King’s analysis of the dual-queue fragility in Pablo Sabater’s v5 series, proposing a **fundamental redesign** of the lookahead mechanism.

Kristofer confirms Jeff’s observation that `rewrite_parents()` leaks parents into `commit_queue` in topo mode without consuming them, creating a latent bug risk in history simplification scenarios. He diagnoses the root problem: `revs->commits` is overloaded as both a work queue and an output buffer, making it unsafe for `graph.c` to peek directly. While his original goal was performance optimization (`kk/prio-queue-get-put-fusion`), he now argues for a **semantic cleanup** of the queue architecture.

The key insight is that **multi-element peeking is inherently unsafe**—after processing the first commit, the second peeked commit may change due to simplification. Kristofer proposes replacing the peek-based abstraction with a **two-slot lookahead buffer** inside `struct git_graph`, populated by `get_revision_internal()`. This ensures commits are fully simplified before inspection, eliminating the need for manual filtering in `graph.c`. His prototype resolves the three failing test cases in `t4218` without requiring timestamp tweaks, though he notes the approach still has a documented edge case (redundant indentation when filtered parents are present).

**Technical details for follow-up:**
- **Files affected:** `graph.c`, `graph.h`, `revision.c` (abstraction layer)
- **Proposed symbols:** `struct git_graph.lookahead[2]`, modified `graph_is_interesting()`
- **Edge case resolved:** The three `test_expect_failure` cases in `t4218` now pass
- **Compatibility:** Works with Jeff King’s planned `revs->commits` removal
- **Open question:** Whether the lookahead buffer requires timestamp adjustments for deterministic output

Kristofer’s proposal **directly challenges the v6 peek-based abstraction** (conditionally approved by Junio and Phillip Wood) and offers a technically sound alternative. The thread is now at a **critical architectural crossroads**—the maintainer must decide between incremental improvements to v6 or adopting the lookahead redesign. Either path will require follow-up work, but the lookahead approach appears to address the core fragility more robustly. Pablo Sabater’s next steps will likely involve prototyping the lookahead buffer in a v7 series.
