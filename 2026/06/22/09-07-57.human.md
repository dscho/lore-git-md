This is the **stacked-branch safety design discussion** for Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series, now in its 17th iteration. Phillip Wood responds to Junio C Hamano’s proposal for handling branches that serve as upstreams for other branches (e.g., `feature1` if `feature2` stacks on it). The thread has already settled on preserving such branches by default, but this email drills into the user-facing behavior when the protection triggers.

Phillip endorses the safest option: **aborting the deletion** and notifying the user, rather than automatically retargeting the dependent branch’s upstream. His concern is that automatic retargeting (e.g., moving `feature2@{u}` from `feature1` to `origin/master`) would break `git rebase --fork-point`, which relies on the upstream’s reflog to exclude stale commits. This could silently pull in unwanted history during rebases. He also reaffirms that the protection should apply equally to both `git branch -d` and `git branch --delete-merged`, since both commands check whether a branch is merged to its upstream.

The discussion is now focused on implementation details: how to detect upstream dependencies, how to notify the user, and whether to offer retargeting as an opt-in flag (e.g., `--retarget-upstreams`). The outcome will shape the final safety mechanism in the series, which already includes per-branch opt-out, `--dry-run` preview, and stacked-branch protection. This is a low-risk, high-impact refinement—Phillip’s feedback is substantive and addresses a real edge case, making it worth attention from anyone following the series.
