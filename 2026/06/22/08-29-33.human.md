Kristofer Karlsson responds to Jeff King’s analysis of the dual-queue fragility in the revision walker, advancing the architectural debate toward a concrete redesign.

Kristofer confirms Jeff’s observation that `rewrite_parents()` leaks parents into `commit_queue` without consuming them in `--topo-order` mode, calling the current state of `revs->commits` "multi-purpose and unclear." While he hasn’t yet traced how this affects the graph lookahead logic, he frames the problem as a broader technical debt issue: the revision walker’s dual-container design (`commits` vs. `commit_queue`) creates ambiguity about which queue to inspect for what purpose. This reinforces the need for the abstraction layer proposed in Pablo’s v6 series—but also hints at a larger cleanup effort Kristofer may undertake separately.

The key insight comes in Kristofer’s critique of the peek-based approach. He argues that peeking at the second element is inherently unsafe because the queue’s state can change after the first element is processed. Instead, he proposes a **lookahead buffer** inside `struct git_graph`, populated by `get_revision_internal()`, which would apply all filtering (including `get_commit_action()`) before commits are inspected. This eliminates the need for manual filtering in `graph.c` and directly addresses the three failing test cases in `t4218`. His prototype suggests this redesign is viable, and he plans to post it as a formal alternative to Pablo’s v6 abstraction layer.

The discussion leaves two architectural paths forward: Pablo’s peek-based helpers (with iteration support) or Kristofer’s lookahead buffer. The latter appears more robust but would require a v7 resend. Either way, the thread is now **architecturally blocked** until the maintainer (Junio) weighs in on which direction to pursue.
