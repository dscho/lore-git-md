This is the final design resolution for the stacked-branch protection mechanism in Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series. Phillip Wood now proposes a pragmatic simplification: **when a branch cannot be deleted because it is the upstream of another branch, clear its upstream configuration instead of attempting to reassign it**. This approach avoids the complexity of recursive retargeting while still preventing stale references.

The change reflects a shift from the earlier debate (retargeting vs. abort-and-notify) to a middle ground: the command will still abort deletion for dependent branches, but it will also **clear the upstream config of the protected branch** with a warning. This sidesteps edge cases like self-referential upstreams (e.g., `b3→b3`) and chains of dependencies (e.g., `b1→b2→b3`) while ensuring no stale rebase commits are left behind. The implementation is expected to be straightforward, and the behavior remains backward-compatible—retargeting logic can be added later as an optional flag (e.g., `--retarget-upstreams`).

This resolution aligns with Junio C Hamano’s preference for simplicity and Phillip’s earlier endorsement of the abort-and-notify default.
