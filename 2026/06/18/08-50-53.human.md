This is a documentation patch refining the project’s contribution guidelines. Junio C Hamano updates `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to explicitly require contributors to address high-level design or viability critiques before resubmitting patches. The change is a minor but meaningful clarification of existing expectations, not a new policy.

The patch adds two new paragraphs to the “Responding to Reviews” section, instructing authors to defend their design choices on the mailing list, provide clearer justifications in cover letters and commit messages, and document the resolution of design concerns in the commit message body. A single bullet point is also added to the “Commit Message Guidelines” section to reinforce this requirement. The tone is prescriptive but constructive, framing the requirements as best practices to improve transparency and reviewer satisfaction.

This is the second iteration of the patch, incorporating minor wording refinements from the first version. The changes are uncontroversial and align with Junio’s long-standing role in shaping the project’s contribution culture. The patch is narrowly scoped, touching only the `SubmittingPatches` document, and does not introduce new technical terms or affect code, build systems, or tests.

The motivation is clear: reduce the frustration caused by patch series advancing through multiple iterations without resolving fundamental objections, which obscures the project’s decision-making process. The new text makes it explicit that unresolved design critiques will block a topic from being merged, reinforcing the importance of engaging with feedback early.

For a busy reader, this patch is worth noting as a small but useful step toward improving the quality and clarity of contributions. It doesn’t change how Git works, but it may help streamline future discussions by setting clearer expectations for contributors.
