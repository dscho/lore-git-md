This is a **maintainer response** in the `git branch --delete-merged` series thread, weighing in on the **stacked-branch safety** question raised by Phillip Wood. Junio C Hamano questions whether protecting branches that serve as upstreams for unmerged stacked branches is necessary, given typical rebase workflows.

Junio argues that in stacked-branch workflows (e.g., where Topic B depends on Topic A), once Topic A is merged and "graduates," later iterations of Topic B would not recreate the original merge of Topic A into `main`—they would reuse the existing base. Therefore, deleting Topic A after it’s merged should not break Topic B, since Topic B’s base is already part of `main`. He suggests that the proposed safety mechanism (filtering out branches that are upstreams of non-deleted branches) might be unnecessary, as users would not typically need to recreate the merge of a pruned branch.

The response is **substantive but cautious**, acknowledging the practice of stacked branches while questioning the real-world impact of the proposed protection. Junio does not outright reject the idea but implies that the added complexity may not be justified. This leaves the door open for further discussion: if others can demonstrate scenarios where pruning merged branches *would* break stacked workflows, the protection might still be warranted.
