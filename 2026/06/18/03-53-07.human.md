This is a documentation patch — a proposed update to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` aimed at helping new contributors better handle design-level feedback.

Michael Montalbo replies to Junio C Hamano’s recent addition to the `SubmittingPatches` guide, which advises contributors to address high-level design critiques before iterating on implementation details. The original text warns that spending too much effort polishing code is wasted if the design itself is still under question. Michael agrees the guidance is useful and suggests two small wording tweaks: starting with a direct imperative (“Be particularly mindful…”) and replacing “effort in the implementation” with “effort on the implementation.” He also proposes expanding the explanation of what a “clearer justification” should include — explicitly naming the “why,” alternatives considered, and why the chosen approach is correct — to make the advice more concrete for newcomers.

The patch is minor and editorial, but it targets a real pain point for new contributors: distinguishing between design-level and implementation-level feedback, and knowing when to stop polishing code that may never be accepted. The suggestions are sensible and align with the project’s tone in the guide. No controversy is expected.
