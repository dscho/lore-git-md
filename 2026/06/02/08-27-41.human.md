This is a continuation of the discussion about renaming Git's init-related code from `init-db` to `init` to match the modern command name. Junio C Hamano pushes back against Patrick Steinhardt's proposal to rename the internal implementation files and functions, arguing that such renames simply shift the annoyance from one group (those who prefer modern naming) to another (those accustomed to the historical names).

Junio makes a practical point about code navigation - he recalls how previous renames (like `ll-merge`) made files harder to locate. His stance is that if the only motivation is aesthetic consistency, the cost of renaming (in terms of disrupting existing familiarity) may outweigh the benefits. This suggests the internal rename may not happen unless a stronger technical justification emerges.

The exchange highlights Git's ongoing tension between codebase consistency and historical familiarity. With Junio as maintainer expressing clear reservations, and Patrick having already indicated flexibility, this thread appears headed toward keeping the status quo for internal naming while preserving the `git-init-db` alias as previously agreed. The discussion serves as a case study in how even mechanical renames require weighing different contributors' workflows and expectations.
