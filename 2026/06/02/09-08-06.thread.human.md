Junio Hamano has updated the SubmittingPatches documentation to improve guidance for contributors, with a new section on writing effective cover letters. The original two-patch series added organization for typo-fix advice and established three key principles for cover letters: making changes understandable without prior context, enabling new readers to follow along, and summarizing changes between iterations.

After review, Junio has incorporated substantive feedback from Derrick Stolee, who suggested adding explicit guidance about risk/reward tradeoffs. The maintainer agreed this complements the existing advice and will include it as a "0th item" before the bullet points. The discussion reinforced that human-written summaries remain valuable even when automated tools like GitGitGadget provide range-diffs, as purely technical dumps often prove harder to parse than well-crafted explanations.

While Patrick Steinhardt's earlier grammatical suggestions were accepted, the thread maintained a clear boundary between documenting conceptual best practices (in scope) and tool-specific mechanics (out of scope). With Junio's latest response accepting Stolee's substantive addition, the documentation improvements now have clear consensus and will help contributors understand both the how and why of effective patch submission.
