This is a documentation patch in response to review feedback on Junio C Hamano's series updating the SubmittingPatches guide. The thread focuses on improving contributor documentation, specifically around cover letter best practices.

Junio responds to Derrick Stolee's substantive review of the cover letter guidance patch, agreeing with Stolee's suggestion to explicitly address risk/reward tradeoffs in the documentation. Stolee had proposed additional text explaining how contributors should articulate why their changes justify the inherent costs of maintenance and potential regressions. Junio acknowledges this complements his original goal of making cover letters accessible to a wide audience, and suggests incorporating it as a "0th item" before the existing three-bullet list.

The exchange also reinforces the value of human-written summaries in cover letters, even when tools like GitGitGadget provide automated range-diffs. Junio notes that purely automated range-diff dumps without human context are often harder to parse than simply reapplying and diffing the patches locally. This aligns with Git's culture of valuing clear communication alongside technical changes.

The discussion shows consensus forming around these documentation improvements, with Junio accepting the substantive feedback while maintaining the original patch's focus on principles over tool-specific mechanics. The changes will help contributors understand not just how to format cover letters, but why certain content choices lead to more effective communication with reviewers and maintainers.
