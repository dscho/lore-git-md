Performance optimization for Git's priority queue implementation. This patch generalizes a pattern where multiple callers were independently optimizing the common case of popping an item from the queue and immediately pushing a new one (typically a commit's parents during traversal). The optimization defers the actual removal until the next operation, allowing a subsequent push to become a simple replace operation that avoids a full remove-rebalance-insert cycle.

The change folds the optimization into the core prio_queue implementation, eliminating three separate ad-hoc implementations in builtin/describe.c, commit.c, and builtin/show-branch.c. Benchmarks show 3-6% speedups on traversal-heavy operations like merge-base and rev-list, with no regressions in other scenarios.

Key technical details:
- Adds a `get_pending` flag to track deferred removals
- Replaces `prio_queue_replace()` with automatic fusion in `prio_queue_put()`
- Introduces `prio_queue_size()` to handle the logical vs physical count distinction
- Maintains all existing behavior while optimizing the common get+put pattern

The patch is part of an ongoing effort to optimize Git's commit traversal performance, building on previous work like kk/commit-reach-optim. It can land independently of related queue optimizations like cascade sift-down.
