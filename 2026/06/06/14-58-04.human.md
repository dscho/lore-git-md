Performance optimization for Git's priority queue implementation. This patch generalizes a "lazy queue" optimization pattern that was previously implemented independently in three different parts of the codebase (describe.c, commit.c, and show-branch.c) into the core prio-queue data structure itself.

The key insight is that in Git's traversal patterns, a `prio_queue_get()` is almost always immediately followed by a `prio_queue_put()` of the commit's parents. By deferring the actual removal in `prio_queue_get()` until the next operation, these can be fused into a single replace operation when the next call is indeed a put. This avoids a full remove-rebalance-insert cycle.

The patch shows modest but consistent speedups (3-6%) in traversal-heavy operations like `merge-base --all` and `rev-list --count`. More importantly, it simplifies the calling code by removing three separate implementations of this optimization pattern.

The changes are well-contained to the prio-queue implementation and its callers, with no regressions shown in the benchmark suite. The patch appears carefully implemented with clear commit messages and test updates. It's a nice example of identifying and generalizing a common optimization pattern.
