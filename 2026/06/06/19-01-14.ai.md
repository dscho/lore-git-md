This is a performance optimization patch series for Git's priority queue implementation, building on previous discussion about optimizing the common get+put pattern in commit traversals. The series consists of two patches:

1. The main optimization that folds the lazy_queue pattern (deferring removals to allow subsequent puts to become simple replaces) into the core prio_queue implementation. This eliminates duplicate implementations in several callers while maintaining 3-6% speedups on traversal-heavy operations.

2. A hardening change that renames the internal `.nr` field to `.nr_internal` to prevent unsafe direct access from outside the prio_queue implementation, addressing correctness concerns raised by Junio C Hamano in the v1 discussion.

The changes since v1 show thorough responsiveness to review feedback - all identified direct field accesses have been converted to use the new `prio_queue_size()` helper or other appropriate methods, and a new `prio_queue_for_each()` macro was added for callers needing element iteration. The patch now properly handles edge cases across multiple subsystems including fetch-pack, path-walking, and commit-reach calculations.

This represents a significant improvement over v1 in terms of correctness hardening while maintaining the performance benefits. The optimization is particularly impactful for operations like merge-base calculations and revision walking that heavily use priority queues. The changes are well-scoped and the commit messages clearly explain both the optimization and the safety improvements.
