Kristofer Karlsson has completed a performance optimization series for Git's priority queue implementation, generalizing the lazy_queue pattern from builtin/describe.c into core functionality. The two-part series introduces deferred removal tracking and operation fusion to optimize commit traversals, maintaining measured speedups of 1.7-2.7% for operations like merge-base and revision walking.

The first patch focused on interface hardening, implementing requested naming changes (.nr to .nr_) and adding helper functions to enforce proper access patterns across 13 files. The second patch, now finalized, completes the optimization by fusing get+put operations in commit traversals to avoid full remove-rebalance-insert cycles. This eliminates three independent implementations of the same optimization in favor of handling it automatically in the priority queue itself.

Junio C Hamano provided key feedback that shaped the final implementation, particularly around naming conventions and empty-queue safety checks. René Scharfe validated the performance improvements through benchmarking. With all substantive feedback addressed across four iterations and no outstanding questions, the series is now ready for merging. The changes affect multiple subsystems but maintain clean encapsulation, demonstrating measurable performance gains while improving code maintainability.
