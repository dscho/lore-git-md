This is a follow-up in the ongoing worktree diff performance thread, focusing on index refresh behavior and timestamp precision.

D. Ben Knoble digs deeper into why `git diff` remains slow despite internal index refreshes. He traces the issue to a conditional guard (`1 < !!diff_auto_refresh_index`) in `builtin/diff.c` that appears to prevent the refreshed index from being written back. This guard originates from a 2007 commit and may have been effectively dead code since then, though Knoble expresses skepticism about that possibility. He confirms that `git status` behaves differently, explicitly calling `repo_updated_index_if_able` after refreshing.

The email also reports that enabling `USE_NSEC` (nanosecond timestamp precision) at build time resolves the performance issue, but Knoble now views this as a band-aid rather than a proper fix. He plans to send a patch adding `USE_NSEC` support to the Meson build system, though the thread's earlier discussion suggests runtime configuration might be a better long-term solution. The core technical question remains: why doesn't `git diff`'s internal refresh persist, and is the conditional guard truly non-functional?
