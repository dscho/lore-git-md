This is the third patch in Tian Yuchen’s six-part performance series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. The patch implements the core optimization: terminating the merge-base walk early when one side’s queue is exhausted, provided the walk has entered the finite-generation region (where commit-graph generation numbers guarantee topological ordering).

The change is minimal—just 13 lines added to `commit-reach.c`—but the rationale is carefully explained. The early-exit condition checks that (1) the walk is in the finite-generation region (`generation < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`), (2) no merge-base candidates are still pending (`queue.pending_merge_bases == 0`), and (3) either the PARENT1-only or PARENT2-only queue is empty (`queue.p1_count == 0 || queue.p2_count == 0`). This ensures correctness: without both paint sides present, no new merge-base can form, and the topological ordering guarantees no future commits will revive the exhausted side.

The commit message highlights the performance impact: 100–1000x speedups for merge-base queries in large repositories (e.g., PR branches against a massive mainline history). The patch builds on the refactoring in patches 1–2, which introduced the per-side counters (`p1_count`, `p2_count`) and decoupled the ahead/behind logic from the queue abstraction. Kristofer Karlsson (the patch author) credits Derrick Stolee and Elijah Newren for feedback on the halt condition, suggesting the optimization has already undergone scrutiny.

This looks like a well-motivated, targeted fix for a known bottleneck. The finite-generation guard is conservative but necessary for correctness, and the benchmarks cited in the series cover both large and small repositories. The patch itself is clean and focused, with no obvious red flags. If the earlier refactoring patches hold up under review, this one should too.
