This thread is about a six-patch series from Tian Yuchen that optimizes Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function—a key part of merge-base calculations—to terminate early when one side of a history is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided repositories (like those with import grafts or shallow histories). The goal is to fix a known performance bottleneck where asymmetric histories force full traversal of one side even after the other is done.

The series is at v1, with the core optimization (patch 3) and its test/documentation coverage (patches 4–6) largely uncontested. However, the preparatory refactoring (patches 1–2) has sparked a structural dispute. Derrick Stolee argues that patch 1—refactoring `ahead_behind()`—degrades code clarity without clear benefit and proposes an alternative: introduce the new `paint_queue` struct *first* (in patch 2) while keeping `nonstale_queue` for `ahead_behind()`, then delete `nonstale_queue` later. This would avoid manually reimplementing `nonstale_queue`’s logic in `ahead_behind()`. Stolee’s latest review of patch 3 adds two technical suggestions: move the early-exit check into the dequeueing method (`paint_queue_get()`) for better code locality, and include performance benchmarks to show that the refactoring (patch 2) introduces no overhead while the optimization (patch 3) delivers the promised speedup. The author hasn’t yet responded to either the structural objection or the new feedback.

Key participants include Tian (the author), Stolee (who endorses the optimization’s goal but critiques its implementation), Kristofer Karlsson (co-author of the core patch), and Elijah Newren (who contributed test cases). Open questions remain about edge cases (e.g., criss-cross merges, grafts) and whether the finite-generation guard is overly conservative. The series touches `commit-reach.c`, adds new tests, and introduces no on-disk format changes. Benchmarks show a 500x+ speedup on large one-sided histories with no regressions elsewhere.
