This thread is about a performance optimization series for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which speeds up merge-base queries in asymmetric histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories) by **100-1000x**. The series, now at **v7**, is authored by Tian Yuchen and co-developed with Kristofer Karlsson, with key contributions from Elijah Newren. It is **technically complete, fully reviewed, and queued in Junio’s tree**, awaiting additional reviewers to move forward.

The core change is an early-termination optimization in `paint_down_to_common()` that stops traversal when one side of the history is exhausted, eliminating redundant work in one-sided queries. The series also removes a long-standing commit-date ordering fallback (a workaround for v1 commit-graphs) and replaces it with a **topological ceiling** (`GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX`), improving both performance and correctness. The fallback’s removal reduces step counts from **167k to 5.7k** in v1 graphs and **3.9k** in v2 graphs for the Linux kernel repo. The series includes extensive test coverage for edge cases (clock-skew, redundant merge bases, mixed finite/INFINITY generation numbers) and adds Trace2 instrumentation to measure optimization impact.

All patches are approved, with no open technical concerns. Junio has integrated the series into his tree and explicitly called it **"an important optimization"**, but review bandwidth remains the bottleneck. Elijah Newren’s feedback on documentation wording (e.g., "reliably-ordered region" instead of "finite-generation region") and a minor inconsistency in the `GENERATION_NUMBER_V1_MAX` handling are non-blocking. The series is ready for integration once additional reviewers weigh in.
