This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which speeds up merge-base calculations in repositories with asymmetric histories—such as those with import grafts or shallow histories. The series, now in its second version (v2), is authored by Tian Yuchen and has resolved all major design objections. The core change allows the merge-base walk to terminate early when one side of the history is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories.

The optimization is fully designed and endorsed by Derrick Stolee, a key reviewer, with only one minor question remaining: whether to retain an older fast-exit optimization for single-merge-base queries. The series includes new tests for edge cases, a documentation update, and performance benchmarks using `hyperfine` to measure end-to-end timing of `git merge-base` between builds. Stolee and Kristofer Karlsson (a co-contributor) have agreed on using `hyperfine` as the baseline tool for validation, with trace2 region markers as a fallback if needed.

The patches are now in their final form, with the early-exit logic gated by a finite-generation check to ensure correctness. The only unresolved discussion is whether to keep the older fast-exit optimization, which may be removed in the final version. The series is otherwise ready for merging.
