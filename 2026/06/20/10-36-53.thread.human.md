This thread is about a performance optimization and cleanup series for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases. The series, now at version 4, is authored by Tian Yuchen and addresses a critical regression introduced in v3 while delivering dramatic speedups (100-1000x) for asymmetric merge-base queries, such as those involving import grafts or shallow histories.

The optimization terminates early when one side of the commit queue is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories. Version 4 fixes the regression by restoring a guard condition in the generation-monotonicity check, renames counters for clarity, consolidates termination conditions, adds comprehensive documentation, expands test coverage with new edge cases, and removes dead code. All patches are now fully reviewed and uncontroversial, with key contributors—including Derrick Stolee, Kristofer Karlsson, and René Scharfe—endorsing the changes. The series is ready to merge, with benchmarks showing significant performance improvements and the test suite robustly verifying correctness across all commit-graph modes.

The thread also touches on minor follow-up work, such as retroactive trace2 instrumentation and step-count checks in side-exhaustion tests, but these are optional and not blocking. The new technical document, `paint-down-to-common.adoc`, provides a detailed explanation of the algorithm, use cases, and termination conditions, ensuring the optimization is well-documented for future maintainers.
