This thread is about a performance optimization and cleanup series for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases. The series, now at version 7, is ready to merge and has been queued in Junio’s tree, but it’s awaiting additional review to move forward.

The core goal is to optimize merge-base queries for one-sided histories—common in repositories with import grafts or shallow histories—by terminating early when one side of the history is exhausted. This yields dramatic speedups (100-1000x) in asymmetric queries, such as those involving a long linear history and a short branch. The series also removes a long-standing commit-date ordering fallback, which was originally added to avoid performance regressions in v1 commit-graphs but introduced correctness issues. The new approach replaces this fallback with a topological ceiling for v1 graphs, ensuring the queue remains generation-ordered while preserving the early-exit optimization’s correctness.

Key contributors include Tian Yuchen (the original author) and Kristofer Karlsson, who co-authored all nine patches in this version. The series has been thoroughly reviewed by Derrick Stolee and SZEDER Gábor, with Elijah Newren providing substantive feedback on the technical documentation. Junio has confirmed the procedural base resolution and integrated the series into his tree, but additional reviewers are needed to advance it further. The series is technically complete, with all open questions resolved, and the only remaining bottleneck is review bandwidth.
