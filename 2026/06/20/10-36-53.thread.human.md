This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which speeds up merge-base calculations in repositories with asymmetric histories (like those with import grafts or shallow clones). The v3 series, posted by Tian Yuchen, introduced four new patches and three follow-ups to consolidate, harden, and document the optimization—but one of those follow-ups (patch 7/8) introduced a critical regression by widening a BUG assertion, breaking correctness when `min_generation` isn’t set. Junio C Hamano ejected the series from his `seen` branch after it failed test 12 in `t6600-test-reach.sh`.

The regression is now confirmed by both the author and Kristofer Karlsson, who co-authored parts of the series. They acknowledge the test suite caught the bug, though it was missed locally before submission. The fix for v4 is straightforward: either revert the problematic assertion change or unify the halt conditions while preserving correctness. The rest of the series—including new edge-case tests, trace2 instrumentation, and a technical deep-dive document—remains stable and uncontroversial. Derrick Stolee and others have praised the non-broken patches, particularly the step-counting instrumentation and test infrastructure improvements.

Key participants include Tian (the author), Kristofer (who helped refine the optimization and authored the documentation), Derrick Stolee (who reviewed thoroughly and confirmed the regression), and Junio (who ejected the series and reported the test failure). The series is well-tested, with deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes and benchmarks showing 100-1000x speedups in asymmetric cases. Once the regression is fixed, the remaining patches should merge smoothly.
