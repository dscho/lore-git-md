Here’s the human-mode summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread is about optimizing Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases but can waste time traversing large one-sided histories (e.g., in repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). Tian Yuchen’s v2 patch series proposes an early-exit optimization to stop the walk once one side’s commit queue is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary work. The series has converged on a design after feedback from Derrick Stolee and Kristofer Karlsson, with all major objections resolved.

The core change moves the termination check into `paint_queue_get()`, where it now combines side-exhaustion (`!p1_count || !p2_count`) and all-zero checks under a shared guard (`!pending_merge_bases`). To avoid edge cases, the side-exhaustion check only activates in the finite-generation region (`last_gen < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`). The series also adds documentation, tests for edge cases (e.g., criss-cross merges, grafts), and performance instrumentation using `hyperfine` for timing and `trace2_data_intmax()` for commit-count validation. The only lingering question is whether to retain an older fast-exit optimization for single-merge-base queries, though its value is now diminished.

Stolee and Karlsson have both endorsed the revised design, and Elijah Newren contributed test cases. The series is nearly ready for merging, pending minor implementation tweaks. If you’re reviewing, focus on the termination logic in `paint_queue_get()` and whether the finite-generation guard is sufficiently conservative.
