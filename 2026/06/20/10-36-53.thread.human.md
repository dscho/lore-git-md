This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases between commits. The series, now at version 4, aims to speed up queries involving one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories) by terminating early when one side of the traversal exhausts its commit queue. The optimization delivers dramatic speedups—100-1000x in some cases—but introduced a critical regression in v3 that was caught by the test suite and fixed in v4.

The key participants are Tian Yuchen (the author), Kristofer Karlsson (who co-authored the regression fix and test improvements), and reviewers like Derrick Stolee and René Scharfe. The series is now stable, with all feedback incorporated and no open technical concerns. The v4 updates include a new technical document explaining the algorithm, renamed counters for clarity, consolidated termination conditions, and expanded test coverage for edge cases like mixed finite/INFINITY generation numbers and redundant merge-base candidates.

The third patch in this series (the one just summarized) adds regression tests to ensure the side-exhaustion optimization works correctly in edge cases, such as when one merge-base candidate is an ancestor of another. These tests are deterministic, self-contained, and verify all commit-graph modes. The series is well-tested, instrumented with trace2 step-counting for performance verification, and ready to merge. The only remaining loose ends are minor follow-ups, like improving trace2 diagnostics, but these are optional and don’t block progress.
