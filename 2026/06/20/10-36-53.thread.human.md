This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which speeds up merge-base calculations in repositories with asymmetric histories (e.g., import grafts or shallow histories). The v3 series, authored by Tian Yuchen with contributions from Kristofer Karlsson and Elijah Newren, introduces the core optimization, test coverage, instrumentation, and documentation—but also includes a critical regression in patch 7/8 that broke correctness when `min_generation` is unset. The regression was caught by the test suite and confirmed by Junio C Hamano, Derrick Stolee, and Kristofer Karlsson, leading Junio to eject the series from his `seen` branch. A fix is planned for v4.

The series is otherwise stable and well-tested, with patches 1–6/8 and 8/8 ready to merge. Key improvements include deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes, trace2 instrumentation to measure performance, and a new technical document (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`) explaining the algorithm’s mechanics. René Scharfe’s recent review of patch 5/8 raised minor stylistic points about type safety (`size_t` vs. `int`) and naming consistency (`p1_count` vs. `parent1_count`), but these are non-blocking and do not affect functionality. The regression in patch 7/8 remains the sole blocker, with the author and Kristofer planning to either revert the problematic change or unify halt conditions while preserving correctness. The test suite’s robustness in catching the regression has been validated, reinforcing confidence in the series’ edge-case coverage.
