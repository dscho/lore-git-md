This thread is about a performance optimization to Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases between commits. The series, now at version 5, is fully reviewed and ready to merge. Authored by Tian Yuchen and co-developed with Kristofer Karlsson, it eliminates unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories), delivering **100-1000x speedups** in asymmetric queries—such as reducing a 2.6-million-commit monorepo query from 3.67 seconds to 5 milliseconds.

The final iteration (v5) is a polished, regression-free update that incorporates all prior feedback. Key improvements include the removal of the obsolete commit-date fallback (now handled by the side-exhaustion optimization), trace2 diagnostics for step-count instrumentation, new test coverage for clock-skew topologies and edge cases, and a dedicated technical document (`Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc`) explaining the algorithm. Mechanical fixes—like squashing patches to resolve `-Wunused-function` warnings—were applied without altering behavior, and benchmarks confirm no performance regressions.
