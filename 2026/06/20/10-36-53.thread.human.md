This thread covers the final wrap-up of a now-merged performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which dramatically speeds up merge-base calculations in one-sided histories. The series, originally authored by Tian Yuchen and reviewed by Derrick Stolee, Kristofer Karlsson, and Elijah Newren, landed all seven patches after addressing design feedback and test coverage concerns.

The optimization itself—terminating early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue—is complete and fully integrated, delivering 100-1000x speedups in asymmetric histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts). Benchmarks using `hyperfine` and `trace2_data_intmax()` confirm the gains, though some step-count data for specific cases was initially omitted and will be provided shortly. The core technical dispute from the first version (whether to decouple `nonstale_queue` from `ahead_behind()`) was resolved by preserving the existing structure, and the termination condition was finalized with a generation-number guard to ensure correctness.

Post-merge discussion has shifted to cleanup and test infrastructure. Stolee suggested minor stylistic improvements, like a doc-comment for `paint_queue_get()` and integrating `min_generation` into the `paint_state` struct, which Kristofer Karlsson plans to address in follow-up patches. A more substantial proposal—to extend `test_all_modes` to automatically capture and assert trace2 step-count data—gained traction as a way to make performance regressions self-verifying, though implementation details are still being explored. The only lingering open item is whether to retroactively add step-count checks to earlier test patches for cleaner history, but this is low-priority and unlikely to block further progress. The thread reflects a collaborative effort to polish what is already a significant performance win for Git.
