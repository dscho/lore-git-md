This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s merge-base calculation, specifically the `paint_down_to_common()` function in the commit-reach subsystem. The series, now at its sixth iteration (v6), is fully reviewed and ready to merge. The originating author, Tian Yuchen, collaborated closely with Kristofer Karlsson, who co-signed and contributed most of the patches, including the new technical documentation and test infrastructure.

The core goal is to eliminate unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories—common in repositories with import grafts or shallow histories—by terminating early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue. This yields dramatic speedups (100-1000x) for asymmetric queries. The final patch in the series, just posted, removes the obsolete commit-date ordering fallback introduced in 2018, completing the optimization. This change is safe because earlier patches introduced side-exhaustion logic that prevents deep traversals regardless of queue ordering, and benchmarks confirm the performance gains without regressions.

The series has been thoroughly vetted, with approvals from Derrick Stolee and SZEDER Gábor, and Junio C Hamano has confirmed the rebase on `kk/commit-reach-find-all-fix` (now in `next`) resolves the last procedural blocker. The patches include comprehensive test coverage for edge cases (clock-skew topologies, self-references, duplicate inputs), deterministic step-count assertions via Trace2 instrumentation, and a new technical document explaining the algorithm. Two minor design points—the test diagnostic helper and the fallback removal—remain open for feedback but are non-blocking. The series is now ready for merging.
