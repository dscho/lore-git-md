This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases between commit histories. The series, now at v2, aims to terminate early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories—a known bottleneck in repositories with import grafts or shallow histories.

The core design is settled, with all substantive objections resolved. The optimization targets the commit-reach subsystem, specifically `commit-reach.c`, and introduces no on-disk format changes. Key participants include Tian Yuchen (the author), Derrick Stolee (who endorsed the core logic and provided critical feedback on termination conditions and state encapsulation), and Kristofer Karlsson (who co-authored the optimization and tests). Elijah Newren also contributed test cases to validate edge cases.

The final implementation detail—how to encapsulate state like `last_gen` in the `paint_state` struct (renamed from `paint_queue` in v2)—has now been resolved. Stolee and Karlsson agreed to store `last_gen` directly in the struct, making `paint_queue_get()` self-contained and simplifying the loop’s termination logic. The halt condition, `(!p1_count || !p2_count) && !pending_merge_bases`, is gated by `last_gen < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY` to ensure correctness. The older fast-exit optimization for single-merge-base queries, controlled by `mb_flags`, is slated for removal in v2, as its value is diminished by the new side-exhaustion logic.

Performance validation uses **hyperfine** to benchmark `git merge-base` end-to-end timing between builds, with **trace2_data_intmax()** exposing the "commits walked" metric for testable validation. The series is now technically complete, with no open questions or loose ends. Expect the final v2 to land shortly with these refinements.
