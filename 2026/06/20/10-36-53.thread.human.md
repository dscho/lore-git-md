This thread is about a six-patch series from Tian Yuchen that optimizes Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function—a key part of merge-base calculations—to terminate early when one side of a history is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). The series targets a known performance bottleneck where asymmetric histories force full traversal of one side even after the other is exhausted.

The optimization is now complete at v1, with all patches submitted. The core change (patch 3) introduces an early-exit condition when one side’s queue is exhausted, provided the walk has entered the finite-generation region, delivering 100–1000x speedups for large asymmetric histories. Earlier patches refactor the code to support this change (e.g., replacing the `nonstale_queue` abstraction with a `paint_queue` struct and per-side counters), while later patches add extensive test coverage for edge cases (e.g., criss-cross merges, grafts, shallow histories) and regression scenarios. The final patch, just arrived, adds technical documentation explaining the algorithm, its use cases, and the side-exhaustion termination condition.

Key contributors include Tian Yuchen (series author), Kristofer Karlsson (author of the core optimization patch), Elijah Newren (contributed test cases from parallel work), and Derrick Stolee (provided feedback on the halt condition). The series is now ready for review, with open questions centering on the correctness of the halt condition in edge cases and whether the refactoring preserves the original behavior. No regressions have been reported, and the optimization is internal to Git’s commit-reach subsystem with no on-disk format changes.
