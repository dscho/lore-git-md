This thread is about a now-merged performance optimization to Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which speeds up merge-base calculations in asymmetric histories by 100-1000x. The series, authored by Tian Yuchen, is complete and all patches (v2 1-7/7) have been merged with full consensus on correctness and design.

The optimization itself—terminating early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue—is uncontroversial and endorsed by Derrick Stolee, Kristofer Karlsson, and Elijah Newren. The structural dispute from v1 (whether to decouple `nonstale_queue` from `ahead_behind()`) was resolved by preserving the existing structure, and the termination condition was finalized as `(!state->p1_count || !state->p2_count) && !state->pending_merge_bases`, gated by a generation-number check for correctness. Performance instrumentation, including trace2 step counters and hyperfine benchmarks, is fully integrated and validates the speedups.

Post-merge discussion has focused on minor improvements and open questions. Stolee raised a neutral but conspicuous question about why the benchmark table omitted step-count data for a specific 2.6M-commit case, despite the wall-clock improvement being substantial. He also provided low-weight stylistic feedback on test readability (e.g., blank lines, test ordering) and proposed a medium-weight enhancement to make the tests self-verifying for performance by capturing trace2 step counts. Additionally, he suggested two forward-looking code improvements: adding a doc-comment to `paint_queue_get()` to clarify the termination condition, and integrating `min_generation` into the `paint_state` struct for better maintainability. None of these affect the merged optimization’s correctness, but they represent opportunities for future cleanup and test infrastructure work.

The latest email from Stolee is a surface-level review of the final patch, praising its inline documentation and calling it a “nice climax” to the series. His feedback is purely stylistic—suggesting a cleaner diff presentation and noting a negligible efficiency tradeoff—and reiterates his earlier proposal to extend the test infrastructure. No substantive questions remain, and the thread is effectively closed.
