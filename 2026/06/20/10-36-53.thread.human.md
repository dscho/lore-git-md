This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases. The goal is to speed up queries where one side of the history is much larger than the other—common in repositories with import grafts or shallow histories. The optimization can yield **100-1000x speedups** in asymmetric cases, but the latest version (v3) introduced a regression in one patch (7/8) that breaks correctness when `min_generation` isn’t set.

The series is a **post-merge follow-up** to v2, which is already fully merged. The new patches add edge-case tests, trace2 instrumentation, state refactoring, dead-code removal, and documentation. Most of these are stable and uncontroversial, but the regression in patch 7/8 is blocking progress. The author (Tian Yuchen) and co-contributor (Kristofer Karlsson) acknowledge the issue and plan to fix it. Derrick Stolee, a key reviewer, confirmed the test suite catches the regression, which is a good sign for coverage, and endorsed the rest of the series as "in good shape."

The regression aside, the series is well-tested and instrumented. It includes deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes (none, full, half, no-gdat) and self-verifying performance benchmarks. The new `paint-down-to-common.adoc` document fills a long-standing gap in Git’s technical documentation, explaining the walk mechanics, termination conditions, and safety guarantees. Stolee praised the trace2 instrumentation (patch 4/8) as "clean" and "over-achieving," and Junio C Hamano called the documentation "great" and "very clear."

What’s still open:
- The regression in patch 7/8 needs a fix to restore the `min_generation` guard while keeping the side-exhaustion optimization.
- Minor cleanup items (doc-comments, retroactive trace2 instrumentation) are deferred but not blockers.
- A philosophical debate between Stolee and Kristofer about whether to add trace2 step-count checks to *all* relevant tests remains unresolved, but it doesn’t affect this series.

The next step is for the authors to post a fix for the regression. Once that’s done, the series should be ready to merge.
