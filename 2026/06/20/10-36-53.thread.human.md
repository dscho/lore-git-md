This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases. The series, now at version 3, is follow-up work after the core patches from v2 were merged. The goal is to speed up merge-base queries in repositories with asymmetric histories (e.g., those with import grafts or shallow histories) by terminating early when one side of the query exhausts its commit queue. The optimization delivers dramatic speedups—100-1000x in some cases—but introduced a regression in patch 7/8 that breaks correctness when `min_generation` is not set.

The v3 series adds four new patches (2-5/8) and three follow-ups (6-8/8) that consolidate, harden, document, and fix the core optimization. Most of the series is stable and ready to merge: patches 2-6/8 add edge-case tests, trace2 instrumentation, and mechanical refactoring, while patch 8/8 consolidates termination conditions. Derrick Stolee and Kristofer Karlsson have reviewed the changes thoroughly, with Stolee praising the instrumentation in patch 4/8 as "clean" and "over-achieving." The only blocker is the regression in patch 7/8, where an unconditional generation-monotonicity check breaks correctness in some commit-graph modes. The author and Karlsson acknowledge the issue and plan to fix it in v4, either by reverting the problematic change or fully unifying halt conditions.

The series is well-tested, with deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes and benchmarks showing significant improvements (e.g., a 2.6M-commit monorepo query dropping from 3.67s to 5ms). The new technical documentation (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`) fills a long-standing gap, explaining the paint-walk mechanics, generation-number regions, and termination conditions. Junio C Hamano endorsed the documentation as "great" and "very clear." Once the regression is fixed, the series should be ready for merging.
