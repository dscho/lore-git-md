This thread is about optimizing Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function to terminate early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, delivering dramatic speedups for asymmetric histories. The series, now at v3, is post-merge follow-up work with a critical regression in patch 7/8 that Junio C Hamano ejected from his `seen` branch after it triggered a BUG assertion in `t6600-test-reach.sh`. The regression—an unconditional generation-monotonicity check that breaks correctness when `min_generation` is unset—has been confirmed by Derrick Stolee and Kristofer Karlsson, who plan to fix it in v4.

The rest of the series is stable and well-reviewed. Patches 2–6 and 8 introduce edge-case tests, trace2 instrumentation, a new `paint_state` struct, and documentation, all of which have received positive feedback. Derrick Stolee praised patch 4/8’s trace2 step-counting as “clean” and “over-achieving,” while Junio called the new technical documentation “great” and “very clear.” Kristofer and Tian Yuchen have incorporated all substantive feedback, including René Scharfe’s recent suggestions to use `size_t` for counters and adopt more explicit naming (`parent1_count`, `mb_candidate_count`). The test suite’s robustness is now doubly validated: it caught the regression in patch 7/8, and the new edge-case tests (including Elijah Newren’s contributions) cover self-merges, duplicate commits, and mixed finite/INFINITY generation numbers.

The open work is narrowly scoped: the regression fix in patch 7/8 and minor stylistic tweaks. Once those are addressed, the series should be ready to re-enter Junio’s integration branches. The instrumentation and documentation added in v3 will make future regressions easier to diagnose and prevent.
