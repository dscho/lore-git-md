This thread is about a performance optimization series for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases. The v7 iteration, authored by Tian Yuchen and co-developed with Kristofer Karlsson, is now ready to merge and has been queued in Junio’s tree. The key improvement is early termination when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories—such as those with import grafts or shallow histories. Benchmarks show **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric queries, with no regressions on smaller repositories.

The series is technically complete and procedurally unblocked, incorporating all prior feedback. This includes a topological ceiling for v1 commit-graph support (where generation numbers saturate), René Scharfe’s cosmetic test refinement, and clarified documentation for `min_generation` to address forward-compatibility concerns. The final patch removes the commit-date ordering fallback entirely, ensuring the queue remains generation-ordered regardless of graph version. The work touches `commit-reach.c`, adds a technical document (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`), and expands test coverage for edge cases like clock-skew topologies and mixed finite/INFINITY generation numbers.

Review bandwidth remains the primary bottleneck. Derrick Stolee and SZEDER Gábor have approved the series, and Elijah Newren’s resumed review may provide the final substantive feedback needed for advancement. Junio has explicitly requested additional reviewers to move the series forward. Kristofer Karlsson has proposed non-blocking simplifications to reduce series complexity, but the current version is already well-motivated, thoroughly tested, and addresses a long-standing performance issue. It’s a clear win for repositories with asymmetric histories.
