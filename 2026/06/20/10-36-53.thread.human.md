This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases. The series, now in its fourth iteration (v4), is ready to merge after addressing a critical regression and incorporating all feedback.

Tian Yuchen proposed the optimization to terminate early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, yielding 100-1000x speedups for asymmetric histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). The v4 series fixes the regression introduced in v3, renames counters for clarity, moves termination conditions into `paint_queue_get()`, improves documentation, adds a new technical document (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`), expands test coverage, and removes dead code. A key regression—affecting the `!FIND_ALL` early-exit optimization in repositories with v1 commit-graph data—was resolved by removing the fallback to commit-date ordering entirely, as benchmarks confirmed the side-exhaustion optimization makes it obsolete.

The series is fully reviewed and approved, with all patches stable and uncontroversial. Derrick Stolee’s final approval signals readiness to merge, pending two mechanical adjustments in a v5 reroll: squashing patches 5/8 and 6/8 to avoid `-Wunused-function` warnings, and incorporating a follow-up patch to remove the commit-date fallback. Kristofer Karlsson and René Scharfe contributed fixes and feedback, while Elijah Newren provided edge-case test coverage. Junio C Hamano ejected v3 from the `seen` branch due to the regression, but v4 is now regression-free and robustly tested across all commit-graph modes. The remaining work is procedural, with no open technical questions.
