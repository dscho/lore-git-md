This thread is about a now-merged performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which speeds up merge-base calculations in repositories with asymmetric histories (like those with import grafts or shallow histories). The series, authored by Tian Yuchen, introduced early termination when one side of the history is exhausted, yielding dramatic speedups—up to 1000x in some cases—while maintaining correctness through careful gating with generation numbers.

The optimization is fully integrated, with all patches (1-7/7) merged after addressing feedback from key reviewers like Derrick Stolee, Kristofer Karlsson, and Elijah Newren. The series included new documentation, regression tests for edge cases, trace2 instrumentation to measure "commits walked," and refactoring to encapsulate state cleanly. Benchmarks using `hyperfine` and trace2 logs confirmed the improvements, though one case—`git merge-tree (across import)` in a 2.6M-commit monorepo—showed a wall-clock speedup (8.11s → 100ms) but omitted the step-count data in the cover letter.

In the latest email, Derrick Stolee asks why the step-count data is missing for that specific case, noting the instrumentation is in place and the omission is conspicuous given the substantial wall-clock improvement. While the series is already merged, the question highlights a minor reporting gap rather than a technical issue, and no further action is expected unless others are curious about the exact step-count reduction.
