This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s merge-base calculation, specifically the `paint_down_to_common()` function in the commit-reach subsystem. The series, now at **v2**, is authored by Tian Yuchen and is ready to land with no remaining open questions.

The goal is to eliminate unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories—common in repositories with import grafts or shallow histories—by terminating early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue. This yields **dramatic speedups** (100-1000x in some cases) for asymmetric queries while maintaining correctness. The optimization activates only after entering the finite-generation region, where generation numbers guarantee the final paint state, ensuring no regressions.

The series is well-structured: it starts with technical documentation, adds regression tests (including edge cases from Elijah Newren’s parallel work), and introduces trace2 instrumentation for deterministic validation. The core refactoring decouples the ahead/behind logic from the non-stale queue, and the final patch implements the early-exit logic, gated by the finite-generation guard and pending merge-base check. Reviewers—Derrick Stolee, Kristofer Karlsson, and Elijah Newren—have endorsed the approach, and all feedback from v1 has been addressed, including structural disputes, termination conditions, and state encapsulation.

Key details include the new `paint_state` struct with per-side counters, the gated early-exit condition, and comprehensive test coverage. Performance is validated via trace2 step counts and wall-clock benchmarks, with no on-disk format changes or external dependencies. The series is technically complete and ready for merging.
