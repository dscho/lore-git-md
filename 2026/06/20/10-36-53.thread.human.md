This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases between commits. The series, now in its third iteration (v3), is follow-up work after the core patches from v2 were merged. The goal is to terminate early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, yielding dramatic speedups (100-1000x) for asymmetric histories, such as those with import grafts or shallow clones.

The author, Tian Yuchen, and key reviewer Kristofer Karlsson have collaborated closely to refine the implementation, adding instrumentation, edge-case tests, and documentation. Most of the series is stable and ready to merge, but one regression in patch 7/8—a widened BUG assertion that breaks correctness when `min_generation` is not set—has been confirmed by test failures. The author and Kristofer acknowledge the issue and plan to fix it in v4, with two proposed approaches: reverting the problematic change or fully unifying halt conditions while preserving correctness.

Derrick Stolee, another reviewer, has praised the non-broken parts of the series, particularly patch 4/8’s trace2 instrumentation, calling it "clean" and "over-achieving." The latest exchange in this thread focuses on patch 8/8, which consolidates termination conditions by moving the `min_generation` check into `paint_queue_get()`. Stolee raised minor concerns about abstraction and documentation, but these are low-weight and don’t block merging. Kristofer agreed to clarify the generation cutoff’s role in the first patch of v4 and proposed optional follow-up work to refine the logic further. The series is well-tested, with deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes and self-verifying performance benchmarks. Once the regression in patch 7/8 is resolved, the rest of the series is poised for merging.
