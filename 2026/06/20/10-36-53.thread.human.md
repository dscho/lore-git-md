This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function, which calculates merge bases between commits. The goal is to terminate early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories—a known bottleneck in repositories with import grafts or shallow histories.

The series, now at v2, is technically complete. All design and technical feedback has been resolved, including a structural dispute over patch 1 (now eliminated) and the finalization of the termination condition: `(!queue.p1_count || !queue.p2_count) && !queue.pending_merge_bases`, gated by a finite-generation check to ensure correctness. The optimization is endorsed by Derrick Stolee, a key reviewer, and includes new tests and documentation. The last implementation detail—encapsulating state in the `paint_state` struct—has been settled, making the code self-contained and simplifying the loop logic.

Key participants include Tian Yuchen (the author), Stolee (who provided high-weight feedback on correctness and performance instrumentation), and Kristofer Karlsson (who co-authored the core optimization and resolved structural objections). Elijah Newren contributed test cases, signaling confidence in the approach. The series is ready to land, with no open questions remaining. Performance is validated using `hyperfine` benchmarks and `trace2` metrics, ensuring the optimization delivers measurable improvements.
