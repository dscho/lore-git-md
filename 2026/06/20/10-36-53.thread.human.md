This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s merge-base calculation, specifically the `paint_down_to_common()` function in the commit-reach subsystem. The series, now in its second version (v2), is authored by Tian Yuchen and targets a long-standing bottleneck where asymmetric commit histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories) force unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories, even after one side of the query is exhausted. The optimization achieves **100-1000x speedups** in such cases and is ready to land.

The v2 series resolves all feedback from v1, including a structural dispute over decoupling `nonstale_queue` (now preserved for `ahead_behind()`) and finalizing the termination condition as `(!state->p1_count || !state->p2_count) && !state->pending_merge_bases`, gated by a finite-generation check to ensure correctness. The series is meticulously structured: documentation (patch 1/7) introduces a new technical guide explaining the merge-base logic, followed by regression tests (patches 2-3/7) contributed by Elijah Newren and Kristofer Karlsson, trace2 instrumentation (patch 4/7) for testable performance validation, and refactoring (patches 5-6/7) to prepare for the final optimization (patch 7/7). Derrick Stolee, Kristofer Karlsson, and Elijah Newren have all endorsed the approach, and the series is technically complete with no open questions.

The latest email covers patch 5/7, a refactoring that introduces a `paint_state` struct to track per-side commit counters (PARENT1-only, PARENT2-only, and pending merge-base candidates). This patch replaces the old `nonstale_queue` termination logic with a counter-based approach, ensuring the function remains behaviorally identical while enabling the final optimization. The refactoring is clean, well-documented, and validated by trace2 instrumentation showing unchanged step counts.
