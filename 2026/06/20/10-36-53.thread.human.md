This thread is about a six-patch series from Tian Yuchen that optimizes Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function—a key part of merge-base calculation—to terminate early when one side of a history is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories. The series targets a known performance bottleneck in repositories with asymmetric histories, such as those using import grafts or shallow clones.

The first two patches refactor the code to prepare for the optimization: patch 1 decouples `ahead_behind()` from the `nonstale_queue` abstraction, and patch 2 introduces a `paint_queue` struct with per-side counters to track commits by paint state. The third patch, just summarized, implements the core optimization—halting the walk when one side’s queue is exhausted, provided the traversal has entered the finite-generation region (where generation numbers ensure topological ordering). Benchmarks show dramatic speedups (100–1000x) for large repositories with no regressions on smaller ones.

The series is still at v1, with no formal reviews yet, but it incorporates prior feedback from Derrick Stolee and Elijah Newren, who helped refine the halt condition and contributed test cases. Open questions remain about the correctness of the halt condition in edge cases (e.g., criss-cross merges, grafts, or shallow histories) and whether the finite-generation guard is overly conservative. The remaining patches add test coverage and documentation, rounding out the series. If the early-exit logic holds up under review, this could be a significant performance win for a long-standing bottleneck.
