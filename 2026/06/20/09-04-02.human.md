This is the latest exchange in the long-running `git branch --delete-merged` series, now at v16. Harald Nordgren responds to Junio C Hamano’s proposal for handling stacked branches—specifically, the idea of automatically retargeting the upstream of dependent branches (e.g., moving `feature2@{u}` from `feature1` to `origin/master` when `feature1` is deleted).

Harald endorses the retargeting approach, citing his own frustration with GitHub’s past behavior around stacked pull requests. He raises two practical concerns: recursion (e.g., `b1 → b2 → b3`, where both `b2` and `b3` are merged) and self-referential upstreams (e.g., `b3` pointing to itself). His suggestion is to handle these cases pragmatically—warn if a branch ends up without an upstream, but avoid complex multi-pass algorithms.

The discussion is now focused on implementation trade-offs: whether to prioritize simplicity (single-pass retargeting with warnings) or correctness (full recursion handling). This will likely shape the final design in v17, which is otherwise ready for review. The series remains well-motivated and technically mature, with stacked-branch safety as the last unresolved detail.
