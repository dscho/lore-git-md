This thread began as a fix for macOS CI hangs in the `t5551` and `t5559` tests, caused by an Apache `mod_http2` bug that triggered timeouts or protocol stalls during large ref advertisements. The root cause was addressed in Git 2.55 with a two-patch series from Jeff King (Peff): increasing Apache’s `Timeout` directive to 600 seconds and isolating the problematic test case in a bare repository. The series is now merged, and a follow-up patch adding a GitLab CI status badge to the `README` was also accepted. A third patch to align GitLab CI’s test coverage with GitHub’s remains on hold pending discussion about resource allocation.

After the immediate CI issue was resolved, the thread shifted to performance analysis of Git’s reftable backend. Peff initially identified micro-optimizations—redundant `stat()` calls and unbuffered reflog writes—that could shave milliseconds off bulk ref operations. However, a later follow-up uncovered a more significant problem: **quadratic-time behavior during bulk ref deletion and re-creation**. When deleting and re-creating thousands of refs (e.g., 8,000 tags), runtime scales poorly (1.8s → 7.1s for a 2x input size increase), likely due to inefficient iteration over tombstone entries in the reftable backend. This issue, while not yet actionable, is now the most pressing performance concern for large repositories using reftable.

Key participants include Michael Montalbo (initial reporter), Peff (who implemented the CI fix and led the performance analysis), Patrick Steinhardt (who diagnosed the Apache bug and proposed the GitLab CI badge), and Junio C Hamano (who merged the patches and raised strategic CI questions). The thread remains open for future work on reftable optimizations, particularly around tombstone handling, but the immediate CI failures are resolved.
