Michael Montalbo’s patch series tackled persistent CI hangs in Git’s macOS (and now Linux) test suite, specifically in `t5551` and `t5559`, where an `ls-refs` advertisement of 100,000 loose refs triggered either indefinite HTTP/2 stalls or mid-transfer aborts (`curl 18`). The root cause was traced to Apache’s `mod_http2` bug 70131, which hits a 300-second timeout during the ref advertisement. Montalbo’s initial two-part fix—packing the refs and aligning `$JOBS` with the runner’s core count—slashed CI runtime from 360 minutes to 30–44 minutes on macOS but didn’t resolve the underlying timeout issue, merely shifting the failure mode to consistent `curl 18` aborts.

The discussion has since converged on a pragmatic solution: increasing Apache’s `Timeout` directive to reduce the likelihood of hitting the problematic state. Patrick Steinhardt diagnosed the root cause and proposed this approach, while Jeff King (Peff) rejected Montalbo’s earlier client-side mitigation (enabling Git’s `http.lowSpeedLimit` and `http.lowSpeedTime`) as merely "punting" on the problem. Junio C Hamano has now endorsed this consensus, reinforcing the project’s preference for addressing flakes at their source rather than masking them with retries or workarounds. The thread remains open, with no concrete patch yet for the timeout adjustment, but the maintainer’s input suggests this direction is likely to be accepted if implemented cleanly. The next steps will involve testing the timeout increase and evaluating its impact on CI reliability.
