The thread is about fixing persistent CI hangs and failures in Git’s macOS (and now Linux) test jobs, specifically in `t5551` and `t5559`, which test `ls-refs` advertisements with 100,000 loose refs. The root cause is an upstream Apache bug (70131) that triggers either mid-transfer aborts (`curl 18`) or indefinite HTTP/2 stalls when the server’s 300-second timeout is hit. Michael Montalbo initially proposed a two-part fix—packing refs and aligning CI job counts with runner cores—which cut runtime from 6 hours to 30–44 minutes but didn’t resolve the underlying timeout issue. Patrick Steinhardt later diagnosed the Apache timeout as the culprit and proposed increasing the `Timeout` directive in the test environment as a pragmatic fix.

The discussion has now converged on this timeout adjustment as the primary solution, with Jeff King (Peff) endorsing it and suggesting a 600-second value to virtually eliminate failures. Peff also reaffirmed Michael’s ref-packing optimization as a complementary improvement, noting it reduces the risk of timeouts while speeding up tests. The group has ruled out client-side workarounds (like retry logic or `http.lowSpeedLimit` tweaks) as they merely mask flakes rather than address the source. The remaining open question is the exact timeout value, but the approach is widely supported. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have both signaled likely acceptance if the patch is cleanly implemented. The next step is for Michael to submit a final patch adjusting Apache’s `Timeout` in `t/lib-httpd.sh` and integrating the ref-packing logic.
