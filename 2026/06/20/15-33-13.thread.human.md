This thread began with a report of CI hangs in Git’s macOS and Linux test suites (`t5551` and `t5559`) during an `ls-refs` advertisement of 100,000 loose refs. The root cause was traced to Apache’s `mod_http2` bug 70131, which triggered mid-transfer aborts or indefinite stalls when the server-side 300-second timeout was hit. The issue was resolved with a two-patch series from Jeff King, now merged for Git 2.55: one patch increased Apache’s `Timeout` directive to 600 seconds, and another isolated the many-tags test case to reduce runtime. A third patch from Patrick Steinhardt, proposing to enable `GIT_TEST_LONG` for GitLab CI, remains on hold pending a policy decision about CI resource allocation and visibility. Junio C Hamano has suggested GitLab could run a superset of GitHub’s coverage, and Patrick has proposed adding a GitLab CI status badge to the project’s `README.md` as a low-cost way to improve visibility.

The thread has since shifted to a post-mortem discussion of reftable’s performance during bulk ref creation. Jeff King and Patrick Steinhardt analyzed reftable’s repeated `fstat` calls on `tables.list`, which account for 85% of runtime in Peff’s `strace` analysis. While reftable’s current performance (50,000 refs in 150ms) is already ~30% faster than the files backend, Patrick acknowledged the `fstat` overhead as a low-hanging optimization opportunity. The proposed fix—trusting the loaded stack after locking—would require deeper refactoring, and Patrick plans to track it as a future issue rather than address it immediately. This exchange reinforces the consensus that the original CI fixes are sufficient for now, while leaving room for incremental improvements in reftable’s internals. The thread remains open only for the GitLab CI badge proposal, which is awaiting Junio’s response.
