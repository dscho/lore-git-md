The macOS CI hang in `t5551` and `t5559`—caused by Apache’s `mod_http2` bug 70131—has been fully resolved with Jeff King’s two-patch series, now merged for Git 2.55. The fix increased Apache’s `Timeout` directive to 600 seconds and isolated the many-tags test case, eliminating CI hangs and protocol errors. Follow-up work added a GitLab CI status badge to the project’s `README.md` (now accepted) and documented the upstream Apache bug in a public location for future reference.

A secondary discussion about reftable backend performance uncovered a quadratic-time scalability issue during bulk ref deletion and re-creation, where tombstone iteration forced full scans of deleted refs. Kristofer Karlsson’s patch, which exposed tombstones to iterator bounds checks, initially showed dramatic improvements (e.g., 8,000 refs: 14.93s → 0.145s). However, Kristofer now reports difficulty reproducing these gains in realistic scenarios, particularly when compaction is enabled. The patch’s practical value is in question, as its benefits appear limited to edge cases while adding complexity to call sites like `reftable_be_reflog_exists()`. Patrick Steinhardt, who previously encouraged submission, may now reconsider the trade-offs. The optimization effort could stall unless further evidence of real-world impact emerges.

The thread’s open questions remain centered on CI resource allocation (whether GitLab should enable `GIT_TEST_LONG`) and reftable’s low-hanging optimizations, such as redundant `stat()` calls and unbuffered reflog writes. The latter, though unaddressed, are now overshadowed by the uncertainty around Kristofer’s patch. Key participants—Peff, Patrick, and Junio—have aligned on the core fixes, but the reftable performance discussion highlights the tension between theoretical improvements and maintainable code.
