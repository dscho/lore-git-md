This is a performance optimization series targeting the commit-reach subsystem, specifically the `paint_down_to_common()` function used in merge-base calculations. The series aims to terminate the graph walk early when one side of the history is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., in repositories with imported histories or grafts).

The series is split into six patches, with the first two refactoring the code to introduce a `paint_queue` structure and per-side counters, decoupling the ahead/behind logic from the non-stale queue. The third patch implements the core optimization: stopping the walk when one side’s queue is exhausted, as no new merge-base candidates can emerge after that point. Patches 4-5 add test coverage for edge cases and regressions, including contributions from Elijah Newren’s parallel work (PR #2150). The final patch adds technical documentation for `paint_down_to_common()`.

Key technical details:
- The optimization activates only after entering the finite-generation region, where generation numbers guarantee final paint states.
- Benchmarks show dramatic speedups (500x+) for merge-base queries across large one-sided histories (e.g., 2.6M commits), with no regressions on smaller repositories like linux.git.
- Files touched: `commit-reach.c` (core logic), `Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc` (new doc), and test scripts (`t6099-merge-base-side-exhaustion.sh`, `t6600-test-reach.sh`).
- The series builds on prior RFC discussion, incorporating feedback from Derrick Stolee (halt condition refinement) and Elijah Newren (test cases).

The motivation is clear: addressing a known performance bottleneck in repositories with asymmetric histories. The patch structure is logical, with refactoring separated from behavior changes, and the commit messages provide concrete examples (e.g., import grafts) and benchmarks. The inclusion of Elijah Newren’s test cases suggests cross-validation of the approach. Likely points of discussion may include the halt condition’s correctness in edge cases (e.g., criss-cross merges) and whether the finite-generation guard is overly conservative.
