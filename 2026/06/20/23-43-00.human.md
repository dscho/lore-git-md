This is a documentation patch updating `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to formalize how contributors should address design critiques in patch resubmissions. It is the fourth iteration (v4) of a maintainer-driven clarification, now incorporating feedback from earlier rounds to soften the tone while preserving the core requirement.

The patch adds 20 lines to two sections of `SubmittingPatches`. In the "Responding to Reviews" section, it instructs authors to pay special attention to high-level design or viability critiques (e.g., whether a feature is worth implementing), defend their choices on the mailing list before revising code, and ensure new versions include clearer explanations in cover letters and commit messages. It explicitly states that topics with unresolved fundamental design critiques will not be merged. In the "Commit Message Guidelines" section, it adds a requirement to document how design concerns raised during review were resolved, ensuring the historical record is complete.

This version refines the language from v3 to be more collaborative ("work with reviewers and other members to improve the design") rather than prescriptive, while keeping the substance intact. The interdiff shows a small but meaningful change: splitting a long sentence into two for clarity and adding "This will" to make the rationale more explicit. The patch remains narrowly focused on contributor workflow documentation, with no technical or code changes.

The unresolved tension from earlier discussions—whether this requirement conflicts with Git's "show me the code" culture—persists, but the softer phrasing may reduce concerns about discouraging early feedback.
