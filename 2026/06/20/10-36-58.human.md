This is the fifth patch in a six-patch series optimizing Git’s merge-base calculation by terminating early when one side of the history is exhausted. The series targets the `paint_down_to_common()` function in the commit-reach subsystem, aiming to eliminate unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories).

This patch adds regression tests for the side-exhaustion optimization introduced earlier in the series. It creates a new test script, `t6099-merge-base-side-exhaustion.sh`, to verify that `git merge-base --all` correctly handles cases where multiple merge-base candidates exist and one is an ancestor of another. The test topology ensures the optimization doesn’t prematurely exit before the `remove_redundant` safety net can filter out redundant candidates. Additionally, it extends `t6600-test-reach.sh` with a test for mixed finite/INFINITY generation scenarios, where one commit lies outside the commit-graph (INFINITY generation) and the other is inside (finite generation). This exercises the transition between regions where the optimization is enabled or disabled.

The tests are well-designed, covering both the core optimization and edge cases like commit-graph absence. The commit message clearly explains the motivation and expected behavior, and the test topologies are documented with ASCII diagrams. This patch strengthens confidence in the optimization’s correctness, particularly in scenarios involving criss-cross merges or grafts. If the series lands, these tests will help prevent regressions in future refactoring.
