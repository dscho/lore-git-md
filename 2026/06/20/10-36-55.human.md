This is the second patch in Tian Yuchen’s six-part performance series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. It introduces a new `paint_queue` struct that replaces the old `nonstale_queue` abstraction with explicit per-side counters (`p1_count`, `p2_count`, and `pending_merge_bases`). The change is purely refactoring—no behavior change yet—but it lays the groundwork for the early-exit optimization in patch 3 by making the queue’s state machine visible to the caller.

The patch touches only `commit-reach.c` and is mechanical: every `nonstale_queue_put_dedup()` becomes `paint_queue_put()`, and the termination check shifts from a pointer-based `max_nonstale` to a simple counter sum. The new `paint_count_transition()` helper keeps the counters in sync with flag changes, ensuring that each non-stale commit is counted exactly once. The commit message is clear and the diff is clean, though the BUG() assertions for unexpected paint states might draw scrutiny in review.

This is a well-structured preparatory step: it decouples the ahead/behind logic from the queue abstraction without altering the algorithm, making the subsequent optimization patch easier to reason about. The series’ overall goal—eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories—is compelling, and this patch looks like a solid foundation for it.
