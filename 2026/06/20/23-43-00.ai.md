This is a documentation patch updating `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to formalize how contributors should address design critiques in patch resubmissions. It is the fourth iteration (v4) of a maintainer-driven clarification, now finalizing the text after incorporating feedback from earlier rounds.

The patch adds 20 lines to two sections of `SubmittingPatches`. In the "Responding to Reviews" section, it instructs authors to prioritize high-level design or viability critiques (e.g., whether a feature is worth implementing or if the approach is appropriate), defend their choices on the mailing list before revising implementations, and ensure new versions include clearer justifications in cover letters and commit messages. It explicitly states that topics with unresolved fundamental design critiques will not be merged. In the "Commit Message Guidelines" section, it adds a requirement for commit messages to document how design concerns raised during review were resolved, preserving the historical record.

This version refines the phrasing from v3, replacing "Defend your design decisions on the list first, work with reviewers..." with "Defend your design decisions on the list first *and* work with reviewers..." to clarify the sequential expectation (discuss first, then revise). The change is minor but reinforces the intended workflow. The patch retains its core intent—reducing wasted effort on implementations with shaky designs—while maintaining a collaborative tone. The unresolved tension with the project's "show me the code" norm remains, but the softer framing and concrete examples (e.g., "questioning if the feature is worth implementing") make the requirement more accessible.

The patch is narrowly scoped to contributor workflow documentation and introduces no new technical dependencies. Given Junio's role as maintainer and the incremental nature of the updates, it is likely to be merged without controversy. However, the broader cultural question—whether this requirement conflicts with the project's bias toward code-first contributions—remains unaddressed. Future discussion may explore whether alternative venues (e.g., GitGitGadget's idea tracker) should be promoted for speculative design discussions, though this patch does not engage with that topic. Key terms introduced include *design critiques*, *fundamental objections*, and *justification trail*.
