This thread began as a simple patch to add a `nanosec` Meson build option mirroring the Autotools/Makefile `USE_NSEC` knob for sub-second file timestamp tracking. What started as a straightforward build system parity effort has evolved into a deeper technical debate about whether `USE_NSEC` should exist at all.

D. Ben Knoble’s patch added the Meson option, and Junio C Hamano queued it for merging. However, Jeff King (Peff) raised concerns about `USE_NSEC`’s reliability on filesystems with coarse timestamp granularity or network filesystems, citing long-standing "racy Git" issues. Patrick Steinhardt then questioned whether the knob was worth exposing if it was subtly broken. The discussion took a turn when Peff tested modern Linux filesystems (ext4, ext2, vfat) and found that nanosecond timestamps are preserved consistently, suggesting the original concerns may be outdated. His results imply `USE_NSEC` is safe on Linux, but the thread remains open: does this hold for other platforms (Windows, macOS, older kernels), and should the knob’s default or existence be reconsidered?

The patch itself is ready to merge, but the broader question of `USE_NSEC`’s viability lingers. No new patch versions or deprecation proposals have been submitted, and the original author hasn’t responded to the expanded discussion. The outcome could influence whether the Meson option remains justified or if the entire knob should be re-evaluated.
