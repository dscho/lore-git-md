Core problem or goal
Optimize the `paint_down_to_common()` function in Git’s commit-reach subsystem to terminate early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). The optimization targets a known performance bottleneck where asymmetric histories force full traversal of one side even after the other side is exhausted.

Current status
Series is **fully designed for v2**; all high-weight objections, substantive technical feedback, and procedural questions have been resolved. The **core optimization (patch 3)** remains functionally correct and endorsed by Derrick Stolee. The **structural dispute** over patch 1 is resolved: `nonstale_queue` is preserved for `ahead_behind()` in v2, eliminating patch 1 entirely. The **termination condition** is finalized as `(!queue.p1_count || !queue.p2_count) && !queue.pending_merge_bases`, replacing the addition-based check to avoid edge cases. The **finite-generation guard** (patch 3) is confirmed as critical for correctness, ensuring early termination only after entering the finite-generation region. **Patch ordering** is settled: the documentation patch (patch 6) will appear first to describe the current algorithm, followed by tests (patches 4–5) to serve as regression checks, and then the optimization (patch 3). **Performance instrumentation** is now fully agreed: **hyperfine** is the baseline tool for benchmarking `git merge-base` end-to-end timing between builds, and **trace2_data_intmax()** will expose the "commits walked" metric for testable validation. The only remaining open item is **edge-case correctness** (finite-generation guard activation timing), which remains unchallenged but unresolved.
