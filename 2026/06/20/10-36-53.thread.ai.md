Thread identity
Subject: [PATCH 0/6] commit-reach: optimize `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories
Series version: v1 (initial submission)
Originating author: Tian Yuchen
Thread type: patch series (performance optimization)

Core problem or goal
Optimize the `paint_down_to_common()` function in Git’s commit-reach subsystem to terminate early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue, eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). The goal is to address a known performance bottleneck where asymmetric histories force full traversal of one side even after the other side is exhausted.

Current status
Series is at v1, not yet reviewed or queued. All six patches are present:
1: refactors `ahead_behind()` to stop using the `nonstale_queue` abstraction, replacing it with a plain `prio_queue` and local `max_nonstale` pointer, decoupling it from the abstraction that will be replaced in patch 2.
2: introduces the `paint_queue` struct with explicit per-side counters (`p1_count`, `p2_count`, `pending_merge_bases`) to track queued commits by paint state (PARENT1-only, PARENT2-only, or both), enabling later early termination. The patch is a refactoring with no functional change; the loop still drains the queue until all counters reach zero, mirroring the old `max_nonstale` logic.
3: implements the core optimization (halt walk when one side’s queue is exhausted, provided the walk has entered the finite-generation region), adds a 13-line early-exit condition inside `paint_down_to_common()`, and cites 100–1000x speedups for large asymmetric histories. Kristofer Karlsson is listed as the author, with help from Derrick Stolee and Elijah Newren.
4–6: add test coverage for edge cases (criss-cross merges, grafts, shallow histories) and regressions, and provide technical documentation in `Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc`.

Key technical details
- Subsystem: commit-reach (merge-base calculation).
- Files touched: `commit-reach.c` (core logic), `Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc` (new documentation), test scripts `t6099-merge-base-side-exhaustion.sh` and `t6600-test-reach.sh`.
- New/renamed symbols: `paint_queue` struct (patch 2), per-side counters (`p1_count`, `p2_count`, `pending_merge_bases`) (patch 2), `paint_count_transition()`, `paint_queue_put()`, `paint_queue_get()` (patch 2), local `max_nonstale` pointer (patch 1).
- Behavior change: walk terminates early when one side’s queue is exhausted, but only after entering the finite-generation region (where generation numbers guarantee final paint states).
- On-disk format: no changes.
- Test coverage: new tests for edge cases and regression scenarios; benchmarks show 500x+ speedup on large one-sided histories (2.6M commits) with no regressions on smaller repositories (linux.git).

Open questions and loose ends
- Correctness of the halt condition in edge cases (e.g., criss-cross merges, grafts, or shallow histories)—whether the finite-generation guard is overly conservative or could activate earlier.
- Integration with Elijah Newren’s parallel work (PR #2150); potential overlap or conflict in test coverage.
- Whether the refactoring in patch 1 correctly preserves the original `nonstale_queue` behavior, particularly the `max_nonstale` pointer logic.
- Whether the per-side counters in patch 2 introduce any subtle behavioral differences in the queue-draining logic, despite the patch’s claim of no functional change.
