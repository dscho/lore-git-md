This is the third patch in Harald Nordgren’s four-patch series introducing `git history squash`, a new subcommand that folds a commit range into its oldest commit while preserving descendant history. The series is queued in `next` and addresses a long-standing workflow gap: efficiently collapsing a range of commits without the repeated conflict stops of `git rebase --interactive`.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review here is substantive. He raises three technical points about the range-resolution logic in `resolve_squash_range()`. First, the function lacks input validation for the revision range string, leaving it vulnerable to ambiguous or malformed input. Second, he questions whether `--ancestry-path` should be used to ensure the resolved commits are true descendants of the range’s base, preventing unintended side-branch commits from being included. Third, he probes whether the current implementation should explicitly reject merge commits at the range boundaries (the base or tip), noting that silently discarding them might not be intuitive for users.

The review also notes that the patch already uses `setup_revwalk()`, which internally filters out merge commits, so the second concern may already be addressed. Patrick’s tone is constructive, framing the questions as design clarifications rather than blockers. The patch itself remains well-motivated and well-tested, with 270 lines of new tests covering edge cases like merge commits inside the range and dry-run mode. The unresolved points are narrow and unlikely to derail the series, but they do highlight areas where the implementation could be made more robust before graduation to `master`.
