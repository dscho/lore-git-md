This thread is about a small but contested cleanup in Git’s test suite. The patch removes a no-op test in `t4216-log-bloom.sh` that checked whether the system’s `char` type is signed by default. The test was harmless until commit 389c83025d tightened TAP output parsing, causing it to fail on systems with unsigned `char`. The author’s fix deletes the unused test infrastructure entirely, restoring TAP compliance.

Taylor Blau reviewed the patch and proposed an alternative: instead of removing the test, convert it into a `test_lazy_prereq` or silence its output to preserve its original purpose. He argues the test, while oddly written, was meant to sanity-check the v1 Bloom filter’s murmur3 hash function and could still guard against future regressions. This introduces a debate: is the test’s marginal utility worth keeping, or is its removal the cleaner solution? The discussion now hinges on whether the murmur3 check remains relevant or is effectively dead code.

No other reviews have been posted yet, so the thread is at a decision point. The outcome will likely depend on whether others weigh in on the value of retaining the check. The patch itself is uncontroversial in its goal—restoring TAP compliance—but the question of how to do so remains open.
