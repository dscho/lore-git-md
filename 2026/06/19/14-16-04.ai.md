This is a follow-up in the ongoing performance optimization series integrating `--delta-islands` with `--path-walk` in `git pack-objects`. Taylor Blau responds to Derrick Stolee's substantive review of patch 2/4, clarifying the intended interaction between reachability bitmaps and path-walk traversal.

The email directly addresses Stolee's concerns about benchmark interpretation and the apparent lack of synergy between the two features. Blau acknowledges a documentation error in the commit message (incorrectly claiming reduced pack sizes when the data showed identical sizes) but maintains that the core functionality is working as designed. He explains that the test setup intentionally demonstrates bitmap acceleration of the object-counting phase rather than path-walk's delta compression benefits, since the two features operate sequentially rather than simultaneously during pack generation.

Key technical details for future reference: the patch enables bitmap usage during path-walk traversal as a fallback mechanism (when bitmaps are insufficient), not as a combined algorithm. The discussion reveals that "working in tandem" was misinterpreted - the goal is compatibility (allowing both features to coexist) rather than active cooperation between their algorithms. Blau remains open to suggestions for improving the actual performance outcomes (smaller packs/faster generation) through better integration.

The exchange leaves open whether additional test cases or documentation adjustments will be needed to satisfy Stolee's request for clearer evidence of non-trivial interaction. No new implementation issues are raised, but the conceptual gap about feature interaction remains unresolved.
