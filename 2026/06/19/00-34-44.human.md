This is a design question about the new `git history squash` subcommand, now queued in `next`. Junio C Hamano raises a subtle but important edge case: what should happen to branch or tag refs that point to commits *inside* the squashed range (e.g., commits A or B in the example `o---A---B---C`)?

The question ties directly to the broader vision for the `git history` command, which Patrick Steinhardt has been championing. Junio contrasts two existing models: `git replay` (which rewrites all refs involved in the operation) and `git rebase` (which updates only the target branch unless `--update-refs` is used). He asks whether `git history squash` should follow the rebase model, with `--update-refs` controlling whether other refs in the range are updated.

Junio then explores two plausible behaviors for those internal refs: either *removing* them (since the commits they pointed to no longer exist) or *redirecting* them to the new squashed commit (X). He offers a concrete use case for removal—manual bisection markers that lose meaning after squashing—while acknowledging that redirecting might also have merit, though he doesn’t yet see a compelling story for it.
