This is the maintainer’s **substantive review** of the exec-failure `--abort` hint in Harald Nordgren’s rebase advice patch. Junio engages directly with the user scenario Harald provided—an exec failure due to a nonexistent test file during a rebase—and questions whether the hint is justified in *all* exec-failure cases. He observes that in Harald’s example, the user doesn’t actually need to "fix" anything; they can simply `--continue` once the missing test file is added later in the rebase. This challenges the patch’s premise that exec failures universally warrant an abort hint, suggesting instead that the hint might be noise in cases where continuing is the natural resolution.

The review is technical and user-focused: Junio doesn’t reject the idea outright but asks whether the hint’s cost (extra output in every exec failure) outweighs its benefit. He implies the hint might be more appropriate for *irrecoverable* exec failures (e.g., a broken script) rather than transient ones (like a missing file). This introduces a new dimension to the discussion—whether the hint should be conditional on the nature of the failure—though the thread’s prior consensus (split messages, suppress hint for conflict-induced failures) remains intact. The tone is probing rather than dismissive, signaling that the patch’s design may need refinement before merging.
