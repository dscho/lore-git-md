Core problem or goal
Remove or repair a no-op test in `t/t4216-log-bloom.sh` that checked whether the system’s `char` type is signed by default. The test infrastructure was unused (guarding only a `true` statement) and caused TAP output parsing to fail on systems with unsigned `char` after commit 389c83025d tightened the test harness’s handling of invalid TAP output. The original intent of the test was to sanity-check the murmur3 hash function’s behavior, but its relevance and accuracy are now contested.

Current status
Patch is ready; no prior versions. The original patch deletes 21 lines (prerequisite setup and the empty test) from the test script. Taylor Blau’s review proposed preserving the test in a TAP-compliant form (via `test_lazy_prereq` or silencing `test_cmp` output) to retain its potential value as a guard against murmur3 regressions. Junio C Hamano’s response reframes the discussion around the test’s *purpose*: whether it was guarding against a real risk of murmur3 regressions or was effectively dead code. He also questions the accuracy of the `SIGNED_CHAR_BY_DEFAULT` prerequisite name, noting it could mislead on platforms where `char` signedness is configurable. No consensus yet; the thread is now focused on whether the test’s original intent is still relevant.

Open questions and loose ends
- **Is the test’s original intent still relevant?** Does the murmur3 hash function’s behavior depend on `char` signedness in a way that could cause regressions, or is the check unnecessary?
- **Should the test be removed entirely, or preserved in a TAP-compliant form?** If preserved, should the prerequisite name (`SIGNED_CHAR_BY_DEFAULT`) be revised to avoid confusion?
- **No other reviews or objections have been recorded.** The outcome depends on whether contributors weigh in on the test’s value or defer to Junio’s framing.
