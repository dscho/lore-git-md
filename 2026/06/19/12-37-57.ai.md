This is a design discussion about the `git history squash` feature, part of the ongoing `git history` subcommand effort. The thread focuses on a previously unaddressed edge case: how to handle branch or tag refs pointing to commits *inside* the squashed range (e.g., `A` or `B` in `o---A---B---C` → `o---X`).

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio C Hamano’s question by rejecting the idea of automatically *removing* such refs as "extremely surprising" to users. He also dismisses the idea of *retargeting* them to the squashed commit (`X`) as lacking a clear use case. Instead, he proposes two additional behaviors: leaving the refs untouched (pointing to now-orphaned commits) or *rejecting* the operation entirely when such refs exist, optionally prompting the user for guidance. He leans toward the latter, arguing that the ambiguity of the situation justifies a conservative approach that allows future iteration.

Patrick notes that the `--update-refs` flag (modeled after `git rebase`) could still be used to explicitly retarget refs to the squashed commit if desired (e.g., `--update-refs=head`). This preserves flexibility while avoiding unexpected behavior. The discussion remains open-ended, with no code changes yet proposed—this is purely about clarifying the intended design before the feature graduates from `next` to `master`. The outcome will shape how users interact with refs during squash operations, particularly in workflows involving manual bisection markers or intermediate branch states.
