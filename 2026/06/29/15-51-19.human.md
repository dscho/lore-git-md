This is the latest substantive review in the `git history squash` thread, now in its sixth iteration. Phillip Wood raises pointed usability concerns about the feature’s current design, focusing on edge cases that could frustrate users if left unaddressed.

The review centers on four key issues. First, **commit message rewording**: Phillip criticizes the lack of support for `fixup!`, `squash!`, and `amend!` commits, arguing that the current `--reedit-message` template (which simply concatenates all commit messages) produces noisy, hard-to-edit output. He proposes a cleaner template that omits redundant messages from `fixup!` commits and replaces original messages with those from `amend!` commits, while still listing all commit subjects in a header comment for context. This would align the feature’s usability with `git rebase -i` but with less clutter.

Second, **merge commit handling**: Phillip clarifies the current implementation’s behavior with merges, noting it already handles cases like squashing `A..G` in a diamond-shaped history (`A-B-C-D` and `A-B-E-F-G`). However, he points out that the lack of `--ancestry-path` support (a flag to restrict the range to commits reachable from the starting point) could lead to confusing outcomes when squashing ranges like `F^@..G`. This isn’t a design flaw but a usability gap—users might expect the option to constrain the range to avoid unintended commits.

Third, **input validation**: Phillip flags two problems: the command accepts single-commit ranges (e.g., `@^!`) where squashing is meaningless, and it silently succeeds when rewriting non-checked-out branches (e.g., `origin/seen^2^^..origin/seen^2`) without warning if no local refs would be updated. These behaviors risk confusing users who might assume the command validates its inputs more strictly. He also notes the **single-argument limitation** prevents scripting-friendly syntax like `^:/base :/tip`, though Patrick Steinhardt defended this as consistent with other `git history` subcommands.

Finally, Phillip suggests **sanitizing rev-list options** to prevent misuse (e.g., `--left-only` or `--right-only` with `A...B`), though this is a minor point compared to the other concerns.

The tone is constructive but firm—Phillip isn’t rejecting the feature but urging improvements to make it more intuitive. His suggestions are technically grounded and address real-world workflows (e.g., cleaning up `fixup!` commits). The ball is now in Harald Nordgren’s court to decide whether to incorporate these changes into a v7 or proceed with the current design. Given that the thread summary describes v6 as "complete" and "ready for Junio’s final assessment," this review could either prompt a quick polish or spark a debate about whether these are must-fix issues. Either way, the concerns are worth watching for anyone interested in the feature’s usability.
