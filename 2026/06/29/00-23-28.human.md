This is a **build system** and **platform compatibility** follow-up in the ongoing `USE_NSEC` discussion. Brian M. Carlson proposes flipping the default of the `USE_NSEC` knob to `true` for most users, arguing that modern filesystems (Linux, NTFS, CIFS, FUSE) reliably support nanosecond timestamps. To handle edge cases, he suggests adding a config knob for users to override the default, with a future enhancement to auto-detect filesystem safety via `statfs`.

The proposal directly responds to Jeff King’s earlier findings that historical "racy Git" concerns are obsolete on modern Linux, but it sidesteps the interoperability risks Peff raised (e.g., performance degradation when mixing `USE_NSEC` and non-`USE_NSEC` implementations like JGit). Brian’s position assumes these risks are either negligible or acceptable trade-offs for the benefits of sub-second timestamp tracking. His focus on Linux-centric filesystems leaves open questions about macOS, Windows, and network filesystems not covered by his list.

This email advances the thread by shifting the debate from "should the Meson option exist?" to "should `USE_NSEC` be enabled by default?"—a more consequential change. The original patch (adding the Meson option) remains valid, but Brian’s proposal would require a follow-up patch to modify the default in both Meson and Autotools. No such patch has been submitted yet, and the discussion now hinges on whether the project is willing to accept the interoperability trade-offs Peff highlighted. For now, the thread is a three-way standoff: keep the knob as-is (original patch), flip its default (Brian’s proposal), or deprecate it entirely (Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier skepticism).
