This is a **build system** and **platform compatibility** follow-up in the ongoing `USE_NSEC` discussion. Brian M. Carlson proposes flipping the default of the `USE_NSEC` knob to `true` for most users, arguing that modern Linux filesystems (ext4, NTFS, CIFS, FUSE) reliably support nanosecond timestamps. To mitigate potential issues on problematic filesystems, he suggests adding a config knob for users to override the default and, in the future, using a Linux-specific `statfs` call to auto-detect safe filesystems.

The proposal directly responds to Jeff King’s earlier findings that historical "racy Git" concerns are obsolete on modern Linux, while also addressing the interoperability risks Peff identified (e.g., performance degradation when mixing `USE_NSEC` and non-`USE_NSEC` implementations like JGit). Brian’s approach prioritizes performance for the majority of users while retaining a fallback for edge cases. However, the suggestion to auto-detect filesystems via `statfs` introduces new complexity and platform-specific code, which may face resistance from contributors who prefer simpler, cross-platform solutions.

No patch has been submitted yet, but this email shifts the thread’s focus toward a concrete change: flipping the default rather than deprecating or removing the knob. The original Meson parity patch (adding the `nanosec` option) remains technically valid, but its justification is now overshadowed by the broader debate about defaults and interoperability. Future discussion will likely center on whether the config knob should be a build-time option (as in the original patch) or a runtime setting, and whether auto-detection is worth the added complexity.
