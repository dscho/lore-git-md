This is a **substantive review reply** in the ongoing `git history squash` feature discussion, part of Harald Nordgren’s v6 patch series. Phillip Wood responds to Harald’s defense of the current `--reedit-message` template format, refining his usability critique while conceding some points.

Phillip clarifies that he is **not opposed to visual delimiters** between folded-in commit messages—only to the *excessive noise* created by including `fixup!`/`amend!` commits in their entirety. His proposed alternative keeps a comment before each message (e.g., `# This is the 1st commit message`) but omits messages that would be *fully commented out*, reducing clutter. The example he cites from `git rebase -i` shows how dense the template becomes when every commit—including those marked for squashing—is included verbatim. His goal is to make the editor buffer more scannable by showing only the *useful* parts of the folded history.

On `fixup!`/`squash!` handling, Phillip’s earlier suggestion to **error out if a `fixup!` commit’s target lies outside the squashed range** remains unchallenged; Harald agreed this is a good idea, and no further discussion is needed. This will likely be implemented in the next revision.

For merge commits, Phillip’s explanation of the current behavior—**allowing fully contained merges (e.g., `A..G` in a diamond history) but rejecting merges with external parents (e.g., `E..G`)**—is confirmed by Harald as correct and well-designed. This resolves the last technical ambiguity about merge handling, leaving only cosmetic template formatting as the remaining point of disagreement.

The tone is collaborative, with Phillip acknowledging Harald’s concerns while standing by his usability argument. The disagreement is narrow: it’s about *how much* visual structure the template needs, not whether it needs any. This is unlikely to block the series, but it may prompt a follow-up patch if Junio or others weigh in on the template’s readability. The series itself is otherwise complete, with all design questions settled.
