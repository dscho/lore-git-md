This is a **CI/build system** policy discussion in the ongoing thread about macOS CI hangs in the `t5551` and `t5559` tests. Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to align GitLab CI with GitHub’s coverage, clarifying his own position and raising a practical concern about visibility.

Junio does not object to Patrick’s goal of consistency but rejects the idea that GitHub CI is the "canonical source of truth." Instead, he suggests GitLab’s coverage could be a *superset* of GitHub’s — running the same tests on the same events *plus* additional ones (e.g., scheduled runs or tag pushes) that GitHub does not cover. This would avoid redundancy while still catching platform-specific issues. The tone is collaborative, not dismissive.

The email then shifts to a practical hurdle: Junio admits he does not know where to find GitLab CI results for his integration branch pushes, unlike GitHub’s workflow page (which he links via a tinyurl shortcut). This lack of visibility makes GitLab’s CI less useful to him as a maintainer. The implication is that even if the technical and policy questions are resolved, GitLab’s CI will remain underutilized unless its results are as easily accessible as GitHub’s.

No decision is reached; the thread remains open on both the policy question (superset vs. mirror coverage) and the visibility issue. The two-patch fix for the CI hangs is still ready to merge, but the third patch (enabling `GIT_TEST_LONG` for GitLab) is still on hold pending these discussions.
