Peff responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier discussion of `REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INITIAL` and the challenges of optimizing bulk ref creation in non-empty repositories. He acknowledges that while Git already has infrastructure to optimize ref creation in empty repositories (like clones), extending it to cases like `git fast-import` or this test would be non-trivial and not worth the effort, especially since the reftable backend will eventually solve these performance issues. His tone is pragmatic, reinforcing the consensus that the third patch is unnecessary.

The email then dives into a **detailed technical exploration** of how the test could be optimized *without* the third patch. Peff proposes a hacky but effective workaround: writing directly to the `packed-refs` file instead of using `git update-ref`. This approach would yield the same ~30% speedup as reftable but is described as "gross and fragile," with caveats about correctness (e.g., zero-padding numbered tags) and backend compatibility. He includes a diff illustrating the idea, though he clearly does not intend to submit it as a real patch—it’s more of a thought experiment to demonstrate the trade-offs.

Peff also shares an **observation about reftable performance**: while benchmarking, he noticed that reftable spends a surprising amount of time stat-ing the `tables.list` file during bulk ref creation. He speculates that this could be optimized by trusting the stack once the lock is taken, though he notes that the current performance (50,000 refs in 150ms) is likely "good enough" compared to the files backend. This is a low-priority nit, but it’s the kind of detail that might resurface if reftable adoption accelerates.

The email is **not a call for action**—the thread’s main patches are already queued for merging—but it’s a useful peek into the kind of low-level optimization work that happens in Git’s test suite and ref backends. For a busy reader, the key takeaway is that the project is **avoiding over-engineering** here: the CI flakes are fixed, and deeper optimizations are deferred until reftable lands. The discussion also underscores how much performance headroom reftable will unlock once it becomes the default.
