This is Junio C Hamano's **substantive review** of the `git history squash` feature, responding to Phillip Wood's feedback on v5. Junio engages with the technical design of input validation and edge-case handling, proposing a more robust approach than Phillip's suggested blacklist of `rev-list` options.

Junio **endorses Phillip's idea** to warn or error when a `fixup!`/`squash!` commit's target lies outside the squashed range, calling it "interesting" and noting it could prevent user confusion. He suggests that if such cases are rare, outright rejection (rather than a warning) would be appropriate, with a clear path for users to reword the commit first.

On **input validation**, Junio **rejects Phillip's proposal** to sanitize `rev-list` options (e.g., `--left-only`, `--right-only`, `--not`). Instead, he advocates a **shape-based validation** approach: after resolving the commit set, inspect the resulting history to ensure it meets structural requirements (connected commits, a single "bottom" commit just outside the range, and a single "top" commit whose children can be rewritten). This avoids the fragility of blacklisting specific options and ensures the command handles all valid cases while rejecting malformed ones. His critique highlights the risk of either over-restricting the command or missing edge cases with a blacklist.

The review is **technically grounded and forward-looking**, focusing on correctness and maintainability rather than surface-level issues. Junio's preference for shape-based validation aligns with Git's existing patterns (e.g., `git rebase`'s ancestry checks) and suggests a path for Harald Nordgren's v6 to refine input handling without sacrificing flexibility. This feedback is likely to influence the final design, particularly around how the command validates and processes commit ranges.
