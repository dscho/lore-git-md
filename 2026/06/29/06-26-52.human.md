This is the latest reply in the ongoing `git history squash` feature series, now at v6. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Phillip Wood’s substantive review of v5, addressing several key design questions that were still open.

Patrick agrees with Phillip that the behavior around `fixup!`, `squash!`, and `amend!` commits should be documented, though he doesn’t propose a specific implementation. He also endorses Phillip’s suggestion that merge commits with parents outside the squashed range should be rejected by default, calling them “a can of worms” and suggesting that any handling of such merges should require an explicit user flag.

On the question of what happens to branches pointing to commits inside the squashed range, Patrick doesn’t specify a behavior but agrees the outcome should be documented. His reply also clarifies that the command intentionally allows rewriting branches that aren’t currently checked out, matching the behavior of other `git history` subcommands. This means the earlier example (`git history squash --dry-run origin/seen^2^!` from `master`) is working as designed, not a bug.

The most concrete takeaway is that merge commits with external parents will likely be rejected unless the user supplies an explicit opt-in flag. The rest of Phillip’s questions (single-commit ranges, non-ancestor ranges, and the single-argument limitation) remain unaddressed in this reply, so those usability concerns are still open. The thread summary indicates that v6 has already shipped, so any further refinements will presumably appear in a follow-up iteration.
