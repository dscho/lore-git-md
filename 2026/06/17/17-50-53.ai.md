This is a substantive review comment from Junio C Hamano on the second patch of Weijie Yuan's v2 documentation series about patch reroll cadence. Junio engages directly with the proposed guidance on when to send updated patches after substantial rework or when a topic is close to acceptance.

The review questions two specific recommendations in the patch: first, that contributors may send a new version sooner if comments require substantial rework, and second, that quicker rerolls may be acceptable when a topic is nearly accepted. Junio argues that substantial rework inherently takes time and rushing risks sending a version the author hasn't thoroughly reviewed, suggesting instead that contributors should explicitly signal which parts of the current series will be invalidated to prevent wasted reviewer effort. He also notes that marking an iteration as a "final candidate" (a practice he observes in Patrick Steinhardt's patches) already serves the purpose of encouraging last-minute feedback without needing to codify timing exceptions.

The email advances the thread by surfacing a tension between the original intent (avoiding wasted reviewer time) and Junio's preference for deliberate, well-reviewed updates. This feedback directly impacts the patch's content and may require the author to revise or remove the contested guidance. The discussion remains focused on the practical implications of reroll timing rather than the broader principle of batching updates.
