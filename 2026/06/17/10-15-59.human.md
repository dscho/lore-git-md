This is the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s five-part series adding reference-writing subcommands to `git refs`. It introduces `git refs delete`, a direct equivalent to `git update-ref -d` that removes a reference after optionally verifying its current value.

The implementation is clean and well-tested. The new subcommand lives in `builtin/refs.c` and follows Git’s CLI conventions: it accepts `--message=<reason>` for reflog entries, `--no-deref` to operate on symbolic references themselves, and an optional `<old-value>` to enforce atomic deletion. Error messages are clear and consistently formatted, and the 130-line test script (`t1464-refs-delete.sh`) covers all documented behavior, including edge cases like stale old-value checks, null OIDs, and empty messages.

This patch is uncontroversial and ready to merge. It does not touch the still-open design questions around atomicity or backwards compatibility that affect the `update` and `create` subcommands in later patches. If you work with ref backends or the `the_repository` removal effort, this is a straightforward addition that aligns with both.
