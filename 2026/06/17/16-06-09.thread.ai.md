Subject: [PATCH v3] SubmittingPatches: require design justification in resubmissions
Series version: standalone patch, v3
Originating author: Junio C Hamano (maintainer)
Thread type: documentation update (project governance, contributor workflow)

Core problem or goal
Clarify and codify the expectation that contributors must explicitly address high-level design or viability critiques from reviewers before resubmitting patches. The update aims to reduce the frequency of patch series advancing through multiple iterations without resolving fundamental objections, which frustrates reviewers and obscures the project’s decision-making process. The discussion has evolved to focus on balancing rigor with accessibility: while the patch enforces a standard for design justification, it now acknowledges the practical challenges of articulating ideas without code, though it does not resolve the broader tension between the project’s "show me the code" norm and the need for early design feedback.

Current status
Patch v3 is under review, incorporating feedback from Kichael and Kristoffer Haugsbakk. The changes soften the prescriptive tone of v2 while preserving the core intent, replacing directives like "You should" with gentler phrasing ("Be particularly mindful of critiques...") and adding concrete examples of design critiques (e.g., "questioning if the feature is worth implementing"). The patch remains a maintainer-driven clarification of existing expectations, not a new policy. Junio’s framing treats code as a tool for precision but no longer implies that contributors who cannot implement their ideas are inherently unable to defend them. However, the unresolved tension from earlier rounds—whether this requirement conflicts with the project’s cultural bias toward code-first contributions—persists, as the patch does not address Haugsbakk’s concerns about accessibility for non-C contributors or the underutilization of GitGitGadget’s idea tracker.

Open questions and loose ends
1. **Policy alignment vs. cultural norms**: The patch’s requirement for design justification remains in tension with the project’s "show me the code" norm. While the softer phrasing in v3 reduces the risk of discouraging early feedback, it does not resolve Haugsbakk’s critique that the patch may disproportionately burden contributors without C experience or deep Git expertise. The project has not yet addressed whether alternative venues (e.g., GitGitGadget’s idea tracker) should be promoted for speculative design discussions.
2. **Accessibility for non-implementers**: The patch assumes contributors can defend their designs in writing, but Haugsbakk’s concern about accessibility for users who cannot implement their ideas remains unaddressed. Junio’s v2 reply implied that such contributors may struggle to articulate their ideas clearly, but v3’s softer tone does not engage with this point. Is there a middle ground (e.g., encouraging "pre-RFC" discussions with a softer framing)?
3. **Idea tracker underutilization**: Haugsbakk noted that GitGitGadget’s idea tracker is rarely used for design discussions. The patch does not mention it, leaving open whether the project should actively promote the tracker or acknowledge its misalignment with the mailing list workflow.
4. **Scope of design critiques**: The patch focuses on *high-level* design objections but does not define what constitutes a "fundamental" critique. Future discussions may need to clarify how contributors should distinguish between minor implementation feedback and design-level concerns.
