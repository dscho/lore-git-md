Subject: [PATCH v2] SubmittingPatches: require design justification in resubmissions
Series version: standalone patch, v2
Originating author: Junio C Hamano (maintainer)
Thread type: documentation update (project governance, contributor workflow)

Core problem or goal
Clarify and codify the expectation that contributors must explicitly address high-level design or viability critiques from reviewers before resubmitting patches. The update aims to reduce the frequency of patch series advancing through multiple iterations without resolving fundamental objections, which frustrates reviewers and obscures the project’s decision-making process. The discussion now also explores whether this requirement conflicts with the project’s historical practice of dismissing design-only proposals ("show me the code"), potentially discouraging early feedback from contributors who lack the time or expertise to implement their ideas.

Current status
Patch is under review; v2 addressed minor phrasing issues while preserving the original intent. The change remains a maintainer-driven clarification of existing expectations, not a new policy. However, Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s substantive review raises a **core tension**: the patch’s call for upfront design justification may clash with the project’s unofficial norm of requiring code for design discussions, creating a barrier for contributors who cannot or will not implement speculative ideas. No revised patch (v3) has been posted yet.

Open questions and loose ends
1. **Policy alignment**: How should the project reconcile the patch’s requirement for design justification with its historical practice of dismissing design-only proposals? Haugsbakk suggests this creates a catch-22 for contributors who want feedback before investing in implementation.
2. **Contributor accessibility**: The patch assumes contributors can readily defend their designs in writing, but Haugsbakk argues this disproportionately burdens users without C experience or deep Git expertise. Is there a middle ground (e.g., encouraging speculative design discussions with a softer framing like "could this be accepted?")?
3. **Idea tracker underutilization**: Haugsbakk notes that GitGitGadget’s idea tracker is rarely used for design discussions. Should the project promote it as a venue for early feedback, or is it fundamentally misaligned with the project’s workflow?
4. **Patch scope**: The review does not propose specific wording changes but questions whether the patch’s prescriptive tone will achieve its goals. Should the documentation acknowledge the practical challenges of design-first contributions?

Participant positions
- **Junio C Hamano (author)**: Positions the change as a best-practice clarification to improve transparency and reviewer satisfaction. v2 adjustments reflect attention to phrasing but no shift in substance. No substantive objections expected from the maintainer, but the review introduces a new dimension (policy alignment) that may require further iteration.

While supportive of the patch’s *intent*, he questions whether it will achieve its goals without addressing these cultural and workflow tensions.
