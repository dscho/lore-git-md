Subject: [PATCH v4] SubmittingPatches: require design justification in resubmissions
Series version: standalone patch, v4
Originating author: Junio C Hamano (maintainer)
Thread type: documentation update (project governance, contributor workflow)

Core problem or goal
Clarify and codify the expectation that contributors must explicitly address high-level design or viability critiques from reviewers before resubmitting patches. The update aims to reduce the frequency of patch series advancing through multiple iterations without resolving fundamental objections, which frustrates reviewers and obscures the project’s decision-making process. The patch finalizes the text after incorporating feedback, balancing rigor with accessibility by softening prescriptive language and adding concrete examples of design critiques (e.g., "questioning if the feature is worth implementing"). The unresolved tension between the project’s "show me the code" norm and the need for early design feedback persists, though the patch no longer implies that contributors who cannot implement ideas are inherently unable to defend them.

Key technical details
- Files touched: `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`
- New text added: 20 lines across two sections ("Responding to Reviews" and "Commit Message Guidelines")
- Key additions:
  - In "Responding to Reviews": Instructions to defend design choices on the mailing list *before* revising implementations, explain decisions in cover letters/commit messages, and ensure reviewers can trace justification for updates. Explicitly states that topics with unresolved fundamental critiques will not be merged. Refined phrasing ("Defend your design decisions on the list first *and* work with reviewers...") clarifies the sequential expectation.
  - In "Commit Message Guidelines": New bullet point requiring commit messages to record how design concerns raised during review were resolved.
- Symbols/terms introduced: *design critiques*, *fundamental objections*, *justification trail*
- On-disk or protocol changes: none
- No code, build system, or test changes

Open questions and loose ends
1. **Policy alignment vs. cultural norms**: The patch’s requirement for design justification remains in tension with the project’s "show me the code" norm. While the softer phrasing and concrete examples reduce the risk of discouraging early feedback, Haugsbakk’s critique—that the patch may disproportionately burden contributors without C experience or deep Git expertise—remains unaddressed. The project has not yet explored whether alternative venues (e.g., GitGitGadget’s idea tracker) should be promoted for speculative design discussions.
2. **Accessibility for non-implementers**: The patch assumes contributors can defend their designs in writing, but Haugsbakk’s concern about accessibility for users who cannot implement their ideas persists. Junio’s earlier replies implied that such contributors may struggle to articulate their ideas clearly, but the patch does not engage with this point or propose mitigations (e.g., encouraging "pre-RFC" discussions with a softer framing).
3. **Idea tracker underutilization**: Haugsbakk noted that GitGitGadget’s idea tracker is rarely used for design discussions. The patch does not mention it, leaving open whether the project should actively promote the tracker or acknowledge its misalignment with the mailing list workflow.
4. **Scope of design critiques**: The patch focuses on *high-level* design objections but does not define what constitutes a "fundamental" critique. Future discussions may need to clarify how contributors should distinguish between minor implementation feedback and design-level concerns, though this is beyond the patch’s scope.
