Subject: [PATCH v2] SubmittingPatches: require design justification in resubmissions
Series version: standalone patch, v2
Originating author: Junio C Hamano (maintainer)
Thread type: documentation update (project governance, contributor workflow)

Core problem or goal
Clarify and codify the expectation that contributors must explicitly address high-level design or viability critiques from reviewers before resubmitting patches. The update aims to reduce the frequency of patch series advancing through multiple iterations without resolving fundamental objections, which frustrates reviewers and obscures the project’s decision-making process. The discussion now centers on whether this requirement conflicts with the project’s historical practice of dismissing design-only proposals ("show me the code"), potentially discouraging early feedback from contributors who lack the time, expertise, or resources to implement their ideas. Junio’s latest reply reframes the "show me the code" norm as a *practical filter for clarity* rather than a blanket dismissal, arguing that code (even incomplete) forces contributors to articulate their ideas with precision, though he acknowledges other methods might achieve the same goal.

Current status
Patch is under review; v2 addressed minor phrasing issues while preserving the original intent. The change remains a maintainer-driven clarification of existing expectations, not a new policy. The exchange between Junio and Kristoffer Haugsbakk has surfaced a **fundamental tension**: the patch’s call for upfront design justification may inadvertently reinforce the project’s cultural bias toward code-first contributions, creating a barrier for contributors who cannot or will not implement speculative ideas. Junio’s reply does not resolve this tension but narrows the discussion to the mechanics of proposal evaluation—code as a tool for precision—rather than addressing the broader accessibility concerns raised by Haugsbakk. No revised patch (v3) has been posted yet, but the thread suggests further iteration may be needed to reconcile the patch’s prescriptive tone with the project’s historical workflow.

Open questions and loose ends
1. **Policy alignment**: How should the project reconcile the patch’s requirement for design justification with its historical practice of dismissing design-only proposals? Haugsbakk’s critique remains unresolved: the patch’s prescriptive tone may discourage early feedback, while Junio’s reply treats code as a necessary tool for precision rather than a cultural barrier.
2. **Contributor accessibility**: The patch assumes contributors can readily defend their designs in writing, but Haugsbakk argues this disproportionately burdens users without C experience or deep Git expertise. Junio’s reply sidesteps this concern, implying that contributors who cannot implement their ideas may also struggle to articulate them clearly. Is there a middle ground (e.g., encouraging speculative design discussions with a softer framing like "could this be accepted?")?
3. **Idea tracker underutilization**: Haugsbakk notes that GitGitGadget’s idea tracker is rarely used for design discussions. Junio’s reply does not engage with this point, leaving open whether the project should promote the tracker as a venue for early feedback or acknowledge its misalignment with the project’s workflow.
4. **Patch scope**: The review does not propose specific wording changes but questions whether the patch’s prescriptive tone will achieve its goals. Should the documentation acknowledge the practical challenges of design-first contributions, or is the current framing sufficient to enforce rigor without discouraging participation?
