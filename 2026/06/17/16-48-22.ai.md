This is a documentation patch series (v2 of 2 patches) refining contributor guidelines around patch review etiquette. The series updates `MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `SubmittingPatches` to make implicit review norms explicit for new contributors, focusing on two key areas: requiring substantive discussion before rerolling patches, and establishing reasonable reroll cadence guidelines.

The first patch (unchanged from v1) clarifies that review feedback should be discussed in the email thread before sending a new version, so reviewers can understand the author's reasoning rather than having to infer it from code changes alone. The commit message now explicitly frames this as encouraging "social interaction" between contributors. However, Patrick Steinhardt's feedback from the thread - that this patch largely restates existing content with more words - remains unresolved, and the author is actively seeking input on whether to keep or drop these changes.

The second patch incorporates Junio C Hamano's suggestions about global participation and patch polishing. It now emphasizes that waiting between rerolls gives reviewers across time zones a fair chance to participate, and encourages authors to polish patches before sending them so reviewers can focus on substantial issues. The patch properly attributes Patrick Steinhardt's original contribution with a `Helped-by` trailer, demonstrating the very practices it seeks to document.

This v2 focuses the series more tightly on the `SubmittingPatches` changes, which have clear consensus, while the `MyFirstContribution.adoc` updates remain in question. The author has shown responsiveness to feedback by implementing suggested improvements to the second patch while thoughtfully considering whether the first patch adds value. The series touches two of Git's most important contributor-facing documents, so getting the tone and content right matters for shaping new contributor expectations.

Key technical details include: the addition of timezone considerations in both documents, explicit guidance about focusing reviewer attention on substantial issues, proper attribution practices, and the unresolved question about redundancy in the first patch. The series now models the collaborative review practices it seeks to document, with the author actively engaging with feedback and demonstrating proper credit-giving.
