This is a **substantive review reply** in the ongoing discussion about Harald Nordgren’s patch to add `--abort` hints to rebase failure and status messages. The patch targets user interface consistency in `git rebase`, aiming to make the abort option more discoverable in all rebase interruption contexts.

Phillip Wood responds to Junio C Hamano’s earlier framing of the `--abort` hint as a harmless "escape hatch" for new users. Phillip clarifies his earlier objection: he wasn’t arguing that experienced users would dislike the option, but that the *exec-failure* case (e.g., a failing test) is fundamentally different from the *conflict* case. In the former, continuing is almost always the right choice, while in the latter, aborting might be necessary. He now accepts Junio’s proposal to decouple the hint from the primary message, suggesting the hint could be a generic, context-independent line (e.g., "You can also use `git rebase --abort` to cancel the rebase.") printed after the specific problem explanation.

The discussion has shifted from *whether* to add the hint to *how* it should be presented. Phillip’s concession on the hint’s presence—provided it’s worded neutrally—removes a key obstacle, but the exact wording and placement remain open. The status-message additions (in `wt-status.c`) are still uncontroversial. If the exec-failure hint is reworked along these lines, the patch could move toward consensus.
