This is a short follow-up in the ongoing `git rebase --squash` thread, where Harald Nordgren responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s endorsement of the `git history squash` alternative. Harald acknowledges the architectural fit but argues for the discoverability and workflow integration of the rebase-based approach: it automatically infers the commit range from the upstream relationship, whereas `git history` would require explicit range specification. His message is brief and does not engage with the efficiency concerns raised earlier by Phillip Wood; instead, it restates the original UX motivation for the rebase feature.

The email shifts the discussion slightly by reintroducing the user-experience dimension as a counterpoint to the architectural preference for `git history`. It does not resolve the efficiency debate, but it reminds the thread that the two approaches serve different workflows: one is tightly coupled to the rebase operation, the other is a standalone history-editing tool. Future replies will likely weigh this UX argument against the technical objections already on the table.
