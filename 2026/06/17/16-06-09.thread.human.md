This thread updates the `SubmittingPatches` documentation to formalize how contributors should address design critiques in patch resubmissions. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has refined the patch through four iterations, incorporating feedback to soften its tone while preserving its core intent: requiring contributors to explicitly justify high-level design choices before revising implementations.

The latest version (v4) adds 20 lines to two sections of `SubmittingPatches`. It instructs authors to defend design decisions on the mailing list before revising code, document how critiques were resolved in commit messages, and ensure reviewers can trace the justification for updates. The patch explicitly states that topics with unresolved fundamental critiques—such as whether a feature is worth implementing or if the approach is appropriate—will not be merged. While the phrasing is now more collaborative (e.g., "Defend your design decisions on the list first *and* work with reviewers"), the requirement remains firm.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk raised concerns in earlier rounds about the patch conflicting with Git’s "show me the code" norm and creating barriers for contributors who cannot implement speculative ideas. Junio’s v3 adjustments acknowledged these tensions but did not resolve them, and v4 does not engage further with Haugsbakk’s critique about accessibility or the underutilized GitGitGadget idea tracker. The patch is narrowly scoped to workflow documentation and is likely to be merged, but the broader question of how to balance rigor with inclusivity remains open. Future discussions may explore alternative venues for design feedback, though this patch does not address them.
