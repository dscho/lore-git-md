This thread is about a small but important update to Git’s contribution guidelines. Junio C Hamano, the project’s maintainer, has proposed a patch to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` that makes explicit what has long been an unwritten expectation: contributors must address high-level design or viability critiques from reviewers before resubmitting patches. The goal is to reduce the frustration of reviewers who see patch series advance through multiple iterations without resolving fundamental objections, and to make the project’s decision-making process more transparent.

The patch itself is straightforward. It adds two paragraphs to the “Responding to Reviews” section and a bullet point to the “Commit Message Guidelines,” clarifying that contributors should defend their design choices on the mailing list, provide clearer justifications in cover letters and commit messages, and document how they’ve resolved design concerns. A minor revision (v2) tweaked the language to be more direct and fixed a small grammatical omission, but the core message remains unchanged. The tone is prescriptive but constructive, framing these requirements as best practices to improve the review process.

So far, the patch has attracted no substantive objections, and none are expected. It’s a narrow, maintainer-driven clarification of existing norms rather than a new policy, and it aligns with how the project already operates. The update is unlikely to be controversial, but it’s a useful step toward reducing ambiguity for new contributors and ensuring that design discussions are properly documented.
