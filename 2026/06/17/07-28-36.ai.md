This is a **reply in the design-review phase** of Patrick Steinhardt’s four-patch series adding `git refs delete`, `update`, and `rename` subcommands. The email responds to Junio C Hamano’s substantive review of the `update` patch, focusing on three key design questions: naming consistency, atomic creation semantics, and error message formatting.

Patrick **accepts two of Junio’s suggestions** outright:
1. **Naming consistency**: He will update the `delete` subcommand’s synopsis to use `<old-value>` (matching `update`’s `<old-value>` and `<new-value>`).
2. **Error message formatting**: He will add quotes around object IDs in error messages (e.g., `invalid old object ID: 'abc123'`) to align with the `delete` subcommand.

However, he **pushes back on the atomic creation design pivot**. Junio had proposed adding a dedicated `git refs create` subcommand and making `update` reject modifications to non-existent refs. Patrick argues for retaining `update`’s current behavior, which allows implicit creation (via `0{40}` or omitted `<old-value>`) and deletion (via `0{40}` as `<new-value>`). His rationale:
- **Backwards compatibility**: This matches `git update-ref`’s existing behavior, reducing friction for scripts/tools.
- **Ergonomics**: Users can perform create/delete operations with a single command rather than switching between `create`, `update`, and `delete`.
- **Consistency**: The special syntax (`$NULL_OID` for deletion, `$NEW_OID $NULL_OID` for creation) is already familiar to users of `git update-ref`.

The **open question** is whether the project will prioritize **explicit atomicity** (Junio’s preference) or **backwards compatibility and ergonomics** (Patrick’s preference). This tension is central to the series’ design and will likely require further discussion or a maintainer decision. The next iteration (v2) may include the `create` subcommand alongside `update`’s retained implicit creation, or it may drop the latter entirely.

For downstream consumers, this email signals that the series is **not yet ready for merging**—key design decisions remain unresolved. The outcome will shape how users interact with reference creation in Git, particularly in safety-critical workflows (e.g., CI/CD pipelines). The discussion also touches on broader themes: balancing ergonomics with explicitness, and the trade-offs of consolidating vs. fragmenting Git’s command-line interface.
