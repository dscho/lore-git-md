This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) on the second patch of Pablo Sabater’s v5 series improving `git log --graph` rendering. Peff identifies a potential interaction issue between the new `graph_peek_next_visible()` function and the priority-queue-based commit traversal introduced in commit `dd4bc01c0a` (May 2026). The problem: the function iterates over `revs->commits`, but since `dd4bc01c0a`, most commits live in `revs->commit_queue` (a prio-queue) during traversal, leaving `revs->commits` nearly empty. Peff’s instrumentation confirms this—only one commit is visible in `revs->commits` at a time, while the queue holds the rest.

The review is technically sharp, with a clear reproduction path (instrumentation diff + example command) and a precise diagnosis. Peff isn’t certain whether this is a latent bug or a test-coverage gap, but the risk is real: the lookahead logic could miss commits entirely, undermining the cascading indentation feature. He notes that his own in-progress topic (dropping `revs->commits` entirely) conflicts with this patch, which suggests the issue isn’t theoretical.

This isn’t a nitpick—it’s a fundamental design question about how the graph subsystem interacts with the revision walker. The series has otherwise been deemed technically sound by Junio and Chandra Pratap, but Peff’s observation could force a rethink of the lookahead implementation. The unresolved NEEDSWORK edge case in the same function may now be more urgent to address. Authors and reviewers will need to decide whether to adapt the patch to the prio-queue world or document this as a known limitation (like the existing lookahead issue). Either way, this feedback raises the stakes for the series’ final review.
