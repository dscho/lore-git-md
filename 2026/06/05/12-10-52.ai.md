This is a technical follow-up in the ongoing discussion about submodule mirroring for archival purposes. Simon Richter responds to Benson Muite's proposal for multiple fallback URLs in `.gitmodules`, highlighting a deeper architectural issue about repository identity versus location.

Simon argues that the core problem stems from conflating a submodule's identity (what it is) with its location (where to fetch from). He points out that current `.gitmodules` URLs force mirror maintainers to either:
1) Accept that old versions become unbuildable when original URLs die
2) Rewrite history to update URLs
3) Rely on fragile `insteadOf` hacks

The email reinforces the need for a solution that separates identity from location, connecting this to last week's discussions about stable project identifiers. Simon frames this as a metadata-vs-data problem, where hosting location (which should be mutable metadata) is currently encoded as version-controlled data in `.gitmodules`.

This continues the thread's tension between immediate archival needs (Benson's fallback URLs) and architectural direction (Junio's repository-level solution). Simon's perspective as a Debian maintainer adds weight to the discussion by emphasizing real-world mirroring challenges and security implications of URL rewriting approaches. The email doesn't propose new technical mechanisms but sharpens the conceptual framing that any solution must address.
