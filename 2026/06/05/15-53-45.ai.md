Platform compatibility discussion about compiler version check implementations.

First-time contributor Dominik Loidolt follows up on Patrick Steinhardt's request to understand the historical rationale behind GCC's bit-shift version check implementation. Loidolt traces the current approach to a 2015 Git commit (89c855ed3c) that inherited the style from glibc, while noting that NetBSD and other BSD systems have long used the more readable comparison form. He finds no technical reason to prefer the current bit-shift style, providing source links to both the glibc origin and NetBSD's alternative implementation.

This moves the discussion forward by establishing that:
1. The bit-shift style was not Git-specific but came from glibc
2. Major BSD systems demonstrate the viability of the comparison approach
3. No clear technical advantage of the current implementation is apparent

The exchange remains focused on the narrow technical question of version check implementations in `compat/posix.h`, with Loidolt demonstrating the research Steinhardt requested. The next step likely depends on whether Steinhardt or other reviewers can identify any unstated benefits to the glibc-derived approach that would outweigh readability improvements.
