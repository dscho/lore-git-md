This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing thread about improving `git log --graph` visualization for commits with excluded parents. Junio C Hamano responds to Pablo Sabater's explanation about why commit-graph behavior needs to be disabled for testing the visualization changes.

He questions whether the test approach is too brittle since traversal order isn't guaranteed to remain stable even without commit-graph. Junio then examines Pablo's concrete output examples to understand the visualization rules. He seeks confirmation that the vertical alignment rules still correctly represent parent-child relationships in both rendering variants, noting that the outputs show the same commit relationships just with different layouts. His analysis suggests the test failures are due to layout differences rather than incorrect relationship representation, implying the test expectations might need adjustment rather than commit-graph disabling.

The exchange remains focused on test methodology rather than the core visualization changes. Junio's questions highlight two key points: (1) whether there's a more robust way to test the visualization that doesn't depend on exact output matching, and (2) confirming that the visualization rules properly represent commit relationships regardless of layout variations. These are important considerations before finalizing the patch series, though they don't indicate fundamental problems with the technical approach.
