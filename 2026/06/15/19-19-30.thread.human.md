This thread is about a small but user-facing improvement to Git’s rebase interface: adding `--abort` hints to failure and status messages where only `--continue` was previously mentioned. The goal is to make the abort option more discoverable for users stuck in interrupted rebases, framed as a low-cost "escape hatch" rather than a workflow recommendation.

The patch series, authored by Harald Nordgren, is at its first version (v1). The changes to status messages (in `wt-status.c`) are uncontroversial and ready to go. The exec-failure case (in `sequencer.c`) has seen the most discussion, but consensus has now emerged: the message will be split into two parts—first explaining the failure and how to continue, then neutrally suggesting `--abort`. This design avoids implying that aborting is the recommended action, addressing earlier concerns from Phillip Wood. Junio Hamano, the maintainer, has endorsed this approach, agreeing with Phillip that the hint should only appear for exec failures, not conflicts.

The only remaining task is implementing the split-message design in `sequencer.c`; no further objections or alternative proposals are expected. The series touches three files (`sequencer.c`, `wt-status.c`, and the test script `t/t7512-status-help.sh`) and follows established patterns for rebase advice. With the maintainer’s approval, the next revision should be the last before merging.
