Harald Nordgren’s patch series aims to make `git rebase --abort` more discoverable by adding hints in two places where only `--continue` was previously mentioned: when an "exec" step fails during rebase, and in the status help text shown during commit splitting or editing. The uncontroversial parts—adding `--abort` to status messages—are ready to go, but the exec-failure addition in `sequencer.c` sparked debate.

Phillip Wood initially objected that the hint could mislead users into thinking abort is the recommended action for minor exec failures, like test failures. Junio C Hamano countered that the hint is a harmless "escape hatch" for new users, and Harald provided real-world examples of users getting stuck in ill-planned rebases. The discussion has now shifted toward consensus: Phillip accepts the hint as long as it’s presented as a neutral option, not a recommendation. The plan is to split the exec-failure message into two parts—one explaining the failure and how to continue, followed by a generic `--abort` suggestion. No code changes have been proposed yet, but the path forward is clear, and the series is likely to land in its next iteration.
