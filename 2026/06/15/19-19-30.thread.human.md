This thread is about a documentation patch by Harald Nordgren that adds `git rebase --abort` hints to failure and status messages, aiming to improve consistency and discoverability for users interrupted during a rebase. The series is at v1, with the status-message additions uncontroversial, but the exec-failure hint in `sequencer.c` has sparked debate.

The core disagreement centers on whether the `--abort` hint should appear for *all* exec failures or only irrecoverable ones. Harald argues for universal inclusion as a low-cost "escape hatch" for new users, while Phillip Wood counters that aborting is rarely the right response—users often lose work unnecessarily when they could instead `--continue` after fixing the issue or `--skip` transient failures. Phillip proposes teaching `--skip` to handle rescheduled exec commands and adding hints to guide users toward editing the todo list for bad commands. Junio Hamano has also weighed in, questioning whether transient failures (e.g., missing files) warrant the hint, though Harald hasn’t addressed this concern.

The patch touches `sequencer.c`, `wt-status.c`, and tests in `t/t7512-status-help.sh`, splitting exec-failure messages into a failure-specific warning and a neutral `--abort` hint. The status-message changes remain stable, but the exec-failure logic may need further refinement to address the open question: *what criteria should suppress the hint?* The thread now hinges on competing visions of user guidance—universal hints versus context-sensitive advice—with no clear resolution yet. Key participants include Harald (author), Phillip (reviewer), and Junio (maintainer), all engaging substantively with workflow and user experience.
