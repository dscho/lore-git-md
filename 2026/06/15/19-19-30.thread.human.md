This thread is about a small but user-facing improvement to Git’s rebase interface: adding `--abort` hints to failure and status messages where only `--continue` was previously mentioned. The goal is to make the escape hatch more discoverable for users stuck during a rebase, particularly in scenarios like failed `exec` steps or interrupted commit editing.

The patch, now at v1, has two parts. The status-message additions (in `wt-status.c`) are uncontroversial and ready to go. The exec-failure addition (in `sequencer.c`) has reached consensus on its design—splitting the message into a failure-specific warning and a neutral `--abort` hint, with the hint suppressed if the failure is due to a conflict. However, Junio has raised a new question: should the hint also be suppressed for *transient* exec failures (like missing files), where users might reasonably `--continue` after fixing the issue? Harald’s latest reply reaffirms his support for the hint as a low-cost training aid but doesn’t engage with this technical nuance, leaving the question unresolved.

Phillip Wood and Junio have both weighed in substantively. Phillip, initially skeptical, now fully supports the change after the message design was refined. Junio agrees in principle but questions whether the hint’s universal applicability justifies its cost in all cases. The thread is now waiting on either a revised patch addressing Junio’s concern or a clearer consensus on whether the hint should be conditional. No one has objected to the status-message additions, which could land independently if the exec-failure part remains stalled.
