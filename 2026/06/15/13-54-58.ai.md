This is a technical patch in Patrick Steinhardt's `git history drop` series, specifically addressing an assumption in the reset machinery about index cleanliness. The change modifies `reset_working_tree()` to only skip cache tree updates during hard resets, rather than assuming all callers have a clean index.

The patch fixes a subtle but important assumption that could cause problems when the new `git history drop` command (which may operate on a dirty index) calls into the reset machinery. While the original optimization from 2022 made sense for rebase/sequencer callers, it doesn't hold for the new use case. The change is straightforward - moving the `skip_cache_tree_update` flag to only apply during hard resets, where we know the index will be clean.

This is a small but meaningful piece of the larger series, showing attention to edge cases as the reset machinery gets reused in new contexts. The patch maintains the performance optimization where safe (hard resets) while correctly handling the new dirty-index case. It's a good example of the series' thoroughness in updating core infrastructure to support new functionality.
