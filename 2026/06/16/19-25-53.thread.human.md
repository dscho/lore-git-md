This thread is a design discussion about whether Git should adopt an `Assisted-by:` trailer to formally attribute AI-assisted commits, mirroring a recent Linux kernel policy. Marius Spix kicked off the conversation by proposing two changes: normalizing how `--trailer` handles newlines to match `--signoff` behavior, and introducing `Assisted-by:` as a new trailer that would auto-propagate during merges and squashes.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk has pushed back, arguing that the proposal misreads Git’s existing trailer-handling as inconsistent when it’s actually intentional. He points out that `Signed-off-by:`’s special treatment (like suppressing empty commits) is a historical quirk, not a template for other trailers, and notes that Git has previously rejected hardcoding project-specific trailers like `Fixes:` in favor of the generic `git interpret-trailers` mechanism. Haugsbakk also dismisses the need for newline normalization, showing that `git commit --trailer "Assisted-by: OpenAI"` already works as intended. His critique frames the proposal as both technically unnecessary and culturally misaligned with Git’s tradition of avoiding project-specific policy decisions—like auto-propagating "taint" during merges. The discussion is still in the exploratory phase, with no patches or decisions yet, but Haugsbakk’s skepticism suggests the proposal faces an uphill battle.
