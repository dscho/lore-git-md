This is a **contributor housekeeping** discussion reply that pushes back on the `Assisted-by:` proposal and questions its technical and philosophical justification.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Marius Spix’s suggestion by framing the existing trailer-handling behavior as intentional rather than inconsistent. He notes that `Signed-off-by:`’s special treatment (suppressing empty-message commits and normalizing newlines) is a historical quirk, not a model for other trailers. The Git project has previously rejected proposals to hardcode project-specific trailers (citing a 2013 patch for `Fixes:` as precedent), instead favoring the generic `git interpret-trailers` mechanism. Haugsbakk argues that `Assisted-by:` would require Git to make project-specific policy decisions—such as auto-propagating "taint" during merges—which the project has historically avoided.

The reply also points out that the `\n` in Marius’s example is unnecessary: `git commit --trailer "Assisted-by: OpenAI"` already works as expected. This undercuts the original motivation for normalizing newline handling. Haugsbakk’s tone is skeptical, suggesting that the proposal would need to overcome significant cultural and technical inertia to gain traction. No alternative implementation is offered; the email is purely a critique of the premise.
