This is a documentation patch with a substantive review raising a design concern.

Phillip Wood responds to Harald Nordgren’s patch that adds `git rebase --abort` hints to rebase failure and status messages. While the patch itself is a straightforward documentation improvement—adding parallel advice text to match existing patterns—Phillip questions whether the two scenarios being modified are truly equivalent.

His concern centers on the "exec step failure" case: when an `exec` command fails during rebase (e.g., a test or script exits non-zero), the user is likely dealing with a minor issue that needs fixing before continuing, not a fundamental problem like a merge conflict. In contrast, conflicts often require aborting the rebase entirely to try a different approach. Phillip argues that mentioning `--abort` in the exec failure message might mislead users into thinking they should abandon the rebase when continuing after a fix is the more natural action.

The review is substantive, engaging with the *purpose* of the advice rather than just its wording or formatting. It doesn’t reject the patch outright but suggests the change might not be universally appropriate. The discussion is now open for others to weigh in on whether the UI consistency argument (Harald’s motivation) outweighs the risk of encouraging unnecessary aborts in exec scenarios.
