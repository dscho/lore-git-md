This thread is about a bug in `git rebase` where notes from dropped commits (those whose changes are already present in the target branch) are incorrectly copied to the current HEAD, corrupting the repository’s note history. Uwe Kleine-König submitted a patch to fix this by skipping note copying for dropped commits, but the patch has hit two major roadblocks: a fixup-commit edge case that could misattribute notes to the wrong commit, and a lack of documentation clarifying when notes are lost during rebase.

Phillip Wood has now stepped in with a deeper review, confirming the core issue but identifying critical flaws in the current approach. His analysis reveals that Uwe’s patch could break `record_in_rewritten()` logic for sequences involving fixup commits, and he surfaces three additional bugs in the rebase machinery related to dropped commits: mishandling of final fixup cleanup, "edit" commands, and "skip" commands. These issues suggest the sequencer’s logic for tracking rewritten commits may need a systemic overhaul. Phillip has offered to take ownership of these problems and send follow-up patches within two weeks.
