This thread began with Uwe Kleine-König’s patch to stop `git rebase` from incorrectly copying notes from dropped commits. That patch has since been superseded by Phillip Wood’s far more comprehensive 10-patch series, now at v2 and queued in Junio’s tree. The series doesn’t just fix the original bug—it audits and repairs how the rebase machinery handles dropped commits across every operation: empty changes, fixup sequences, edit commands, skip operations, final fixup cleanup, external merge-strategy failures (e.g., merge tools crashing or permission errors), and command execution failures (e.g., `git commit` failing to run). Phillip’s patches also restore test coverage for the “apply” backend, expand edge-case testing, and refactor the sequencer internals to make the behavioral change safer and clearer.

The core fix lands in the final patch (10/10), which introduces `PICK_RESULT_DROPPED` to `enum pick_result` and ensures notes and post-rewrite hooks no longer receive information about dropped commits. The earlier patches are pure refactorings—moving helpers, removing redundant checks, and simplifying control flow—so the behavioral change is introduced with minimal churn. Junio has queued the series with minor typofixes and confirmed it’s ready for final review; Phillip will send a rerolled version once he squashes those fixes. One known limitation remains: the “edit” command still incorrectly records dropped commits as rewritten when `rebase --continue` is run, but that’s documented and flagged for a separate fix. Oswald Buddenhagen raised surface-level objections about patch organization, but Phillip rejected them as unnecessary churn, and the series’ technical direction is unchanged. Uwe has tested the series and provided a Tested-by, and no substantive objections remain.
