This thread began with Uwe Kleine-König’s patch to fix a bug where `git rebase` incorrectly copied notes from dropped commits to the current HEAD. The discussion quickly expanded into a systemic audit and repair of how the rebase machinery handles dropped commits, led by Phillip Wood. His nine-patch series (v3) addresses edge cases like empty changes, fixup sequences, edit commands, skip operations, external merge strategy failures, and command execution failures. The series was approved by Junio Hamano and is ready for the `next` branch pending minor typofix squashing.

However, Uwe later reported a regression: the fix works for non-interactive rebase but fails for interactive rebase (`-i`), where notes from dropped commits are still copied in user-initiated drops (e.g., `git rebase --skip` or `--continue` without committing). Phillip clarified that the series only handles commits dropped by `--empty=drop` (non-interactive default), not user-initiated drops in interactive rebase, where `--empty=ask` behaves inconsistently (drops on `--continue`, keeps on `--skip`). This regression will be addressed in a follow-up series.

The thread also included meta-discussions about review culture. Junio validated the value of mailing-list reviews, distinguishing between low-confidence `Acked-by` and substantive `Reviewed-by`. Oswald Buddenhagen countered that Gerrit’s multi-level scoring system (e.g., -2 to +2) already captures this nuance, framing Gerrit as more expressive than Junio suggested. This procedural exchange has no technical impact on the series but may resurface in future tooling debates. The series itself remains on track for `next`, with the interactive rebase regression noted as a follow-up task.
