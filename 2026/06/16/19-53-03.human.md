This is a **contributor housekeeping** discussion about whether Git should adopt an `Assisted-by:` trailer for AI-assisted commits, and how trailer handling should work.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Marius Spix’s proposal with skepticism. He argues that the current behavior of `--signoff`—where a commit message consisting *only* of `Signed-off-by:` is treated as empty—is a historical quirk, not a design principle to emulate. He points out that Git’s trailer system (`git-interpret-trailers`) was intentionally built to be project-agnostic, citing a 2013 patch that proposed adding a `--fixes` option for the Linux kernel’s `Fixes:` trailer but was rejected in favor of a generic trailer-handling mechanism. Haugsbakk implies that Git’s current approach—letting projects define their own trailers and conventions—is deliberate, and that automatically propagating `Assisted-by:` during merges or squashes would go beyond Git’s intended scope.

The email does not engage with the Linux kernel’s adoption of `Assisted-by:` as a precedent, nor does it propose an alternative. Instead, it frames the proposal as misaligned with Git’s existing philosophy of treating trailers as project-specific metadata rather than core functionality. The tone is cautious, suggesting that any change here would need strong justification to overcome the project’s historical reluctance to hardcode trailer semantics. No patch or concrete counter-proposal is offered; this is purely a conceptual objection.
