This is a reply in the ongoing documentation patch series updating Git’s `SubmittingPatches` guidelines, specifically addressing Junio C Hamano’s review of the `Based-on-patch-by` trailer documentation. The series aims to clarify and standardize Git’s trailer conventions, and this patch focuses on how to credit contributors whose patches are incorporated without a full commit message or sign-off.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk acknowledges Junio’s concern that the original wording could be read as encouraging the use of patches that lack proper DCO sign-off, which would create copyright issues. He agrees to revise the text to drop the explicit mention of missing sign-off and instead emphasize that the original author should provide sign-off for non-trivial contributions. The revised guidance will likely mirror Junio’s suggestion: documenting that `Based-on-patch-by` is appropriate when the original author has submitted a preliminary patch or code snippet *with* their sign-off, and encouraging contributors to request sign-off if it was initially missing.

This exchange is a small but important refinement to the series. The patch is well-motivated and addresses a real need—clarifying how to credit contributions that don’t fit neatly into the standard `Helped-by` or `Co-authored-by` trailers—but the legal implications of patch attribution require careful wording. The revision will bring the documentation in line with Git’s DCO requirements without undermining the trailer’s usefulness. The rest of the series remains uncontroversial, and this update should resolve the only substantive feedback on this patch. A v2 is expected soon.
