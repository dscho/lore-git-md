This is Junio C Hamano’s review of the third patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s four-patch series adding reference-writing subcommands to `git refs`. Junio raises a design question about the absence of a dedicated `create` subcommand or mode, noting that the current `update` subcommand implicitly creates references when they don’t exist, which may not be ideal for users who want to ensure they aren’t overwriting existing refs.

The review is substantive: Junio identifies a potential gap in the interface’s safety and suggests two alternative designs—either repurposing `update` with special sentinel values (e.g., an empty string) to distinguish creation from update, or adding a separate `create` subcommand that enforces atomic creation of new refs. He also points out that the test suite doesn’t cover the case where an attempted creation fails due to an existing ref, implying the current implementation lacks this safeguard.

This feedback doesn’t block the patch but signals a likely point of discussion for the series. The question touches on CLI consistency (how other Git commands handle creation vs. update) and user expectations around atomicity and safety. Reviewers or contributors invested in reference backends (e.g., reftable) or the broader `git refs` consolidation effort may weigh in on whether this gap is worth addressing before merging. The patch itself remains technically sound, but Junio’s observation could lead to a v2 revision or follow-up work.
