This is a reply in the ongoing documentation patch series about Git's trailer conventions in commit messages. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Patrick Steinhardt's substantive review of patch 2/6, which discusses non-identity trailers.

Kristoffer pushes back on Patrick's suggestion to list non-identity trailers as "common" in the documentation. He argues that such trailers are rare in Git's own history and mostly relevant to other projects (e.g., for bug trackers). After analyzing Git's commit history, he finds only a handful of non-identity trailers (`Change-Id`, `Message-ID`, `Fixes`), none of which are widely used in the project. Instead of expanding the list of common trailers, he proposes two alternatives: either drop the mention of non-identity trailers entirely as too niche, or provide a single concrete example (e.g., `Closes: <bug link>`) to make the guidance more actionable.

The exchange highlights a tension in the series' goal: balancing clarity for newcomers with the reality that Git's workflow rarely needs non-identity trailers. This reply advances the discussion by grounding it in data (the `git log` analysis) and offering specific alternatives, but leaves the decision open for further input. The author's preference leans toward minimalism, but the thread remains unresolved on whether to include examples.
