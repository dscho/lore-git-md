Junio C Hamano weighs in on the specific controversy Phillip Wood raised: whether the "failed exec" case warrants `--abort` advice alongside `--continue`. He acknowledges Phillip’s workflow argument (that exec failures are typically minor and continuing is usually preferable) but frames the issue differently. His position is that offering `--abort` as an "escape hatch" is valuable for new users who find themselves in confusing situations, even if experienced users rarely need it. He treats the hint as a low-cost training aid rather than a workflow recommendation, suggesting it can coexist with the existing message without steering users toward aborting.

The response does not resolve the disagreement but reframes it: Phillip sees the hint as potentially misleading, while Junio sees it as harmless and possibly helpful. No code changes are proposed yet, but the maintainer’s stance may shift the discussion toward whether the hint should be worded more neutrally or remain as-is. The status-message additions (in `wt-status.c`) are still uncontroversial.
