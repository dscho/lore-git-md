This is a documentation patch series (v3 of 2 patches) refining Git’s contributor guidelines around patch review workflow. The series updates `MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `SubmittingPatches` to codify two long-standing but implicit norms: (1) contributors should discuss review feedback in the thread before sending a new version, and (2) rerolls should be batched to respect reviewer bandwidth and global participation.

The first patch, unchanged since v1, adds a paragraph to `MyFirstContribution.adoc` that explicitly tells new contributors to reply to reviews before rerolling, so reviewers can see the author’s reasoning rather than having to infer it from code changes. The commit message now frames this as encouraging “social interaction” between contributors. Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback—that the new text largely restates existing content with more words—remains unresolved; the author is still seeking input on whether to keep or drop this patch.

The second patch, originally based on an email from Patrick Steinhardt, documents a rough expectation for reroll cadence. The v3 revision responds to Junio C Hamano’s critique of the “substantial rework” exception: instead of suggesting quicker rerolls, the text now advises authors to signal which parts of the series will become obsolete, so reviewers can avoid spending time on doomed code. The “close to acceptance” exception has been removed entirely, and the short guidance in `SubmittingPatches` no longer mentions how close the topic is to being picked up. The patch also incorporates Junio’s suggestions about timezone fairness and patch polishing, and properly attributes Patrick’s original contribution with a `Helped-by` trailer.

The series is now tightly focused on the `SubmittingPatches` changes, which have clear consensus, while the `MyFirstContribution.adoc` updates are likely to be dropped due to redundancy concerns. The author has demonstrated responsiveness to feedback by implementing reviewer suggestions while defending the core goal of making implicit norms explicit. The patches touch two of Git’s most important contributor-facing documents, so the tone and content matter for shaping new contributor expectations.
