This is a **build system** and **platform compatibility** follow-up in the `USE_NSEC` Meson parity thread. Jeff King (Peff) replies to D. Ben Knoble’s patch, which adds a `nanosec` Meson option to mirror the Autotools `USE_NSEC` knob. Peff’s review is **substantive**: he engages with the *purpose* and *trade-offs* of `USE_NSEC` itself, not just the patch mechanics.

Peff endorses the patch’s goal (Meson/Autotools parity) but raises a **technical caution** about `USE_NSEC`’s real-world reliability. He quotes `Documentation/technical/racy-git.adoc`, which explains that enabling sub-second timestamp tracking can cause "meaningless changes" when in-core timestamps (nanosecond resolution) differ from on-disk timestamps (often coarser, especially on network filesystems like CEPH, CIFS, NTFS, and UDF). The issue was partially fixed in Linux 2.6.11 (2005), but Peff notes that some filesystems may still be affected. He suggests that a deeper investigation—platform-by-platform—could justify flipping the default in `config.make.uname`, but that’s beyond the current patch’s scope.

The review is **not a blocker**: Peff explicitly says the patch is reasonable and outside the scope of his concern. However, his feedback surfaces a **long-standing technical debt** around `USE_NSEC` that might resurface in future discussions about build defaults or filesystem compatibility. The thread’s status remains unchanged: the patch is queued for merging, but Peff’s comments add context for anyone considering enabling the option.
