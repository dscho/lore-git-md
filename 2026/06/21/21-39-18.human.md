This is a follow-up in the ongoing worktree diff performance thread. Junio C Hamano responds to Jeff King’s earlier patch proposal with a technical suggestion and a question about Git’s existing behavior.

Junio acknowledges the core issue—`ie_match_stat()` hides whether a "no change" result came from a simple stat check or a full content comparison—and suggests an alternative to Peff’s `DIFF_RACY_IS_MODIFIED` flag: adding a new field to the `istate` (index state) structure to track when racy entries were found and auto-refreshed. This would let the diff machinery detect and count racy-but-clean entries without modifying the `ie_match_stat()` interface. He also raises a tangential question: why `cmd_diff()` calls `refresh_index_quietly()` at all when comparing tree objects or commits, since the index shouldn’t matter in those cases.

The message is brief and technical, offering a design alternative rather than a firm position. It doesn’t reject Peff’s patch but suggests a different way to achieve the same goal—persisting index refreshes for racy entries—while keeping the change localized to the index state rather than the diff machinery. The question about `refresh_index_quietly()` hints at a possible cleanup opportunity but isn’t central to the performance discussion. No new performance data or edge cases are introduced.
