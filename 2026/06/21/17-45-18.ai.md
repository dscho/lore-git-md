This is a substantive technical follow-up in the ongoing worktree diff performance thread. Jeff King (Peff) proposes and tests a concrete patch to address the racy-git entry issue he previously identified. The patch modifies `builtin/diff.c` to pass a new flag (`DIFF_RACY_IS_MODIFIED`) to `ie_match_stat()`, instructing it to treat racy entries as stat-dirty. This ensures they are counted by `diffcore_skip_stat_unmatch()`, triggering the index refresh that persists across subsequent `git diff` invocations.

Peff reports mixed results: the patch successfully forces index refreshes (subsequent diffs drop from ~500ms to ~30ms), but the first diff becomes significantly slower (~1800ms vs ~500ms). He hypothesizes the slowdown stems from redundant work—`diffcore_skip_stat_unmatch()`’s content check (`diff_filespec_check_stat_unmatch()`) may be less efficient than `ie_match_stat()`’s (`ce_modified_check_fs()`). While he questions whether further optimization is worthwhile, the patch demonstrates a viable path to fixing the core issue: racy entries now increment the counter, enabling index persistence.

Key technical details:
- **Files touched**: `builtin/diff.c` (flag addition), with implications for `diffcore_skip_stat_unmatch()` and `ie_match_stat()`.
- **New symbol**: `DIFF_RACY_IS_MODIFIED` flag.
- **Behavior change**: Racy entries are now counted as stat-dirty, ensuring index refreshes persist.
- **Tradeoff**: First-diff slowdown (~3.6x) vs. subsequent-diff speedup (~16x).
- **Open question**: Whether the performance regression in the first diff is acceptable or warrants further tuning.

The patch advances the thread by providing a working implementation of Peff’s earlier proposal (option 1: modify `diffcore_skip_stat_unmatch()` to count racy entries). It also surfaces a new performance consideration—redundant content checks—that may influence the final solution. No on-disk format changes are involved.
