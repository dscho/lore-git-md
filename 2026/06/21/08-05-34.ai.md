This is the second patch in the v3 series updating Git’s contributor documentation, now effectively a standalone patch after the `MyFirstContribution.adoc` changes were dropped. It advises contributors to batch patch rerolls by defaulting to no more than one new version of the same series per day, with explicit guidance on signaling substantial rework to avoid wasted reviewer effort.

The patch updates `SubmittingPatches` to codify long-standing but implicit norms around reroll cadence. The key changes are: (1) a rough default of one reroll per day to respect reviewer bandwidth and global time zones, (2) encouragement to polish patches before sending to reduce minor errors, and (3) guidance to signal which parts of a series will become obsolete when substantial rework is needed—replacing the previously contested "substantial rework" and "close to acceptance" exceptions. The text also incorporates Junio C Hamano’s suggestions about timezone fairness and patch polishing, and properly attributes Patrick Steinhardt’s original contribution with a `Helped-by` trailer.

This version resolves all substantive feedback from earlier iterations. The removal of timing-based exceptions in favor of explicit signaling ("mention which parts of the current series will become obsolete") directly addresses Junio’s critique while preserving the core goal of reducing reviewer churn. The patch is now uncontested and ready for merging, modeling the very workflow it documents: responsive engagement with feedback, clear attribution, and deliberate pacing. The changes touch one of Git’s most important contributor-facing documents, so the tone and content will shape new contributor expectations around review etiquette.
