This is a **CI/platform-compatibility** discussion with a **substantive review** from Jeff King (Peff) on Michael Montalbo’s proposed fix for macOS CI hangs in `t5551` and `t5559`.

Peff acknowledges the root cause Michael identified—an HTTP/2 flow-control stall during `ls-refs` advertisement of 100,000 loose refs—but expresses skepticism that the proposed fix (packing refs and aligning `$JOBS` with core count) fully resolves the underlying issue. He notes that if the problem is a *racy deadlock*, some CI jobs might still succeed by luck, leaving the risk of future hangs. His concern is technical: the fix may reduce the likelihood of hangs but doesn’t eliminate the possibility of the stall recurring under different conditions.

Peff also provides historical context: Git has encountered HTTP/2 stalls before, often tied to specific versions of `libcurl` and Apache’s `h2_mod`. He references a 2023 commit (`8f2146dbf1`) that made SSL/TLS the default for `t5559` to avoid untested cleartext HTTP/2 code paths. While this earlier fix should have addressed those issues, Peff suggests checking whether the GitHub macOS runners are using different versions of these dependencies than Michael’s local environment. This could explain why the stall self-resolves on bare-metal macOS/Linux but not on GitHub’s virtualized runners.

The review is constructive but cautious. Peff doesn’t reject the patch outright but implies that further investigation into the HTTP/2 stack (versions, configurations) might be needed to ensure a robust fix. For a busy reader, this signals that while the patch is a step forward, it may not be the final word on the macOS CI reliability problem.
