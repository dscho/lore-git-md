This is a documentation patch that updates Git’s contributor guidelines to advise batching patch rerolls. It is the second and final patch in v3 of a series that has now converged on a single change to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`.

The patch codifies a rough default of no more than one reroll per day for the same patch series. The guidance is motivated by three practical concerns: giving reviewers across time zones a fair chance to participate, allowing authors to batch feedback and polish their work, and discouraging rapid-fire updates that force reviewers to re-read code that may soon be obsolete. The text explicitly connects the cadence recommendation to patch quality—waiting encourages authors to catch small mistakes themselves, freeing reviewers to focus on deeper issues.

In response to earlier feedback from Junio C Hamano, the patch replaces timing-based exceptions (“substantial rework” or “close to acceptance”) with a more actionable suggestion: if a reroll will invalidate large parts of the current series, the author should signal which parts will become obsolete so reviewers can avoid wasting time on them. This change removes ambiguity while preserving the core principle that rerolls should be deliberate and considerate of reviewer bandwidth.

The patch also incorporates Junio’s suggestions about timezone fairness and patch polishing, and properly attributes Patrick Steinhardt’s original contribution with a `Helped-by` trailer. The `MyFirstContribution.adoc` changes from earlier versions have been dropped due to redundancy, leaving this as a focused update to the project’s central contributor document.

The guidance is now uncontested and ready for Junio to pick up. It models the very workflow it documents—substantive discussion before rerolling, responsiveness to feedback, and proper attribution—making it a well-crafted addition to Git’s documentation.
