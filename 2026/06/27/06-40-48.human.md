This is a review comment on Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC v2 patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. The reviewer, SZEDER Gábor, raises a practical concern about the trade-offs of the reorganization, arguing that the benefits may not justify the disruption.

SZEDER Gábor acknowledges the stated goals—improving discoverability and clarifying the structure of `libgit.a`—but questions whether the change is worth the cost. His primary objection is the impact on everyday workflows: tools like `` `git log` `` will now stop at the rename barrier unless `` `--follow` `` is used, which he describes as an annoyance that replaces one problem (cluttered root directory) with another (hindered navigation of file history). He frames this as a zero-sum trade-off, suggesting the patch merely swaps one inconvenience for another without a clear net gain.

The review is substantive but narrowly focused. It does not engage with the broader technical or architectural merits of the reorganization, nor does it address the build system, CI, or platform compatibility aspects that were central to earlier feedback. Instead, it zeroes in on a specific pain point for developers who rely on Git’s history-tracing tools. This perspective is worth noting, as it highlights a real-world friction that could affect contributors beyond the initial transition period.

Thread context confirms that all prior technical objections have been addressed, and the series is otherwise ready to proceed. However, SZEDER Gábor’s comment introduces a new dimension to the debate: whether the long-term structural benefits outweigh the immediate usability costs for developers. His skepticism aligns with Junio C Hamano’s earlier concerns about disruption to in-flight work, though it comes from a different angle. The discussion now hinges on whether the project is willing to accept short-term inconvenience for the sake of long-term clarity—or if the tooling limitations (like `` `--follow` ``) are seen as too high a price.
