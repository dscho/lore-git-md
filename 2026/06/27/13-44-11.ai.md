This is the third patch in the `git replay --linearize` series (v5), focusing on the core `--linearize` implementation. Phillip Wood joins the thread to clarify the behavioral difference between `git replay --linearize` and `git rebase --no-rebase-merges` when flattening a history with a divergent merge (e.g., `A->X` and `A->Y` merging at `Z`). Where rebase rewrites both branches and drops the merge (producing `A->X'->Y'` or `A->Y'->X'`), replay drops the merge and keeps only one branch (e.g., `A->X'` with `Y'` lost, or vice versa), leaving the other branch dangling. Phillip confirms Junio’s analysis: rebase uses `git rev-list --reverse --no-merges` to cherry-pick commits sequentially onto the previous one, while replay cherry-picks each commit onto its rewritten parent, preserving the original topology except for merges.

The discussion does not dispute the correctness of the implementation but highlights a user-expectation mismatch. Junio’s proposed test (included in the email) creates a true divergent merge (`base->X` and `base->Y` merging at `Z`) and verifies that only one of `X` or `Y` survives after `--linearize`, locking in the current semantics. The test is not yet part of the patch but is likely to be incorporated in the next iteration. Phillip’s reply is substantive, engaging with the algorithmic difference and reinforcing the need for documentation and test coverage of this edge case. The patch itself remains technically complete, addressing all prior feedback, but this new discussion may delay merging until the behavioral clarification is resolved.
