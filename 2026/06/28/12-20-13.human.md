This is a substantive review reply from Johannes Schindelin in the `git replay --linearize` thread. The v5 series, which adds a `--linearize` option to flatten merge commits, is now under renewed scrutiny after a bug fix in the `--onto` handling for multiple branches inadvertently broke the expected behavior for single-branch linearization.

Johannes identifies a regression: when replaying a single branch containing a merge commit (e.g., `master~2..master`) with `--linearize --onto master~2`, the current implementation drops the first replayed commit entirely, producing a shorter history than expected. He traces this to the removal of the `replayed_base` logic, which was originally introduced to prevent exactly this kind of commit-dropping. His example shows the before-and-after topology clearly: the old version preserved both commits in sequence, while the new version drops one.

Beyond the bug, he raises a deeper design question about how `--linearize` should handle multiple branches. He argues that the only consistent approach is to linearize *all* replayed commits into a single sequence, even when multiple branches are involved, because partial linearization (e.g., keeping some branches separate) leads to ambiguous or surprising outcomes when those branches later merge. His example of three branches (two distinct, one a merge of the first two) illustrates the problem: any attempt to keep the rewritten commits of the two distinct branches non-reachable from each other would break down when the merge branch is replayed.

The review is substantive and tested: Johannes built the code, ran the scenario, and provided concrete commit topologies. His feedback suggests the series may need another iteration to restore the `replayed_base` logic while still fixing the original `--onto` bug. The thread remains open, with the interface design and edge-case behavior still under discussion.
