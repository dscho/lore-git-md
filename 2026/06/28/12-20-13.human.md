This is a substantive review from Johannes Schindelin of Toon Claes’s recently merged `git replay --linearize` series. The series was already merged to `master`, but Johannes has identified a **regression in the v5 implementation**: when replaying a single branch containing a merge commit, the new code drops the first-parent commit entirely, producing a history that omits one side of the merge. This differs from the v4 behavior, which preserved both sides (albeit flattened).

Johannes traces the problem to the removal of the `replayed_base` logic in v5’s base-selection fix. He explains that `replayed_base` was originally introduced to prevent exactly this kind of commit-dropping, and he provides a concrete example using the current `master` branch (Git 2.55-rc2) to show the difference: v4 replayed both “Git 2.55-rc2” and the t4216 fix on top of the merge base, while v5 drops the rc2 commit and grafts only the fix directly onto `--onto`.

Beyond the regression, Johannes raises a deeper design question about how `--linearize` should handle multiple branches. He argues that the only predictable approach is to linearize *all* replayed commits into a single sequence, rather than trying to keep individual branches separate. Any attempt to do otherwise, he suggests, will lead to surprising edge cases when branches share commits or are merged together. This implies that the current v5 behavior (which drops commits) may be a symptom of a larger architectural tension.

The email is a clear call for follow-up work: the regression needs to be fixed, and the design trade-offs around multi-branch replay should be revisited before the feature ships in a release. Johannes’s tone is constructive, but the technical stakes are high—this isn’t a nit, but a behavioral change that could silently rewrite history in unexpected ways.
