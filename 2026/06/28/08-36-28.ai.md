This is a follow-up in the worktree diff performance thread, specifically Jeff King (Peff) responding to Junio C Hamano’s edge-case concern about nanosecond timestamp precision. Peff acknowledges the validity of Junio’s scenario—where kernel inode cache eviction could cause inconsistent nanosecond precision between a file and the index—but argues that the failure mode is benign. If the index’s mtime appears truncated (older than it really is), Git errs on the side of caution by treating the file as potentially racy, which is the correct behavior. However, he concedes that the reverse scenario (a file’s mtime appearing truncated while the index retains nanosecond precision) could theoretically occur, though it is improbable in practice.

Peff’s key insight is that the comparison involves *stored* stat information from the index, not a fresh `stat()` call, which limits the window for cache eviction to the same second as the index write. This narrows the edge case to a highly specific sequence: a tracked file is written, its inode is evicted, and it is re-read from disk—all within the same second the index is written—while the index’s inode remains cached. While possible, he considers this unlikely enough to justify making `USE_NSEC` unconditional on Linux, where he suspects the behavior is now reliable. He references a separate discussion with Patrick Steinhardt about platform-specific safety but does not elaborate here.

The email does not propose a revised patch or testing plan but signals Peff’s leaning toward proceeding with the unconditional `USE_NSEC` change, at least for Linux. The tone suggests he views the edge case as a theoretical concern rather than a practical blocker, though he stops short of outright dismissing it. This reply advances the thread by clarifying the failure mode’s directionality and narrowing its scope, but it leaves the door open for further discussion about platform-specific validation or alternative approaches.
