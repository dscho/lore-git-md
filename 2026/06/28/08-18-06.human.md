This is a **build system** and **platform compatibility** follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the `USE_NSEC` build knob. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s skepticism about whether the `nanosec` Meson option should exist at all, offering empirical evidence that the underlying reliability concerns may no longer apply on modern Linux systems.

Peff tested sub-second timestamp persistence on several filesystems (ext4, ext2, and vfat) by comparing timestamps before and after dropping the kernel cache. On all three, the nanosecond values remained consistent, suggesting that `USE_NSEC` is no longer subtly broken on these platforms. His conclusion is tentative but optimistic: the "racy Git" issues that originally motivated caution around `USE_NSEC` may be a non-issue on modern Linux, at least for common local filesystems. He does not address network filesystems (CEPH, CIFS, etc.) or non-Linux platforms, leaving those as potential edge cases.

The exchange shifts the thread’s tone from skepticism to cautious reassurance. Peff’s findings weaken the argument for removing the knob entirely, but the discussion remains open-ended. No new patch or deprecation proposal has been submitted, and the original Meson parity patch is still queued for merging. The key takeaway for readers: if you care about `USE_NSEC`, Peff’s test method (`touch` + `ls --full-time` + `drop_caches`) is a practical way to check your own filesystem’s behavior. For now, the knob’s future hinges on whether others can reproduce issues on less common setups.
