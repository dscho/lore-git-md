This is the final review sign-off for a **performance optimization and hardening series** in Git’s commit-reach subsystem. Derrick Stolee confirms that **version 4 of the series is ready to merge**, following the resolution of a critical regression in patch 7/8 and the incorporation of all prior feedback.

The series optimizes `paint_down_to_common()`—the core function behind `git merge-base`—by terminating early when one side of a merge-base query exhausts its commit queue. This eliminates unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories), delivering **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric queries. The optimization is now **correct, well-documented, and thoroughly tested**, with deterministic step-count assertions covering edge cases (self, duplicate, pending-stale, infinity-both-sides), diamond topologies, mixed finite/INFINITY generation numbers, and all commit-graph modes.

Key improvements since v3 include:
- **Fixing the regression** in patch 7/8 by restoring the `min_generation` guard to the generation-monotonicity BUG assertion.
- **Renaming counters** (`parent1_count`, `parent2_count`, `mb_candidate_count`) and switching their types to `size_t` for clarity and consistency.
- **Consolidating termination conditions** into `paint_queue_get()`, including the `min_generation` cutoff and `last_gen` monotonicity assertion.
- **Adding a new technical document** (`Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc`) explaining the algorithm, use cases, and safety guarantees.
- **Expanding test coverage** with four new edge-case scenarios and trace2 instrumentation for self-verifying performance.

The series is **low-risk and uncontroversial**, touching only the commit-reach subsystem with no on-disk format changes. Stolee’s unqualified approval ("good to go") follows earlier endorsements from René Scharfe and Elijah Newren, and the test suite robustly verifies correctness across all commit-graph modes. The benchmarks—such as a **3.67s → 5ms** reduction for a 2.6M-commit monorepo—demonstrate the optimization’s impact.
