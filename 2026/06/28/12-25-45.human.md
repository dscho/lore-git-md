This is the **core optimization patch** in a well-tested performance series targeting Git’s merge-base calculation. Patch 7/8 of 8 in v4 teaches `paint_down_to_common()` to terminate early when one side of the history is exhausted—no new merge-base candidates can emerge once either the PARENT1 or PARENT2 queue is empty and no pending merge-base candidates remain. The optimization is gated by the finite-generation guard (`generation < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`) to ensure correctness, and this iteration **fixes the regression** introduced in v3 by restoring the `min_generation` guard to the generation-monotonicity BUG assertion.

The patch is **minimal and focused**: it adds the early termination check to `paint_queue_get()`, updates the documentation in `paint-down-to-common.adoc` to describe the new "side-exhaustion" condition, and adjusts test expectations in `t6600-test-reach.sh` to reflect the reduced step counts. The changes are **internal to the commit-reach subsystem** and do not affect the on-disk format or user-visible behavior. Benchmarks show dramatic improvements: for example, `merge-base --all v2.0.0 v2.55.0-rc1` drops from 72,264 to 44,599 steps, and `merge-base --all v2.55.0-rc1 v2.55.0-rc1~5` drops from 110 to 7 steps.

The documentation is thorough and cross-referenced, and the test updates are deterministic. The optimization is **low-risk**—it only fires in the finite-generation region where topological ordering guarantees hold, and the INFINITY gate ensures correctness for commits without commit-graph entries.
