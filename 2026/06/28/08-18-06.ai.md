This is a **build system** and **platform compatibility** follow-up in the `USE_NSEC` Meson parity thread. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s skepticism about the `USE_NSEC` knob by testing its reliability on modern Linux filesystems. His findings suggest the original concerns about coarse timestamp granularity may no longer apply—at least on Linux with ext4, ext2, and even vfat.

Peff describes a simple test using `ls --full-time` and dropping caches to check whether nanosecond timestamps survive a reload from disk. On his ext4 system, the timestamps remain consistent, implying `USE_NSEC` would not trigger spurious "racy Git" mismatches. He extends the test to ext2 (which also preserves nanoseconds) and vfat (which correctly omits them), concluding that modern Linux VFS behavior likely makes `USE_NSEC` safe. This directly contradicts the earlier assumption that the knob is "subtly broken" on network or coarse-grained filesystems.

The email shifts the discussion from "should we expose this option?" to "is the option still problematic?" Peff’s results suggest the original `USE_NSEC` concerns (documented in `Documentation/technical/racy-git.adoc`) may be outdated, but he stops short of proposing a default change or deprecation. The thread now hinges on whether these findings generalize beyond Linux or to other platforms (e.g., Windows, macOS, or older kernels). Key technical details to track: the test methodology, the distinction between filesystem support and VFS caching, and the list of platforms/filesystems where `USE_NSEC` might still fail. No patch changes are proposed yet, but the outcome could influence whether the Meson `nanosec` option remains justified.
