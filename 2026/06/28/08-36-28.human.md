This is a follow-up in the ongoing worktree diff performance thread. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Junio C Hamano’s concern about his proposed patch to unconditionally enable nanosecond-aware timestamp comparisons in `is_racy_stat()`. Peff acknowledges the edge case Junio raised—where kernel inode cache eviction could cause inconsistent nanosecond precision—but argues that the risk is minimal and skewed toward safety.

Peff clarifies that the comparison involves the index’s *stored* mtime (from a file written in the same second) against a fresh `fstat()` of the index itself. If the index’s inode loses nanosecond precision due to eviction, the comparison would see the index as older than it actually is, erring on the side of caution by treating the file as potentially racy. While the reverse scenario (file inode evicted but index inode preserved) is theoretically possible, Peff deems it unlikely: it would require the file’s inode to be evicted and re-read within the same second the index is written, while the index inode remains cached.

Despite this, Peff concedes that the edge case is "sufficiently scary" to retain the `USE_NSEC` conditional, at least for now. He suggests that `USE_NSEC` is likely safe on Linux today (referencing a separate discussion with Patrick Steinhardt) but stops short of proposing to remove the guard entirely. The summary leaves the patch’s fate unresolved: the performance benefit is clear, but the correctness trade-off remains under debate. Readers should watch for further testing or platform-specific validation to address Junio’s concern.
