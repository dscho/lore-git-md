This is Toon Claes’s response to Patrick Steinhardt’s **substantive review** of the `--linearize` option in the `git replay` series (v4 3/3). The email engages directly with Patrick’s **architectural concern** about whether the interface should mirror `git rebase`’s full flexibility (three modes) rather than hardcoding only `--linearize` (equivalent to `git rebase --no-rebase-merges`).

Toon acknowledges the question but does not propose a concrete solution. Instead, he **sketches a possible future CLI design** for `git replay` that would accommodate all `git rebase` modes while keeping `--linearize` as a focused addition. His proposal:
- Default behavior: replay merges while preserving topology (aligning with Johannes Schindelin’s merge-replay series).
- `--linearize`: flatten merges (current patch, `git replay`-only).
- `--no-merges`: abort on merge commits (restoring current behavior without `--linearize`).
- `--replay-cousins`: replay all merges, including cousins (equivalent to `git rebase --rebase-merges=rebase-cousins`).

Toon argues these options are mutually exclusive and could be implemented as separate flags (e.g., `--no-merges`) rather than a single `--replay-merges=<mode>` enum, citing usability preferences. He also **downplays the internal data structure debate** (boolean vs. enum in Patch 1/3), calling it a secondary concern: "Code is easy to change; the command-line options should be designed with the future in mind." This sidesteps Patrick’s earlier critique about extensibility but aligns with his own stance that `--linearize` is a reasonable MVP.
