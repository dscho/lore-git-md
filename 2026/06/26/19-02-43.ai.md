This is the **final patch (10/10)** in Taylor Blau’s RFC series to combine `git repack --geometric` and `--cruft`. It wires together the preparatory work from patches 1–9, enabling the two modes to work simultaneously: geometric repacking rolls up reachable packs while a separate cruft pack collects unreachable objects.

The patch teaches `git repack` to relax the `ALL_INTO_ONE` implication of `--cruft` when `--geometric` is also given, allowing the two modes to coexist. It plumbs the new `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable` mode (introduced in patch 8) into `git repack` to ensure only reachable objects from rolled-up packs appear in the geometric pack, leaving unreachable objects for the cruft writer. The cruft writer (`write_cruft_pack()`) is updated to interpret the geometric split, treating packs above the split as reachable (excluded from cruft) and those below as candidates for cruft collection. Promisor packs are handled similarly, with their own split point. The patch also ensures consistency between `git pack-objects` and the MIDX bitmap writer by using the refs snapshot (introduced in patch 9) when available.

Key changes include:
- **CLI behavior**: `--cruft` no longer implies `-a` when combined with `--geometric`.
- **Reachability filtering**: `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable` ensures only reachable objects from rolled-up packs are included in the geometric pack.
- **Cruft pack generation**: The cruft writer now respects the geometric split, excluding packs above the split (treated as reachable) and including those below (candidates for cruft).
- **Refs snapshot**: Used to synchronize reachability between `pack-objects` and the MIDX bitmap writer.
- **Documentation**: Updates `git-repack.adoc` to explain the combined behavior.
- **Tests**: Adds 8 new tests in `t/t7704-repack-cruft.sh` covering basic functionality, loose unreachable objects, pack deletion, cruft accumulation, `--combine-cruft-below-size`, and `--expire-to`.

The patch is well-tested and addresses the core goal of the series, but its complexity—particularly around reachability traversal and pack selection—may attract scrutiny. The timing (submitted during the -rc phase) and Taylor’s upcoming role change suggest this may require additional iterations before merging. The changes are confined to the repack and cruft pack subsystems, with no cross-cutting impact on other areas. The series as a whole is a significant step toward streamlining maintenance workflows for large repositories.
