This thread is about a patch series (v9) by Tian Yuchen that continues Git's libification effort by moving nine global configuration variables into a repository-specific structure, `struct repo_config_values`. The series builds on recently merged work that migrated `excludes_file` and aims to eliminate globals, centralize memory management, and prepare the codebase for further modularization.

The series is under active review and has seen significant progress in its latest iteration. A memory leak regression in `core_pager_config()`—flagged by Junio C Hamano in v8—has been fixed in v9, ensuring consistent cleanup of heap-allocated strings across all migrated variables. The series is technically complete, CI-clean, and memory-leak-free, with no user-visible behavior changes. Key participants include Tian (the author), Junio (the maintainer), and Pablo Sabater, whose substantive review of the destructor design in patch 1/9 led to a major resolution in the latest email.

The most notable recent development is Tian’s acceptance of Pablo’s proposal to redesign `repo_config_values_clear()` to directly access the private field `repo->config_values_private_`. This change resolves three long-standing issues: the signature mismatch between `_clear()` and `_init()`, the silent leak risk for submodules, and initialization-order crashes during error paths. The resolution marks a philosophical shift from fail-fast behavior to robustness, and the upcoming v10 reroll is expected to adopt this approach, simplifying memory management and removing a `NEEDSWORK` comment about submodule support. The series is now poised to move closer to `next`, though some minor performance nits (redundant function calls in `repo_excludes_file()` and `git_editor()`) and a temporary enum placement workaround for `push_default` remain as loose ends.

While the series has addressed its most critical blocking issue (the memory leak), two RFC questions raised by Tian remain unanswered: whether migrating program paths (editor, pager, askpass) is worthwhile, and how this work relates to prior discussions about `git_branch_track`. The author argues for the value of per-repository program paths, but no reviewer feedback has engaged with this point yet. The broader libification effort continues, with this series serving as a foundational step for future modularization.
