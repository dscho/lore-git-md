This is a **feature RFC** from Taylor Blau, proposing to combine `git repack --geometric` and `--cruft` modes, which are currently mutually exclusive. The series is part of the ongoing repack machinery overhaul and is motivated by the desire to allow repositories to maintain geometric progression of reachable packs while simultaneously collecting unreachable objects into cruft packs.

The series is structured in three phases: first, it prepares the cruft pack machinery by making non-kept pack exclusion unconditional and extracting a helper for pack lookup. Next, it routes geometric pack deletion through the common `existing_packs` machinery, marking packs above the geometric split as retained and teaching the MIDX retention logic to avoid keeping packs slated for rollup. Finally, it extends `git pack-objects` with a new `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable` mode, which walks from reference tips to include only reachable objects from selected packs while respecting excluded packs, and wires everything together in `git repack`.

Key technical details include the introduction of `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable` and its integration with `--refs-snapshot` to ensure consistency between `pack-objects` and the MIDX bitmap writer. The final patch teaches the cruft writer to interpret the geometric split when deciding which packs to include or exclude. The series touches `builtin/pack-objects.c`, `builtin/repack.c`, `repack-cruft.c`, `repack-geometry.c`, and adds new tests in `t/t5331-pack-objects-stdin.sh` and `t/t7704-repack-cruft.sh`.

Taylor notes this is an RFC due to limited review and testing time (sent during the -rc phase) and his upcoming role change at the end of June, though he plans to continue contributing to Git. The series is well-motivated and appears to address a real workflow limitation, but its complexity and the timing of its submission suggest it may require significant discussion before merging. The changes are confined to the repack and pack-objects subsystems, with no cross-cutting impact on other areas.
