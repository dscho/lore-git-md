This is the final patch in a well-tested performance series that optimizes Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function—a key part of merge-base calculation. The patch consolidates termination conditions by moving the `min_generation` check into `paint_queue_get()`, alongside existing stale-entry and side-exhaustion checks. It also widens the generation-monotonicity BUG assertion to fire unconditionally, enforcing correct generation ordering for all callers.

The change is structural: `last_gen` is now stored in `struct paint_state`, ensuring `commit_graph_generation()` is called exactly once per dequeued commit. The loop in `paint_down_to_common()` now reads `state.last_gen` instead of recomputing the generation, and the `min_generation` check is performed immediately after dequeuing. This simplifies the main loop and ensures the `last_gen` assertion is shared across all termination checks. The "pop first" form of `paint_queue_get()` is preserved, keeping the optimization commit’s diff minimal.

He initially questions whether `last_gen` belongs in `struct paint_state` (since the generation can be read from the commit), but concedes the point once he sees it is used for the monotonicity BUG assertion. His only open suggestion is to move one more termination condition—`state.last_gen < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`—into `paint_queue_get()`, but he acknowledges this may not be possible without changing loop semantics. The patch is otherwise ready: it is a low-risk refactoring that consolidates existing logic, improves code clarity, and enforces invariants more strictly.
