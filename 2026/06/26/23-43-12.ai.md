This is a **CI/platform-compatibility** discussion follow-up in the ongoing thread about macOS CI hangs in `t5551` and `t5559`. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to increase Apache’s `Timeout` directive as a mitigation for the HTTP/2 stalls and `curl 18` mid-transfer aborts caused by Apache bug 70131. Peff endorses the timeout adjustment as a pragmatic solution, clarifying that the issue stems from two related but distinct problems: (1) test failures due to timeouts in `t5551` (HTTP/1.1) and (2) indefinite hangs in `t5559` (HTTP/2). Both are triggered by the same root cause—Apache’s 300-second timeout being hit during slow `ls-refs` advertisements—but manifest differently depending on the protocol.

Peff’s email provides concrete timing data from local testing, showing that a cold-cache `upload-pack` advertisement of 100,000 loose refs takes nearly 10 seconds on his SSD-equipped machine, while a warm-cache or packed-ref scenario completes in under 0.5 seconds. These measurements underscore why CI runners—often under heavy I/O and memory pressure—consistently hit the timeout. He suggests bumping Apache’s `Timeout` to 600 seconds as a "fine mitigation," arguing that while it doesn’t solve the underlying problem, it reduces the likelihood of failures to near-zero in practice. Peff also revisits Michael Montalbo’s earlier proposal to pack refs, now endorsing it as a principled optimization that could further reduce timeout risk while improving test efficiency.

The email advances the thread by solidifying consensus around the timeout adjustment as the primary fix, with ref-packing as a complementary optimization. Peff’s tone is pragmatic, dismissing concerns about papering over real-world issues by noting that servers handling such large ref advertisements would likely need broader optimizations (e.g., better hardware or ref management). No new patches are proposed here, but the discussion now centers on finalizing the timeout value and integrating both changes into the test environment. Key technical details include the `t/lib-httpd.sh` file (for Apache configuration), `t5551-protocol-v2.sh` (for ref-packing logic), and the distinction between HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2 failure modes. The email also implicitly addresses the earlier debate about client-side mitigations, reinforcing the project’s preference for addressing flakes at the source.
