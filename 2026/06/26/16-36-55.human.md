This is a **regression report** from Junio C Hamano, the Git maintainer. The v3 patch series (specifically patch 7/8 or 8/8) introduces a **generation-monotonicity BUG assertion** that fires unconditionally during the `t6600.12 'get_merge_bases_many'` test, causing the test to fail. The failure occurs when the series is applied on the same base as v2, which previously passed all tests.

The BUG message (`bad generation skip 9 > 8 at 772be737f220103f706a39013c0d115009feefec`) indicates that the commit-reach code encountered a generation number that violates the expected monotonic ordering (a commit with generation 9 was processed after one with generation 8). The assertion was widened in v3 to fire unconditionally, not just when `min_generation` is set, but this change **breaks correctness** in cases where the caller does not provide a `min_generation` value.

Junio has **ejected the series from the `seen` integration branch** pending a fix. The regression is **confirmed by the test suite**, which now catches the bug in some commit-graph modes (e.g., "none" or "no-gdat"). The author (Tian Yuchen) and Kristofer Karlsson have acknowledged the mistake and plan to rework the logic to preserve the original behavior while enabling the side-exhaustion optimization. Two approaches are proposed for v4:
1. Revert the problematic change (simpler, but leaves halt conditions partially unified).
2. Fully unify halt conditions while preserving correctness (cleaner, but more complex).

The rest of the series (patches 1-6/8 and 8/8) remains stable and uncontroversial, with Derrick Stolee endorsing the non-broken parts as "in good shape." The regression does not affect the core optimization itself—only the overly aggressive assertion—but it blocks merging until resolved. The test failure demonstrates that the test suite is robustly catching regressions, which is a positive sign for the series' long-term correctness.
