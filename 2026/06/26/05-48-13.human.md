This is the final patch in Toon Claes's three-part series introducing the `--linearize` option to `git replay`. The series is now ready for merging after addressing all feedback from the v4 review cycle.

This patch implements the core `--linearize` functionality, which mimics `git rebase --no-rebase-merges` by flattening merge commits into linear history. When `--linearize` is specified, merge commits are dropped entirely, and only their first-parent lineage is replayed. The patch adds the CLI option, documents it (including its incompatibility with `--revert`), and updates the replay logic in `replay.c` to handle merge commits by mapping them to their first parent in the `replayed_commits` OID map. This ensures that subsequent commits are replayed onto the correct ancestor.

The test coverage is thorough, verifying edge cases like root commit handling, `--advance` combinations, and complex rebase scenarios involving multiple divergent branches. A particularly strong test uses `git range-diff` to confirm that the replayed commits produce identical patches to the originals, demonstrating that the linearization preserves content while altering topology.
