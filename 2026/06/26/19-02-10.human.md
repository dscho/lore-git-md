This is an RFC patch series from Taylor Blau that teaches `git repack` to combine the `--geometric` and `--cruft` options, which are currently mutually exclusive. The series is part of the ongoing repack machinery overhaul and is motivated by Taylor’s upcoming role change (leaving GitHub at the end of June), though he plans to continue contributing to Git in his new position.

The goal is to allow repositories to maintain reachable objects in a geometric progression of packs while simultaneously collecting unreachable objects into cruft packs. For example, `git repack -d --geometric=2 --cruft --combine-cruft-below-size=1G` would keep reachable packs in a geometric progression and combine small cruft packs (plus newly unreachable objects) into a fresh cruft pack. The series is structured in three broad phases: preparing the cruft pack machinery, routing geometric pack deletion through the common `existing_packs` infrastructure, and teaching `pack-objects` the new `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable` mode (which walks from reference tips to include only reachable objects from selected packs).

The series touches core repack logic (`builtin/repack.c`, `repack-geometry.c`, `repack-cruft.c`) and `pack-objects` (`builtin/pack-objects.c`), with new documentation and tests covering the behavior. The changes are non-trivial but well-scoped, addressing a long-standing limitation in repack flexibility. Given the timing (deep in the -rc phase) and Taylor’s note about limited testing, this is explicitly an RFC rather than a polished submission, but the design and implementation look serious and well-motivated. If the approach holds up under review, it could be a significant usability improvement for large repositories.
