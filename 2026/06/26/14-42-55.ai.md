This is the eighth and final patch in the v3 series optimizing Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function. Derrick Stolee’s review focuses on the consolidation of the `min_generation` check into `paint_queue_get()` and the introduction of `last_gen` into `struct paint_state`.

The patch moves the `min_generation` termination condition into `paint_queue_get()`, alongside existing stale-entry and side-exhaustion checks. It also stores `last_gen` in `struct paint_state` to ensure `commit_graph_generation()` is called exactly once per dequeued commit, sharing the result across all termination checks and the generation-monotonicity BUG assertion. The loop in `paint_down_to_common()` now reads `state.last_gen` instead of recomputing the generation, and the `min_generation` check is performed immediately after dequeuing, before other conditions.

Stolee’s review is **substantive and constructive**, though not critical. He initially questions whether `last_gen` belongs in `struct paint_state` but quickly resolves his concern after seeing its use in the generation-monotonicity check. He confirms the patch correctly reuses `generation` for the side-exhaustion and finite-generation guard checks, avoiding redundant calls to `commit_graph_generation()`. His only suggestion is to consider moving the finite-generation guard (`state.last_gen < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`) into `paint_queue_get()` as well, but he acknowledges this may not be feasible due to the loop’s control flow. The suggestion is **low-weight** and does not block merging; it could be addressed in a follow-up if desired.

The patch is **stable, well-tested, and uncontroversial**. It consolidates existing logic, improves code clarity, and enforces invariants more strictly. The test suite (including deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes) already validates the behavior, and the benchmarks from earlier patches demonstrate the optimization’s impact. The series is **ready for merging**, with the only open question being whether to further unify termination conditions in a follow-up.
