This is the **final patch in Taylor Blau’s RFC series** that enables `git repack` to combine `--geometric` and `--cruft` modes. The patch wires together the preparatory work from earlier patches (refactoring, `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable`, and `--refs-snapshot`) to deliver the core feature: geometric repacks that roll up reachable objects while simultaneously collecting unreachable ones into a separate cruft pack.

The implementation teaches `git repack` to relax the `ALL_INTO_ONE` implication of `--cruft` when `--geometric` is also given. Instead, it uses `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable` to ensure only reachable objects from the rolled-up packs (and any reachable loose objects) appear in the geometric pack, leaving unreachable objects for the cruft writer. The cruft writer is updated to interpret the geometric split, so it knows which non-kept packs are below the split (excluded, so their unreachable objects are candidates for the cruft pack) versus above the split (included, so they are treated as reachable). Promisor packs are handled separately, since they have their own split point. When a refs snapshot is available, it is passed to both `pack-objects` and the MIDX bitmap writer to ensure reachability consistency.

The patch touches `builtin/repack.c`, `repack-cruft.c`, and `repack.h`, and adds **251 lines of new tests** in `t/t7704-repack-cruft.sh` covering basic functionality, loose unreachable objects, pack deletion, cruft accumulation, `--combine-cruft-below-size`, and `--expire-to`. The tests are thorough and appear to exercise the main edge cases, including promisor packs and snapshot consistency.

This is a **well-motivated feature** that addresses a real workflow limitation: users who want both geometric repacks and cruft packs currently have to run two separate commands. The implementation is careful to avoid regressing existing behavior, and the use of `--stdin-packs=follow-reachable` and `--refs-snapshot` suggests attention to correctness. However, the complexity of integrating geometric and cruft logic — particularly around reachability traversal, pack selection, and snapshot consistency — means this will likely require **substantive review and testing** before merging.
