This is a **CI/platform-compatibility** discussion in the ongoing thread about macOS CI hangs in `t5551` and `t5559`. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to increase Apache’s `Timeout` directive as a fix for the HTTP/2 stalls and `curl 18` mid-transfer aborts caused by Apache bug 70131.

Peff clarifies that the issue stems from two related problems: (1) the EXPENSIVE tests in `t5551` and `t5559` sometimes trigger Apache’s default 60-second timeout, causing test failures, and (2) when this timeout occurs in HTTP/2 mode, it results in an indefinite hang that forces CI jobs to time out after six hours. He agrees that increasing Apache’s timeout (e.g., to 600 seconds) would address the root cause and mitigate both failure modes. While he acknowledges this is not a perfect solution—real-world users with slow servers might still encounter timeouts—he considers it a pragmatic fix, given that the test environment is intentionally underpowered and the default timeout is unrealistically low for such a large request.

Peff also revisits Michael Montalbo’s earlier suggestion to pack the 100,000 loose refs, which he initially viewed as a partial fix. After benchmarking, he now sees value in packing the refs as a general efficiency improvement, noting that cold-cache performance drops from ~10 seconds to ~0.15 seconds when refs are packed. While this doesn’t eliminate the timeout risk entirely, it reduces the likelihood of hitting it and speeds up the tests overall. He suggests combining both approaches: packing the refs for efficiency and increasing Apache’s timeout to 600 seconds as a safety net.

The email reinforces the consensus against client-side workarounds and retry logic, aligning with the project’s preference for addressing flakes at their source. Peff’s benchmarks provide concrete evidence that the proposed changes are reasonable, and his tone suggests the patch is likely to be well-received if it implements these adjustments cleanly. The discussion is now focused on finalizing the timeout value and ensuring the fix is robust across both HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2.
