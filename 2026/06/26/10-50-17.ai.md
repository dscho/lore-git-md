This is a **CI/platform-compatibility** discussion follow-up in the ongoing thread about macOS CI hangs in `t5551` and `t5559`. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King’s (Peff) skepticism about the proposed client-side mitigation for Apache’s `mod_http2` bug (70131), which causes HTTP/2 stalls or `curl 18` mid-transfer aborts during `ls-refs` advertisements of 100,000 loose refs.

Patrick clarifies that the Apache bug affects both HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2, meaning an upstream fix alone would not fully resolve the flakiness. He aligns with Peff’s preference for addressing flakes at the source rather than introducing retry logic or client-side mitigations, which he sees as masking underlying issues. However, he proposes a new potential solution: **increasing Apache’s timeout value** to reduce the likelihood of hitting the problematic state. This approach avoids the downsides of client-side workarounds (e.g., spurious failures) while directly targeting the root cause—Apache’s 300-second timeout being triggered by slow or busy CI runners. Patrick suggests this as a low-effort, Git-side fix that could be tested quickly, though he acknowledges the ultimate resolution may still depend on an upstream Apache patch.

The email advances the discussion by narrowing the focus to timeout adjustments as a pragmatic middle ground between waiting for upstream fixes and accepting flaky tests. No new code changes are proposed yet, but the suggestion shifts the thread toward evaluating the feasibility and side effects of modifying Apache’s `Timeout` directive in Git’s test environment. The tone remains collaborative, with Patrick deferring to Peff’s and the maintainers’ judgment on the best path forward.
