This is a **performance instrumentation patch** in the commit-reach subsystem, part of the ongoing `paint_down_to_common()` optimization series. The patch adds **trace2 step-counting** to `paint_down_to_common()` so that the number of commits visited during the paint walk becomes observable via `GIT_TRACE2_EVENT`. This enables precise measurement of optimization impact without relying solely on wall-clock benchmarks.

The change is minimal and focused: it increments a `steps` counter for each commit processed and emits the total via `trace2_data_intmax()` at the end of the walk. The patch also extends the test infrastructure to **assert deterministic step counts** across all commit-graph modes (none, full, half, no-gdat). The new `test_paint_down_steps()` helper checks that the step count matches expectations for each mode, and `run_all_modes()` now captures trace2 output per mode for self-verifying performance.

The test suite is updated to include step-count assertions for existing edge-case tests (self, pending-stale, infinity-both-sides, mixed-finite-infinity) and adds a new test (`merge-base --all commit-walk steps`) that verifies step counts for a large topology (81 steps in git.git). The step counts are **mode-dependent**: for example, the "self" test expects 45 steps in "none" mode but only 2 in "full" mode, reflecting the commit-graph’s impact on traversal efficiency.

This patch is **low-risk and uncontroversial**. The instrumentation is purely additive and does not alter the algorithm’s behavior. The test updates are **self-contained** and do not affect existing functionality. The step-count assertions provide **strong regression protection** for future optimizations, aligning with Derrick Stolee’s preference for broad instrumentation. The patch is **ready for review and merging** as part of the v3 series.
