This is a **post-merge follow-up correction** to the v3 series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` in Git’s commit-reach subsystem. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, reports that **patch 7/8 (the core optimization) accidentally broke behavior for some commit-graph modes** due to a logic error in the generation-number check. The rest of the series (patches 1–6 and 8) remains unaffected and is still considered ready.

The issue stems from removing the `min_generation` guard in the generation-monotonicity check, which was intended to ensure the optimization only activates in the finite-generation region (where topological ordering is reliable). Without this guard, the early-exit condition could trigger incorrectly for commits with `INFINITY` generation numbers, where ordering is based on commit dates and not topologically sound. This regression surfaces in commit-graph modes like "half" or "no-gdat," where some commits lack generation data.

Kristofer plans to rework the logic to restore the original behavior while preserving the optimization’s intent. The fix will likely reintroduce the `min_generation` guard or an equivalent check to gate the early-exit condition. The rest of the series—including edge-case tests, trace2 instrumentation, and documentation—is unaffected and remains valid. Reviewers are encouraged to focus on the other patches while the author prepares a corrected version of patch 7/8.

This is a **low-risk regression** confined to the commit-reach subsystem, with no on-disk format impact. The series’ test suite, which includes deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes, will catch similar issues in future iterations. The author’s transparency about the mistake and prompt response are positive signs of a well-managed follow-up.
