This is a brief but important clarification in the ongoing `git history squash` feature discussion. Phillip Wood corrects a misunderstanding about the proposed input syntax for the new command. Harald Nordgren had assumed that `^:/base..:/tip` would work as a range, but Phillip points out that Git’s revision parser treats everything after the first `/:` as a regex search, not as part of a range. This means the two-argument form (`^:/base :/tip`) is actually necessary to support searching for commits by message content, not just by ref or OID.

The exchange doesn’t introduce new design questions, but it resolves a key usability detail: the command will need to accept two separate arguments (or find another way to support message-based revision selection) if users are to squash ranges identified by commit messages. This is a small but real constraint on the interface, and it’s now clear that Harald’s initial assumption was incorrect. The next version of the series will likely need to address this, either by supporting two arguments or by documenting the limitation.
