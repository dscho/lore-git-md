This is Junio C Hamano's review of the final patch in Toon Claes's `--linearize` series for `git replay`. The series is already merged to the 'seen' branch, but Junio raises a subtle behavioral difference between `git replay --linearize` and the traditional flattening rebase (`git rebase --no-rebase-merges`).

The review identifies that when replaying a merge commit (Z) that merges two divergent branches (X and Y), `git replay --linearize` drops the merge and grafts only one of the two branches onto the new history, leaving the other branch dangling. In contrast, a flattening rebase would replay both branches, producing a linear history that incorporates both X and Y (as X' and Y'). Junio illustrates this with a diagram and suggests that the current behavior may be intentional but could surprise users, as it silently discards one side of the merge. He proposes adding a test to document this behavior and wonders if users should have control over which branch survives.

The review includes a concrete test case that would expose the issue: a setup with a base commit, two divergent branches (X and Y), and a merge commit (Z). The test verifies that only one of the two branches (X or Y) appears in the replayed history, confirming the "loss" of the other branch. Junio's patch suggestion is ready to apply and would serve as both a regression test and a documentation of the current behavior.
