This is the third patch in the `git replay --linearize` series, now at v5. Junio raises a behavioral difference between `git replay --linearize` and `git rebase --no-rebase-merges` when flattening a history with a divergent merge: where rebase rewrites both branches and drops the merge, replay drops the merge and keeps only one branch, leaving the other dangling. The example history is `A->X` and `A->Y` merging at `Z`; rebase produces `A->X'->Y'` (or `A->Y'->X'`), while replay produces `A->X'` (with `Y'` lost) or `A->Y'` (with `X'` lost), depending on which branch is replayed first.

Junio’s concern is not a correctness bug but a potential user-expectation mismatch. He suggests documenting the behavior and adding a test to lock in the current semantics. The proposed test creates a true divergent merge (`base->X` and `base->Y` merging at `Z`) and verifies that only one of `X` or `Y` survives after `--linearize`. The patch itself is technically complete and addresses all prior feedback; this new discussion is about behavioral clarity and test coverage.
