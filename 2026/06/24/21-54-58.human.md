This is the fifth iteration of Harald Nordgren’s `git history squash` feature, a new subcommand that folds a commit range into its oldest commit while preserving descendant history. The series is now code-complete and addresses all prior feedback, including the final open question about `--ancestry-path` handling.

The key change in v5 is the adoption of `--ancestry-path` for range walks, ensuring only commits descended from the base are folded. This resolves Patrick Steinhardt’s suggestion from v4 and tightens input validation: single revisions like `HEAD` or `HEAD~1` are now rejected with a clear error message ("not a `<base>..<tip>` range") rather than being treated as a squash down to the root. The implementation remains a single tree operation, avoiding the repeated conflict stops of a rebase-based approach.

Test coverage has expanded significantly, now including complex merge topologies: nested merges, octopus merges, merges with arms forked before the base, and merges among descendants replayed above the range. The ref-handling logic—rejecting operations where refs point to commits inside the squashed range—is thoroughly validated, including edge cases like refs pointing at interior merge commits. The `--update-refs=head` flag (to retarget the current branch) and the configurable advice message are also tested.
