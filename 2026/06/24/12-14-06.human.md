This is a performance optimization series targeting Git’s commit-reach subsystem, specifically the `paint_down_to_common()` function used in merge-base calculations. The series is now in its second version (v2) and is ready to land, with all technical and procedural feedback resolved.

The goal is to terminate the graph walk early when one side of the history is exhausted, avoiding unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). The optimization activates only after the walk enters the finite-generation region, where generation numbers guarantee that paint on visited commits is final. Benchmarks show dramatic speedups—up to 1000x—for merge-base queries in large repositories, with no regressions on smaller ones like `git.git`.

The series is well-structured, with patches ordered to introduce documentation first, followed by tests (including edge cases from Elijah Newren’s parallel work), and then the core optimization. Key changes include:
- A new `paint_state` struct to track per-side commit counters (PARENT1-only, PARENT2-only, or pending merge-base candidates).
- Early termination when one side’s exclusive count drops to zero, gated by the finite-generation guard to ensure correctness.
- Trace2 instrumentation to measure step counts and validate the optimization.
- Removal of unused `nonstale_queue` dedup wrappers.

The commit messages are detailed, providing benchmarks, edge-case analysis, and clear rationale for the changes.
