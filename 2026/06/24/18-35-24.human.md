This is a **substantive review comment** from Junio C Hamano on the second patch in Justin Tobler’s six-patch series converting `git-receive-pack` to use ODB transactions. The series is part of the broader ODB abstraction effort led by Patrick Steinhardt.

Junio’s feedback focuses on a **latent correctness issue** in the patch’s error-handling logic. The patch converts the "files" transaction backend’s `prepare`, `begin`, and `commit` functions from `void` to `int` to propagate previously ignored errors, which is a necessary step for robust transaction management. However, Junio points out that **two existing callers** (`write_loose_object()` and `odb_source_loose_write_stream()`) are not prepared to handle errors from `odb_transaction_files_prepare()`, even though the patch now surfaces them. This means the patch could introduce a regression: if `tmp_objdir_create()` fails, the callers will proceed as if the transaction succeeded, potentially writing objects to the primary ODB instead of a temporary directory. This mirrors a pre-existing bug (the callers already ignored errors from `tmp_objdir_replace_primary_odb()`), but the patch makes the problem more visible without fixing it.

Junio also asks whether an explicit "abort-transaction" mechanism is needed, though he doesn’t insist on an answer in this patch. The question is forward-looking and suggests a potential follow-up task for the ODB transaction subsystem.

The review is **surface-level in scope** (it doesn’t engage with the broader ODB abstraction goals or the series’ architecture) but **substantive in impact**: it identifies a real risk of silent failure that could undermine the patch’s goal of robust error handling. The feedback is **non-blocking** in the sense that it doesn’t require a redesign, but it **does highlight a latent bug** that should be addressed in a follow-up patch or a v2 of this series. Justin’s response will likely determine whether this becomes a blocker for the series’ progress.
