This is the fourth patch in the v2 series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. It adds trace2 instrumentation to expose the number of commits walked during the paint walk, enabling deterministic measurement of optimization impact without relying solely on wall-clock benchmarks.

The patch introduces a step counter in `paint_down_to_common()` and logs it via `trace2_data_intmax()` at the end of the walk. This allows developers to observe the "commits walked" metric through `GIT_TRACE2_PERF` or `GIT_TRACE2_EVENT` logs. The test in `t6600-test-reach.sh` validates the instrumentation by comparing step counts across different commit-graph configurations (none, full, and half), ensuring the metric is consistent and testable. The test reuses the existing `commit-9-9`/`commit-9-1` topology and verifies that the step count matches expectations (e.g., 80 steps with a full commit-graph).

This instrumentation is a prerequisite for the final optimization (patch 7/7), as it provides a way to empirically validate the early-exit logic's effectiveness. The patch is minimal and focused, touching only `commit-reach.c` and the test script, with no behavior changes to Git itself. The commit message clearly states the purpose: enabling observable measurement of future optimizations. The test coverage is thorough, accounting for different commit-graph states, and the trace2 integration is idiomatic (using `trace2_data_intmax` for integer metrics).

The patch is uncontroversial and aligns with the series' goal of making performance improvements measurable. It follows the agreed-upon instrumentation approach from the v1 discussion, where Derrick Stolee recommended trace2 for deterministic validation. The test infrastructure (`test_trace2_data`) is already in place, and the patch leverages it effectively. No open questions remain; this is a straightforward, well-motivated addition.
