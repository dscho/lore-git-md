This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. The thread is now focused on weighing the practical benefits of the reorganization against its disruption to in-flight patches and maintenance workflows.

Patrick acknowledges Junio C Hamano’s concerns about the disruption to ongoing development but offers a contrarian perspective: he argues that Git’s resistance to handling file moves gracefully is itself a usability issue worth addressing. By subjecting Git to the same pain points that other projects face when reorganizing their code, he suggests the project might gain motivation to improve its own tooling—such as better handling of file renames in patches or merges. This framing positions the reorganization not just as a cosmetic cleanup but as a way to identify and fix friction in Git’s development workflow.

He also notes that feedback so far has been more positive than he expected, though he remains open to the community deciding against the change. The tone is collaborative, not defensive, and he doesn’t push back on Junio’s logistical concerns. Instead, he reframes the disruption as an opportunity to improve Git’s tooling for everyone.

This reply doesn’t resolve the debate but shifts it slightly: the discussion is no longer just about whether the reorganization is worth the cost, but whether the cost itself is a symptom of a larger problem that Git should address. The outcome will likely hinge on whether others share Patrick’s view that the pain is worth the potential tooling improvements. For now, the patch remains in limbo, with no clear consensus on timing or necessity.
