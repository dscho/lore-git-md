This is the final patch in the **v2 performance optimization series** for Git’s commit-reach subsystem, implementing the core early-exit logic in `paint_down_to_common()`. The patch teaches the merge-base walk to terminate early when one side’s commit queue is exhausted—no new merge-base candidates can form without both paint sides meeting—yielding **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). The optimization is gated by two conditions: the walk must have entered the finite-generation region (where topological ordering guarantees final paint states), and no pending merge-base candidates may remain. This ensures correctness while eliminating unnecessary traversal.

The patch updates the technical documentation (`Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc`) to describe the new "side-exhaustion" termination condition, explaining why it is safe to exit early and why the finite-generation guard is critical. The implementation itself is concise: the early-exit check is added to `paint_queue_get()`, where it tests `(!state->p1_count || !state->p2_count) && !state->pending_merge_bases` and the dequeued commit’s generation number. The change is validated by trace2 step counts, which show dramatic reductions (e.g., 72,264 → 44,589 steps for `git merge-base --all v2.0.0 v2.55.0-rc1` in git.git).
