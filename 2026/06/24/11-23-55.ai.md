This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC v2 patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. Oswald Buddenhagen weighs in with a brief but substantive review, supporting the reorganization and offering a tactical suggestion about timing.

Oswald argues that the reorganization should be applied *before* the upcoming release (Git 2.55), rather than during the prerelease freeze. His rationale is that the change affects only the build system, which CI already validates thoroughly, so the risk of introducing regressions late in the cycle is low. This directly counters Junio C Hamano’s concern about disrupting backporting and in-flight patches, though Oswald acknowledges that it won’t solve the problem of backporting fixes to older versions or rebasing pending branches—he defers to Patrick’s earlier response on those points.

The review is substantive but concise, focusing on the practical trade-offs of timing rather than the technical details of the patch itself. Oswald explicitly endorses the change, calling the current source tree "messy and confusing," which aligns with Patrick’s goal of improving navigability. His feedback adds weight to the argument for merging the series sooner rather than later, though it doesn’t address Junio’s broader skepticism about whether the benefits justify the disruption. The tone is pragmatic, framing the reorganization as a low-risk, high-reward cleanup.
