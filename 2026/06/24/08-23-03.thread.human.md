This thread is about a 12-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that hardens Git’s reftable backend against security vulnerabilities triggered by maliciously corrupted files. The series, now at version 2, addresses a range of issues—out-of-bounds reads/writes, NULL pointer dereferences, uninitialized memory usage, and calls to `abort()`—discovered through fuzzing with libFuzzer. While reftable files aren’t stored remotely (exploitation requires local disk access), the fixes are comprehensive and include new fuzzing infrastructure to prevent regressions.

The series is split into three parts: fuzzing infrastructure (patches 1–2), a test helper to reduce boilerplate (patch 5), and the security fixes themselves (patches 3–4 and 6–12). All fixes are now complete, each accompanied by unit tests, and the fuzzer runs for over two hours without surfacing new issues. The only feedback so far came from Christian Couder, who suggested a surface-level test refactoring in version 1; Patrick addressed this in version 2 by adding the `cl_reftable_write_block` helper. Junio C Hamano recently acknowledged this improvement, thanking both Patrick and Christian for the cleaner test code, but his reply didn’t engage with the core security fixes or signal readiness to merge.

The series touches the reftable internals, build system, and test suite, introducing no user-visible changes—just safer handling of corrupted files. The fuzzing infrastructure is now integrated into Git’s CI and may be reused for other subsystems. With no open technical questions or objections, the series appears ready for deeper review, though no maintainer has yet weighed in on the security fixes themselves.
