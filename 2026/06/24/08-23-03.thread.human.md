This thread is a security-hardening patch series for Git’s reftable backend, now at version 3 with all 12 patches submitted. The series systematically addresses vulnerabilities in the reftable parser that could be triggered by maliciously corrupted files, including out-of-bounds reads/writes, NULL pointer dereferences, and calls to `abort()`. While reftable files are not stored remotely, the hardening is comprehensive and includes new fuzzing infrastructure to prevent regressions.

The latest patch (8/12) fixes an out-of-bounds read when a reftable block contains a bogus restart count. The fix adds a bounds check in `reftable_block_init()` to validate the restart offset, replacing a crash with a graceful error return. This follows the pattern of earlier patches in the series, which have all been technically complete and received substantive review. Junio C Hamano has already approved two of the fixes (patches 6 and 7) as "obviously correct," and the rest have addressed all prior feedback, including test improvements suggested by Toon Claes and Christian Couder. The series is now fully integrated into Git’s build system and CI, with no remaining technical blockers.

Key participants include Patrick Steinhardt (author), who has driven the series through three iterations, and reviewers Junio C Hamano, Christian Couder, and Toon Claes, whose feedback has been fully addressed. The series is part of the broader reftable backend effort and introduces reusable fuzzing infrastructure that may benefit other subsystems. No user-visible changes are introduced—only safer handling of corrupted input.
