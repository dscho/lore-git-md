Patrick Steinhardt has sent an 11-patch series hardening Git’s reftable backend against security vulnerabilities triggered by maliciously corrupted files. The series is split into two parts: the first two patches add fuzzing infrastructure (including this one, which introduces a libFuzzer-based fuzzer for reftable parsing), and the remaining nine patches fix the vulnerabilities discovered by the fuzzer. The fixes are comprehensive, addressing out-of-bounds reads/writes, NULL pointer dereferences, and uninitialized memory usage, and are accompanied by new unit tests. The fuzzer now runs for over two hours without finding new issues, suggesting the fixes are effective.

So far, the series is uncontroversial and appears ready for review. The fuzzing infrastructure is designed to be reusable for other subsystems, though no immediate follow-up work is tied to this series. The changes are internal-only, with no user-visible impact or on-disk format changes. The series is part of the ongoing reftable backend effort led by Patrick and Karthik Nayak. No reviews or objections have been recorded yet.
