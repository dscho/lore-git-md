Here’s the updated human-mode summary for the thread:

Patrick Steinhardt’s twelve-patch series hardens Git’s reftable backend against security vulnerabilities in corrupted files, discovered through fuzzing with libFuzzer. The fixes address crashes, infinite loops, and memory corruption in the reftable parser, though exploitation requires local disk access. The series is now at v2, with all feedback addressed except for two narrowly scoped test improvements proposed by Toon Claes.

The patches fall into three groups: fuzzing infrastructure (patches 1–2), a test helper to reduce boilerplate (patch 5), and individual security fixes (patches 3–4, 6–12). Junio C Hamano and Christian Couder have both acknowledged the test helper, which simplifies unit tests for block corruption scenarios. Toon Claes has provided substantive feedback on two test cases—patches 6 and 7—suggesting minor adjustments to ensure the tests reliably trigger the vulnerabilities they’re meant to catch. The fixes themselves remain uncontested, and the series is otherwise ready for merging. The fuzzing infrastructure introduced here may also be reused for other subsystems in the future.
