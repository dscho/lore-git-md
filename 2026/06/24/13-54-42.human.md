This is a **substantive review** of patch 5/7 in the now-merged series optimizing `paint_down_to_common()` for one-sided histories. The reviewer, Derrick Stolee, engages deeply with the code’s structure and future implications, offering both praise and forward-looking suggestions.

The patch introduces a `paint_state` struct to track per-side commit counters (PARENT1-only, PARENT2-only, and pending merge-base candidates), replacing the old `nonstale_queue` logic with a counter-based approach. Stolee’s review is overwhelmingly positive: he explicitly appreciates the real-time documentation updates and the clean use of a `delta` parameter in `paint_count_update()`. His feedback is technical but constructive, focusing on two main areas.

First, he suggests adding a **doc-comment to `paint_queue_get()`** to make the termination condition more explicit. This is a minor readability improvement, not a functional critique, and aligns with the patch’s goal of clarifying the codebase.

Second, and more significantly, he proposes **incorporating `min_generation` into the `paint_state` struct** as a termination condition in `paint_queue_get()`. This would unify all termination logic in one place and could simplify the final optimization (patch 7/7). Stolee recommends doing this in a **separate, intermediate patch**—before the one-sided early-exit logic—so that the existing trace2-based tests (which validate step counts) can verify the behavior remains unchanged. His rationale is pragmatic: the tests already cover `min_generation` cases, and this refactoring would make the code more maintainable without risking regressions.

The review is **tested in spirit**: Stolee references the trace2 instrumentation and test coverage to justify his suggestions, showing he has engaged with the series’ validation strategy. His tone is collaborative, framing the proposals as opportunities to strengthen the code rather than as blockers. While the series is already merged, his feedback is worth noting for anyone maintaining or extending this subsystem.
