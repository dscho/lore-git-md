Performance optimization discussion for `git ls-files` pathspec filtering.

Jeff King raises a thoughtful question about the arbitrary-seeming cutoff in Tamir Duberstein's optimization patch for `git ls-files --deleted` and `--modified`. The current version only applies the pathspec pre-filtering optimization when there's exactly one pathspec, avoiding the O(n) cost of `match_pathspec()` when there are multiple pathspecs. Peff points out that this creates an artificial boundary - the optimization would likely still be beneficial with 2 or 10 pathspecs, just with diminishing returns.

This is more of a philosophical design question than a technical flaw - the patch works as-is and provides measurable improvements for the single-pathspec case. But Peff is considering the long-term maintainability of having this hard-coded threshold, anticipating future contributors might want to tweak it based on additional profiling data.

The exchange highlights Git's careful approach to performance optimizations - even when a change clearly helps some cases, the project values general solutions over case-specific tweaks when possible. No immediate action is required from the author, but this may influence how similar optimizations are framed in future patches.
