Performance optimization discussion about benchmark reporting style. D. Ben Knoble weighs in on the ongoing conversation about how much detail to include in commit messages, particularly for performance-related changes.

The email responds to Jeff King's (Peff) critique that Tamir Duberstein's v2 patch for `git describe` optimization included overly verbose benchmark details in the commit message. Knoble offers context that such detailed reporting is common in academic papers for reproducibility, but acknowledges Git commit messages may warrant a different standard.

Key points raised:
1. Notes the academic convention of including hardware specs and methodology for benchmark reproducibility
2. Observes this level of detail may not align with Git's commit message norms
3. Suggests the `--command-name` flag used in the benchmarks serves a practical purpose for distinguishing versions
4. Proposes looking at historical examples of performance-related commit messages as guidance

The email doesn't take a strong position but surfaces relevant considerations in the style discussion. It reflects broader project questions about balancing technical completeness with readability as AI-assisted contributions become more common. The underlying optimization itself remains uncontested, with this being purely a documentation style discussion.
