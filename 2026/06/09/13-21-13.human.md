This is a substantive review from Junio Hamano on the `git history reword` behavior discussion. He strongly disagrees with the characterization that the current behavior of `git commit --amend` and `git rebase -i` is "wrong", pushing back against the idea that new commands should unilaterally break from established patterns.

Key points:
- Junio clarifies that while avoiding no-op rewrites might be a good optional behavior, the current always-rewrite approach isn't inherently wrong
- He proposes a more general solution - a `--avoid-unnecessary-rewrite` flag that could work across commands (`history`, `rebase -i`, `commit --amend`) rather than command-specific behaviors
- The review suggests this should be an opt-in feature first, with potential default flip in a future major version if proven better
- Junio emphasizes that consistency across commands is more important than any perceived "correctness" of one approach over another

This review significantly raises the bar for the proposed change, moving the discussion from a simple `--force` flag for one command to a cross-command design question about when and how to avoid no-op history rewrites. Junio's position as maintainer makes this feedback particularly important for the direction of the feature.
