This is a performance optimization review for `git ls-files` pathspec filtering.

Jeff King (Peff) raises a thoughtful question about the patch's approach to optimizing pathspec matching in `git ls-files --deleted` and `--modified`. While acknowledging the optimization is correct for the single-pathspec case, he questions whether the hardcoded `pathspec.nr == 1` condition is too arbitrary, suggesting the same logic could benefit cases with 2 or more pathspecs up to some reasonable limit.

The review doesn't challenge the technical correctness or measured performance improvements (which were substantial in v1), but rather probes the design choice of limiting the optimization to exactly one pathspec. Peff's concern is forward-looking - he anticipates future contributors might want to extend the optimization to slightly larger pathspec counts, implying the current solution might not be the most maintainable approach.

This is a substantive review that engages with the patch's architectural implications rather than surface-level issues. The feedback suggests the optimization could be made more general while still preserving the performance benefits, though the reviewer stops short of demanding changes since the current version is technically sound.
