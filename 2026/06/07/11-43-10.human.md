This is a performance optimization patch for Git's priority queue implementation, now in its third iteration. The patch folds the "lazy_queue" pattern (which defers removals to allow subsequent puts to become simple replaces) into the core prio_queue implementation. This eliminates duplicate implementations in several callers while maintaining 3-6% speedups on traversal-heavy operations like merge-base and rev-list.

The key optimization is deferring the actual removal in prio_queue_get() until the next operation, allowing get+put sequences to fuse into a single replace operation. This matches Git's common commit traversal pattern of getting a commit then putting its parents. The patch also includes safety hardening by renaming internal fields to prevent unsafe direct access.

The changes affect multiple subsystems including commit-reach, fetch-pack, and revision walking, but the impact is well-contained through consistent use of the new access patterns. Benchmark results show consistent 1.7-2.7% improvements on traversal-heavy operations while maintaining neutral performance for code paths that already had manual fusion.

The patch has seen thorough review and benchmarking, with all substantive feedback addressed. René Scharfe provided benchmark validation and suggested inlining the deferred removal logic directly into get()/peek() functions, which was incorporated in this version. With all correctness concerns resolved and performance benefits validated, this optimization appears ready for merging.
