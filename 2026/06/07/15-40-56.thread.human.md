Tamir Duberstein's `git ls-files` optimization, which filters pathspecs early to avoid expensive `lstat` operations, has progressed through thorough technical review. Initial measurements showed dramatic speed improvements (60.7s to 1.06s) for large repositories when most files don't match the pathspec, while follow-up tests confirmed only minimal overhead (14-42ms) in worst-case scenarios where all paths match.

Peff acknowledged this edge case exists but noted it would only occur in already-slow operations that few users run, like `git ls-files -- $(git ls-files)`. His historical perspective on abandoned pathspec optimization attempts lent weight to the conclusion that the real-world benefits for selective pathspec cases far outweigh these theoretical downsides.

With the submodule behavior confirmed unaffected and all significant performance tradeoffs examined, the optimization appears technically sound and ready for maintainer consideration.
