This thread began with a performance optimization for `git ls-files` that dramatically speeds up operations on large repositories when most entries don’t match the given pathspec—cutting runtime from over a minute to just over a second in representative cases. After review from Junio C Hamano and Jeff King (Peff), the patch was refined to avoid a quadratic edge case when most paths *do* match, and now applies the optimization only to single-pathspec cases, preserving the speedup where it matters while keeping worst-case overhead negligible. All technical concerns have been addressed, and the series appears ready for maintainer consideration.

The discussion has since shifted to metadata: Kristoffer Haugsbakk points out that the Linux kernel recently simplified its LLM attribution policy, now requiring only `Assisted-by: LLM` rather than naming specific models. He suggests Git adopt the same approach, which would replace Tamir Duberstein’s earlier `Assisted-by: Codex gpt-5.5` trailer. The change is framed as a practical alignment with emerging best practices, not a critique of the technical work, and doesn’t reopen any settled aspects of the patch.
