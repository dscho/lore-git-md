This thread discusses Tamir Duberstein's performance optimization for `git ls-files` that filters pathspecs early to avoid expensive lstat operations, showing dramatic speed improvements (60.7s->1.06s) in large repositories when most entries don't match the pathspec. The optimization has been thoroughly reviewed by both Junio C Hamano and Jeff King (Peff), with all major concerns addressed through iterative improvements.

Peff's latest analysis confirms the patch's benefits for the common case of selective pathspecs far outweigh a minor regression in an edge case (all files as pathspecs), which is both rare and inherently slow. He measured the worst-case overhead at ~800ms vs ~15ms for 10,000 files, noting this stems from the current code's ability to skip pathspec matching for unmodified files - a tradeoff the community appears willing to accept given the optimization's substantial wins in realistic workflows. 

With the technical discussion now covering performance measurements for both best and worst cases, preservation of submodule behavior, and expert validation of the tradeoffs, the patch seems ready for maintainer consideration. The consensus suggests this is a worthwhile optimization that addresses real performance pain points in large repositories while maintaining acceptable behavior in edge cases.
