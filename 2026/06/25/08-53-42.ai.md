This is the latest reply in the ongoing `--track=fetch` feature discussion, targeting the human audience of the Git mailing list digest.

Harald Nordgren pushes back against the characterization of frequent-fetch workflows as "bad," arguing that Git’s current norms are overly idealized and out of touch with the chaotic reality of most development environments. He frames the feature as a pragmatic concession to common practice—citing his own experience as an individual contributor (IC) where stale branches routinely caused pain—and suggests that Git should prioritize the needs of the 99 % of users who operate in fast-paced settings rather than enforcing a workflow that assumes careful inspection of every upstream change. The email is short and polemical, aiming to reframe the debate from "is this risky?" to "is Git serving its users?" without introducing new technical arguments or addressing Junio’s specific concerns about blind updates.
