Performance optimization for Git's priority queue implementation. This is a follow-up to Kristofer Karlsson's initial patch that proposed a more efficient cascade-down sift algorithm for `prio_queue_get()`. 

Junio C Hamano weighs in, noting that while the optimization looks good overall, the patch's claim that the new implementation is "functionally equivalent" to the original misses an important point - René Scharfe had previously optimized the `replace()` operation, and this change effectively undoes that optimization by simplifying `prio_queue_replace()` to use get+put operations instead. 

The core optimization remains compelling - benchmarks show 15-23% speedup for Git's common case of ascending-key workloads (where parents have lower priority than children), with smaller but still positive gains (1-4%) for worst-case descending keys. Real-world `rev-list` operations on wide commit graphs see ~4% speedup as the priority queue operations become more efficient.

The change is small (22 lines) and well-tested by existing unit tests. While not a major bottleneck, the optimization provides measurable gains with minimal risk. Junio's comment suggests the patch may need to better acknowledge the tradeoff with René's earlier `replace()` optimization before proceeding.
