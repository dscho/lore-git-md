Patrick Steinhardt has completed his v3 series adding a "drop" subcommand to the experimental `git history` command. The final patch implements the core functionality: removing a specified commit and replaying its descendants on top of its parent, while preventing drops of root or merge commits. The implementation builds on extensive infrastructure changes from earlier patches, including modernized reset machinery and refactored reference updates.

The series now addresses all substantive feedback from v2 reviews, with robust handling of dirty working trees (via pre-check dry-runs), comprehensive test coverage (537 lines across four scripts), and cleanups like removing `the_repository` usage. Key reviewers Phillip Wood and Junio Hamano shaped improvements to flag naming and conflict detection in v3. Only two design questions remain open for follow-up: the API's opt-in/opt-out approach for HEAD updates, and how to handle root/merge commits. These were intentionally deferred as they don't block the current functionality.

With all technical concerns resolved and the drop command fully implemented, this series appears ready for maintainer consideration. The changes align with broader efforts like `the_repository` removal and reset machinery modernization, while delivering a useful new capability for rewriting history.
