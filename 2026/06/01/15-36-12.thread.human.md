The `git history drop` subcommand series by Patrick Steinhardt has reached a major milestone. After eight iterations and 11 patches, the series is now complete and ready for merging. The final hurdle—a critical technical dispute over ref resolution in `find_head_tree_change()`—has been resolved in this version, with Junio C Hamano confirming the fix works as intended.

The series introduces a new `git history drop` command that lets users remove a commit from history and replay its descendants on top of its parent. Along the way, it modernizes Git’s reset API, advances the project’s long-running effort to eliminate global state, improves reference update architecture, and adds dry-run verification. Key features include conflict detection, bare repository support, and preservation of local changes. The reset machinery has been refactored to handle dirty indexes, split ref-update phases for better conflict detection, and make HEAD updates opt-in—all without breaking existing behavior.
