Patrick Steinhardt has sent an updated version of his `git history drop` series, which adds commit-dropping functionality to the experimental `git history` command. The series is now in its second iteration, having incorporated feedback from Pablo Sabater about error messages and test helper improvements. 

The core functionality allows removing a commit from history while replaying its descendants on top of its parent, building on the existing `git history replay` subcommand. The implementation carefully handles ref updates in prepare/apply phases and includes thorough test coverage across four new scripts. Notable limitations remain around dropping root and merge commits.

Junio C Hamano raised substantive concerns in his review about potential code duplication in the worktree update helper. Patrick has now responded thoughtfully, analyzing why reusing the existing `reset_head()` function may not be straightforward - it makes different requirements about index state, lacks dry-run support, and handles ref updates differently. While Patrick is working on adapting it, he questions whether the complexity might outweigh the benefits compared to maintaining a separate helper. This exchange reflects a recurring tension in Git development between specialized helpers and general-purpose ones.

The series appears technically sound otherwise, with consistent error messages and simplified test helpers. Remaining work includes addressing Junio's style concerns about error message backticks and the broader question of worktree update implementation. Patrick has deferred test helper consolidation to follow-up work, maintaining his characteristic approach of careful, incremental changes with clear scope boundaries. The discussion remains focused on implementation quality rather than feature design, with all participants engaging substantively on the technical merits.
