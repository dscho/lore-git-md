This is a discussion about Git's deprecation workflow, prompted by Kristoffer Haugsbakk's series removing deprecated commit list functions. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King's (Peff) earlier question about whether the standard deprecation process (mark → migrate → remove) is always necessary.

Patrick acknowledges Peff's point about the overhead of maintaining deprecation periods but suggests the approach should be context-dependent. He notes that for larger refactorings with wider impact, a transition period makes sense to avoid breaking in-flight work. The challenge lies in determining where to draw the line between changes that warrant deprecation versus those that could be removed immediately.

The exchange doesn't require any action on the current patch series (which both agree is correct) but reflects ongoing project thinking about process optimization. Patrick's perspective as the original author of these APIs carries weight in this discussion, though he frames it as an open question rather than a definitive position. This continues the thread's meta-level examination of Git's deprecation policies while leaving the technical implementation of the current cleanup unchanged.
