This is a follow-up discussion on a build system patch addressing duplicate library references in Makefile link recipes. The thread centers around a tradeoff between build cleanliness and warning suppression.

Harald Nordgren responds to Junio Hamano's substantive review of their initial patch, which had proposed removing duplicate `.a` file references from three Makefile link rules (for test helpers, fuzz targets, and unit tests). Junio had pointed out that the explicit duplicate listings serve an important purpose - they ensure proper left-to-right symbol resolution during linking by processing object files before library archives.

In this reply, Harald acknowledges Junio's concern about build brittleness but notes that other parts of the Makefile already follow the same pattern of relying on `$(LIBS)` being a superset of required archives. While not dismissing the correctness issue, Harald suggests this approach is already somewhat established in the codebase rather than being a new pattern introduced by their patch.

The exchange highlights an ongoing tension between maintaining robust build behavior (with explicit ordering guarantees) and reducing compiler warnings (particularly on macOS). The discussion remains technical and constructive, with both participants engaging on the merits of the approach rather than surface-level concerns. The thread appears headed toward either finding a solution that satisfies both requirements or explicitly documenting the tradeoff.
