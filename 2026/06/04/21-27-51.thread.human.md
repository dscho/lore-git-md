Here's the human-readable summary of the thread so far:

Kristoffer Haugsbakk reported a bug where Git's trailer parser incorrectly treats URLs in commit messages as trailers, particularly when they appear at the end of messages. This causes malformed output like converting "https://example.com" to "https: //example.com". The issue affects both command-line and formatting operations involving trailers, and Kristoffer provided real-world examples from the Linux kernel history showing this happens frequently.

Jeff King (Peff) has now responded with a deeper technical analysis, explaining that the problem stems from Git's general trailer matching logic rather than being specific to certain operations. He proposed a targeted fix that would modify the parser to exclude any line containing "://" from being considered as a trailer. This solution would cleanly handle all URL schemes while preserving normal trailer functionality. The patch is small and focused, though it will need testing to ensure it doesn't inadvertently affect legitimate uses of colons in commit messages.

The discussion has progressed from identifying the problem to having a concrete solution proposal from one of Git's most experienced contributors. Peff's involvement suggests this will likely be resolved soon, though the patch may undergo some refinement based on further testing and review.
