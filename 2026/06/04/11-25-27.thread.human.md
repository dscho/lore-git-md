Patrick Steinhardt’s 17-part ODB abstraction series is now mechanically complete, with the final callback—`freshen_object()`—wired up in the fourteenth patch. This series represents the last major ODB restructuring Patrick plans, converting the packed object storage to use `struct odb_source` and exposing deeper architectural flaws in the refdb infrastructure, including memory leaks and initialization ordering issues. While the ODB changes themselves are now finished, the series remains blocked by an 11-patch fix series addressing these refdb problems, which in turn depends on the "ps/setup-centralize-odb-creation" series.

The core work of the series is done: all required callbacks (`close()`, `reprepare()`, `read_object_info()`, `read_object_stream()`, `for_each_object()`, `count_objects()`, `find_abbrev_len()`, and now `freshen_object()`) are registered, and the type system is fully consistent across loose and packed sources. The `find_pack_entry()` helper, which was temporarily exposed during the migration, is now properly encapsulated as a file-local function in `odb/source-packed.c`. Justin Tobler has provided surface-level mechanical reviews for most of the series, confirming the changes align with the loose source precedent, but no deeper technical review of the callback logic, refdb issues, or MIDX implications has yet occurred. The only remaining loose end in the ODB work is the optional `write_object()` callback, which was not addressed in this series.

The refdb problems—memory leaks, duplicate refdb creation, and initialization ordering—remain unresolved and are the current blocker. Patrick has diagnosed these issues and is preparing an 11-patch fix series, but that work cannot proceed until the "ps/setup-centralize-odb-creation" series is merged. Meanwhile, the path handling architecture and chdir notification behavior still need a comprehensive redesign, though these are now secondary to the refdb fixes. The series has also surfaced questions about the permanence of some exposed symbols and the naming of the prepare/reprepare callbacks, but these are deferred for future work.

With the ODB abstraction now complete, the focus shifts to the refdb fixes and their dependencies. Once those land, the series should be ready to merge, though the lack of substantive review on the callback logic and refdb implications leaves some uncertainty about whether additional issues will surface during integration. For now, the thread is in a holding pattern, waiting on the prerequisite series to move forward.
