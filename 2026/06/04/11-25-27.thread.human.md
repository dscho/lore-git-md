Patrick Steinhardt’s 17-part ODB abstraction series has now reached its third iteration, completing the mechanical conversion of the packed object storage backend to the new `struct odb_source` interface. The series decouples the packed backend from its loose-source parent, aligns all 13 ODB callbacks (including `reprepare()`, which was the focus of patch 7/17), and refactors the MIDX subsystem to use the concrete packed source type. Every callback is now registered, the type system is fully consistent, and the packed source manages its own path reparenting after the final patch removes the parent pointer.

The work is technically complete, but the series remains blocked by critical refdb infrastructure flaws—memory leaks, duplicate refdb creation, and initialization-ordering issues—that Patrick has diagnosed and is addressing in an 11-patch fix series. Justin Tobler’s reviews have been mechanical, confirming the alignment with loose-source precedent and the 1:1 relocation of logic but offering no deeper engagement with callback correctness, edge cases, or the broader ODB design. Karthik Nayak, who flagged unused parameters in earlier versions, has not yet commented on the latest iteration.

The prepare/reprepare naming confusion noted in patch 7/17 is acknowledged as suboptimal, and Patrick has already drafted a follow-up series to replace the separate callbacks with a single `prepare()` callback and a force-reprepare flag. For now, the ODB abstraction effort is functionally frozen until the refdb fixes land, but the foundation is in place for future incremental work.
