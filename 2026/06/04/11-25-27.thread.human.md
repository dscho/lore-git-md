Patrick Steinhardt’s ODB abstraction series is nearing completion with its third iteration, a 17-patch effort to fully decouple packed object storage from the rest of Git’s object database. The goal is to establish a clean, consistent interface for all ODB sources—loose, packed, and in-memory—while resolving architectural flaws in the refdb layer that surfaced during implementation. So far, the mechanical groundwork is done: patches 1-3 restructured the codebase to mirror the loose source’s design, extracting packfile list logic and relocating the packed source implementation to its own files. Patch 4, just summarized, makes the parent-child relationship between the "files" and "packed" sources explicit, a temporary step before removing the parent pointer entirely in patch 17.

Justin Tobler has reviewed the mechanical changes in patches 1-7, 11, 16, and 17, confirming the alignment with the loose source’s precedent and approving the callback wiring and MIDX interface refactoring. However, the series remains blocked by critical refdb issues—memory leaks, duplicate refdb creation, and initialization ordering problems—that Patrick has diagnosed and is addressing in a separate 11-patch fix series. Until those fixes land (and they depend on the "ps/setup-centralize-odb-creation" series being merged first), the ODB abstraction can’t progress. No substantive review has yet engaged with the callback logic, refdb implications, or MIDX edge cases, leaving open questions about performance and correctness. Patrick has stated this is the last large-scale ODB restructuring he plans; future work should be incremental.
