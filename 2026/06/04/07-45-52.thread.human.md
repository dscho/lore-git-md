This thread completes a six-patch series introducing `greplint.pl`, a linter that enforces the use of `test_grep` over bare `grep` in Git’s test suite. The goal was to standardize test assertions for better debuggability—`test_grep` prints file contents on failure—while ensuring the conversion tool didn’t mask or introduce bugs. The series evolved from a simple infrastructure improvement to a broader audit after reviewers (notably SZEDER Gábor) flagged risks of automated conversions preserving pre-existing test bugs. Version 3 addressed all feedback, including auditing exemptions (`# lint-ok:` comments), fixing edge cases (e.g., reftable backend compatibility, NTFS 8.3 short names), and documenting the previously undocumented `test_grep` helper in `t/README`.

The final patch adds the linter itself, which reuses `chainlint.pl`’s shell parser to avoid false positives in heredocs or command substitutions. It flags bare `grep` assertions and `! test_grep` (preferring `test_grep !` for diagnostics), with exemptions for platform-specific paths or files that may not exist. The linter is integrated into the build system with targets for testing (`test-greplint`), validation (`check-greplint`), and cleanup. Alongside the linter, the series fixed three pre-existing bugs where `grep` was missing file arguments (now reading empty stdin), extracted a shared shell parser for future linters, and performed a bulk conversion of ~2800 `grep` assertions across 340 test files. Junio C Hamano, initially skeptical of exemption mechanisms, is now satisfied, and the series is ready for merging. The work also lays groundwork for future test-suite improvements by sharing infrastructure with `chainlint.pl`.
