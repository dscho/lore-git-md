This is the latest review in Harald Nordgren’s `--track=fetch` series, now at v14. Phillip Wood provides a substantive review of the second patch, which implements the user-facing feature for `git checkout` and `git switch`. His feedback is largely positive on the technical execution but presses for a clearer justification of the feature’s value.

Phillip’s review is substantive, not surface-level. He engages with the design rationale, suggesting the commit message should explain *why* combining fetch and checkout is convenient (e.g., minimizing merge conflicts in fast-moving projects) rather than just *how* it works. He also raises a few technical points: the use of `RESOLVE_REF_NO_RECURSE` in ref resolution, redundant refname validation, and minor code style improvements (e.g., zero-initializing structs at declaration). The test suite is noted as thorough but overly repetitive; Phillip recommends consolidating redundant test cases and clarifying terminology (e.g., avoiding confusion with Git’s namespace feature).

The review does not revisit the fundamental debate over whether the feature should exist—Junio has already signaled sustained reservations about automatic network operations during checkout—but it does underscore that the series still lacks a compelling narrative for why the workflow benefit outweighs those concerns. Phillip’s tone is constructive, and his technical feedback is actionable, but he stops short of endorsing the feature’s inclusion. The ball is now back in Harald’s court to either refine the justification or accept that the series may not be merged.
