This is the latest exchange in a performance-focused patch series that optimizes Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function—used in merge-base calculations—by terminating early when one side of a history is exhausted. The series, now in its second version (v2), targets asymmetric repositories (e.g., those with import grafts or shallow histories) where the current implementation unnecessarily traverses large one-sided histories even after one queue is empty.

The email centers on a design discussion between Derrick Stolee and Kristofer Karlsson about how to structure the termination logic in the `paint_queue` data structure. Karlsson proposes consolidating all halt conditions—including the side-exhaustion check (triggered when one queue is empty) and the all-zero check (when both are empty)—into `paint_queue_get()`, gated by whether the walk has entered the finite-generation region (`last_gen < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`). Stolee approves the approach but suggests exploring whether `last_gen` could be stored in the `paint_queue` struct to avoid passing it as a parameter, though he acknowledges the current solution is acceptable. The discussion also touches on whether to retain an older fast-exit optimization for single-merge-base queries, which Karlsson argues is less valuable now and may be removed in v2.

This is a well-crafted, narrowly scoped optimization with clear performance benefits for a known bottleneck. The technical details are thoroughly vetted, and the remaining open question (fast-exit retention) is minor. The series has already resolved all substantive feedback, including correctness concerns about the finite-generation guard and edge cases. If you care about merge-base performance or follow Git’s commit-reach subsystem, this is worth tracking—it’s likely to land soon.
