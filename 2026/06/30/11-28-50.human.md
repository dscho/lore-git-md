This is a **follow-up discussion** in Patrick Steinhardt’s six-patch series refactoring `struct object_info` to track object sources, part of the broader ODB abstraction effort. The email responds to Justin Tobler’s **substantive review** of patch 1/6, which questioned the architectural choice to pass an optional `source` parameter through `packed_object_info()` rather than tracking the source at the `packed_git` level.

Patrick clarifies the rationale: a `packed_git` object can exist independently of its source, making it a poor place to store provenance data. He acknowledges that while most direct callers of `packed_object_info()` don’t currently need source information, the long-term goal is to phase out direct use of this function in favor of `odb_read_object_info()`, which *does* care about sources. He notes ongoing work to adapt `git cat-file` and hints at future challenges with the commit-graph’s integration into pluggable backends.

The exchange highlights a **medium-weight design debate**—whether to centralize source tracking (simpler but less flexible) or keep it opt-in (more flexible but requires caller awareness). No resolution is reached here, but Patrick’s response suggests he’s leaning toward the opt-in approach for now. If you’re tracking the ODB abstraction effort, this thread is worth monitoring for how this architectural question evolves. The discussion remains conceptual, with no immediate code changes proposed.
