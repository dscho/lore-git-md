This is a **technical deep-dive follow-up** in the macOS CI hang thread, focusing on performance micro-optimizations in Git’s reftable backend. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier analysis of redundant `fstat` calls during bulk ref creation, digging into the root cause and measuring the real-world impact of eliminating them.

Peff traces the `fstat` overhead to `refs_resolve_ref_unsafe()`, which checks for reflogs on every ref update. By toggling `core.logallrefupdates` between `true` (the default, which checks for existing reflogs) and `always` (which skips the check but writes reflogs unconditionally), he benchmarks the difference: **25% of runtime** (32ms out of 129ms) is spent on these checks when creating 50,000 refs. While significant in relative terms, the absolute cost is small—just 0.64 microseconds per ref. He also notes that the reflog-writing code lacks buffering, suggesting another potential optimization (though not explored in depth here).

The email is **not a call for immediate action** but a detailed post-mortem for future reference. Peff’s tone is pragmatic: the `fstat` overhead is "probably not worth spending too much time on," though he flags the unbuffered reflog writes as a low-hanging fruit. The discussion remains tied to the reftable backend’s internals, with no changes to the already-merged CI fix patches. For readers following the thread, this is a niche but informative look at how Git’s ref storage could be incrementally improved.
