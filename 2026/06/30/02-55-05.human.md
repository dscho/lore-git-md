This is a **user feedback email** on the `git history squash` feature, part of Harald Nordgren’s ongoing patch series (now at v6). Matt Hunter, a newcomer to the thread, shares hands-on impressions after testing the feature and weighs in on the unresolved `--reedit-message` template debate.

Hunter aligns with Phillip Wood’s usability critique: he finds the current template’s retention of commented-out `fixup!`/`amend!` messages visually overwhelming and supports omitting them entirely, while keeping a summary list of commit subjects at the top for context. He also suggests that `squash!` messages (unlike `fixup!`) should retain their content, as they often contain intentional rewording notes. His perspective reinforces the consensus that the template needs simplification, though he doesn’t propose a specific format.

The email raises two broader questions about default behavior:
1. **Why isn’t `--reedit-message` the default?** Hunter notes that `git rebase -i` defaults to opening the editor, and wonders if `git history squash` should follow suit for consistency. This touches on a design tension between speed (automatic squashing) and safety (manual review).
2. **Undo safety**: He asks whether there’s a foolproof way to revert a `git history` operation, given that `--update-refs` can move multiple branches. This highlights a potential usability gap for users who discover mistakes post-operation.

The feedback is constructive and grounded in real-world testing, but it doesn’t challenge the core design. The questions about defaults and undo safety are worth noting for future iterations, though they’re unlikely to block the current series. The thread’s focus remains on the template formatting issue, which Harald is expected to address in v7.
