This is a substantive design discussion in the `git replay --linearize` series (v5), focusing on the command-line interface for handling merge commits. Johannes Schindelin replies to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to mirror `git rebase`'s `--rebase-merges=<mode>` syntax, arguing strongly against it on usability grounds.

Schindelin acknowledges the functional overlap but rejects the `git rebase` interface as a poor precedent, calling its history "full of usability warts" and criticizing the term "rebase" itself as user-hostile. He advocates for distinct, intuitive option names that reflect the "replay" concept rather than reusing `git rebase`'s terminology. Specifically, he opposes conflating `--linearize` with a `--replay-merges=<mode>` enum, arguing that `--no-rebase-merges` (or its hypothetical `--no-replay-merges` equivalent) would misleadingly suggest an abort-on-merge behavior rather than flattening. His position is that `--linearize` is conceptually separate from merge-handling modes and should remain a standalone flag.

The email does not resolve the interface design question but shifts the framing: the debate is no longer just about consistency with `git rebase` but about whether Git should repeat its past UX mistakes. Schindelin’s feedback raises the bar for retaining the current `--linearize` interface, as it now requires either (a) a principled justification for deviating from `git rebase` or (b) a redesign of the interface to avoid the perceived usability pitfalls. This discussion, combined with the unresolved regression in `--linearize` behavior (commit-dropping when replaying a single branch with merges), remains a blocking issue for the series. The thread’s technical focus is now split between algorithmic correctness (the `replayed_base` parameter) and interface design.
