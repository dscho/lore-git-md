Patrick Steinhardt’s 13-patch refactoring series to separate repository discovery from setup in Git’s core code has reached its second version with no code changes—only minor updates to commit messages. The series aims to clean up `setup.c` by introducing a new `struct repo_discovery` to hold discovery results, making the phases of repository initialization more distinct and self-contained. All patches are now submitted, and the series is technically complete.

Justin Tobler, who provided substantive feedback on the first three patches and patch 5 in the initial version, has signed off on the v2 updates without raising new concerns. His earlier reviews flagged design questions—like the use of environment variables for global state and the assumption that `apply_repository_format()` is always called after `setup_explicit_git_dir()`—but these remain unresolved. Junio C Hamano has queued patch 2/13 after fixing a typo, but the rest of the series is still under review with no merge decisions.

The refactoring touches `setup.c`, `git.c`, `repository.h`, and related files, introducing new symbols like `repo_discover()` and `struct repo_discovery`. While no behavior changes are intended, the series may expose edge cases in repository initialization, particularly around environment variable handling and worktree path resolution. The work is foundational for consolidating setup logic into `repo_init()`, a long-term architectural goal, and aligns with ongoing efforts like `the_repository` removal and ODB abstraction. The next steps will likely involve deeper review from other maintainers or follow-up work to address the unresolved design questions.
