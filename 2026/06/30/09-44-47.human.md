This is a substantive design discussion in the ongoing `git replay --linearize` series (v5). Johannes Schindelin weighs in on the interface debate between Toon Claes and Patrick Steinhardt, siding firmly with Toon’s incremental approach and rejecting the idea of mirroring `git rebase`'s `--rebase-merges=<mode>` syntax.

Schindelin argues that `git rebase`'s interface is a poor precedent, calling its history "full of usability warts" and criticizing the term "rebase" itself as user-hostile. He advocates for clearer, more intuitive option names that use "replay" instead of "rebase" and warns against conflating `--linearize` with a multi-mode `--replay-merges` flag. Specifically, he points out that `--no-rebase-merges` would misleadingly suggest *aborting* on merges rather than *flattening* them, reinforcing that `--linearize` is conceptually distinct and should remain a standalone option.

The email does not resolve the interface question but shifts the burden of justification: Patrick’s call for consistency is now countered by Schindelin’s strong preference for a cleaner, more intentional design. The regression in `--linearize` behavior (commit-dropping when replaying a single branch with merges) remains the primary technical blocker, but this discussion is now a second open question that could delay merging. The thread’s next steps will likely hinge on whether Toon can address Schindelin’s regression fix while also documenting a principled rationale for deviating from `git rebase`'s interface—or whether the project will accept the incremental approach as-is.
