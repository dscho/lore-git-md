This is a **substantive review** of Jeff King’s patch fixing a memory leak in `git format-patch`. Patrick Steinhardt engages with the technical details of the patch, confirms its correctness, and expands the discussion to broader leak-detection infrastructure.

The patch itself adds a single `release_revisions(&revs)` call in `prepare_bases()` to free a `rev_info` struct that was allocated for a custom revision walk. Patrick’s review is positive: he confirms the fix is correct (the `revs` struct is always initialized before the exit path) and notes no other early returns need adjustment. He also **tests the patch** and reports that six test suites (`t0095`, `t3451`, `t3452`, `t3453`, `t4013`, `t4211`) still fail under LeakSanitizer, but the failures are unrelated to this patch—most stem from missing `repo_unuse_commit_buffer()` calls in `git-history` and bloom filter leaks.

Beyond the patch, Patrick proposes a **process improvement**: enabling LeakSanitizer for the `linux-TEST-vars` CI job, which exercises non-default code paths. He benchmarks the overhead (~3x slowdown) and concludes it’s too high for all jobs but feasible for this targeted one. This suggestion ties into the series’ broader goal of improving leak detection without disrupting existing workflows.

Key technical details for follow-up:
- **File**: `builtin/log.c` (one-line addition in `prepare_bases()`).
- **Function**: `release_revisions(&revs)` (fixes leak of `rev_info` struct).
- **Trigger**: commit-graph presence (allocates `topo_walk_info`).
- **Test failures**: unrelated to this patch; involve `git-history` and bloom filters.
- **CI proposal**: enable LSan for `linux-TEST-vars` job to catch similar leaks earlier.
