This is a **user experience discussion** about recoverability and reflog design in the `git history` suite, specifically the new `squash` subcommand. Matt Hunter, a newcomer to the `git history` subsystem, raises a practical concern: **how to comprehensively undo a botched `git history` operation** when `--update-refs` has moved multiple branch pointers. His question highlights a gap in Git’s safety net—while `git reset --hard` can restore the current branch, it doesn’t account for other branches that may have been retargeted, leaving the repository in an inconsistent state.

Matt’s observation is sharp: the reflog for `HEAD` doesn’t clearly show which other refs were affected by a `history` operation, making it difficult to perform a full recovery. He notes that `git status` uses reflog entries to track detached HEAD origins (e.g., "HEAD detached from v2.55.0-rc2"), but this mechanism doesn’t extend to multi-branch operations like `git history squash --update-refs`. His concern is **not theoretical**—it’s a real usability pain point for users who rely on branch pointers to track state.

Harald Nordgren’s earlier reply in this thread pivots to a broader critique of Git’s reflog design, proposing a **"human-centered reflog"** with **visual hierarchy** (e.g., indentation for sub-steps) to reflect workflow structure. While Matt doesn’t directly engage with Harald’s design vision, his question underscores the need for such improvements. The current reflog treats every operation as equally important, which obscures the logical relationships between actions (e.g., a squash operation and its sub-steps).

This exchange is **not a technical debate** but a **usability reality check**. Matt’s feedback is surface-level in the sense that it doesn’t propose a specific fix, but it’s valuable because it comes from a user perspective—something the Git project often lacks in its mailing-list discussions. His question about recoverability is likely to resonate with other users, and it may prompt follow-up work on either:
1. **Improving the reflog’s output** (e.g., adding metadata about affected refs or visual hierarchy), or
2. **Adding a safety mechanism** (e.g., a `--dry-run` mode that lists all refs that would be updated, or a `git history undo` subcommand).

For now, this thread serves as a reminder that even well-designed features like `git history squash` can introduce usability friction when they interact with Git’s broader ecosystem. The discussion is **not blocking the `squash` series** (which is code-complete and ready for Junio’s review), but it could inspire future patches to improve Git’s recoverability story.
