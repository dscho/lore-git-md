This thread began with Jeff King (Peff) submitting a two-patch series to fix memory leaks in `git format-patch` and the test harness. The fixes themselves are straightforward and uncontroversial: one line in `builtin/log.c` to free a `rev_info` struct, and a small redirection in `t/test-lib.sh` to ensure LeakSanitizer (LSan) output doesn’t break TAP parsing. Both patches are effectively approved, with Patrick Steinhardt confirming the `format-patch` fix is correct and Junio Hamano endorsing the minimalist approach.

The discussion has since shifted to CI infrastructure, where the group has converged on a plan to consolidate leak-detection jobs. The proposal—now agreed upon by Peff, Patrick, and Junio—is to merge the `linux-reftables-leaks` and `linux-TEST-vars` jobs into a single `linux-TEST-vars-leaks` job, drop the redundant non-leak `linux-reftable` job, and rename `linux-TEST-vars` to `linux-exotic-flags` for clarity. The overhead of running LSan in CI (~3x slowdown) is deemed acceptable for targeted use, though expansion to macOS or compiler-specific jobs is off the table. Peff and Patrick also clarified that while compiler diversity (clang vs. gcc) can occasionally reveal latent undefined behavior (UB), such cases are rare, and UBSan is the more effective tool for systematic detection. The original patches remain ready for merging, and the CI changes are procedural follow-up. OpenSSL SHA-1/SHA-256 leaks and bloom filter leaks are being addressed separately.
