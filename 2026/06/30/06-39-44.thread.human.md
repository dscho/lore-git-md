This thread is a focused cleanup effort by Jeff King (Peff) to fix two small but long-standing memory leaks: one in `git format-patch` (a leak in `prepare_bases()` that has existed since 2016) and another in the test harness (where LeakSanitizer output was misdirected, causing TAP parse errors). The patches themselves are straightforward, low-risk, and ready to merge—both fixes are mechanical and touch only a few lines in `builtin/log.c` and `t/test-lib.sh`.

The discussion has since expanded to broader CI improvements for leak detection. Peff and Patrick Steinhardt agree on consolidating the `linux-reftables-leaks` and `linux-TEST-vars` jobs into a single `linux-TEST-vars-leaks` job and dropping the redundant `linux-reftable` job. The naming of the `linux-TEST-vars` job is also under review, with Peff advocating for clearer alternatives like `linux-changed-defaults` or `linux-exotic-flags`. However, Peff is skeptical about extending this pattern to macOS (`macos-TEST-vars`), arguing that most `TEST-vars` are feature-related and unlikely to reveal macOS-specific issues. He also questions the value of redundant compiler-specific jobs (clang/gcc), though he concedes that folding `linux-reftable` into `linux-TEST-vars` is reasonable.

The original patches remain uncontroversial and are effectively merged in principle. The CI discussion is procedural and doesn’t block the series, though it reflects the project’s ongoing efforts to improve memory hygiene and testing infrastructure. Follow-up work includes polishing patches for OpenSSL SHA-1/SHA-256 leaks and addressing unrelated leaks in bloom filters and `git-history`, but these are outside the scope of this series.
