This thread began as a straightforward bugfix series from Jeff King (Peff) to plug two small memory leaks—one in `git format-patch` and one in the test harness—and has since expanded into a discussion about improving Git’s CI infrastructure for leak detection. The original patches (v1) are effectively complete: Peff’s fixes for `builtin/log.c` and `t/test-lib.sh` are mechanical, low-risk, and have received substantive review from Patrick Steinhardt, who confirmed the `format-patch` fix is correct. No further changes are expected for the series itself, and it is ready for merging.

The broader conversation has shifted to CI improvements, where Peff and Patrick have converged on a plan to consolidate redundant jobs. The proposal now is to merge the `linux-reftables-leaks` and `linux-TEST-vars` jobs into a single `linux-TEST-vars-leaks` job (running LSan with `TEST-vars` coverage) and drop the obsolete non-leak `linux-reftable` job. Patrick has also suggested extending this consolidation to macOS by creating a `macos-TEST-vars` job and questioned whether the `linux-TEST-vars` job should be renamed to something more descriptive, like `linux-changed-defaults`. These are procedural refinements, not blockers, and the discussion remains uncontroversial.

While the original leak fixes are self-contained, the thread has surfaced related work, including Peff’s draft patches for OpenSSL SHA-1/SHA-256 leaks and his earlier fixes for bloom filter leaks. Unrelated LSan failures in other test suites (e.g., `t3451`, `t3452`, `t3453`) were noted but are outside the scope of this series. The key takeaway for developers catching up: the patch series is ready to go, and the CI changes—while still under discussion—are aimed at making leak detection more efficient without disrupting the current workflow.
