This is a **substantive technical follow-up** in the ongoing macOS CI hang thread, focusing on **reftable backend performance optimization**. Jeff King (Peff) digs deeper into the redundant `stat()` calls Patrick Steinhardt identified earlier, providing concrete performance measurements and a detailed call stack to quantify the overhead.

Peff traces the bottleneck to `reftable_stack_reload()`, which repeatedly checks whether `tables.list` is up-to-date via `stat()`—2,000 calls during bulk ref creation. Using `hyperfine` benchmarks, he measures the impact: disabling reflog checks (via `core.logallrefupdates=always`) paradoxically *slows* the operation from 129ms to 195ms, revealing that the `stat()` overhead (~25% of runtime) is dwarfed by the cost of writing reflog entries. A hypothetical "never" reflog mode cuts runtime to 96ms, confirming the `stat()` calls account for ~33ms (0.64µs per ref).

Peff also spots a secondary inefficiency: `fd_writer` writes reflog entries individually without buffering, suggesting a potential 2x speedup (e.g., 120ms → 60ms) by switching to stdio. He notes a bizarre reproducibility issue—`git for-each-ref | git update-ref` triggers an infinite loop in `merged_iter_next_entry()`—but defers investigation.

**Key technical details for future reference**:
- **Subsystem**: reftable backend (`reftable/stack.c`, `refs/reftable-backend.c`).
- **Root cause**: `reftable_stack_reload()`’s `stat()` calls on `tables.list` during bulk ref creation.
- **Performance impact**: 2,000 `stat()` calls consume ~25% of runtime (33ms for 50,000 refs).
- **Reflog overhead**: Writing reflog entries is far costlier than `stat()` checks (70ms vs. 33ms).
- **Proposed optimizations**:
  - Buffer reflog writes via stdio (potential 2x speedup).
  - Trust the loaded stack after locking to avoid `stat()` calls (low-priority).
- **Files touched**: None in this email, but future work would target `reftable/stack.c` and `reftable/writer.c`.
- **Test method**: `hyperfine` benchmarks with `git update-ref --stdin` and `core.logallrefupdates` tweaks.

This exchange is **not a call for immediate action**—Peff frames the findings as "probably not worth spending too much time on" given the small absolute gains. However, it provides a clear roadmap for future optimizations (buffering, stack trust) and quantifies the trade-offs between reflog overhead and filesystem checks. The tone remains pragmatic, balancing micro-optimizations against broader architectural priorities.
