This is Patrick Steinhardt’s response to Junio C Hamano’s **medium-weight architectural concern** about the opt-in `source` parameter design in the ODB refactoring series. The email clarifies the rationale behind the current approach and pushes back on the suggestion to centralize source tracking at the `packed_git` level.

Patrick argues that the `source` parameter is *not* "almost always NULL"—rather, most callers use the higher-level `odb_read_object_info()` API, which already has access to the source and thus doesn’t appear in the diff. The low-level `packed_object_info()` callers, which *do* need adjustment, are the exception, not the rule. He defends the opt-in design as a pragmatic step toward eliminating direct use of `packed_object_info()` in favor of `odb_read_object_info()`, aligning with the long-term ODB abstraction goals. The current design avoids a layering violation (packfiles existing independently of their source) and keeps the API flexible for backends that don’t need source tracking.

On naming, Patrick acknowledges Justin Tobler’s earlier **surface-level concern** about `sourcep` being ambiguous (it could imply a pointer to `struct odb_source` rather than `struct object_info_source`). He explains the current implementation avoids casting by assigning the base struct (`source->base`), but doesn’t propose a rename, leaving that as a non-blocking follow-up.

The email advances the thread by justifying the design choices but doesn’t resolve the architectural tension. Junio and Justin’s preference for centralized source tracking remains unaddressed, and Patrick’s response suggests he’s unlikely to change course without further pushback. The series is still conceptually approved ("Great" from Junio) but may require additional discussion before merging.
