This is a substantive review of an RFC feature proposal for `git clone` sparse-checkout path specification. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Pushkar Singh’s RFC, which proposed adding `--only` and `--except` options to `git clone` to specify sparse-checkout paths directly during clone.

Peff’s review is cautious and exploratory, reflecting his limited hands-on experience with sparse-checkout. He suggests an alternative design: allowing users to provide a patterns file at clone time (e.g., `.git/info/sparse-checkout`) rather than introducing new CLI options. This approach would reuse the existing sparse-checkout machinery more directly, avoiding the need for new flags or pathspec parsing logic. He notes that this might not align with how users typically interact with sparse-checkout (e.g., via `git sparse-checkout set`), but it’s worth considering as a simpler or more flexible alternative.

On the performance front, Peff raises a question about whether the feature could enable partial clones of specific paths, but he quickly dismisses this as impractical due to server-side limitations (most servers disable path-based filters for performance reasons). This observation underscores that the feature’s primary value would be ergonomic, not performance-related, and that it would still rely on lazy-loading blobs during checkout.

Peff also suggests cc’ing contributors like Derrick Stolee, who have worked extensively on sparse-checkout and related optimizations (e.g., sparse-index). This is a practical step to ensure the proposal gets feedback from domain experts, though it’s not a technical endorsement of the idea.

The review is substantive but not deeply technical—Peff engages with the design space and potential trade-offs, but his lack of direct experience with sparse-checkout limits the depth of his critique. The key takeaway is that the feature’s ergonomic benefits are plausible, but the interface and implementation details (e.g., CLI flags vs. file-based patterns) remain open questions. The proposal’s prospects hinge on whether other contributors (especially those with sparse-checkout expertise) see value in it.
