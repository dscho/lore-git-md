This is a follow-up discussion about how to position the `b4` tool in Git's contribution documentation. Weijie Yuan responds to Patrick Steinhardt's defense of promoting `b4` as a first-class workflow option for new contributors.

Yuan acknowledges Patrick's efficiency argument but maintains that `b4` is more like a "porcelain" layer - something built atop fundamental concepts that contributors should still understand. He suggests the documentation should present `b4` as an optimization of the traditional email workflow rather than a fully parallel option, since users will eventually need to understand concepts like cover letters and Message-IDs anyway.

The exchange reveals an underlying tension in onboarding design: whether to prioritize immediate productivity (Patrick's view) or conceptual foundations (Yuan's preference). Yuan concedes that both approaches have merit, noting that some beginners will happily adopt `b4` without deep understanding. His metaphor comparing `b4` to magit (an Emacs Git interface) helps clarify why he sees value in learning fundamentals first, even as he accepts the practical benefits of automation.
