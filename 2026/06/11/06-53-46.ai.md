This is a technical review from Jeff King (Peff) raising a performance consideration for Patrick Steinhardt's ref backend refactoring series. The review engages substantively with the historical context of the changes being proposed.

Peff notes that the series' move to absolute paths might regress a performance optimization from 2008 that used relative paths to speed up operations when changing directories. He references two specific commits (8500e0de3f and 044bbbcb63) that introduced the chdir-notify mechanism and demonstrated measurable speed improvements from relative paths.

The review is thoughtful but not blocking - Peff acknowledges the performance impact may be negligible today and the original optimization wasn't dramatic. He leaves open whether this tradeoff matters in practice, suggesting it could depend on repository depth in the filesystem hierarchy.

This feedback adds an important dimension to the discussion by surfacing a historical optimization that the series might inadvertently undo. While not opposing the change, Peff ensures the performance implications are explicitly considered as part of the design tradeoffs. The review demonstrates deep familiarity with Git's internals and evolution while maintaining a constructive tone focused on technical merits.
