Jeff King has identified key technical nuances in the Perl prerequisite detection logic and platform-specific shell behaviors. His analysis reveals:
1. Original Perl detection was `set -e` safe due to `&&` operator behavior
2. macOS's Bash 3.2.57 incorrectly propagates `set -e` failures through `command` invocations on the left side of `&&` - a POSIX violation that Apple won't fix due to licensing constraints

Jeff now proposes two alternative approaches:
- Manually toggling `set -e` around problematic invocations (acknowledged as "gross")
- Not enabling `set -e` on known-buggy shells like macOS's Bash 3.2.57

The discussion reveals Jeff's skepticism about whether the complexity justifies the benefits, suggesting simpler approaches like stderr checking might achieve similar robustness with less maintenance burden. This reflects tension between Patrick's strict error handling goals and practical cross-platform constraints, particularly macOS's outdated Bash version.
