Patrick Steinhardt's ongoing effort to make Git's test suite robust under `set -e` continues with another incremental improvement in the v5 series. The foundation of this work - establishing `set -e` compatibility through careful test script adaptations - was merged earlier in a 12-patch set, with subsequent iterations methodically updating individual test cases.

The latest patch (10/12) focuses on the bisection test script, replacing fragile `expr` commands with more reliable shell arithmetic expressions. This addresses a subtle issue where `expr` fails when returning zero - a case that would break tests under `set -e`. The changes follow the series' established pattern of minimal, behavior-preserving modifications: converting three `expr` calls to `$(( ))` arithmetic, adding explicit conditionals for negative checks, and modernizing some test syntax.

Multiple contributors including Jeff King (Peff) and D. Ben Knoble have verified the approach across different shells and platforms. With all tests passing and no new technical questions raised, the series appears ready for final review.
