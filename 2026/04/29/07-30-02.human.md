This is a technical discussion about tarball reproducibility in Git's `git archive` functionality, continuing the thread about Debian packaging needs. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Theodore Tso's questions about historical inconsistencies in archive generation.

Peff highlights several past issues that affected tarball reproducibility: a 2013 change in tar path handling that broke Homebrew recipes, a 2023 gzip-related change, and a 2019 pax header calculation fix. He notes these changes were rare (every 5-10 years) but significant when they occurred. The core challenge is that bug fixes and compatibility improvements inherently change output, making true reproducibility difficult without versioning the archive format to preserve old behaviors.

The email reinforces the thread's emerging consensus that achieving byte-for-byte reproducibility would need explicit versioned formats to lock in behaviors, including known bugs. Peff's perspective as a long-time contributor adds historical weight to the discussion, showing this isn't a new problem but one with documented precedents. His pragmatic tone suggests that while perfect reproducibility is theoretically possible, the practical impact of occasional breaking changes may be acceptable given their infrequency.
