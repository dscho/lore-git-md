This is a technical analysis from Jeff King (Peff) regarding the mksh test suite hang regression. He confirms Jan Palus's findings that commit dd3693eb08 causes indefinite hangs when Git's test suite runs with mksh as the shell, due to differences in how shells handle SIGTERM and child processes.

Peff's key observations:
1. The root issue is that mksh waits for child processes to complete when receiving SIGTERM, while bash/dash terminate immediately and reparent children
2. The current clean_on_exit mechanism fails its original purpose (preventing zombie processes) since child processes often survive anyway
3. A proper fix would require complex signal handling to track and terminate all descendant processes

Proposed solutions:
1. Immediate fix: Revert dd3693eb08 as it doesn't reliably solve the zombie problem and causes hangs
2. Longer term: Either accept processes being reparented to pid1 (recommending containers use proper init systems), or implement more sophisticated process tracking

Peff also identifies broader issues with run-command.c's clean_on_exit when shells are involved, noting several existing cases where similar hangs could theoretically occur, though they're less severe than the transport-helper case. He suggests the current behavior is mostly acceptable for non-interactive cases, as mksh will often optimize away for simple commands.

This is a substantive technical analysis that digs deeper into the process handling implications, validating the reporter's findings while providing context about the tradeoffs involved in potential fixes. The recommendation to revert appears well-justified given the analysis.
