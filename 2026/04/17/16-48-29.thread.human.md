This thread addresses a series of bugfixes for Git's bisect functionality when using alternate terminology (like "old"/"new" instead of the default "good"/"bad"). The third iteration of patches has already addressed memory leaks and improved test coverage based on feedback from Phillip Wood and Junio Hamano, focusing on making the status output and ref lookup respect configured terms while maintaining backward compatibility.

Junio has now raised a deeper concern about how these custom terms interact with translations. While the patches correctly substitute the terms, the surrounding message structure assumes they'll function grammatically as adjectives (like "good commit"), which may not hold for all term choices or languages. He suggests potential solutions like quoting terms or rephrasing messages, though notes some phrasing might be awkward. This isn't a blocking objection but rather a thoughtful consideration of edge cases that the author may want to address in a future iteration. The series appears technically sound otherwise and has satisfied prior review feedback, but this internationalization consideration adds another dimension to the solution.
