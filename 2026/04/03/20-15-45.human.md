This is a discussion about Git's graph visualization improvements, specifically addressing how to visually distinguish root commits in `--graph` output. Junio C Hamano responds to Tian Yuchen's proposal to use special symbol (■) for root commits, explaining why this approach was previously rejected.

Junio notes that using distinct symbols for roots creates complications with other graph display modes like `--left-right` and `--boundary`, where roots may need different visual treatment based on their role. He mentions this design question has come up multiple times in the past (2019 and earlier) and the project consistently rejected special symbols in favor of maintaining a consistent visual language where all commits are shown as `*`.

The email reinforces the project's historical position on this issue while acknowledging the UX concerns Yuchen raised. It suggests the minimal-padding approach (adding blank rows between unrelated roots) remains the preferred solution despite some visual ambiguity, as it avoids the complexity of special-case symbols. This positions the thread as closing off an exploration path rather than opening new ones, with Junio steering the discussion back toward the previously established direction.
