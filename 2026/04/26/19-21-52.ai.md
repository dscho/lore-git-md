This is a continuation of the discussion about fixing the regression in Git's alias handling where hierarchical aliases (like `pull.sub`) stopped working in Git 2.54.0. Jonatan Holmgren, the author of the original patch that introduced the regression, responds to Jeff King's proposal for handling the fix.

Holmgren expresses concerns about Jeff King's suggested "dual interpretation" approach where `alias.foo.bar` would be treated both as a single alias name and as a structured key. He argues this creates ambiguity in configuration interpretation and makes the system harder to document. Instead, he advocates for his original approach of reserving specific key names (like `command`) for future metadata while maintaining backward compatibility for other dotted aliases.

The email reveals an important underlying question - whether dotted aliases were an intentional feature or an implementation artifact. Holmgren suggests they may have been accidental, but acknowledges they're now part of real workflows. He strongly opposes moving to a completely separate namespace (`nalias`) as Junio had previously suggested, seeing it as worse for users than his reserved-prefix approach.

This exchange highlights the tension between maintaining backward compatibility, keeping the codebase clean, and planning for future extensibility. The discussion has moved beyond just fixing the regression to considering the long-term design of Git's alias system.
