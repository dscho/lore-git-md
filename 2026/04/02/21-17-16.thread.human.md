The `git log --graph` cascading indentation feature, now at v9 after nine revisions, introduces progressive indentation for visual roots to clarify parent-child relationships in complex histories. Pablo Sabater’s series has resolved all technical hurdles—including a lookahead buffer to safely peek ahead in the revision walk and fixes for edge cases like `--author` and `--grep` filters—earning architectural approval from Junio Hamano and robust test coverage. The patches are now technically complete and ready for final review.

However, Mirko Faina’s recent review raises a significant usability concern: the indentation can clutter output for simple histories, such as sequences of single-patch series, where ambiguity doesn’t exist. He proposes making the feature opt-in, gated behind `--oneline` or `--format=reference`, to preserve the traditional flat layout as the default. This shifts the discussion from correctness (already addressed) to user experience and backward compatibility. The timing is critical, as the series is poised for `next`; whether this feedback triggers a design pivot or is deferred as post-merge refinement will depend on maintainer priorities.

Key participants—Junio Hamano, Jeff King, and Phillip Wood—have yet to weigh in on this usability question, though their earlier technical feedback shaped the current implementation. The series remains compatible with related work (e.g., Jeff King’s `revs->commits` removal) and is well-tested, but Mirko’s objection ensures the usability trade-off is now part of the record. The next steps will likely focus on whether to adjust the default behavior or proceed as-is.
