This thread is about Pablo Sabater’s patch series implementing cascading indentation for visual roots in `git log --graph`, which has reached its sixth iteration. The series aims to improve the clarity of commit graphs by progressively indenting visual roots (except the first) while preserving parent-child relationships. After resolving earlier architectural concerns with a clean abstraction layer in `revision.c`, the series was nearly complete, with only follow-up work—such as defensive checks and minor readability improvements—remaining before final approval.

However, Kristofer Karlsson has now proposed a **fundamental redesign** of the abstraction layer in patch 2/3. His review identifies critical flaws in the current peek-based approach: the pending commit queue may not yet contain all relevant commits, and commits in the queue haven’t undergone `simplify_commit()`, leading to incorrect parent lists. This causes the three `test_expect_failure` cases in `t4218` to fail. Instead, Kristofer suggests a **lookahead buffer** inside `struct git_graph`, populated by `get_revision_internal()` before processing each commit. This ensures commits are fully simplified and their parent lists are final, resolving the test failures and eliminating the need for the two new peek functions. While this redesign simplifies the interface, it represents a significant shift from the current approach and would likely require a v7 resend.

Key participants include Pablo Sabater (author), Kristofer Karlsson (architectural reviewer and proposer of the lookahead solution), Jeff King (who confirmed the fragility of the original approach and clarified the need for iteration support), and Junio Hamano (who conditionally approved the abstraction layer pending follow-up work). The redesign has strong technical backing and local testing, but its adoption would delay the series further. The remaining open questions include whether the lookahead buffer approach will be accepted, how it interacts with ongoing priority queue refactoring (`kk/prio-queue-get-put-fusion`), and whether the test brittleness concerns raised by Junio can be resolved. The series is now at a crossroads, with a technically sound but architecturally divergent proposal on the table.
