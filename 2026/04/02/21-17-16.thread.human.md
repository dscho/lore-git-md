This thread is about Pablo Sabater’s patch series implementing cascading indentation for visual roots in `git log --graph`, now at its eighth version (v8). The goal is to improve the clarity of commit graphs by indenting commits with excluded parents, making it easier to distinguish parent-child relationships when some parents are filtered out.

The series is now complete, with all four patches ready for final review. The first patch, a test infrastructure cleanup, has already been merged. The second and third patches introduced a unified lookahead buffer to address a subtle bug in earlier versions where the buffer’s population source varied depending on command-line options like `--max-count-oldest`, breaking tests. This redesign ensures deterministic behavior and compatibility with other in-flight topics, such as Jeff King’s removal of `revs->commits` and Kristofer Haugsbakk’s `kk/prio-queue-get-put-fusion`.

The fourth and final patch, just posted, adds the visualization logic. It introduces a new `GRAPH_PRE_ROOT` state to connect indented roots to their children, tracks indentation depth, and excludes merge parents from indentation to avoid redundant edges. The patch includes extensive test coverage in `t4218-log-graph-indentation.sh`, which now passes all previously failing cases, including those involving `--max-count`, `--max-count-oldest`, and octopus merges. A minor edge case—redundant indentation when filtered parents are present—is documented as needing future work but is considered acceptable for now.

The series has received substantive feedback from Kristofer Karlsson, who proposed the lookahead buffer redesign, and Junio Hamano, who approved the architectural approach. Jeff King and Karthik Nayak have also weighed in, with the latter approving the test infrastructure changes. Phillip Wood’s final review of the visualization logic is the last remaining step before the series can be merged. The patches are technically sound, address all known edge cases, and are compatible with other ongoing work in the project.
