This thread is about Pablo Sabater’s patch series (now at v6) implementing cascading indentation for visual roots in `git log --graph`, which has reached a critical architectural decision point. The series aims to improve the clarity of commit graphs by progressively indenting visual roots (except the first) when commits have excluded parents, while preserving parent-child relationships.

The core challenge is safely accessing future commits in the revision walk to determine visual root status, given that commits may be rewritten or simplified after being enqueued. The v6 series introduced a peek-based abstraction layer in `revision.c` to allow `graph.c` to inspect upcoming commits without direct access to the revision queue. While this approach was conditionally approved by Junio Hamano and Phillip Wood, Kristofer Karlsson proposed a fundamental redesign using a lookahead buffer inside `struct git_graph`. This redesign resolves three failing test cases and addresses the fragility of the peek-based approach, which relies on the overloaded dual-queue system (`revs->commits` and `revs->commit_queue`). Jeff King confirmed the fragility of direct queue access and revealed latent bug risks in history simplification scenarios.

The thread is now at a crossroads: Junio must decide between incremental improvements to the v6 peek-based abstraction (e.g., defensive checks and robustness improvements) or adopting Kristofer’s lookahead buffer, which would require a v7 resend. The lookahead approach eliminates the need for peek functions entirely and is compatible with Jeff King’s planned removal of `revs->commits`, but it introduces a minor edge case (redundant indentation when filtered parents are present) and may require timestamp tweaks for deterministic output. Pablo Sabater’s next steps depend on Junio’s decision, which will likely hinge on his preference for incremental vs. radical change. The visualization implementation (patch 3/3) is pending final review once the architectural direction is resolved. Taylor Blau’s input on commit-graph interactions and Junio’s concern about test brittleness remain open.
