This thread is about Pablo Sabater’s patch series adding visual root indentation to `git log --graph`, now in its ninth iteration. The series aims to clarify commits with excluded parents by indenting visual roots—except the first—while preserving parent-child relationships. The technical foundation, including a lookahead buffer to safely inspect future commits, has been approved by Junio Hamano, but the series is stalled on a late-stage usability decision about how the indentation should behave.

The debate has narrowed to a choice between two bounded wrapping designs. Mirko Faina proposed capping indentation at two levels before resetting to the left margin, avoiding excessive indentation while addressing earlier objections about ambiguity in merge topologies. Pablo Sabater has now countered with a four-level cap, arguing that it makes the reset more visually abrupt and reduces ambiguity in edge cases where Mirko’s two-level wrap might create a "pyramid effect." Both designs resolve the original concerns raised by Chandra Pratap and others, and neither requires changes to the underlying infrastructure. Pablo also raised whether an opt-out flag (`--no-graph-indent`) is still needed, though the bounded wrapping design itself no longer depends on formatting flags.

The series is otherwise complete and ready for final review. Junio and Phillip Wood have not yet signed off on the remaining patches, and the maintainers must now decide between the two indentation styles before the feature can graduate. The discussion is purely about usability trade-offs—two-level wrap is simpler but may risk subtle ambiguity, while four-level wrap is more visually distinct but could feel excessive in some cases. No technical blockers remain, and the series is poised for `next` once the maintainers weigh in.
