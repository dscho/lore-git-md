This is part 4 of Christian Couder's 10-patch series refactoring promisor-remote handling. The patch tightens validation of server-advertised promisor remotes by rejecting empty names or URLs at parse time, rather than later during processing.

The change modifies `parse_one_advertised_remote()` to check for both NULL and empty strings (using `!*info->name` and `!*info->url`). While Git servers shouldn't advertise such remotes (they're already ignored later in `promisor_config_info_list()`), this early rejection makes the behavior more robust against buggy or malicious servers. The warning message is also updated to be more explicit that such remotes will be ignored.

This is a small but meaningful hardening of the promisor-remote subsystem, maintaining the series' focus on cleaning up edge cases before introducing new functionality. The change is straightforward and unlikely to be controversial, though reviewers may want to confirm the warning message wording.
