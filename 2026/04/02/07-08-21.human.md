April Fools' Day joke with a technical demonstration. Patrick Steinhardt follows up on his satirical MD5 hash algorithm patch (sent April 1) to note that despite being a joke, the implementation actually works with only 15 test failures. He highlights this as evidence of Git's improved hash algorithm agility, crediting brian m. carlson's foundational work on making the codebase hash-agnostic.

While maintaining the lighthearted tone, Steinhardt transitions to a serious observation: the exercise revealed how feasible it would be to add a third hash algorithm optimized for speed (rather than cryptographic security) for internal use cases. He explicitly marks this as a topic for future discussion, separating it from the April Fools' context.

The email serves both as a playful conclusion to the joke thread and a subtle demonstration of Git's technical flexibility, while carefully distinguishing between satire and genuine engineering considerations. The maintainer's earlier minimalist response (a single wink emoji) had already signaled the thread's non-serious nature, making this follow-up more about the underlying technical point than continuing the humor.
