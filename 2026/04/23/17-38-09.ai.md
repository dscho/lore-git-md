This is a technical discussion about extending the cherry-pick prefetch optimization to `git rebase`, following up from the recently completed performance optimization series for `git cherry` and `git grep` in partial clones.

Elijah Newren responds to Phillip Wood's questions about generalizing the prefetch logic. He confirms that while rebase's patch ID computation does path-based filtering (only looking at commits modifying relevant files), it still requires walking and tree-diffing all upstream commits - an operation that can become expensive in very large repositories. 

The exchange highlights two practical barriers to wider adoption of this optimization:
1. The lack of a config setting for `--reapply-cherry-picks` (currently only available as a CLI option)
2. The apply backend's incompatibility with this feature

Elijah shares context about the original motivation for the `git cherry` optimization (a specific customer workflow) and explains why he's reluctant to pursue the rebase optimization himself - the performance impact is less clear and there are alternative workflows available. He notes that GitHub's focus is primarily server-side, and the client-side investigation was driven by a special case.

The discussion remains technically aligned but pragmatically divergent - Phillip sees potential value in extending the optimization while Elijah prefers to leave that work to future contributors. The thread surfaces important implementation considerations (path filtering, config options, backend support) that would need to be addressed in any future generalization effort.
