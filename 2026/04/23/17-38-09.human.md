This is a follow-up discussion in the performance optimization series for partial clone behavior, specifically addressing potential extensions to `git rebase`.

Elijah Newren responds to Phillip Wood's questions about generalizing the cherry-pick prefetch optimization to `git rebase`. While the technical path is clear (the current implementation could be adapted), Elijah maintains his position that this may not be worth pursuing. He explains that even with path-limited optimizations, rebase would still need to examine every upstream commit's tree, which could be expensive in very large repositories. 

The exchange reveals some workflow context: GitHub's primary focus on server-side operations means they haven't seen many client-side rebase complaints, and one particular customer's use of `git cherry` (rather than rebase) motivated the current optimization. Elijah notes this customer could potentially switch to using `git replay` with its empty-commit handling (added by Phillip), but he's hesitant to push workflow changes.

The discussion surfaces real constraints: the lack of a config option for `--reapply-cherry-picks` and its incompatibility with the apply backend. While Phillip expresses surprise about the lack of rebase performance complaints, Elijah's response suggests the optimization may remain narrowly scoped to `git cherry` unless others demonstrate concrete need. The technical groundwork is laid for future expansion, but with no immediate plans to implement it.
