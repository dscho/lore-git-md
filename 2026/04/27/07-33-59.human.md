This is a feature patch discussion about the `--reverse` option in Git's revision walk machinery, now in its final review stage. Johannes Sixt weighs in on Junio Hamano's suggestion to rename the `--reverse=before/after` options to `--reverse=oldest/newest` for better user clarity.

Johannes agrees that the current names are too implementation-focused, but raises concerns that the proposed alternatives might not be intuitive either. He questions what "reverting the oldest" would actually mean to users, suggesting that perhaps the functionality should be implemented as a separate `--max-count-oldest` option instead of modifying `--reverse`.

The discussion shows the project's careful attention to user interface design - even at this late stage where all implementation details have been settled through four iterations. The core question is how to expose this relatively advanced revision-walking capability in a way that makes sense to users who don't understand the internal mechanics. Johannes' comment suggests the naming debate may need more discussion before the feature can be finalized.

This exchange highlights how Git's most technical contributors still prioritize usability concerns, even small naming decisions receive scrutiny when they affect the end-user experience. The patch series appears technically sound but remains blocked on reaching consensus about the most intuitive interface.
