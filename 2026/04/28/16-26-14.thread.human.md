Johannes Schindelin continues his series addressing Windows' limitations with large objects (>4GB) during cloning, now in its second iteration with 11 patches. The core issue stems from 32-bit type truncation on Windows paths, which the series systematically fixes by introducing 64-bit size_t handling across multiple components including index-pack, zlib wrappers, and delta processing.

The discussion has evolved with substantive technical reviews from Torsten Bögershausen, who raised type precision questions in patch handling byte-reading variables and type safety concerns in pack-objects delta reuse paths. Meanwhile, Derrick Stolee's earlier concerns about test execution times led to optimizations like unsafe SHA-1 hashing (3-5x speedup) and precomputed packfiles (5-10x speedup), now gated behind CI's EXPENSIVE test flag for push builds only.

In the latest exchange, Schindelin clarifies for Stolee that the precomputed pack optimization's performance benefits (reducing test times from ~90s to ~15s) are independent of the SHA-1 hashing changes, which aren't available on Windows due to OpenSSL constraints. This maintains the series' tight focus on measurable improvements while addressing platform-specific limitations. Junio Hamano has weighed in on broader CI philosophy, emphasizing that contributors should catch issues pre-submission rather than relying on integration testing. The series remains technically sound but continues to refine implementation details through active review.
