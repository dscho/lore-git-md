This is a performance optimization discussion about the buffer size changes in `index-pack` and `unpack-objects`. Jeff King (Peff) provides additional technical analysis in response to Junio Hamano's earlier explanation of why the buffer size increase shows less improvement than theoretically possible.

Peff notes that the input to `index-pack` during a fetch comes through git-fetch's sideband demuxing, which typically flushes in 64KB packets. This limits how much `index-pack` can read at once unless it's processing slower than the input rate. He suggests the real bottleneck may be FUSE filesystem writes rather than pipe reads/writes, and that the sideband demuxing layer explains why the total write reduction wasn't as large as initially hoped.

The discussion remains technical and exploratory - Peff isn't proposing changes to the already-approved patch, but rather helping to understand its performance characteristics more precisely. He notes that while further syscall reductions might be possible by having `index-pack` handle demuxing directly, this likely wouldn't help much with the FUSE performance issues that motivated the original change.

This email represents substantive technical discussion from a domain expert (Peff has deep knowledge of Git's I/O layers) that helps clarify the optimization's boundaries without changing the consensus that the patch is ready to merge.
