This Git mailing list thread continues to debate Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving ~700 files into a new `lib/` directory, a refactoring-only effort aimed at improving discoverability for newcomers. The discussion remains polarized, with proponents (Steinhardt, Johannes Schindelin, brian m. carlson) arguing that the one-time disruption is justified by long-term clarity, while critics (Junio C Hamano, Taylor Blau, Phillip Wood) emphasize the ongoing mental overhead of file moves and the structural flaws of the `lib/` approach.

Junio’s latest reply engages constructively with Michael Montalbo’s data-driven counterproposal, which uses commit history patterns and call-graph relationships to identify cohesive subsystems for incremental reorganization. Junio calls the methodology a "valid and interesting thought experiment" and praises its concrete, intuitive results—particularly the "transport" group (`connect.c`, `fetch-pack.c`, etc.)—as proof that mechanical signals can surface meaningful groupings. However, he cautions that the approach’s reliance on header inclusion patterns could be fragile if files include headers for isolated definitions, suggesting that human review will still be needed to refine the groupings. This reply shifts the debate from *whether* to reorganize to *how*, offering a potential path forward that aligns with Junio’s preference for incremental, motivated changes over sweeping mechanical moves.

The thread now hinges on whether Montalbo’s methodology can address Junio’s caveats and gain broader community support. Junio also hints at a future reorganization target: decoupling the `bisect` machinery from `revision.c`, framing it as a cleanup opportunity rather than a blocker. Meanwhile, the `lib/` proposal remains deadlocked, with Schindelin and Steinhardt doubling down on the newcomer-discoverability rationale but failing to address Junio’s structural critique or the persistent workflow disruptions (e.g., `git log --follow`, rebasing challenges). The timing of the patch—sent close to the upcoming release—suggests Steinhardt aims to land it early in the next cycle if accepted, but Junio’s and Taylor Blau’s skepticism may prove decisive. Montalbo’s counterproposal introduces a third way, but its traction depends on whether the community engages with its technical merits.
