Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory has reached its second version, addressing all prior feedback and completing the mechanical relocation of ~700 files. The goal—reducing root directory clutter to improve shell tab completion and clarify the `libgit.a` boundary—has been met with no outstanding technical objections, and the series has been tested across GitLab and GitHub CI pipelines. Johannes Schindelin has preliminarily approved the transition for Git for Windows, while Derrick Stolee’s earlier concerns about merge conflicts for long-lived forks (e.g., GitHub, Microsoft Git) are being mitigated through incrementalism and Patrick’s offer to assist.

However, Junio C Hamano has now weighed in with skepticism about the timing and practical impact of the change. While he acknowledges the intent to improve navigability, he questions whether the reorganization will meaningfully achieve this goal and highlights the disruption it will cause to in-flight patches and backporting efforts, particularly during the prerelease freeze period. His tone suggests reluctance, though he stops short of outright rejection, noting that he has historically accepted changes he personally disagreed with. The focus of his concern is logistical—coordination with ongoing development—rather than technical, leaving the door open for further discussion about transition timing and downstream compatibility. For now, the series remains in limbo, with its fate hinging on whether the perceived benefits outweigh the disruption to the project’s workflow.
