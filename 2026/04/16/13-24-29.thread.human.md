Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving core components into a new `lib/` directory continues to spark intense debate, with the latest reply from Johannes Schindelin hardening the pro-reorganization camp’s position. The series, now in its fourth iteration, remains a **refactoring-only effort** aimed at reducing root-directory clutter to improve discoverability for newcomers, but the discussion has become a philosophical standoff over whether the benefits justify the disruption. Schindelin’s response frames the current disorganized tree as an "ongoing burden" that outweighs the one-time cost of the patch, arguing that even an imperfect `lib/` directory makes entry points like `builtin/` and `Documentation/` more visible. He dismisses critics’ concerns about rebasing and tooling gaps as short-sighted, positioning the patch as a necessary first step toward deeper reorganization—though he stops short of addressing Junio C Hamano’s core objection: that the `lib/` directory is an arbitrary intermediate layer that fails to clarify the codebase’s architecture.

The debate remains **polarized and deadlocked**, with proponents (Schindelin, Patrick Steinhardt, brian m. carlson) prioritizing structural cleanup and newcomer onboarding, while critics (Junio, Taylor Blau, SZEDER Gábor, Phillip Wood) emphasize the **persistent mental overhead** of file moves and the lack of meaningful organization. Junio’s latest critique, which shifts the focus from workflow disruption to **structural design**, carries significant weight; he argues that the patch may worsen navigation by forcing contributors to re-learn the structure later, and prefers incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem moves (e.g., migrating `refs/` or `odb/` directly to root-level directories when they undergo major changes). Schindelin’s reply doubles down on the "entry points" rationale—highlighting how a tidier root directory could make files like `RelNotes` more discoverable—but does not engage with Junio’s alternative proposal or the long-term friction of file moves (e.g., `wt-status.c` → `lib/wt-status.c`). His suggestion that the patch’s disruption could spur tooling improvements (e.g., better `git log --follow`) also sidesteps whether such changes are realistic.

The series itself consists of two patches: a preparatory fix for include paths and the main patch moving ~700 files into `lib/` while preserving internal structure. The relocation is **purely mechanical**, updating build systems, CI workflows, and `.gitmodules` without modifying symbols, APIs, or on-disk formats. Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a hint in `git show` (e.g., suggesting `lib/wt-status.c` when a file appears renamed) remains a potential middle ground, though its feasibility is unclear. For now, the patch’s fate hinges on whether Junio’s maintainer-level skepticism prevails or whether the community rallies around an alternative—such as incremental moves or tooling improvements—to address the root-directory clutter without the disruption of a mass rename. The timing of v4, sent close to the upcoming release, suggests Patrick aims to land this early in the next cycle if accepted, but the deadlock shows no signs of breaking.
