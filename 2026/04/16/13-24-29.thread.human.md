Patrick Steinhardt has proposed reorganizing Git's source tree by moving libgit.a components into a new "lib/" directory, now in its second iteration. The change would relocate about 700 files from Git's root directory while maintaining their internal structure, primarily to reduce root directory clutter that interferes with shell completion. The proposal has gained support from Junio Hamano based on concrete usability benefits, with Phillip Wood and others providing substantive technical feedback that has been addressed in the current version.

Derrick Stolee's recent follow-up acknowledges the benefits but raises important considerations about the practical impact of such a large-scale change. He highlights potential merge conflicts with in-flight patches and challenges for long-lived forks when rebasing. Stolee suggests timing the change after a major release to minimize disruption and floats the idea of going further by also moving command implementations into a "cmd/" directory if the community sees value in a more comprehensive reorganization.

The core proposal appears technically sound with no outstanding objections, but Stolee's email shifts the discussion toward execution planning - particularly how to minimize ecosystem disruption during the transition. The thread has moved from design consensus to practical considerations about timing and coordination with downstream consumers.
