Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory continues to spark intense debate, with the latest exchange sharpening the philosophical divide. The series, now in its fourth iteration, remains a **refactoring-only** effort aimed at improving discoverability for newcomers by reducing root-directory clutter. Patrick’s most recent reply doubles down on this rationale, arguing that a cleaner root directory—even without deeper classification—makes the codebase’s entry points (like `builtin/` and `Documentation/`) more visible, which he sees as inherently valuable for onboarding. He acknowledges the subjectivity of the change but insists that the one-time disruption is justified by the long-term benefit of a less overwhelming starting point for new contributors.

The core disagreement has narrowed to a fundamental question: whether a *mechanical* reorganization (moving files into `lib/` without deeper semantic grouping) is worthwhile as a first step, even if further changes are needed later. Junio C Hamano, however, remains unconvinced, arguing that the `lib/` directory is an **arbitrary intermediate layer** that fails to clarify the project’s architecture and may even worsen navigation by forcing contributors to re-learn the structure later. His preference for **incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem moves** (e.g., relocating `refs/` or `odb/` to the root when they undergo major changes) remains unaddressed in Patrick’s latest reply, leaving the deadlock intact. Junio’s maintainer-level skepticism carries significant weight, suggesting the patch is unlikely to progress without a fundamental redesign or a compelling counterargument.

The debate remains polarized, with proponents like Johannes Schindelin and brian m. carlson prioritizing structural cleanup and newcomer onboarding, while critics like Taylor Blau and SZEDER Gábor emphasize the **ongoing mental overhead** of file moves and the **structural flaws** of the `lib/` approach. Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a hint in `git show` to suggest renamed file paths (e.g., `lib/wt-status.c` instead of `wt-status.c`) remains a potential middle ground, though its feasibility is still uncertain. For now, the series is stalled, with Patrick’s latest reply reinforcing his stance but failing to bridge the gap with Junio’s concerns. The patch’s fate likely hinges on whether Patrick can propose a compromise—such as incremental moves or tooling improvements—or whether the community rallies around an alternative. Given Junio’s and Taylor’s skepticism, the series may need significant revision to move forward.
