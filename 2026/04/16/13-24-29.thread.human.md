Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series (v3) to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving core components into a new `lib/` directory continues to spark debate, with the latest input from Phillip Wood adding fresh nuance to the discussion. The series aims to reduce root-directory clutter and establish a clearer boundary for Git’s core library code, but the trade-offs—disrupted history tracking, rebasing challenges, and workflow friction—remain contentious.

Phillip Wood’s review challenges the series’ primary justification, arguing that moving files into `lib/` does little to improve discoverability since the number of files remains the same. Instead, he suggests that tools like `git grep` or `ctags` are better suited for navigating the codebase. However, he finds merit in the idea of a dedicated `lib/` directory as a way to enforce coding conventions, such as avoiding `the_repository` or `die()` in library code, calling this a stronger rationale than mere discoverability. His review also highlights a practical downside: the mental overhead of remembering where files have moved, illustrated by his own difficulty locating `wt-status.c` in its new location. This adds to the existing concerns about disrupted workflows, such as `git log --follow` and rebasing, framing them as recurring annoyances rather than one-time costs.

The discussion remains polarized. Proponents like Patrick Steinhardt, Oswald Buddenhagen, and Johannes Schindelin argue that the reorganization improves code structure and could incentivize tooling improvements, while opponents like Junio C Hamano, SZEDER Gábor, and Derrick Stolee emphasize the disruption to ongoing work and historical tracking. Phillip’s input shifts the focus toward whether the structural benefits—particularly enforcing library conventions—justify the ongoing friction. The series itself is a pure mechanical relocation, updating build system files and CI workflows without changing symbols, APIs, or on-disk formats. Open questions include whether the tooling-incentive argument gains traction, how to mitigate rebasing pain for downstream consumers, and whether the logistical disruption outweighs the structural benefits, especially during the prerelease freeze.
