This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series (now at v4) to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving all `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory—a refactoring-only effort aimed at reducing root-directory clutter and enforcing coding conventions. The debate has grown increasingly polarized, with key maintainers split over whether the benefits justify the disruption.

Junio C Hamano, the project’s maintainer, remains the most vocal critic, arguing that the reorganization creates *ongoing* friction for contributors and maintainers—disrupting `git log --follow`, complicating rebases, and introducing merge conflicts—without delivering meaningful structural improvements. His latest reply challenges the patch’s core rationale, noting that the `lib/` directory merely shifts clutter one level deeper rather than solving it. He proposes an alternative: incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem moves (e.g., `refs/`, `odb/`) directly to the root, avoiding the intermediate `lib/` layer entirely. This reflects his broader preference for stability and motivated, incremental changes over sweeping mechanical moves.

Supporters like Johannes Schindelin and brian m. carlson frame the reorganization as a long-overdue cleanup, arguing that the one-time cost is justified by permanent improvements in codebase hygiene and discoverability. However, critics like SZEDER Gábor and Phillip Wood counter that the disruption is *persistent*, not temporary, and question whether the tradeoff is worthwhile. Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a hint in `git show` to suggest renamed file paths (e.g., `lib/wt-status.c`) remains a potential middle ground, though its feasibility is unproven.

The series itself is a pure mechanical move, updating build systems, CI workflows, and `.gitmodules` to reference the new paths, with no functional or API changes. The debate hinges on whether the structural benefits—such as enforcing coding conventions (e.g., avoiding `the_repository` in library code)—outweigh the ongoing friction, particularly during the prerelease freeze. Junio’s skepticism and preference for incremental changes suggest the patch is unlikely to land without significant revision or a stronger justification. For now, the discussion remains deadlocked, with no clear path forward.
