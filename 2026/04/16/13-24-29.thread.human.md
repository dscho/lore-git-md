Patrick Steinhardt's RFC patch series (v4) to reorganize Git's source tree by moving all `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory continues to spark intense debate, with no clear path toward resolution. The series is framed as a refactoring-only effort to improve discoverability for newcomers by reducing root-directory clutter, but critics—led by Junio C Hamano—argue that the `lib/` directory is an arbitrary intermediate layer that fails to deliver meaningful organization. Junio's latest reply doubles down on this critique, proposing a concrete alternative: a **subsystem-by-subsystem approach** that moves specific components (e.g., `refs/`, `odb/`) to well-defined directories when they undergo major changes, rather than a sweeping mechanical move to `lib/`. This alternative sidesteps the `lib/` directory's structural flaws while preserving the ability to iterate, aligning with Junio's preference for incremental, motivated changes over sweeping mechanical moves.

The debate remains polarized. Proponents like Johannes Schindelin and brian m. carlson argue that the current disorganized source tree imposes an "ongoing burden" on newcomers and that the one-time disruption of the patch is justified by permanent improvements in discoverability. Schindelin dismisses critics' objections as short-sighted, framing the `lib/` move as a necessary first step toward deeper reorganization. However, Junio's latest reply flips this argument: the current patch is not a minimal first step but a **maximalist move** that preempts future decisions by committing to an arbitrary intermediate layer. His alternative preserves the flexibility to refine the structure incrementally, avoiding the need for contributors to re-learn the layout later. Taylor Blau and Phillip Wood echo Junio's skepticism, questioning whether the reorganization's benefits justify the ongoing mental overhead of file moves (e.g., `wt-status.c` → `lib/wt-status.c`) and the disruption to workflows like `git log --follow`.

The series itself consists of two patches: a preparatory fix to `t/helper/test-example-tap.c` and the main patch moving ~700 files into `lib/` while preserving internal structure. The relocation is purely mechanical, updating build system files, CI workflows, and `.gitmodules` to reference the new paths. No symbols, APIs, or on-disk formats are modified. Proposed UX improvements, such as Kaartic Sivaraam's hint in `git show` to suggest renamed file paths, remain speculative and have not been implemented. The timing of v4—sent close to the upcoming release—suggests Patrick aims to land this early in the next cycle if accepted, but Junio's and Taylor's maintainer perspectives may prove decisive. Without a fundamental redesign or a compelling counterargument to Junio's incremental alternative, the patch is unlikely to progress. The deadlock persists, with the pro-reorganization camp yet to address Junio's structural critique or the long-term mental overhead of file moves.
