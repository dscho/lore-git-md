Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory continues to spark intense debate, with the latest exchange sharpening the divide over whether the change is worth its costs. The series, now in its fourth iteration, remains a **refactoring-only effort** aimed at reducing root-directory clutter to improve discoverability—particularly for newcomers—while preserving internal structure and avoiding functional changes. Patrick’s latest reply reframes his motivation explicitly around **onboarding friction**, arguing that the current layout overwhelms outsiders, though he offers no concrete examples to support this claim. This shift in emphasis has done little to bridge the gap with critics like Junio C Hamano and Taylor Blau, who remain unconvinced that the benefits justify the disruption.

The core disagreement now centers on **two irreconcilable perspectives**: Patrick and supporters (Johannes Schindelin, brian m. carlson, Oswald Buddenhagen) see the reorganization as a necessary cleanup that trades short-term pain for long-term clarity, while Junio and Taylor view it as an **arbitrary and ongoing burden** that fails to deliver meaningful organization. Junio’s critique—that the `lib/` directory is an ill-defined intermediate layer that doesn’t solve the problem it claims to address—remains unanswered, as does his preference for incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem moves (e.g., relocating `refs/` or `odb/` directly to the root). Taylor’s skepticism about the patch’s impact on newcomers, given that many moved files aren’t suitable for external linking, also lingers. Meanwhile, SZEDER Gábor’s objection that the disruption is **persistent** (not one-time) and Phillip Wood’s concern about recurring mental overhead from file moves highlight the practical costs that continue to weigh against the patch.

The series itself is mechanically sound: it moves ~700 files (core subsystems like `object.c`, `refs.c`, and utility files like `strbuf.c`) into `lib/` while updating build systems, CI workflows, and `.gitmodules`. No symbols, APIs, or on-disk formats are altered. However, the **structural flaws** of the approach—particularly the ambiguity of the `lib/` directory—remain the biggest obstacle to acceptance. Junio’s and Taylor’s maintainer perspectives carry significant weight, and their reluctance to endorse the patch suggests it may stall unless a compromise emerges. Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a `git show` hint for renamed files (e.g., suggesting `lib/wt-status.c` instead of `wt-status.c`) could mitigate some workflow friction, but its feasibility and performance implications are still unclear. For now, the thread remains **deadlocked**, with Patrick’s newcomer-focused rationale unlikely to sway critics who prioritize stability and incremental progress over sweeping mechanical changes. The patch’s fate may hinge on whether Junio’s incremental alternative gains traction or if a tooling solution like Kaartic’s can address the practical pain points.
