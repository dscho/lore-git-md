Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving core `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory continues to face strong opposition, with the debate now firmly deadlocked. The series, which frames the mass rename as a one-time cost justified by long-term improvements in code organization and discoverability, has drawn sharp criticism from Junio C Hamano, who argues that the disruption is ongoing and the structural benefits illusory. Junio’s latest critique—endorsed by Taylor Blau in a substantive review—challenges the very premise of the `lib/` directory, calling it an arbitrary intermediate layer that fails to meaningfully organize the codebase. Both maintainers now advocate for an incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem approach (e.g., moving `refs/` or `odb/` directly to the root) as a less disruptive alternative, though no one has yet volunteered to drive such an effort.

The technical details remain unchanged: the patch moves ~700 files (including core subsystems like object handling, refs, diff, and utility files like `strbuf.c`) into `lib/` with no functional changes, updating build systems and CI pipelines to match. Proponents like Johannes Schindelin and brian m. carlson argue the reorganization enforces coding conventions (e.g., avoiding `the_repository` or `die()` in library code) and reduces root-directory clutter, while critics like SZEDER Gábor and Phillip Wood emphasize the persistent mental overhead of file moves and the lack of realistic tooling improvements. Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a hint in `git show` suggesting renamed paths (e.g., `lib/wt-status.c`) remains a potential middle ground, but its feasibility is untested.

The core dispute now centers on whether the patch’s benefits outweigh its costs. Junio and Taylor Blau dismiss the discoverability argument, noting that relocating files into `lib/` does little to clarify the project’s structure for newcomers, and Taylor further questions the directory’s name, given that many moved files aren’t suitable for external linking. With the series sent close to the upcoming release, its chances of landing in the next cycle appear slim unless Patrick revisits its structural goals or justification. For now, the thread remains polarized, with no clear path toward consensus.
