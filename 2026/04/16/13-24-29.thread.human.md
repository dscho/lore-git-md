Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory is now facing skepticism from Junio C Hamano over its practical benefits versus the disruption it would cause to in-flight patches and backporting efforts. The series itself—now at v2—has addressed all prior technical feedback, including Meson build logic, ODB source handling, and submodule complications, with no outstanding objections from reviewers. The reorganization aims to reduce root directory clutter and establish a clearer boundary for `libgit.a`, but Junio has framed it as a cosmetic change that may not justify the maintenance overhead, particularly during the prerelease freeze.

Patrick’s latest reply pushes back on Junio’s concerns, arguing that Git’s resistance to handling file moves gracefully is itself a usability issue the project should confront. He suggests that experiencing this pain firsthand—akin to other projects that reorganize their code—would motivate improvements to Git’s tooling (e.g., `git diff`, `git rebase`) to better handle such changes. While he acknowledges the logistical challenges, he positions the reorganization as an opportunity to identify and fix gaps in Git’s functionality, not just a cosmetic cleanup. Feedback on the series has been more positive than expected, but Patrick remains open to the community rejecting the idea.

The discussion now hinges on whether the structural benefits outweigh the disruption, with timing and coordination emerging as key concerns. Downstream consumers should monitor whether Patrick’s argument for tooling improvements gains traction or if the focus remains on the immediate overhead.
