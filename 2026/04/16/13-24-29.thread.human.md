Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving core library files into a new `lib/` directory continues to spark debate over whether the benefits justify the disruption. The series, now at v4, aims to reduce root-directory clutter and enforce coding conventions like avoiding `the_repository` or `die()` in library code, but critics argue the mass rename creates ongoing workflow friction—disrupting `git log --follow`, complicating rebases, and forcing contributors to constantly adjust to new file paths.

SZEDER Gábor’s latest reply reinforces the skepticism, arguing that the costs of the reorganization aren’t one-time but will persist indefinitely. He dismisses Patrick’s suggestion that the pain could spur tooling improvements (like better rename detection) as unrealistic, questioning who would implement such changes and whether they’d even work. Gábor’s stance aligns with earlier critiques from Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood, who’ve highlighted the mental overhead of remembering file moves (e.g., `wt-status.c` → `lib/wt-status.c`) and suggested non-structural alternatives like documentation or tooling tweaks. Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a hint in `git show` to suggest renamed paths remains a potential middle ground, though its feasibility is still unclear.

The thread remains at an impasse, with proponents like Patrick and Oswald Buddenhagen prioritizing long-term codebase hygiene and critics like Gábor, Junio, and Phillip Wood emphasizing contributor ergonomics. The timing—sent close to the upcoming release—suggests Patrick is aiming to land this early in the next cycle if consensus emerges, but no clear path forward has materialized. The debate now hinges less on technical details and more on whether the structural benefits outweigh the ongoing friction for contributors.
