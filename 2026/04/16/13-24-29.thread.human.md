This thread remains a polarized debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series (v4) to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving ~700 files into a new `lib/` directory. The goal is to reduce root-directory clutter and improve discoverability for newcomers, but critics—led by Junio C Hamano—argue the `lib/` directory is an arbitrary intermediate layer that fails to deliver meaningful organization and imposes ongoing mental overhead for contributors.

The latest development comes from Michael Montalbo, who advances a **data-driven counterproposal** to Junio’s preferred subsystem-by-subsystem approach. Montalbo’s refined methodology combines **commit history patterns** (weighted by commit size) and **call-graph relationships** (from `cscope`) to mechanically identify 14 cohesive subsystems—such as `transport/`, `index/`, and `revision/`—with higher cohesion scores than existing directories like `refs/` or `odb/`. The script groups 163 of Git’s 231 `libgit.a` files, leaving 68 utility files (e.g., `strbuf.c`, `hashmap.c`) ungrouped due to weak signals. Boundary cases, like `shallow.c` (called by `transport/` but labeled `revision:`), are flagged as review targets, offering a roadmap for refinement. This approach avoids the arbitrariness of `lib/` while enabling incremental progress, appealing to both Junio’s preference for motivated moves and Patrick’s "start somewhere" pragmatism.

The debate remains deadlocked, with proponents (Patrick, Johannes Schindelin, brian m. carlson) emphasizing structural cleanup and newcomer onboarding, while critics (Junio, Taylor Blau, Phillip Wood, SZEDER Gábor) highlight the disruption to workflows like `git log --follow` and the `lib/` directory’s semantic ambiguity. Montalbo’s proposal introduces a **third way**, but its reliance on a script and technical rigor may limit immediate traction. Junio’s and Taylor’s maintainer perspectives will likely prove decisive, as their skepticism about the reorganization’s merits and preference for incremental changes remain unaddressed. The series is unlikely to progress without a fundamental redesign or a compelling counterargument to Junio’s structural critique.
