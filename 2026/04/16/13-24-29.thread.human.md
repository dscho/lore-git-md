Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving all `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory remains deadlocked, with the debate now centering on whether the change actually improves the codebase’s structure—or makes it worse. The series, now in its fourth iteration, is framed as a way to reduce root-directory clutter and improve discoverability for newcomers, but Junio C Hamano and Taylor Blau continue to challenge both the rationale and the execution. Junio’s latest reply argues that the `lib/` directory is an arbitrary intermediate layer that fails to clarify the codebase’s architecture, forcing contributors to relearn the structure later when further reorganization becomes necessary. He contrasts this with past subsystem moves (like `builtin/`, `refs/`, or `odb/`), which were tied to substantial updates and created intuitive groupings. The patch’s mechanical relocation of ~700 files, he argues, trades short-term disruption for long-term confusion rather than solving the problem it claims to address.

The series itself consists of two patches: a preparatory fix for include paths and the main patch moving core subsystems (object handling, refs, diff, merge, platform compatibility shims, and utility files like `strbuf.c` and `hashmap.c`) into `lib/` while preserving internal structure. The change is purely mechanical, updating build systems, CI workflows, and `.gitmodules` to reference the new paths, with no functional or API changes. Proponents—including Johannes Schindelin, brian m. carlson, and Oswald Buddenhagen—argue that the current disorganized state is a persistent burden that justifies the one-time cost, while critics like Junio, Taylor, SZEDER Gábor, and Phillip Wood emphasize the ongoing mental overhead of file moves and the disruption to tools like `git log --follow`. Gábor, in particular, dismisses the idea that the patch will spur tooling improvements, calling the tradeoff "not worth it."

The discussion has shifted from workflow disruption to structural design, with Junio’s maintainer perspective carrying significant weight. His preference for incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem moves (e.g., relocating `refs/` or `odb/` when they undergo major changes) remains a potential alternative, though Patrick has not yet engaged with this idea. Meanwhile, Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a hint to `git show` when a file appears renamed (e.g., suggesting `lib/wt-status.c` instead of `wt-status.c`) could mitigate some workflow friction, but its feasibility hinges on efficient rename detection. The patch’s fate likely depends on whether Patrick can address Junio’s structural concerns or whether the community rallies around an alternative approach. For now, the series remains under active debate with no clear consensus, and the timing of v4—sent close to the upcoming release—suggests Patrick aims to land it early in the next cycle if accepted.
