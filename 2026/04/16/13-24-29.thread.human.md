Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series (v4) to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving all `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory continues to spark intense debate. The proposal aims to reduce root-directory clutter—shrinking the number of files from ~550 to ~80—and enforce clearer boundaries for Git’s core library code, but critics argue the disruption outweighs the benefits. Johannes Schindelin and brian m. carlson have doubled down on their support, framing the current disorganized state as an "ongoing burden" that justifies the one-time cost of the move. However, Junio C Hamano’s latest reply sharply challenges this framing, calling the fallout an *ongoing* maintenance headache. As the maintainer, Junio reports spending significant time daily resolving conflicts caused by the patch, particularly when new topics touch moved files or introduce files that should also be relocated. His skepticism centers on the lack of clear organizational improvement, contrasting the `lib/` move with past subsystem relocations (e.g., `builtin/`, `refs/`) that were tied to substantive updates rather than mechanical churn.

The core disagreement remains polarized: proponents (Steinhardt, Schindelin, carlson) prioritize structural cleanup and codebase hygiene, while critics (Junio, SZEDER Gábor, Phillip Wood) emphasize the persistent mental overhead of file moves and the disruption to tools like `git log --follow`. Gábor’s earlier objection—that the costs are not one-time but recurring—now gains weight from Junio’s firsthand account of daily friction. Meanwhile, Kaartic Sivaraam’s proposal to add a hint in `git show` suggesting renamed file paths (e.g., `lib/wt-status.c` instead of `wt-status.c`) remains a potential middle ground, though its feasibility hinges on efficient rename detection. The series itself is a pure mechanical move, updating build systems, CI workflows, and paths without altering symbols or APIs, but the debate has shifted from technical details to the broader question of whether the benefits justify the ongoing disruption.

With Junio’s maintainer-level pushback, the patch’s prospects appear dim unless proponents can address the practical concerns about rebasing pain and tooling limitations. The timing—sent close to the upcoming release—suggests Steinhardt may be aiming for early-cycle consideration, but the lack of consensus and Junio’s preference for stability over "prettiness" make this unlikely without further refinement. For now, the thread remains deadlocked, with no clear path forward.
