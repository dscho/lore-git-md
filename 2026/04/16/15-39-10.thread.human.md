Runxi Yu has uncovered an unexpected limitation in Git's handling of tree objects. While `git cat-file -p` can show a tree's contents normally, both `git checkout-index` and `git archive` fail to properly extract files when given a tree hash directly. 

The behavior is particularly puzzling for `checkout-index`, which claims to check out "all files in the index" with the `-a` flag, yet leaves files unextracted even after `read-tree` loads the tree into the index. The issue persists across both SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories, suggesting a fundamental behavior rather than a hash-specific bug.

This appears to be either an undocumented limitation (that these commands only work with commits, not trees) or a genuine inconsistency in Git's object handling. The thorough report includes verification of object existence and index contents, but no maintainers or regular contributors have yet weighed in on whether this is working as intended. The author, who is new to the list, has requested replies be CC'd to them.
