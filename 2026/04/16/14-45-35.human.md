This is a process discussion about improving patch submission workflows, following up on the recently merged autostash feature series. Junio Hamano responds to Harald Nordgren's suggestions for making version-to-version changes more readable in cover letters.

Junio expresses skepticism about purely technical solutions like a proposed `git range-diff --rebase` option or GitGitGadget enhancements. He argues that even with such tools, the key issue remains - distinguishing between mechanical caller updates (like consolidating helper functions) versus substantive changes requires human judgment. The maintainer reiterates that thoughtful author commentary remains essential to guide reviewers through non-obvious changes.

The exchange highlights Git's emphasis on clear communication in patch submissions, where even after successful merges, contributors and maintainers discuss how to improve future collaboration. While technical solutions are worth exploring, Junio maintains that human-written context in cover letters remains irreplaceable for effective code review. This reflects the project's culture of valuing both technical excellence and clear communication in the contribution process.
