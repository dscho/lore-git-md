This is a process discussion about improving patch submission workflows, following up on the recently merged autostash feature series. Junio Hamano responds to Harald Nordgren's suggestions for better diff presentation in cover letters.

Junio expresses skepticism that automated tools could effectively distinguish between meaningful changes (like consolidating two helper functions into one) and the resulting mechanical updates to callers. He argues that even with identical base versions, a range-diff would still show the caller updates as noise, making it hard to spot the structural improvement. His key observation is that no tool can replace the author's judgment in highlighting what matters - the cover letter commentary remains essential for guiding reviewers to the important changes.

The exchange shows the project's attention to both technical implementation and contribution process quality. While the autostash feature itself is complete, this follow-up discussion focuses on making future contributions more reviewable. Junio's response demonstrates the maintainer's perspective on the limits of automation in code review - some human judgment will always be needed to separate signal from substance in patch evolution.
