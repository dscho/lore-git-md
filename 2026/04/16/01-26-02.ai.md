This is a follow-up in the ongoing `git subtree` bug thread, where Colin Stagner responds to Ian Jackson's original report about split failures with merge-heavy histories. The email provides technical context and proposes a solution.

Colin identifies that the recursion depth issue in Git 2.45.2 stems from Debian's POSIX shell implementation artificially limiting recursion to 1000 calls, which is not typical behavior in other distributions. He references a patch series he's proposed to address this specific limitation. The patch appears to target the shell recursion aspect rather than the underlying merge-handling logic.

For the older Git 2.20.1 behavior (incorrect history inclusion), Colin notes that even with his fixes, the split operation still yields over 12,000 commits rather than the expected few dozen. He traces this to commit e7b07376e5 ("Merge branch 'rs/subtree-fixes'"), which changed how merge bases are handled in subtree operations. The email suggests that determining "important" history becomes inherently ambiguous when merges are involved, implying this may be a straightforward bug but rather a fundamental limitation in how subtree splits interact with merge commits.

The response advances the thread by both offering a concrete fix for one failure mode (recursion depth) and providing context for why the other (history inclusion) may not have a clear resolution. Colin positions his patch as addressing the immediate technical blocker while acknowledging the deeper architectural challenges around merge handling in subtree operations.
