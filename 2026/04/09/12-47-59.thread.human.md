This thread addresses a platform-specific compatibility issue where NonStop systems have strict `MAX_IO_SIZE` limits that aren't properly respected in Git's `writev()` implementation. Patrick Steinhardt proposed adding an `xwritev()` wrapper to handle these cases safely, similar to how `xwrite()` already manages size limits for regular writes.

The discussion has involved detailed technical review from Jeff King (Peff) and Junio C Hamano, focusing particularly on ensuring robust overflow protection when summing iovec lengths. Peff suggested restructuring the logic to count down remaining bytes rather than summing totals, making the overflow protection more transparent while maintaining all safety properties. This approach has now been incorporated into the patch, with both reviewers agreeing the new structure properly handles all edge cases. Junio had initially raised concerns about the overflow checking but has since confirmed the solution's correctness.

The patch will be deferred until after the Git 2.54 release, with NonStop users advised to use the `NO_WRITEV` workaround in the meantime. The remaining open questions are minor, primarily around final comment wording, and the timing for inclusion in a future release. Randall S. Becker, who originally reported the NonStop issue, hasn't commented on the latest refinements but the solution appears to address his initial concern.
