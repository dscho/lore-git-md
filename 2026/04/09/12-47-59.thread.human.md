This thread addresses a platform-specific issue with `writev()` on NonStop systems, where the I/O size limits are smaller than on other platforms. Patrick Steinhardt proposed adding an `xwritev()` wrapper to handle these limits consistently, similar to how `xwrite()` already does for regular writes. The patch will coalesce iovec entries under the platform's `MAX_IO_SIZE` limit and fall back to `xwrite()` for oversized entries, with careful error handling.

Junio C Hamano approved the general approach but requested specific improvements before merging, particularly around overflow protection and comment clarity. The patch will be deferred until after the Git 2.54 release, with NonStop users advised to use a `NO_WRITEV` workaround in the meantime.

Jeff King (Peff) recently weighed in on the overflow checking implementation, confirming that the current approach is correct but could be clearer. He suggested restructuring the logic to count down remaining allowed bytes rather than summing up total length, which would make the overflow protection more transparent while also simplifying the special case for oversized first entries. The discussion remains highly technical, with all participants aligned on the need for robust handling of platform limits while maintaining consistency with Git's existing I/O patterns.
