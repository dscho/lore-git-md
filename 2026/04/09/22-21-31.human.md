This is a follow-up discussion about the `writev()` compatibility issue on NonStop systems, where Jeff King (Peff) suggests a more targeted revert approach. 

Jeff agrees with Junio Hamano's assessment that the current `writev()` implementation needs to be addressed, either through proper `MAX_IO_SIZE` support or a revert. However, he proposes a more surgical solution than Junio's full revert of four commits - suggesting instead to only revert the sideband usage of `writev()` (commit 26986f4cba) while leaving the wrapper infrastructure in place. 

This would make the wrappers temporarily dead code, but Jeff argues it would simplify reintroducing proper `writev()` support later. The approach demonstrates Git's typical pragmatism - when faced with a platform compatibility issue, the project prefers minimal, reversible changes that unblock users while leaving the door open for better solutions. 

The discussion highlights the technical nuance in balancing platform support with performance optimizations, where even well-intentioned changes can have unintended consequences on less common systems. Jeff's suggestion shows his characteristic attention to minimizing disruption while maintaining forward progress.
