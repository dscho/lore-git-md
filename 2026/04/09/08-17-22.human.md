This is a platform compatibility discussion about `writev()` optimizations in Git's network code, continuing from test failures in Git 2.54.0-rc1 on NonStop systems. Patrick Steinhardt weighs in with a proposed solution to handle platforms with small SSIZE_MAX limits.

Patrick agrees with brian m. carlson's position that platforms with poor `writev()` support should use NO_WRITEV, but proposes a more sophisticated runtime solution as a long-term approach. His patch introduces an `xwritev()` wrapper that handles large writes by splitting them when they exceed MAX_IO_SIZE, falling back to `xwrite()` for oversized chunks. He's tested this with an artificially small MAX_IO_SIZE (128 bytes) to verify the fallback behavior.

The patch represents a middle ground between Jeff King's two proposed approaches - it keeps the `writev()` optimization for platforms that support it well while providing a runtime fallback for constrained systems. Patrick asks Randall to verify whether NonStop's MAX_IO_SIZE applies to individual iovec entries or the total size, as this affects the patch's applicability.

This continues the thread's central tension between performance optimization and broad platform compatibility. Patrick's solution attempts to preserve the optimization's benefits while accommodating platform limits, though it introduces some complexity. The discussion now awaits feedback from Randall about whether this approach works on NonStop systems.
