This is a maintainer follow-up on the `--reverse=before` feature discussion. Junio C Hamano engages with Tian Yuchen's performance concerns about loading the full reversed commit history into memory, but raises deeper questions about the feature's conceptual model.

Junio questions whether the current framing as a `--reverse` extension is the right approach, suggesting the behavior might be better understood as "late limiting" rather than "early reversing". He probes edge cases: what happens with `--max-count` alone versus combined with other limiting options like `--author` or `--min-parents`. This moves the discussion beyond implementation details to consider whether the feature's mental model aligns with how users think about commit selection.

The thread had previously reached technical consensus on implementation, but Junio's intervention indicates the conceptual design may need re-evaluation before finalizing. His questions imply the current approach might not cleanly handle all combinations of limiting options, and that the user-facing explanation could be clearer about what the option fundamentally changes in the walk behavior.
