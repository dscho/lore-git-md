Karthik Nayak's ref backend refactoring series continues to progress, with the latest discussion focusing on a stylistic question about typedef usage. The 8-patch series aims to consolidate object validation and peeling logic across Git's reference backends (files, packed, reftable) into a shared refs layer, eliminating backend-specific implementations that could diverge.

Patrick Steinhardt, who has been providing thorough reviews throughout the series, suggested expanding the scope of patch 1 (which removes an unused typedef) to eliminate all typedefs by inlining their definitions into the ref backend struct. In his latest reply, Karthik pushes back on this suggestion, arguing that typedefs serve a useful documentation purpose by abstracting function signatures while still making implementation requirements clear. He indicates this broader cleanup can be deferred to a future series, keeping the current changes focused on their primary goal.

The technical work appears sound, with all patches reviewed except the final two (7-8), which complete the architectural shift by moving peeling operations to the shared layer. Patrick's feedback has been largely positive on the technical direction while suggesting minor wording and implementation refinements. The remaining open questions are primarily about whether to incorporate some of Patrick's non-blocking suggestions in a potential v3, but the core changes seem ready for integration. This continues the project's ongoing effort to unify ref backend behavior while maintaining compatibility.
