This is a review of Derrick Stolee's patch adding the `--negotiation-require` option to `git fetch`, part of a series improving fetch/push negotiation for monorepos. Patrick Steinhardt raises two main points:

First, he suggests the option name `--negotiation-require` might be misleading, as it could imply the fetch would abort if the required refs aren't present (like `--require` in other contexts). He proposes alternatives like `--negotiation-include` or `--negotiation-expand` that might better convey the additive nature of the feature.

Second, he questions whether the implementation should flush the packet buffer when sending many required refs, though he concludes this may not be necessary given the feature's purpose of sending all specified refs. This is a substantive technical consideration about protocol behavior under heavy load.

The review engages with the patch's core design choices while acknowledging the technical soundness of the implementation. Patrick's naming concern reflects careful attention to user experience and consistency with Git's existing CLI patterns, while his buffer-flush question shows he's thinking through edge cases of the proposed behavior.
