This is a maintainer follow-up on the subtree recursion limit patch series, highlighting the lack of review engagement for an under-maintained component. Junio C Hamano reiterates his concern about merging changes to `contrib/subtree` without review from active users, despite the patches appearing technically sound. 

The core issue remains unchanged: Colin Stagner's series replaces recursive processing in `git subtree split` to avoid shell recursion limits on Debian/Ubuntu systems. Junio notes the first two patches seem benign (refactoring trailer processing and functionalizing traversal logic) but declines to assess the core algorithmic change in patch 3/3, emphasizing his lack of subtree expertise. 

The key development is Junio's explicit call for volunteers to review the changes, framed as a test case for how the project handles patches to components without active maintainers. The technical solution appears complete but risks stalling without validation from subtree users. This follow-up underscores the project's reliance on domain experts for niche features, even when the maintainer is willing to trust apparently clean patches. The ball remains in the court of subtree users to either approve the changes or request modifications.
