This is a follow-up in Patrick Steinhardt's series to enable `set -e` (errexit) in Git's test suite, specifically addressing platform-specific shell behavior. Patrick now expresses growing skepticism about the approach, echoing Jeff King's earlier concerns about inconsistent shell implementations.

The key development is Patrick's shift from blanket `set -e` adoption to proposing shell-specific allowlisting, suggesting limiting it to Bash 5+ where behavior is more consistent. This represents a significant concession from the original series goal, acknowledging that cross-platform differences (particularly macOS's outdated version) make universal `set -e` problematic.

The email reveals two important technical considerations: first, that test failures would still be caught by CI jobs using modern Bash even if other platforms skip `set -e`, and second, that the maintenance burden of handling all edge cases may outweigh the benefits. This aligns with Jeff's earlier position that simpler approaches like stderr checking might achieve similar robustness with less complexity.

The discussion now centers on finding a pragmatic middle ground - maintaining the error-checking benefits where possible while avoiding platform-specific headaches. Patrick's willingness to adjust the approach based on implementation challenges demonstrates the project norm of technical correctness over rigid adherence to initial designs. The unresolved question is whether this narrowed scope still provides enough value to justify the series.
