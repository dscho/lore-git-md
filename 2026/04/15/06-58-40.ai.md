This is a follow-up discussion about enabling `set -e` (errexit) in Git's test suite, specifically addressing shell behavior quirks. The exchange between Patrick Steinhardt, Junio Hamano, and Jeff King has revealed platform-specific inconsistencies in how shells handle error propagation within functions when `set -e` is active.

Patrick notes he's observed shells where `set -e` does propagate into functions, contrary to the POSIX example Junio cited. This discovery reinforces his decision to narrow the scope of the change to only enable `set -e` for Bash 5+ in CI environments, where the behavior is more predictable. The approach balances error-checking benefits against platform compatibility constraints, particularly macOS's outdated Bash version.

The thread exemplifies Git's pragmatic approach to cross-platform challenges - when universal adoption proves infeasible due to shell implementation differences, the project opts for partial adoption where it provides value (modern CI environments) while avoiding brittle behavior elsewhere. This follows the pattern seen throughout the series of adapting ambitious goals to technical realities while preserving core benefits.
