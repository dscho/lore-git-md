This is a performance optimization series from Derrick Stolee that aims to make `git rev-list --maximal-only` faster by leveraging the existing `merge-base --independent` algorithm when possible.

The cover letter explains that `rev-list --maximal-only` and `merge-base --independent` produce equivalent results when no other walk modifiers are present, but the merge-base version can be faster when refs share most of their history. The series first adds tests to verify this equivalence, then introduces a performance benchmark, and finally implements the optimization by checking for incompatible walk options and falling back to the faster algorithm when safe.

The three patches follow a logical progression: establishing test coverage, measuring current performance, then implementing the change with its expected speedup. Stolee notes the patches could theoretically be squashed but are kept separate for clarity. The changes touch the rev-list builtin and add tests in both functional and performance test suites.

This appears to be a well-structured optimization targeting a specific but potentially impactful case where two Git commands can share algorithmic work. The approach is methodical - first proving correctness, then measuring, then optimizing - which suggests it's likely to be well-received. The optimization is particularly relevant for large repositories where the history walk dominates performance.
