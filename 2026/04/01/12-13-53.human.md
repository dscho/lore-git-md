This is a follow-up in the reftable portability series, specifically addressing compiler compatibility concerns. Patrick Steinhardt responds to brian m. carlson's question about why the `REFTABLE_INLINE` macro is needed given C99's universal `inline` support. 

Patrick explains that libgit2 (one of the target consumers of this reftable work) still maintains C90 compatibility and supports niche platforms like Amiga OS, though he expresses some skepticism about whether this support is actively tested. The exchange highlights the tension between modern compiler capabilities and the project's commitment to broad portability.

The response doesn't fundamentally change the technical approach - the macro remains justified for libgit2 compatibility - but it provides important context about why this abstraction layer exists. The tone is matter-of-fact, acknowledging the validity of brian m. carlson's point while standing by the design decision for this specific use case. This patch remains part of the larger effort to make reftable usable as a shared library between Git and libgit2.
