This is a review response in Jeff King's (Peff) series addressing C23 const-correctness warnings. Phillip Wood raises a concern about patch 8's clever macro solution for `skip_prefix()` type safety.

Phillip notes that while the CONST_OUTPARAM macro is technically clever, it subtly changes `skip_prefix()`'s documented behavior by potentially evaluating the out-parameter even when the function returns false. This could affect any of the ~600 existing call sites that rely on the out-parameter remaining untouched on failure. He suggests exploring type-generic macros as an alternative, despite their added complexity, since they wouldn't alter the function's behavior.

The comment reflects Phillip's characteristic attention to subtle behavioral contracts and his preference for solutions that don't introduce new edge cases, even when fixing compiler warnings. His feedback demonstrates the careful balancing act required when modifying widely-used helper functions like `skip_prefix()`, where even small behavioral changes can have far-reaching consequences.
