This is a follow-up in the reftable portability series, specifically addressing compiler compatibility concerns raised about the `REFTABLE_INLINE` macro. Patrick Steinhardt responds to brian m. carlson's question about the necessity of abstracting the `inline` keyword by pointing out that libgit2 still targets C90 compatibility, including support for niche platforms like Amiga OS. His reply acknowledges the theoretical nature of this concern while maintaining the justification for the abstraction layer.

The exchange highlights the practical tension between modern compiler capabilities (where C99's `inline` is indeed universally supported) and the project's commitment to broad portability. Patrick's response suggests the abstraction serves libgit2's needs more than Git's own requirements, reinforcing that this series aims to create shared infrastructure between the two projects. His slightly skeptical tone about actual Amiga OS usage indicates he may view this as more of a principle than a pressing practical concern, but the architectural decision to maintain the abstraction stands.

This reply doesn't resolve the underlying philosophical question about where to draw the portability line, but it does clarify that libgit2's compatibility requirements are a key driver for the design. The thread continues to show the careful balancing act between modern practices and legacy support that characterizes low-level systems work in Git.
