This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing debate about `git checkout` versus `git switch`/`git restore`. Junio C Hamano responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's earlier commentary by clarifying the project's stance on these commands.

Junio pushes back against the characterization of `git checkout` as "superseded" in the BreakingChanges document, arguing that this framing implies an eventual removal that is neither planned nor desired. He emphasizes that all three commands remain equally viable, describing the original goal of replacing `checkout` with `switch`/`restore` as a "misguided attempt" that didn't work as intended. While he notes that `switch` and `restore` themselves are not failures, he makes clear that the project no longer views them as replacements for `checkout`.

The email reinforces Junio's earlier rejection of making `git switch` configurable to match `checkout`'s behavior, framing it as part of a broader realization that the commands serve different mental models. For users who conceptually distinguish between checking out files and branches, `checkout` remains the appropriate tool. This authoritative statement effectively closes any remaining ambiguity about the project's direction on this topic.
