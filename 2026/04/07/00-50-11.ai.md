This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing debate about `git checkout` versus `git switch`/`git restore`. Junio C Hamano responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's commentary on the thread, clarifying the project's stance on these commands.

Junio pushes back against the characterization of `git checkout` as "superseded" in the BreakingChanges document, arguing that this framing implies an eventual removal that is neither planned nor desired. He emphasizes that all three commands (`checkout`, `switch`, and `restore`) remain equally viable options, with no clear consensus that the newer commands have succeeded in replacing the older one. While he doesn't consider `switch`/`restore` to be failures in themselves, he characterizes the attempt to replace `checkout` with them as a "misguided attempt" that didn't achieve its goal.

The email reinforces Junio's earlier rejection of making `git switch` more `checkout`-like through configuration, as it would undermine the distinct design purposes of each command. His position is that for users who conceptually understand both file-checkout and branch-switching operations, there's no compelling reason to abandon `git checkout` in favor of the newer commands. This maintains the status quo where all three commands coexist without any being officially favored over the others.

The discussion remains closed, with Junio's response serving as the authoritative final word on the matter unless new technical arguments emerge. The exchange highlights the tension between documentation that suggests a migration path and the reality that `git checkout` remains widely used and functionally equivalent to its newer alternatives.
