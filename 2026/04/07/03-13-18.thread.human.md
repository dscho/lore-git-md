This thread explores how Git should handle change-id headers during commit rewriting operations like rebase and cherry-pick - metadata that tools like Gerrit, GitButler and Jujutsu rely on. The discussion had been moving toward a policy-based approach where projects could define rules for preserving these identifiers, with Nico Williams proposing a concrete design involving project policies and named hooks. Junio C Hamano maintained his preference for using trailers rather than headers, while Phillip Wood's analysis of three key scenarios (cherry-picks, splits, and squashes) framed the technical requirements.

The conversation has now taken a sharp turn with brian m. carlson's intervention, which challenges the fundamental premise that preserving change-ids is desirable. They raise privacy concerns about tracking and information leakage, drawing parallels to past metadata issues and suggesting the feature should be opt-in rather than default. This introduces new governance questions about whether and how such metadata should be handled at all, complicating the previously emerging technical consensus. The thread now balances between Nico's policy mechanism design, Junio's trailer preference, Phillip's scenario analysis, and brian m. carlson's privacy objections - with no clear resolution yet in sight.
