This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about preserving change-id headers during commit rewriting operations. Nico Williams responds to Junio Hamano's concerns by proposing additional configuration options and header variants to handle the semantic complexities.

The email suggests two new concepts: first, a configurable approach where users could specify which operations should preserve change-id headers (copy vs dropping them), and second, the introduction of an `original-change-id` header that would track the lineage of commits across operations where the main change-id might legitimately change. This represents a more nuanced approach than the original patch's blanket preservation of change-id headers.

Nico's proposal acknowledges Junio's concerns about semantic consistency by suggesting hooks that could modify header behavior during operations. The mention of relating commits that undergo transformation indicates awareness of the inheritance and tracking challenges Junio raised. However, the email doesn't yet address the deeper design questions about whether Git should be opinionated about change-id formats or behaviors.

This moves the discussion toward a potentially more flexible solution, though it introduces new complexity points around configuration management and the relationship between multiple header types. The thread remains in design discussion territory rather than implementation, with core questions about Git's role in change-id handling still unresolved.
