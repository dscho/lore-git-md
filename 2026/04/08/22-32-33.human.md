This is a platform compatibility discussion about the `writev()` optimization in Git's network code, specifically regarding NonStop systems with small `SSIZE_MAX` limits. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Junio Hamano's skepticism about keeping the `writev()` optimization given the partial write handling complexity.

Peff analyzes the tradeoffs: while `writev()` provides performance benefits by halving write calls for sideband packets, it works reliably only on platforms that can handle 64k writes or have reasonable `MAX_IO_SIZE` limits. He proposes two potential solutions - either declaring `writev()` unsupported on problematic platforms via `NO_WRITEV`, or implementing a runtime check that falls back to individual `write()` calls when needed.

The discussion reveals deeper concerns about using `writev()` for unbounded data in the future, suggesting this may be an ongoing source of platform compatibility issues. Peff leans toward simplicity, noting the marginal performance gain on constrained platforms may not justify the complexity. The exchange highlights how performance optimizations can conflict with broad platform support, with both contributors showing willingness to revert the change if it proves too problematic.
