The negotiation-control series from Derrick Stolee (at v3, with v4 planned but not posted) is moving forward. Matthew Cheetham reviewed all seven patches, finding bugs in patches 4 and 5 and raising a design consistency question. Stolee has now replied to Cheetham's most substantive review covering patch 5, accepting every point.

Cheetham caught a real abstraction leak in patch 5: the `--negotiation-include` code sets a COMMON flag (bit 6 in `fetch-pack.c`) that the default negotiator's `get_rev()` checks at a different bit (bit 2 in `negotiator/default.c`), meaning protocol v0/v1 could produce duplicate "have" lines. Stolee called it "a great catch" and plans a structural fix - adding a callback to the negotiator struct so the consumer can notify the negotiator of chosen "have" lines without the consumer directly manipulating flags. On the design inconsistency (why does `--negotiation-restrict` resolve refs in the builtin layer but `--negotiation-include` passes raw strings to transport?), Stolee agrees and will unify the resolution layer in v4. Documentation mismatches, missing test coverage for silent-ignore of missing refs, and a typo were all acknowledged and will be fixed.

Cheetham's review has substantially shaped the v4 direction beyond what Stolee had already planned. The series remains ready for integration once v4 addresses: the negotiator abstraction change, unified ref resolution, the bugs and fixups from patches 2-4 (already described by Stolee in earlier replies), and the remaining surface issues in patches 5-6. No other participants have weighed in.
