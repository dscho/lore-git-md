This is a platform compatibility discussion about `writev()` system call limitations on NonStop systems, continuing from earlier in the thread.

Jeff King confirms Randall S. Becker's report that the NonStop platform's `SSIZE_MAX` of 53248 bytes is indeed smaller than Git's packet-line protocol assumption of 64k maximum packet size. This explains why the `writev()` optimizations introduced in Git 2.54.0-rc1 are failing on NonStop systems - the vectorized writes are exceeding the platform's hard size limit.

The exchange highlights a fundamental mismatch between Git's network protocol design and platform constraints that weren't visible in the code's assumptions. While the immediate issue can be worked around with `NO_WRITEV=Nope`, this raises deeper questions about whether the `writev()` optimization is worth keeping if it requires handling partial writes across different platforms. The discussion remains technical and focused, with Jeff validating Randall's findings while acknowledging the underlying compatibility challenge.
