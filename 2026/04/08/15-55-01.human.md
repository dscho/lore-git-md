This is a follow-up in an ongoing thread about SIGPIPE handling in Git hooks, where a fix for pre-receive hooks introduced a regression in pre-push hooks. Randall Becker reports that what initially appeared to be an infinite hang in test t5571.11 ("sigpipe does not cause pre-push hook failure") actually completes successfully after a delay. This suggests the issue may be as severe as initially feared.

The test in question involves a pre-push hook that exits without reading its input, similar to the pre-receive case that originally triggered the fix. While the hang was concerning, the eventual completion indicates the underlying mechanism is working, albeit with unexpected delays. This observation helps narrow the scope of the regression - it's a performance/blocking issue rather than a deadlock.

The thread continues to involve key contributors: Jeff King (Peff) who proposed the original SIGPIPE fix, Adrian Ratiu who is working on parallel hooks, and Randall Becker testing on NonStop systems. With this new data point, the pre-push case appears to work correctly in principle, though the delayed execution warrants further investigation to understand why the operation takes longer than expected.

This update suggests the fix may be viable for inclusion after all, though the performance characteristics need to be examined. The original plan to proceed with the fix (either as a standalone patch or as part of the parallel hooks series) can likely move forward, with potential follow-up work to optimize the timing behavior.
