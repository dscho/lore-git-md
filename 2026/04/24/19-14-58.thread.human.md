Scott Bauersfeld proposed optimizing `index-pack` and `unpack-objects` by increasing their I/O buffer sizes from 4KB to 128KB, matching Git's existing hashfile buffer size. Benchmarks showed a 66% reduction in pack file writes during HTTPS clones of the git/git repository, with all clones passing verification checks. The implementation introduces a shared buffer size constant across both commands.

Maintainer Junio Hamano has reviewed the patch and generally approves the change, while making two technical observations. He notes a minor documentation correction - `unpack-objects` writes loose objects rather than pack files - and questions why the measured 66% write reduction falls short of the theoretical ~97% improvement expected from the buffer size increase. Junio suggests investigating whether small writes that can't benefit from larger buffers might explain the difference, proposing additional metrics. He also floats the idea of centralizing the 128KB buffer size into a shared header as a potential follow-up, though doesn't consider this blocking. The review maintains a constructive tone while probing for deeper understanding of the performance characteristics.

The patch appears technically sound and likely to be merged after addressing Junio's questions about the performance measurements. The documentation correction is straightforward, while the performance analysis may require additional benchmarking to fully explain the observed versus expected improvements.
