Scott Bauersfeld's performance optimization for `index-pack` and `unpack-objects` has reached merge-readiness after three iterations, with Junio Hamano and Derrick Stolee approving the change to increase I/O buffer sizes from 4KB to 128KB. Benchmarks show 10-11% faster clones on FUSE filesystems, though the theoretical maximum improvement isn't fully realized due to architectural constraints.

Jeff King (Peff) has now provided additional technical context explaining why the optimization's impact is bounded. During fetches, `index-pack` receives data through git-fetch's sideband demuxer, which flushes in 64KB packets regardless of the downstream buffer size. This means the buffer optimization primarily benefits the packfile writing phase where larger contiguous writes can accumulate, rather than the network transfer portion. Peff's analysis confirms the bottleneck lies in FUSE filesystem writes rather than pipe I/O, validating the focused nature of the improvement.

The thread now concludes with a complete picture: while the change delivers measurable gains for its target use case, the layered architecture of Git's fetch/pack pipeline means some syscalls remain unavoidable. All maintainers agree the patch is ready to merge, with the understanding that further I/O optimizations would require changes to the sideband protocol or demuxer behavior.
