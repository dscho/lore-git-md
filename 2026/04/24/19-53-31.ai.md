This is a review of D. Ben Knoble's per-worktree ignore patterns patch, with Phillip Wood raising concerns about the design approach. The review is substantive, engaging with the technical merits of the proposal rather than surface-level issues.

Phillip questions the fundamental need for per-worktree ignore patterns, noting that shared patterns across worktrees may be preferable to avoid repetitive configuration. He suggests the current implementation could cause problems by reusing the same path (`info/exclude`) for both shared and per-worktree patterns, making it impossible to have both simultaneously. Instead, he proposes using a distinct path like `info.worktree/exclude` to allow coexistence of shared and worktree-specific patterns.

The review also hints at a broader architectural concern - that this pattern of `.worktree` suffixes could proliferate if other worktree-specific files (like attributes or sparse-checkout patterns) are added later. This connects to brian m. carlson's earlier suggestion in the thread for a more general `extensions.worktreeInfoFiles` mechanism rather than an ignore-specific solution.

Phillip's feedback indicates the patch may need reconsideration of its file location strategy and whether it should be part of a more comprehensive worktree configuration framework. His comments suggest the current approach could create future compatibility headaches if the design isn't generalized upfront. The review maintains a constructive tone while raising significant technical concerns that would require design iteration to address.
