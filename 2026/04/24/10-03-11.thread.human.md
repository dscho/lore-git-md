This thread began as a proposal to extend `git checkout` and `git switch` with a `--track=fetch` option, which would automatically fetch the latest version of a remote branch when creating a local tracking branch. The goal was to address a common workflow pain point: users often forget to fetch before checking out a remote branch, leading to stale local copies and unnecessary merge conflicts. The series was split into two parts—a refactoring to expose tracking remote lookup functions (1/2) and the feature implementation itself (2/2).

The technical work was completed and approved by Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood in earlier versions, with all feedback addressed by v15. However, the feature’s inclusion has been contentious from the start. Junio has consistently opposed it on workflow grounds, arguing that automatically fetching during checkout could encourage "blind updates" by discouraging users from inspecting upstream changes before forking. He views the current two-step process (`git fetch` followed by `git checkout`) as a deliberate design choice that promotes safer practices. Harald Nordgren, the author, reframed the debate as a question of whether Git should serve its users’ practical needs or enforce an idealized workflow, citing his own experience and anecdotal evidence from corporate environments where frequent integration is prioritized over careful inspection. D. Ben Knoble added nuance by noting that developers in such environments often skip inspecting upstream changes when forking, while Kristofer Karlsson argued the use case is better addressed outside Git’s core (e.g., as a Git alias).

Junio has now formally marked the series for discard in the upcoming "What’s cooking" report. His final decision hinges on a specific workflow concern: automatically fetching the remote branch could silently update other branches that forked from the same remote-tracking ref, making them appear stale without the user’s awareness. He dismissed the two-command workaround as a feature rather than a flaw, reinforcing his view that the current design encourages safer practices. The series is technically complete and uncontroversial, but the merge decision was ultimately a matter of maintainer discretion over workflow trade-offs. Unless new arguments are presented, the feature will not be merged.
