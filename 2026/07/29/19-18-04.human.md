This is a substantive follow-up in the ongoing RFC discussion about allowing `git index-pack` to accept duplicate objects. Taylor Blau responds directly to Junio C Hamano’s philosophical objection, pushing back on the idea that historical concerns should dictate the project’s stance and arguing that Git is now better equipped to handle duplicate objects safely.

Blau’s reply makes three key points. First, he cites a 2014 thread where Jeff King (Peff) argued that even "buggy" packs containing the only copy of an object must be readable, suggesting that the current `--strict` vs. non-strict distinction already reflects a pragmatic trade-off. Second, he describes real-world scenarios—like servers appending existing packs to avoid costly repacks in large, fast-moving repositories—where allowing duplicates could yield meaningful performance gains. Third, he proposes a narrower design: `--allow-duplicate-objects` should carve out *only* the duplicate-OID check from `--strict`, preserving all other validation (connectivity, delta resolution, object integrity), rather than making the two options mutually exclusive.

The tone is constructive and forward-looking. Blau acknowledges Junio’s concerns but frames the feature as a controlled, opt-in exception that builds on existing hardening work (his `tb/pack-with-duplicates` series). He also hints at a future where Git’s own `upload-pack` might use this capability to save CPU cycles, suggesting the patch could eventually benefit upstream Git, not just out-of-tree optimizations. The ball is now back in Junio’s court to either accept the refined design or articulate why even this narrower exception remains unacceptable.
