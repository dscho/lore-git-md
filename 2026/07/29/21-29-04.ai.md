This is a substantive follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread about allowing Git to accept packfiles containing duplicate objects. Taylor Blau (ttaylorr@openai.com) replies to Friel’s earlier message, which had agreed with Junio C Hamano’s suggestion that duplicates should be deduplicated during `git index-pack` rather than stored on disk.

Taylor reinforces his support for the underlying goal of the patch series—better handling of duplicate objects—but clarifies that this is a defensive measure rather than an encouragement to generate duplicates. He acknowledges Junio’s concern about preventing duplicates from proliferating ("these cannot go forever unchecked") but argues that the feature can coexist with a strong normative stance against duplicates. His tone is pragmatic: the patch is not about endorsing duplicates but about ensuring clients can recover gracefully when they encounter them, similar to Jeff King’s earlier argument in the thread.

The key technical question remains unresolved: how to handle deduplication at the client side. Taylor suggests that repacking the entire pack (as Friel proposed) might be overkill if only a few duplicates are present, and floats the idea of efficiently removing duplicates during the fetch/clone stream. This aligns with Junio’s preference for deduplication during indexing but leaves the implementation details open. He also reaffirms his support for the `tb/pack-with-duplicates` series, which this patch builds on, as a foundation for safer handling of duplicates.

The email is brief but substantive, bridging the gap between Junio’s philosophical objection and Friel’s willingness to redesign the feature. It keeps the discussion focused on the practical trade-offs (performance vs. integrity) and signals that the next step is to explore lightweight deduplication mechanisms. No new code or test implications are introduced, but the thread’s direction is now clearer: the feature will likely evolve toward deduplication during indexing, with Taylor and Friel collaborating on the implementation. The tone remains constructive and welcoming, reflecting the project’s norms for newcomers.
