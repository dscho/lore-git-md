This is a **substantive review** of the `git add --resolved` series (`v2`), focusing on the user-facing interface and conceptual design. Michael Montalbo raises a thoughtful critique of the `--resolved` option name, arguing that it conflates two distinct operations: selecting unmerged paths (a structural property of the index) and enforcing a conflict-marker safety policy (a content-based check).

The review suggests an alternative design: split the functionality into a more general `--unmerged` option (which would stage all unmerged paths by default) and allow explicit policy overrides like `--allow-conflict-markers` or `--skip-conflict-markers`. This would decouple the selection mechanism from the safety check, making the command more flexible and the naming more precise. Montalbo acknowledges the workflow-oriented appeal of `--resolved` but notes that the current implementation is fundamentally about selecting unmerged paths and applying a marker check, not about determining whether conflicts are semantically "resolved."

The feedback is well-reasoned and targets a core design decision. It does not dispute the technical correctness of the series but questions whether the interface aligns with the underlying implementation. This could become a point of contention in further review, as naming choices in Git often carry long-term implications for user expectations and future extensibility. The review is not a blocking objection but a request for reconsideration of the command's conceptual model.
