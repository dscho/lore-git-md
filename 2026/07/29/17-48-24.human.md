This is a **maintainer process discussion** sparked by the recent ejection of `hn/history-squash` from `next` after Phillip Wood’s review exposed fundamental correctness flaws. Junio proposes a temporary moratorium on accepting new topics into `seen`—even those he has personally spotted—until they receive a substantive review from someone other than himself. The goal is to break the cycle where patches languish in "Needs review" for so long that he feels pressured to promote them to `next` after only a superficial read, a habit that led to the `hn/history-squash` episode.

The tone is pragmatic rather than punitive: Junio acknowledges the project’s reliance on a small pool of reviewers and the time constraints that force him to triage topics based on impressions rather than deep scrutiny. His proposal is a self-imposed brake, not a policy change—he is not closing the list to new patches, but he will stop queuing them in `seen` until they attract at least one substantive review. This could slow the flow of new topics into the integration pipeline, but it may also incentivize more contributors to engage with patches earlier in their lifecycle.

For readers tracking Git’s development, this is a signal that the bar for entering `next` (and eventually `master`) is effectively rising. Topics that previously might have advanced on Junio’s cursory approval will now need to demonstrate broader community engagement before they are even queued. The trade-off is clear: fewer half-baked patches in the pipeline, but potentially longer delays for well-crafted ones that lack a champion. The discussion also underscores the fragility of the review process—Phillip’s thoroughness on `hn/history-squash` was the exception, not the rule, and the project’s ability to catch such flaws depends on contributors like him stepping up consistently.
