This is a follow-up in the ongoing `pack.allowDuplicateObjects` RFC thread, where Junio C Hamano responds to Taylor Blau’s defense of the feature. Junio acknowledges the potential performance benefits of allowing servers to send packs with duplicate objects (e.g., concatenating packs to avoid deduplication overhead) but draws a hard line: such packs must be deduplicated *during* `index-pack` rather than stored on disk. He reiterates his philosophical objection to allowing "broken" packs to persist, even as an opt-in feature, and suggests that the longer-term direction should be to reject duplicates in *all* `index-pack` modes—including shallow and filtered clones—while providing tools to deduplicate packs at the receiving end.

Junio’s reply does not engage with Taylor’s proposed design refinement (e.g., making `--allow-duplicate-objects` a carve-out from `--strict` mode) or the technical hardening demonstrated in `tb/pack-with-duplicates`. Instead, he frames the issue as a correctness trade-off: while concatenating packs may be a cheap optimization, it risks proliferating "broken" packs unless the receiver actively deduplicates them. His tone is firm but pragmatic, leaving the door open for a solution that deduplicates during indexing rather than storing duplicates. The ball is now back in the author’s court to either propose a deduplication mechanism or justify why storing duplicates is unavoidable for the performance use case.

Key unresolved questions:
- Can the feature be redesigned to deduplicate objects during `index-pack` (as Junio suggests) without sacrificing the performance gains?
- If not, is the performance benefit sufficient to justify storing duplicates, given the risks of proliferation?
- Should Git’s longer-term direction be to reject duplicates in *all* `index-pack` modes (including shallow/filtered clones) and diagnose existing duplicates in `fsck`?

The thread remains at an impasse, but Junio’s suggestion of deduplicating during indexing offers a potential path forward if the author can demonstrate its feasibility. No new technical details or test implications are introduced in this reply.
