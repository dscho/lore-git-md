This is a follow-up in the ongoing RFC discussion about whether Git should allow packfiles containing duplicate objects, even as an opt-in exception. Taylor Blau (ttaylorr@openai.com) replies to Friel’s earlier message, which had agreed with Junio C Hamano’s suggestion that duplicates should be deduplicated *during* `git index-pack` rather than stored on disk.

Taylor reinforces his support for the underlying goal—better handling of duplicate objects—but tempers it with Junio’s philosophical objection: Git should not encourage or normalize invalid packfiles, even if it can safely recover from them. He suggests that the existing `tb/pack-with-duplicates` series (which demonstrates Git’s ability to handle duplicates safely) is still worth merging as a defensive measure, while the current RFC patch’s opt-in allowance for duplicates should be reconsidered. The key distinction is between *recovering* from duplicates (acceptable) and *allowing* them (not acceptable).

The discussion now centers on how to deduplicate objects efficiently during indexing. Taylor notes that repacking the entire pack just to remove a few duplicates would be wasteful, hinting at a preference for a more surgical approach—perhaps plucking duplicates out of the stream during fetch or clone. This aligns with Friel’s earlier idea of marking packs as "dirty" for later cleanup, but neither has yet proposed a concrete mechanism. The tone remains collaborative, with Taylor welcoming Friel to the mailing list and signaling openness to further refinement.

For a busy reader, this email clarifies that the debate is no longer about *whether* to deduplicate, but *how*—and whether the feature should remain opt-in at all. The patch’s future hinges on demonstrating that deduplication can be done efficiently without encouraging bad practices. If you care about packfile handling or server-side optimizations, this thread is worth watching, but the current design is unlikely to land as-is.
