This is a **substantive review** of the `hn/branch-delete-merged` topic, part of Junio’s July 2026 "What’s cooking" report. Phillip Wood identifies two key issues in the 7-patch series that adds `--delete-merged` to `git branch`:

1. **Upstream push-check logic**: The current implementation’s method for determining whether pushing a branch would update its upstream is described as "dodgy" — a red flag for correctness. The review implies the check may be unreliable or incomplete, though no specific failure case is cited.
2. **Behavior change for merged hierarchies**: The series alters how Git handles branches that are both merged *and* upstream of other branches. Previously, only the topmost merged branch (the one directly upstream of an unmerged branch) was preserved; now, the entire merged hierarchy is kept. Phillip prefers the old behavior but concedes the new approach may have broader support.

The review is narrowly scoped to these two points, with no broader critique of the series’ design or implementation. Phillip’s tone is constructive, and he does not demand a reroll — suggesting the issues are fixable without a full rewrite. The topic remains in `next` (per Junio’s report), but this feedback may delay its graduation to `master` until the upstream-check logic is addressed. No test failures or edge cases are mentioned, so the concerns appear to be about design intent rather than outright breakage.
