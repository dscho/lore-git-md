This is a follow-up in the `pack.allowDuplicateObjects` RFC thread, part of the ongoing discussion about whether Git should allow packfiles containing duplicate objects as an opt-in performance optimization. The author (Friel) responds to Junio C Hamano’s suggestion that duplicates should be deduplicated *during* `git index-pack` rather than stored on disk.

Friel acknowledges Junio’s concern about preventing duplicates from proliferating across repositories and agrees that deduplication at the receiving end is the right long-term approach. They clarify that duplicates would only occur as an exceptional side effect of a server-side optimization (e.g., concatenating packs to avoid deduplication overhead during `upload-pack`), not as a normal operation. The trade-off is between the cost of deduplication (paid on every request) and the cost of handling duplicates (paid only when they occur). Friel plans to explore whether Git already has mechanisms to mark packs containing duplicates as "dirty" for later repacking or retransmission, ensuring the cost is only incurred when necessary.

The email is conciliatory and pragmatic, signaling willingness to redesign the feature to align with Junio’s philosophical objection. It does not propose a specific implementation but frames the next steps as a collaboration with Taylor Blau to determine the best way to handle deduplication at the client side. The tone is respectful and defers to the project’s norms, reflecting the author’s relative newcomer status. No new technical details or test implications are introduced, but the thread now has a clear direction: deduplicate during indexing rather than storing duplicates. The ball is back in the author’s court to propose a concrete mechanism.
