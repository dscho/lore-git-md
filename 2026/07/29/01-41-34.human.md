This is a brief but pointed reply from Junio C Hamano (the maintainer) to the RFC patch introducing `pack.allowDuplicateObjects` and the `--[no-]allow-duplicate-objects` flag for `git index-pack`. Junio expresses strong reluctance to accept the feature, framing it as a slippery slope that could complicate Git’s internals and undermine repository integrity. He references a past discussion with Shawn Pearce (a key early Git contributor) to underscore his long-standing opposition to relaxing packfile validation, even when opt-in.

The tone is firm but not dismissive: Junio acknowledges the value of *detecting* broken packs early but makes it clear that *accepting* them—even conditionally—is not aligned with his vision for Git. His message does not engage with the patch’s technical details or the specific use case (streaming packs from overlapping sources), instead focusing on the broader principle of strict validation. This suggests the feature faces an uphill battle unless the author can address his philosophical concerns or demonstrate that the trade-offs (e.g., performance vs. correctness) are unavoidable and well-justified.

For readers following the thread, this is a significant development: Junio’s stance is likely to shape whether the patch progresses beyond RFC status. The ball is now in the author’s court to either revise the approach (e.g., by tying the feature more tightly to existing strictness controls) or provide compelling evidence that the performance benefits outweigh the risks. The earlier substantive feedback from Taylor Blau (about the interaction with `--strict`) may now be overshadowed by this higher-level objection.
