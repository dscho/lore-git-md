This thread explores why Git defaults to showing the *author date* (when the commit was originally created) rather than the *committer date* (when it was last modified, e.g., by a rebase or merge) in commands like `git log`. Omri Sarig, the thread starter, observes that in rebase-heavy workflows—common in many teams—the committer date is often more meaningful, as it reflects when a commit was actually integrated into the main branch. His teams routinely override Git’s default to display the committer date, and he’s curious whether there are workflows where the author date is more useful, or if this choice is simply a legacy decision.

The question is open-ended, inviting perspectives from the community rather than proposing a change. So far, no responses have been posted, so the discussion’s direction will depend on who weighs in and what use cases they highlight. If you’ve ever wondered about this default or have strong opinions on which date is more useful, this is the thread to follow.
