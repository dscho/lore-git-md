This is a substantive review of Domen Kožar’s patch series introducing `post-worktree-add` and `post-worktree-remove` hooks. Phillip Wood, a seasoned reviewer with deep knowledge of the worktree and sequencer subsystems, engages directly with the design choices and edge cases raised in the cover letter.

Phillip endorses the need for a `post-worktree-add` hook, citing his own stalled patch as evidence that the feature is wanted. He confirms the current behavior—if `post-checkout` fails, the worktree is removed entirely—justifying the decision to skip the new hook in that case. He also flags a documentation gap: the relative order of `post-worktree-add` and `post-checkout` should be explicitly documented, since the code runs the new hook *before* the existing one, not after as the cover letter implied.

On `post-worktree-remove`, Phillip is neutral, noting that filesystem watches could achieve the same goal but conceding that a hook does no harm. His most pointed question concerns the pruning edge case: when the gitdir file is missing or corrupt, the hook receives an empty string, leaving it unable to identify which worktree was removed. This is a real usability issue that will likely draw further discussion.

The review is constructive and technically precise, focusing on correctness and clarity rather than style. It advances the thread by clarifying the intended behavior and surfacing a concrete edge case that needs resolution. Anyone following the series should pay attention to the hook-order documentation and the pruning path-resolution question.
