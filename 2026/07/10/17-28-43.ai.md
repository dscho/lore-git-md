This is the second patch in Kristofer Karlsson’s priority-queue performance series, now withdrawn by the author. The patch aimed to optimize `prio_queue_get()` by replacing the standard sift-down algorithm with a cascade-down approach for unfused calls, reducing comparison counts by 14–17% in benchmarks. However, follow-up testing by René Scharfe on Apple M1/M5 hardware revealed a puzzling 1% wall-clock slowdown despite the reduction in comparisons, suggesting a hardware-specific microarchitectural penalty—possibly from increased memory traffic or cache pressure in the new implementation. Kristofer, lacking Apple hardware to investigate further, concludes that the theoretical improvement does not justify the real-world regression and proposes dropping the patch entirely.

The decision to withdraw is pragmatic: the optimization’s benefits (a ~2% speedup on some hardware) are now offset by a measurable slowdown on Apple silicon, and the code change (20 lines added) is no longer clearly justified. The discussion highlights how micro-optimizations can have counterintuitive performance tradeoffs across different CPU architectures, even when algorithmic correctness is preserved. No further action is expected on this patch unless future changes to the priority queue’s usage patterns (e.g., more expensive comparator functions) revive the case for revisiting it. The first patch in the series (a refactoring to extract `sift_up()`) remains uncontroversial and may still be merged independently.
