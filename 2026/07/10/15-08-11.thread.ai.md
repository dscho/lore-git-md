Subject: Why does Git default to showing author date instead of committer date?
Series version: N/A (discussion thread, not a patch series)
Originating author: Omri Sarig
Thread type: design discussion / workflow question

Core problem or goal
The thread asks why Git’s porcelain commands (e.g., `git log`) default to displaying the *author date* rather than the *committer date*, and whether this choice is driven by specific workflows or is merely a historical artifact. Omri notes that in rebase-heavy workflows the committer date is often more relevant because it reflects when a commit was actually introduced into the main branch, whereas the author date remains static.

No decisions or agreements yet; the thread is in the open-discussion phase.

- Symbols: `GIT_AUTHOR_DATE` vs. `GIT_COMMITTER_DATE` environment variables; `--pretty=format:` placeholders `%ad` (author date) and `%cd` (committer date).
