This is a **substantive review** of the final patch in Pablo Sabater’s v8 series implementing cascading indentation for visual roots in `` `git log --graph` ``. The reviewer, Mirko Faina, identifies a **real edge case** where the feature fails: when using `` `--author` `` (or presumably other filters that exclude commits without explicitly filtering their parents), visual roots are not indented because the logic in `` `graph_is_visual_root_candidate()` `` only considers commits with *no parents at all* (`c->parents == NULL`) or boundary commits. In this scenario, the parents exist but are excluded by the filter, so the commit is not recognized as a visual root until the next commit is processed—too late to apply indentation.

The review is **technically precise**: Mirko traces the issue to the interaction between `` `simplify_commit()` ``, `` `get_commit_action()` ``, and the graph renderer’s lookahead buffer. The problem is not a simple oversight but a **design limitation** in how visual roots are detected. The current logic assumes that excluded parents will be filtered out *before* the commit is enqueued, but filters like `` `--author` `` operate *after* the commit is enqueued, leaving the parent pointers intact. This creates a gap between the graph renderer’s expectations and the revision walker’s actual behavior.

The patch series is otherwise **well-crafted and nearly complete**, with all known edge cases addressed except this one. The failure mode is subtle and likely affects other filters (e.g., `` `--grep` ``, `` `--since` ``) that exclude commits without modifying their parent lists. Mirko’s observation suggests the series may need a **follow-up patch** to broaden the definition of a visual root to include commits whose parents are excluded *at any stage* of the revision walk, not just during initial filtering. This would require plumbing additional state from the revision walker into the graph renderer, potentially complicating the lookahead buffer logic.

The review is **high-weight**: Mirko is not a frequent contributor, but the issue they raise is **reproducible, specific, and technically grounded**. It directly impacts the feature’s correctness and could lead to misleading graph output in common workflows. The series is otherwise ready for merging, but this feedback may delay it until the edge case is resolved.
