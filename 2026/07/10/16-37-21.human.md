This is the latest update in Kristofer Karlsson’s priority-queue optimization series, specifically the second patch that introduces the cascade-down optimization for unfused `prio_queue_get()` calls. René Scharfe reports unexpected benchmark results: while the patch reduces the total number of comparisons (from ~70M to ~60M for Git’s repo and ~474M to ~440M for Linux’s repo), it produces a consistent 1% slowdown in wall-clock time on Apple Silicon (M1/M5) despite no regression on x86. The slowdown appears in user time, not system time, and persists even when replacing struct assignments with a swap macro.

The patch itself remains technically sound and uncontroversial. It implements the well-documented bottom-up heapsort variant, replacing the standard sift-down with a cascade-then-sift-up strategy that René’s exhaustive testing confirms is strictly faster in terms of comparisons. The implementation is minimal (20 lines added, 2 removed) and preserves API compatibility. The only open question is whether the observed 1% slowdown on Apple Silicon is a real concern or a measurement artifact; René notes the Ryzen laptop is too noisy to confirm the regression, and the comparison counts clearly show the optimization is working as intended.

The thread has reached technical consensus on the approach, and the patch is ready to merge if Junio decides the modest performance improvement justifies the code change. The discussion now centers on platform-specific performance quirks rather than the patch’s correctness or design.
