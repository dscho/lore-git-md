This is a bugfix patch for the sequencer subsystem, specifically targeting `--autosquash` behavior in `git rebase`. The author, Farid Zakaria, addresses a long-standing edge case where `fixup!` or `squash!` commits that cancel out their target commit (e.g., a revert) are not handled according to the user's `--empty` option (`drop`, `keep`, or `stop`). Instead, Git currently halts the rebase with an error, making it cumbersome to back out changes from a series.

The patch modifies `sequencer.c` to correctly identify when a `fixup!` or `squash!` commit empties its target by comparing the index against the parent of `HEAD` (rather than `HEAD` itself) during amending. It also updates `allow_empty()` to classify such commits as "became empty" (subject to `--empty` handling) unless the target was already empty. The patch includes thorough test coverage in `t/t3415-rebase-autosquash.sh` and updates the documentation to clarify the new behavior.

This is the second iteration of the patch (`v2`), responding to substantive feedback from Phillip Wood. The key change is the addition of a *new* emptiness check (rather than modifying the existing one) to preserve the current behavior for `fixup!` commits that become empty on their own. This addresses Phillip's concern that the original patch would break his workflow, where empty commits are used as placeholders and later `fixup!` commits may themselves become empty. The patch also fixes a correctness issue by removing the `rewritten-pending` file when dropping commits, ensuring `--update-refs` and other consumers see the correct state.

The patch is well-motivated by a real-world workflow at Meta, where internal LLVM patches are managed as single commits using `fixup!` autosquash. The fix enables automatic dropping of both a commit and its revert when `--empty=drop` is used, reducing the need for manual intervention during rebases. While the patch still requires a force-push (as noted by Phillip), it improves ergonomics by eliminating one source of manual rebase conflicts.

The discussion remains open on a potential follow-up feature: a `revert!` autosquash directive, which would further streamline backing out changes. The author has deferred this to a future RFC, focusing first on landing the current fix. The patch looks technically sound and addresses a clear pain point, though the design trade-offs between the author's and Phillip's approaches highlight the complexity of balancing backward compatibility with new workflows.
