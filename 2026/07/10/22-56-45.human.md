This is a post-merge discussion about test structure conventions in Git's test suite, sparked by the recently merged commit-graph regression fix. Taylor Blau responds to Junio C Hamano’s caution about restructuring tests to split bug demonstration and fix across two patches. Junio had noted that if the test body is long, the patch diff might show only the `test_expect_failure` → `test_expect_success` flip without the test logic, making the behavioral change harder to spot at a glance.

Taylor disagrees with Junio’s practical concern, arguing that splitting test and fix into separate commits is a useful pattern, especially when either the test setup or the bugfix is complex. He offers two reasons: it keeps commit messages focused (avoiding awkward explanations of unrelated complexity), and it cleanly demonstrates that the bug existed before the fix and is resolved afterward. He suggests documenting this style in `CodingGuidelines` or `SubmittingPatches` if the project wants to formalize it.

The exchange is collegial and non-blocking — the series itself is already merged to `master` — but it touches on a recurring tension in Git’s test suite: how to balance clarity in patch diffs with the desire to clearly demonstrate bugs and their fixes. No immediate action is required, but the discussion may influence future test-writing conventions.
