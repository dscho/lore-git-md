This is the final review feedback on the second patch of Kristofer Karlsson’s two-patch series that fixes a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend. The patch moves the `suppress_deletions` flag from the merged iterator to `struct reftable_stack_options`, allowing callers to control tombstone suppression at stack creation time. This change enables early termination of ref-reading operations when tombstones exceed the relevant bounds, eliminating the O(n) per-call behavior in tombstone-heavy scenarios.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review is surface-level, addressing only minor wording and documentation nits. He suggests a small wording tweak in the commit message (`s/applied/applies/`) and notes that while a comment explaining the new `suppress_deletions` field in `reftable_stack_options` would be helpful, it doesn’t warrant a reroll. The rest of the patch—including the core logic in `refs/reftable-backend.c` and the API compatibility changes—meets his approval. No substantive concerns are raised, and the series appears ready for integration.

The patch is well-motivated, addressing a real-world performance issue observed in production-scale repositories, and the design choices are uncontroversial. All prior feedback has been incorporated, and the series is now finalized. Downstream users like libgit2 will need to update to use the new `reftable_stack_options` field in future versions, but the break is already inevitable due to libgit2’s use of pre-options-stack code.
