This is a **workflow discussion** in the ongoing thread about Farid Zakaria’s `--autosquash` bugfix for `git rebase`. Yuxuan Chen, a colleague of Farid’s at Meta, replies to Phillip Wood’s review to clarify the team’s design intent for a potential future `revert!` (or `drop!`) autosquash directive.

Yuxuan pushes back on Phillip’s suggestion of using an empty commit as a marker for `drop!`. Instead, he argues that a `drop!` commit should contain the inverse of the patch it targets, mirroring the behavior of `git revert`. This ensures the repository state reflects the intended removal even before the rebase runs, rather than relying on a no-op marker. While he acknowledges that applying the inverse patch may introduce conflicts, he frames this as an existing problem (shared with `git revert`) rather than a new drawback.

The email is brief but substantive, focusing on the semantic correctness of the proposed `drop!` directive rather than the mechanics of the current patch. It signals Meta’s preference for a design that maintains consistency with Git’s existing conflict-handling patterns, even if it means accepting occasional merge conflicts during autosquash. No code changes are proposed here—this is purely a design discussion about future work.
