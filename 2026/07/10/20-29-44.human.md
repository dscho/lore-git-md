This is the fourth and final patch in Pablo Sabater’s v8 series implementing **cascading indentation for visual roots in `git log --graph`**, now under active discussion after Mirko Faina identified a **design-level edge case**. The series as a whole is a **feature** that improves graph readability by indenting commits whose parents are excluded (e.g., by `--max-count`, `--first-parent`, or filters), preventing them from appearing falsely adjacent to unrelated commits. This patch specifically implements the visualization logic, introducing a new `GRAPH_PRE_ROOT` state, indentation depth tracking, and cascading behavior for adjacent visual roots.

The core issue raised in this email is that the current logic in `graph_is_visual_root_candidate()` assumes parents are either present or explicitly filtered to `NULL`—an assumption that fails for filters like `--author` or `--grep`, which exclude commits *after* enqueuing without modifying their parent pointers. Mirko’s test case (`git log --graph --oneline --author="Mirko Faina"`) reveals that visual roots remain unindented because the function cannot detect their status until the next commit is processed, by which point it is too late to indent. Pablo acknowledges the problem and proposes a fix: iterating over each parent and calling `graph_is_interesting()` (which internally uses `get_commit_action()`) to determine if any parent would actually be rendered. This approach would generalize the logic to handle filters that exclude commits without filtering their parents, addressing the immediate edge case while likely covering similar filters like `--grep`.

The patch itself is **well-crafted and thoroughly tested**, with 473 lines of new tests in `t4218-log-graph-indentation.sh` covering edge cases like `--max-count`, `--first-parent`, octopus merges, and boundary commits. The commit message is detailed, explaining the visualization rules, the new `GRAPH_PRE_ROOT` state, and the cascading behavior for adjacent visual roots. However, the **lack of documentation** for how indentation levels are calculated remains a gap noted in earlier reviews. The series is otherwise **architecturally sound**, having resolved a major fragility in v6/v7 by replacing peek functions with a lookahead buffer (approved by Junio Hamano and Jeff King). The current blocker is this edge case, which Pablo’s proposed fix appears to address directly, though it may require revisiting the lookahead buffer’s role or the timing of visual root detection.
