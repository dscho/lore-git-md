This is the second patch in Kristofer Karlsson’s priority-queue performance series, now reporting a puzzling 1% slowdown despite the optimization’s proven reduction in comparison count. René Scharfe’s follow-up benchmarks on Apple M1/M5 hardware show the cascade-down optimization actually increases wall-clock time by ~1% for `git describe` on both Git’s and Linux’s repositories, even though the total number of `prio_queue` comparisons drops by 14–17%. The discrepancy is hardware-specific: the slowdown appears on Apple silicon but not on a Ryzen laptop (where noise masks any 1% change). René’s instrumentation confirms the optimization is working as designed—comparisons fall from 70M to 60M for Git’s repo and from 474M to 439M for Linux’s—but the extra struct assignments in `cascade_down()` may be triggering microarchitectural penalties on Apple’s cores. The patch itself remains technically sound, and René’s crude `print_compares()` instrumentation rules out algorithmic regressions; the mystery is purely performance measurement.
