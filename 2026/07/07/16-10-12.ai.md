This is a substantive review comment in the ongoing `git history` feedback discussion, specifically about the user experience of silent success in `git history reword` and `git history fixup`. D. Ben Knoble responds to Dominique Martinet’s earlier demonstration of usability problems when operating on commits that are not ancestors of HEAD, offering both agreement and additional design considerations.

Knoble endorses the core proposal—printing old and new commit hashes on success—as a way to make silent operations visible, but expands the discussion with concrete output format ideas and operational flexibility. He suggests borrowing the `--update-refs=print` machinery from `git replay` to emit ref updates in a familiar format (e.g., `+ $old...$new $ref (forced update)`), and proposes two distinct output patterns: one for rewritten refs and another for unreferenced objects (e.g., `$new [unreferenced object]`). He also introduces the idea of a "strict" mode that would verify input commits are reachable from some ref, or alternatively, a warning when only unreferenced objects are created—though he stops short of requiring reachability as a hard precondition.

The review keeps the command’s porcelain nature in focus, suggesting that output niceties are appropriate for a user-facing tool and that a quiet mode could be added later if needed. This contribution adds nuance to the feedback requirements, offering a middle ground between Dominique Martinet’s safety-first approach and the original silent-success design.
