This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s 11-patch series to make Git’s object database (ODB) optimizations pluggable, part of the broader ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to restructure Git’s ODB so backends like reftable or custom storage can define their own optimization strategies (repacking, geometric repacking, auto-gc) without hardcoding them in `builtin/gc.c`.

So far, Junio C Hamano has reviewed the first three patches. The first two patches—extending the "pre-auto-gc" hook to all automated maintenance tasks and reordering maintenance tasks to group ODB-related optimizations—were substantively endorsed, with Junio raising minor, non-blocking points (e.g., absolute paths in hook logs, test brittleness). The third patch, a pure refactoring to extract repack and prune logic into a new helper function, received a surface-level review noting a harmless error-handling adjustment. No objections to merging have been raised, and the series is under initial review with no prior versions or merge status recorded.

The series touches `builtin/gc.c`, `odb.c`, `odb.h`, and introduces new files like `odb/source-files.c` to relocate optimization logic. Key changes include introducing `struct odb_optimize_options`, unifying repacking strategies, and parameterizing helpers to remove reliance on `the_repository`. The final patch will expose this logic through ODB source vtable callbacks, completing the pluggable interface. Open questions are minor (e.g., Junio’s preference for absolute paths in logs), and the series preserves all existing behavior while enabling future backend flexibility.
