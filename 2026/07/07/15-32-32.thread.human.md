This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s 12-patch series **"Make ODB optimizations pluggable"**, which refactors Git’s object database (ODB) to allow different backends to implement their own optimization strategies. The series is part of the broader ODB abstraction effort and is currently at v2, with all feedback from Junio C Hamano on the first three patches addressed.

The latest update is the sixth patch in v2, which eliminates backend-specific configuration values from the generic `gc_config` struct. This patch inlines eight "files"-specific settings (like `gc_auto_threshold` and `repack_filter`) at their call sites in `builtin/gc.c`, ensuring future backends (e.g., reftable) won’t need to handle these values. The change is purely mechanical, with no behavioral impact, and prepares the codebase for later patches that relocate ODB optimization logic into the "files" backend.

The series has been uncontroversial so far, with Junio endorsing the design goals and Patrick addressing feedback on test robustness and task reordering. The remaining patches focus on further refactoring to enable pluggable optimizations, including unifying repack strategies, centralizing auto-condition checks, and relocating the optimization logic into the ODB source backend. No objections or open design questions remain, though reviewers may still scrutinize edge cases in memory estimation or pack selection. The series is under active review and is a prerequisite for pluggable ODB backends.
