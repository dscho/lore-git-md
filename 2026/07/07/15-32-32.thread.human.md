This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s 11-patch series to make Git’s object database (ODB) optimizations pluggable, part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort. The series restructures Git’s maintenance logic so that different ODB backends—like reftable or custom storage—can define their own optimization strategies (repacking, geometric repacking, auto-gc) without hardcoding them in `builtin/gc.c`.

The first patch, which Junio C Hamano reviewed, extends the "pre-auto-gc" hook to guard *all* automated maintenance tasks, not just `git gc`. Junio agreed with the reasoning: if the hook is meant to block automated housekeeping, it should apply uniformly, whether the task is reflog expiry, geometric repack, or rerere gc. His feedback was minor—preferring an absolute path for the hook’s log file and noting a potential brittleness in the test’s use of `GIT_TRACE2_EVENT`—but he didn’t question the patch’s correctness or design. The series is still under initial review, with no objections raised so far.

The rest of the series (patches 2–11) refactors `builtin/gc.c` to extract repack/prune logic, eliminate global state, introduce a unified `odb_optimize()` entry point, and relocate the optimization logic into the "files" ODB backend. No user-visible behavior changes are introduced, and the series is now complete. Key technical details include new vtable callbacks for ODB sources, parameterized optimization helpers to remove reliance on `the_repository`, and signedness fixes to ensure clean compilation. The series is a prerequisite for pluggable optimizations, enabling future backends to implement their own housekeeping tasks.
