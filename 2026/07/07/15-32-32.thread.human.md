This patch series from Patrick Steinhardt continues its march toward making Git’s object database optimizations pluggable. The latest update is the eleventh patch in the twelve-patch v2 series, addressing signedness issues in three ODB-related functions to prevent compiler warnings when the code moves into `odb/source-files.c` in the final patch.

The changes are narrow and mechanical: `too_many_loose_objects()` now bails early if its limit is non-positive, avoiding a signed/unsigned mismatch; `find_base_packs()` casts pack sizes to `uintmax_t` when comparing against an unsigned limit; and `odb_optimize()` casts `gc_auto_pack_limit` to `unsigned long` when checking against the number of kept packs. Each fix is explained in the commit message, and the patch preserves all existing logic, including the 256-multiple rounding of loose-object thresholds. There are no behavioral changes, and the patch is uncontroversial—its sole purpose is to ensure clean compilation in the new backend context.

The series as a whole remains under review, with Junio’s earlier feedback on v1 patches 1–3 already addressed. The only remaining open points are mechanical: reviewers may wish to double-check that the casts don’t introduce subtle edge cases in pack selection or memory estimation, though the commit message explicitly dismisses such concerns. The final patch, which relocates the ODB optimization logic into the "files" backend, will complete the series and enable future backends to define their own housekeeping tasks.
