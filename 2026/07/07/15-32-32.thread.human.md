Patrick Steinhardt’s 12-patch series to make Git’s object database (ODB) optimizations pluggable has now reached its final form. The goal is to restructure Git’s housekeeping logic—repacking, geometric repacking, and auto-gc—so that alternative ODB backends (like reftable or custom storage) can define their own optimization strategies without hardcoding them in `builtin/gc.c`. The series has been uncontroversial in design, with Junio C Hamano’s feedback on the first three patches fully addressed in this second iteration.

The latest patch completes the effort by relocating the core optimization logic (`odb_optimize()` and `odb_optimize_required()`) from `builtin/gc.c` into the "files" ODB source backend, exposing them through new vtable callbacks. This is a mechanical move—466 lines of code shifted with minimal adjustments to adapt to the new context—preserving all existing behavior for incremental and geometric repacking, auto-condition checks, cruft pack handling, and memory estimation. The patch also introduces thin wrappers in `odb.c` to delegate to the backend’s implementation, ensuring future backends can plug in their own housekeeping logic.

The series has been a model of incremental refactoring: earlier patches reordered maintenance tasks, extracted repack/prune logic into reusable helpers, eliminated global state, relocated backend-specific configuration, encapsulated optimization settings, unified repack strategies, centralized auto-condition checks, and prepared the codebase for `the_repository` removal. The only open questions now are whether the relocated code compiles cleanly and whether the vtable delegation works as intended—both low-risk given the mechanical nature of the changes. With no behavioral regressions expected, the series is ready for integration once this final patch is reviewed.
