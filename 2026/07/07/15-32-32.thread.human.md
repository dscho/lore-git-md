Patrick Steinhardt’s 11-patch series to make Git’s object database (ODB) optimizations pluggable has now been fully posted. The series is part of the broader ODB abstraction effort, aiming to let different backends—like reftable or custom storage—implement their own housekeeping logic (repacking, geometric repacking, auto-gc) without hardcoding it in `builtin/gc.c`. The first ten patches laid the groundwork: they fixed a long-standing inconsistency in the "pre-auto-gc" hook, reordered maintenance tasks to group ODB-related work, refactored `cmd_gc()` to isolate repack and prune logic, eliminated global state in repack argument assembly, relocated backend-specific configuration reads, introduced `struct odb_optimize_options` to encapsulate optimization settings, unified incremental and geometric repacking under a single `odb_optimize()` entry point, centralized auto-condition checks, and parameterized ODB optimization helpers to remove reliance on the global `the_repository`.

The final patch completes the series by relocating the `odb_optimize()` and `odb_optimize_required()` functions from `builtin/gc.c` into the new "files" ODB source backend (`odb/source-files.c`), exposing them through vtable callbacks in the ODB source interface. This allows any backend to implement its own optimization logic while preserving all existing behavior—geometric split factors, cruft pack handling, multi-pack-index writes, and auto-condition checks remain unchanged. The patch is purely mechanical, moving 489 lines out of `builtin/gc.c` and adding 470 lines to `odb/source-files.c`, with no new user-visible features or on-disk format changes. The series is now under initial review, with no feedback recorded yet. Reviewers may want to verify that the parameterized helpers and signedness fixes in patches 9 and 10 don’t introduce subtle edge cases in memory estimation or pack selection, though the patches maintain identical logic to the original code.
