This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s 12-patch series "Make ODB optimizations pluggable," which restructures Git’s object database to allow different backends to implement their own optimization strategies. The series is part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort and has reached its second version. All patches have been reviewed, with Junio C Hamano providing feedback on the design and minor technical points, all of which have been addressed.

The core goal is to extract Git’s existing optimization logic (repacking, geometric repacking, auto-gc) into a modular system, enabling future backends like reftable to define their own housekeeping tasks. The series refactors `builtin/gc.c` to isolate ODB-related optimizations, introduces a new `struct odb_optimize_options` to encapsulate settings, and unifies repacking strategies under a single `odb_optimize()` entry point. It also improves test robustness and fixes a long-standing inconsistency in the "pre-auto-gc" hook, ensuring it intercepts all auto-maintenance tasks, not just `gc`.

The only remaining technical point is a minor signedness issue in `find_base_packs()`, where Junio questioned whether casting only the left side of a comparison to `uintmax_t` could introduce edge cases on platforms where `off_t` is wider than `unsigned long`. Patrick clarified that the cast is safe for the current code path, as the right-hand side would be automatically promoted to `uintmax_t` under C’s conversion rules. No further changes are planned, and the series is ready for integration. The patches touch `builtin/gc.c`, the ODB abstraction layer, and related test files, but do not change on-disk formats or user-visible behavior.
