This thread began as a two-patch series from Kristofer Karlsson that fixed a performance regression in Git’s commit-graph subsystem, where the `topo_levels` slab was incorrectly propagated only to the topmost layer of a split commit-graph chain. The bug, introduced in September 2025 by commit `199d452758`, caused incremental commit-graph writes to re-walk the entire commit ancestry, ballooning runtime from 233ms to over 4 seconds on large repositories. The fix—changing a single loop variable from `g->topo_levels` to `chain->topo_levels`—restored the intended behavior, and the series was merged to `master` after a v2 reroll corrected a commit-message date error.

Since the merge, discussion has shifted to patch presentation style. Taylor Blau and Junio C Hamano are exchanging views on whether a failing test and its fix should live in separate commits. Taylor defends the practice, arguing it improves clarity when either the test setup or the bugfix is non-trivial, and asks if Junio feels strongly enough to codify the guidance in the project’s documentation. Junio’s earlier caution—that a long test body could make the diff of the second patch show only the expectation flip—remains a practical consideration, but neither participant treats it as a blocker. The conversation is purely stylistic; no further code changes are expected.
