This thread began as a bugfix for a performance regression in Git’s commit-graph subsystem, where a slab tracking topological levels was incorrectly propagated only to the topmost layer of split commit-graph chains, causing incremental writes to slow from 233ms to over 4 seconds in large repositories. The two-patch series by Kristofer Karlsson—first introducing a failing test, then fixing the bug—was merged to `master` in September 2025 after a quick review cycle involving Taylor Blau and Patrick Steinhardt, both of whom endorsed the fix.

Since the merge, the thread has shifted to post-mortem discussion about review ergonomics and tooling. The main debate centered on whether authors should split failing tests and their fixes into separate commits to make reviews clearer. Taylor Blau advocated for this practice, while Junio C Hamano argued that the project’s existing tooling—such as selectively reverting test files during review—already achieves the same goal without complicating the commit history. Kristofer initially proposed a middle ground: tooling to let authors expand context in patch emails where needed. However, after Junio demonstrated that reviewers can already isolate tests using `git show ':!t/' | git apply -R && make test`, Kristofer conceded the point and confirmed he’ll follow the convention of keeping tests and fixes in the same atomic commit. The discussion is now closed, with no plans to formalize guidance or introduce new tooling.

The thread also touched on longer-term architectural questions, particularly whether the `topo_levels` slab should be refactored to align with Git’s ongoing ODB abstraction effort. Patrick Steinhardt raised deeper concerns about whether commit graphs are a property of the object database or a specific backend, but these remain exploratory and non-blocking. For now, the immediate regression is resolved, and the post-merge conversation has settled without requiring further changes.
