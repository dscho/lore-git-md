This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series from Kristofer Karlsson that addresses a performance regression in Git's commit-graph subsystem. The regression, introduced in September 2025 by commit `199d452758`, caused the `topo_levels` slab to be incorrectly propagated only to the topmost layer of a split commit-graph chain, forcing unnecessary re-walks of commit ancestry and degrading performance from 233ms to 4133ms for incremental writes in large repositories.

The fix itself is a one-line change in `commit-graph.c` (replacing `g->topo_levels` with `chain->topo_levels`), ensuring the slab is correctly propagated to all layers of the chain. The accompanying test in `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh` was restructured to first demonstrate the bug (using `test_expect_failure`) and then verify the fix (flipping to `test_expect_success`). The series has received positive reviews from Taylor Blau, who called the fix "obviously good" and suggested a non-blocking architectural follow-up, and Junio C Hamano, who requested a minor commit message correction (fixing a misstated date from April to September 2025). Patrick Steinhardt's final review endorsed the fix as "embarrassing but correct" and approved the test changes.

The series is now uncontested and ready for merge. Kristofer has confirmed he will post a v2 with only the commit message correction, after which Junio is prepared to merge it. No open questions or technical objections remain, and the fix is narrowly scoped to address the immediate performance issue without disrupting other workflows. Users with `fetch.writeCommitGraph` enabled or running `git maintenance` after fetch will benefit from the restored performance.
