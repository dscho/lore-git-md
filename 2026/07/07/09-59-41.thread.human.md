This thread began as a two-patch bugfix series by Kristofer Karlsson to restore performance in Git’s commit-graph subsystem after a regression in split commit-graph chains slowed incremental writes from 233ms to over 4 seconds. The fix itself—a one-line correction to propagate the `topo_levels` slab to all layers of the chain—was uncontroversial, reviewed by Taylor Blau and Patrick Steinhardt, and merged to `master` after a minor commit-message clarification in v2.

Since merging, the discussion has shifted to patch presentation style. Taylor Blau advocates splitting a failing test and its fix into separate commits, especially when either the test setup or the bugfix is non-trivial, arguing this structure cleanly demonstrates the bug’s existence and resolution. Junio C Hamano, while not opposing the practice in principle, cautions that it can make initial review harder: when a reviewer sees only a few lines of context in a patch email, a multi-line test body with `test_expect_failure` in the first patch followed by a flip to `test_expect_success` in the second may obscure what behavior is actually changing. He frames this as a trade-off between author convenience and reviewer clarity, noting the project’s chronic imbalance between patch volume and reviewer capacity. The exchange remains stylistic and non-blocking; no code changes or formal guidance updates are proposed or required. Kristofer has not engaged in this post-merge discussion, and the architectural follow-up—moving the `topo_levels` slab to `struct object_database`—remains deferred.
