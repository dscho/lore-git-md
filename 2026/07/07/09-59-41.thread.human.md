This thread began as a two-patch bugfix series by Kristofer Karlsson to address a performance regression in Git’s commit-graph subsystem, where incremental writes slowed dramatically due to a mispropagated `topo_levels` slab in split commit-graph chains. The fix—merged to `master` in its second iteration—restored performance by ensuring the slab is correctly propagated across all layers of the chain. Post-merge, the discussion has shifted to two stylistic and architectural themes.

First, Taylor Blau and Junio C Hamano have debated the ergonomics of patch presentation: whether failing tests and their fixes should be split into separate commits when either the test setup or the bugfix is complex. Taylor argues this structure cleanly demonstrates the bug’s existence and resolution, while Junio highlights a trade-off in the initial review experience, where limited context in patch emails can make it harder for reviewers to understand behavioral changes. The discussion remains non-blocking, with no consensus on formalizing guidance in the project’s documentation.

Second, Patrick Steinhardt has weighed in on the architectural follow-up proposed by Taylor and Kristofer: moving the `topo_levels` slab from `struct commit_graph` to `struct object_database`. Patrick confirms he’s explored this idea but found it complicated by the commit-slab API’s global scope and its reuse across subsystems. He raises a deeper question about whether commit graphs are a property of the object database or of specific backends, noting that distributed or alternative backends might not fit the current monolithic design. While no immediate changes are proposed, the discussion frames long-term paths—such as making commit graphs pluggable or backend-specific—as part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort. The merged bugfix remains uncontested, and the thread now serves as a venue for broader design considerations.
