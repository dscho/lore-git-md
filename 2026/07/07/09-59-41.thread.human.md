This thread is a two-patch bugfix series addressing a performance regression in Git's commit-graph subsystem, specifically in split commit-graph chains. The issue was introduced in April 2025 by commit `199d452758` (`ps/commit-graph-via-source`) and causes a dramatic slowdown—from 233ms to 4133ms—during incremental commit-graph writes on large repositories with multi-layer split graphs. The root cause is a loop variable mismatch (`g` instead of `chain`) that prevents the `topo_levels` slab from being propagated beyond the topmost layer, forcing unnecessary DFS re-walks of commit ancestry.

The first patch added diagnostic trace2 instrumentation, while the second (now summarized) delivers the fix: a one-line change in `commit-graph.c` to correct the loop variable, ensuring the `topo_levels` slab is set for all layers. The fix is accompanied by an updated test in `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh`, which now expects just 1 DFS step (down from 7) to confirm the regression is resolved. The series is narrowly scoped, well-documented, and includes clear performance metrics and reproduction context. It targets users with `fetch.writeCommitGraph` enabled or those running `git maintenance` after fetch. No review feedback has been recorded yet, but the series appears uncontroversial and ready for review.
