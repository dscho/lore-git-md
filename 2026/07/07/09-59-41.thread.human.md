This thread is about a now-merged bugfix series that resolved a performance regression in Git’s commit-graph subsystem, specifically in split commit-graph chains. The regression, introduced in September 2025 by commit `199d452758`, caused incremental commit-graph writes to slow dramatically—from 233ms to over 4 seconds—by forcing unnecessary re-walks of commit ancestry. The fix, authored by Kristofer Karlsson, consisted of two patches: the first added diagnostic trace2 instrumentation, and the second corrected a one-line bug where the `topo_levels` slab was incorrectly propagated only to the topmost layer of the split graph.

The series was uncontested and merged to `master` after a minor reroll (v2) addressed a commit-message date error. Reviews from Junio C Hamano, Taylor Blau, and Patrick Steinhardt were uniformly positive, with Patrick calling the bug "embarrassing" and the fix "obviously good." Post-merge, the discussion has shifted to a non-blocking refinement: Taylor Blau suggested restructuring the test in `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh` to assert the expected behavior (a single DFS step) in the first patch, reducing the second patch to a minimal flip from `test_expect_failure` to `test_expect_success`. Junio’s recent reply raises a practical concern—longer tests could obscure the behavioral change in the patch diff—but the exchange remains purely advisory, with no impact on the fix’s integration status. The architectural follow-up (moving the `topo_levels` slab to `struct object_database`) is deferred, and the thread is effectively closed.
