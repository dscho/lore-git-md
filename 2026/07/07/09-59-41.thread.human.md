This thread is a focused bugfix for a performance regression in Git’s commit-graph subsystem, introduced by an earlier series (`ps/commit-graph-via-source`). The issue causes incremental commit-graph writes to slow dramatically—from 233ms to over 4 seconds—on repositories with split commit-graph chains, due to a mispropagation of the `topo_levels` slab that forces unnecessary ancestry traversals.

The series, authored by Kristofer Karlsson, consists of two patches: the first adds diagnostic trace2 instrumentation, and the second fixes the core bug with a one-line change to ensure the slab is propagated to all layers of the split chain. Taylor Blau reviewed both patches, approving the fix as "obviously good" while suggesting a broader architectural change to prevent similar bugs. Kristofer deferred that refactoring as a follow-up, prioritizing the immediate fix, and the two have since resolved minor feedback on test structure and instrumentation details.

The discussion has now expanded to explore Taylor’s architectural suggestion in more depth. Kristofer analyzed three potential refactoring paths—threading the slab through the call chain, attaching it to the object database, or introducing a dedicated chain-wide struct—but concluded that the current minimal fix remains the best near-term solution. While the exploration confirms the feasibility of a larger refactor, it doesn’t block the series, which is uncontested and ready for merge once the test restructuring is finalized. The regression affects users with `fetch.writeCommitGraph` enabled or those running `git maintenance` after fetch, making this a practical fix for a real-world performance issue.
