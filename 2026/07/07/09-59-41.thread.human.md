This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series addressing a performance regression in Git's commit-graph subsystem, specifically in split commit-graph chains. The regression was introduced by an April 2025 commit (`ps/commit-graph-via-source`) and causes incremental commit-graph writes to slow from 233ms to over 4 seconds in large repositories with multi-layer split graphs. The issue stems from the `topo_levels` slab being incorrectly propagated only to the topmost layer of the chain, forcing unnecessary re-computation of commit ancestry.

The series is currently at version 1 and under review. Patch 1/2 adds trace2 instrumentation to help diagnose the problem, while Patch 2/2 fixes the core issue with a one-line change—correcting a loop variable to ensure the `topo_levels` slab is propagated to all layers of the split graph. Taylor Blau reviewed both patches, approving the fix as "obviously good" but suggesting an exploratory architectural change to move the `topo_levels` field from the `commit_graph` struct to `write_commit_graph_ctx` to prevent similar bugs. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, agreed the refactoring is feasible but deferred it as a follow-up, prioritizing the immediate fix to restore performance. Minor refinements to the trace2 instrumentation (using `uintmax_t` instead of `intmax_t`) and test structure (restructuring as `test_expect_failure`) remain open but are non-blocking.

The series touches `commit-graph.c` and the test file `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh`, with no changes to the on-disk format. The regression affects users with `fetch.writeCommitGraph` enabled or those running `git maintenance` after fetch. No objections have been raised, and the fix is uncontested. The thread remains focused on the immediate performance recovery, with the architectural proposal now explicitly deferred.
