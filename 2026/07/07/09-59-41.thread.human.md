This thread is a straightforward bugfix for a performance regression in Git’s commit-graph subsystem. The issue, introduced in September 2025 by commit `199d452758`, caused topological-level data to be incorrectly propagated only to the topmost layer of a split commit-graph chain, forcing expensive ancestry re-walks during incremental writes. On a large repository with a four-layer split graph, this degraded performance from 233ms to over 4 seconds.

Kristofer Karlsson’s two-patch series fixes the problem. The first patch adds trace2 instrumentation for diagnostics, while the second—a one-line change—corrects the propagation logic to use `chain->topo_levels` instead of `g->topo_levels`, ensuring the slab is visible to all layers. The test in `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh` was updated to reflect the fix, flipping from `test_expect_failure` to `test_expect_success` to confirm the DFS now stops early. Reviews from Taylor Blau and Patrick Steinhardt were positive, with no technical objections. Junio Hamano merged the v2 series to `master` after a minor commit-message correction (fixing the regression’s date from April to September 2025).

The fix is now complete, with no open questions or follow-ups required. A proposed architectural refactor—moving the `topo_levels` field to `write_commit_graph_ctx`—was discussed but deferred as a non-blocking follow-up. Users with `fetch.writeCommitGraph` enabled or running `git maintenance` after fetch will benefit from the restored performance.
