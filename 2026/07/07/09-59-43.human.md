This is the second patch in a two-part bugfix series targeting a performance regression in Git’s commit-graph subsystem. The regression, introduced in April 2025 by commit `199d452758` (`ps/commit-graph-via-source`), causes the `topo_levels` slab to be incorrectly propagated only to the topmost layer of a split commit-graph chain. As a result, incremental commit-graph writes force a full re-walk of the commit ancestry, degrading performance from 233ms to 4133ms in large repositories with multi-layer split graphs.

The fix itself is minimal and surgical: it corrects a single line in `commit-graph.c` where the loop variable `chain` was mistakenly replaced with `g` (the topmost layer) during the original refactoring. This ensures the `topo_levels` slab is properly assigned to *all* layers in the chain, allowing `fill_commit_graph_info()` to store topological levels for commits parsed from any layer. The accompanying test in `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh` verifies the fix by checking that the DFS now takes only 1 step (reading from the graph) instead of 7 (re-walking the ancestry).

The patch is well-motivated and narrowly scoped. The commit message clearly explains the bug’s impact, cites the exact commit that introduced it, and provides concrete performance numbers. The test coverage is thorough, and the fix aligns with the existing codebase’s patterns. There’s no indication of controversy or alternative approaches—this looks like a straightforward correction to a clear regression. Users with `fetch.writeCommitGraph` enabled or those running `git maintenance` after fetch will benefit immediately.
