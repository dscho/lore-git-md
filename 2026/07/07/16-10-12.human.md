This is a review comment in the ongoing `git history` RFC thread, specifically about the user experience of silent success in `git history reword` and `git history fixup`. D. Ben Knoble responds to Dominique Martinet’s earlier demonstration of usability problems when these commands operate on commits that are not ancestors of HEAD.

Knoble agrees with Martinet that silent success (exit 0) without feedback is problematic—it leaves users unable to distinguish between a successful rewrite and a no-op. He endorses Pablo Sabater’s proposal to print the old and new commit hashes on success, noting that similar machinery already exists in `git replay --update-refs=print`. Knoble suggests adopting the fetch/push-style output format (`+ $old...$new $ref (forced update)`) for rewritten refs, and proposes either silence or a simple message (`$new [unreferenced object]`) for new objects that aren’t pointed to by any ref.

While Knoble does not think the operation should be restricted to reachable commits (citing potential use cases for unreferenced objects), he floats the idea of a "strict" mode or a warning when only unreferenced objects are rewritten. His tone is pragmatic and user-focused, treating `git history` as porcelain that should prioritize clear feedback over silent operation. The review is substantive, engaging with both the UX implications and the technical feasibility of feedback output.
