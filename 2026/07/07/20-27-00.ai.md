This is a reply in the "Make ODB optimizations pluggable" series, specifically addressing patch 2/11. Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s refactoring that moves worktree pruning and rerere garbage collection before object database optimizations. The review is substantive but brief, focusing on the conceptual framing of Git’s maintenance tasks.

Junio observes that Git’s `gc` and `maintenance` commands have evolved beyond pure object database optimization to encompass broader housekeeping—rerere database cleanup, reflog management, packed-refs and reftable compaction. He endorses Patrick’s reordering as a logical step toward modularity, noting that grouping these tasks under the `gc/maintenance` umbrella will make them easier to refactor into pluggable components later. The comment does not critique the patch itself (which is a mechanical reordering with no behavior change) but rather situates it within the larger goal of making maintenance tasks more flexible.

No objections or action items are raised; the tone is supportive. The patch remains on track for inclusion as part of the ODB abstraction effort. Future reviewers may still want to verify that the theoretical edge case Patrick mentioned (pruning worktrees before ODB optimizations) does not introduce subtle regressions in practice, though both author and maintainer consider it negligible.
