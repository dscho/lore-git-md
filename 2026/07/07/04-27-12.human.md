This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Trevor Gross’s `git rebase -i -x` patch series. Jeff King (Peff) replies to Phillip Wood’s review, refining the proposed alternative of extending `git cherry-pick` with an interactive mode (`-i`).

Peff agrees with Phillip that generalizing the rebase todo-list editor for `cherry-pick -i` should be straightforward, though he acknowledges the open question of whether all rebase commands (like `edit -x` or `pick -x` followed by `fixup`) make sense in a cherry-pick context. His stance is permissive: he’d default to trusting users to handle edge cases (e.g., edited commits with `-x` trailers) by documenting changes in the commit message, though he admits this might be optimistic.

On Phillip’s tangential suggestion of adding `--exec` to `git cherry-pick`, Peff notes that most post-pick operations (like running tests) can already be achieved by cherry-picking first and then rebasing with `--exec`. The `-x` flag is unique, he argues, because it inherently depends on the original commit ID, making it harder to replicate with a two-step workflow. This reinforces the case for an interactive cherry-pick mode, which would handle `-x` natively while avoiding the semantic mismatches of Trevor’s rebase-centric approach.

The exchange leaves the design direction unresolved but clarifies that the `cherry-pick -i` alternative is technically viable and aligns better with Git’s existing workflows. The thread’s next step likely hinges on Trevor’s response to the core objection: whether the rebase-centric design can be justified, or if the effort should pivot to `cherry-pick`.
