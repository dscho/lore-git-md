This is the author's reply to Junio's design-level question about patch 7/7 in the `git_hash_*()` API hardening series. Jeff King (Peff) clarifies the reasoning behind the asymmetry that allows `git_hash_discard()` after `git_hash_final()` but forbids calling `git_hash_final()` twice. He links to an earlier discussion where the behavior was debated and explains that the asymmetry stems from the fundamental difference between the two operations: `git_hash_final()` attempts to produce a meaningful result (a hash), while `git_hash_discard()` is purely a cleanup operation. Peff argues that calling `git_hash_final()` on a discarded context should trigger a `BUG()` because it likely indicates a programming error, and silently returning a null or default hash could mask bugs. The patch remains unchanged, as the current design is intentional and no real-world use cases exist where a different behavior would be desirable. This reply effectively closes the open design question, leaving the API's invariants as they are.
