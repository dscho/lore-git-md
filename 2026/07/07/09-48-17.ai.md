### Key Points
1. **Default behavior (`--reedit-message`)**
   Phillip revisits the earlier discussion about whether `--reedit-message` (or `--edit`) should be the default. Harald dismissed this as settled, but Phillip’s tone ("*was that overlooked?*") suggests the debate is still open. The implication is that the current opt-in design may violate user expectations, as `git rebase -i` forces message review by default. No resolution is proposed here, but the issue remains a potential sticking point for integration.

2. **Template formatting (autosquash reordering)**
   Phillip **successfully argues** that the `--reedit-message` template should reorder commits to place `fixup!`/`squash!`/`amend!` markers immediately after their targets, mirroring `git rebase -i`’s behavior. Harald agrees to explore this ("*I can take a look*"), but the lack of a concrete plan suggests this may slip to a later iteration. The current implementation’s mixed presentation of markers (e.g., `fixup!` messages scattered throughout the template) could confuse users, especially in ranges with multiple targets.

3. **Input validation (marker targets)**
   Phillip **expands the scope of `reject_fixupish_oldest()`**, proposing it check *all* commits in the range (not just the oldest) and verify whether marker targets lie within the range. This would address edge cases like a `fixup!` commit whose target is outside the squashed range, which could otherwise produce misleading results. Harald acknowledges the need but warns this "*makes the logic a lot bigger*"—a valid concern, but one that Phillip mitigates by suggesting incremental support (e.g., starting with exact subject matches and deferring prefix/object-id matches).
