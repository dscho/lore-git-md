This thread is a cleanup series from Jeff King (Peff) that refactors Git’s `git_hash_*()` API to make it more robust and consistent. The series addresses a long-standing issue where `git_hash_discard()` was not idempotent—calling it multiple times could leave the hash context in an inconsistent state. Peff’s patches standardize the API’s usage, improve documentation, and add defensive runtime checks to catch misuse early. The series is now in its final stages, with all substantive feedback addressed.

Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the patches, focusing on minor cosmetic issues like unnecessary braces and typos. Junio also raised a design question about the asymmetry between `git_hash_discard()` (now idempotent) and `git_hash_final()` (which intentionally triggers a `BUG()` if called on an inactive context). Peff clarified that this asymmetry is intentional, as `git_hash_final()` is meant to produce a meaningful result, while `git_hash_discard()` is purely for cleanup. The only remaining loose ends are trivial fixes—such as braces in `object-file.c` and `rerere.c`—which Peff plans to address in a v2 before the series is queued for integration. No technical objections remain, and the series is expected to land soon.
