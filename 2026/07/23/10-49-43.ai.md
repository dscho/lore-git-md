This is a follow-up in the ongoing ODB performance regression thread, specifically Peff’s reply to Junio’s clarification about the separability of the immediate performance fix and Patrick’s long-term transaction-aware ODB redesign. Peff reinforces Junio’s point by emphasizing that the regression is fundamentally an **alternates performance issue**, not just a quarantine-specific problem. The root cause—redundant loose object checks due to the ODB rework’s shift to a "pack-loose, pack-loose" lookup order across backends—affects any use of alternates, including `clone -s`, `--reference`, and large-scale hosting deployments (e.g., GitHub, GitLab) that rely on object sharing between forks. These scenarios would experience the performance penalty on nearly every Git operation, not just during `git receive-pack` connectivity checks. Peff’s observation broadens the scope of the regression beyond the quarantine area, underscoring the urgency of a near-term fix (e.g., restoring the "pack-first, loose-second" lookup order across backends) while Patrick’s architectural work remains in progress. The email is concise but substantive, framing the regression as a systemic alternates performance issue rather than a niche edge case.
