This is Junio’s final verdict on Harald Nordgren’s `--track=fetch` feature series. After fifteen revisions and months of discussion, Junio has decided to abandon the topic, marking it for discard in the upcoming “What’s cooking” report.

The core objection remains unchanged: Junio views the feature as a risky automation that could encourage “blind updates” by hiding network operations during checkout. He reiterates that automatically refreshing the remote-tracking branch tip might silently invalidate other branches forked from the same point, leaving users unaware of the change. The commit-message phrasing “workaround” for the existing two-command sequence (`git fetch` followed by `git checkout -b`) continues to signal, in Junio’s eyes, that the author has not fully grappled with the trade-offs. Despite the author’s reframing—arguing that the feature reduces merge conflicts and aligns with user expectations—Junio concludes the downside outweighs the benefit and is unwilling to merge the series in its current form. No new technical feedback is offered; the decision is purely about workflow philosophy.
