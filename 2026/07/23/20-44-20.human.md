This is a refactoring patch for the `git show-branch` command, now in its tenth iteration (v10). Junio C Hamano’s reply to Gatla Vishweshwar Reddy is not a technical review of the patch itself, but a stern meta-level message about the human and procedural expectations of the Git mailing list.

The patch itself converts `show-branch` from using the shared `object.flags` field to a dedicated commit-slab, removing the long-standing 27-branch limit and fixing all previously identified behavioral regressions. Gatla’s v10 explicitly restores the `init_commit_name_slab(&name_slab)` call that was accidentally dropped in v9, causing a division-by-zero crash, and he reports manual testing across every mode (`master`, `-a`, `--merge-base`, `--independent`, `--reflog`, `--list`, `--topics`) plus a full test-suite pass. The patch is technically ready; what remains is Junio’s confirmation that the regression is fixed.

Junio’s email, however, focuses on the *process* by which the patch has been developed. He expresses frustration that new iterations have been sent without first reaching rough consensus on the mailing list, and he underscores that the space below the `---` line is for summarizing agreed changes, not for debate. Most pointedly, he questions whether Gatla is genuinely prepared to explain every change in his own words, suggesting that heavy reliance on AI tools may have produced a patch the author cannot fully defend. Junio does not re-review the code in this message; instead, he sets an expectation that Gatla must engage in a human, back-and-forth discussion before any further iterations are posted. Until that happens, the patch remains in limbo, regardless of its technical correctness.
