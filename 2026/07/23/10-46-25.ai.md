This is a **substantive technical review** from Jeff King (Peff) engaging with Patrick Steinhardt’s proposed architectural fix for the `git receive-pack` connectivity check performance regression. Peff challenges the feasibility of Patrick’s suggestion to route ODB writes directly to the transaction object during active transactions, arguing that the problem spans process boundaries and cannot be solved solely within the ODB API.

Peff identifies two critical cross-process dependencies that complicate Patrick’s proposal: (1) the `index-pack` process, which must be explicitly directed to write objects into the quarantine area (transaction object) rather than the primary ODB, and (2) the `rev-list` connectivity check and user-specified hooks, which must transparently read objects from *both* the quarantine area and the original ODB. These requirements, he argues, would force the solution to replicate the existing "alternates juggling" (modifying the ODB source list) in some form, undermining the elegance of Patrick’s transaction-aware approach. His critique is grounded in the practical constraints of Git’s multi-process architecture, particularly the need to coordinate object visibility between `index-pack`, `rev-list`, and arbitrary hook processes.

The email does not propose an alternative fix but instead narrows the scope of what a transaction-aware ODB redesign can achieve. By highlighting the process-boundary limitations, Peff implies that the regression may still require a targeted fix (e.g., restoring the "pack-first, loose-second" lookup order across backends) to address the immediate performance issue, even if Patrick’s long-term architectural work proceeds. This reinforces the earlier thread consensus that the near-term and long-term solutions are separable, with the former likely needing attention before Patrick’s return in late July or August.
