This thread is about a two-patch series (v6) by Tian Yuchen that moves Git’s global `fetch_if_missing` flag into `struct repository`, continuing the project’s broader effort to eliminate global state. The series is now technically complete and ready for queuing after addressing all prior feedback, including Junio Hamano’s concerns about design elegance and edge cases. The change preserves existing behavior—`fetch_if_missing` defaults to enabled (1)—while making it a per-repository setting, a step toward libification and the removal of the `the_repository` global.

Key participants include Tian (the author), Junio (the maintainer), and Patrick Steinhardt, who raised early concerns about correctness in `index-pack.c` and `revision.c`. Junio’s latest review confirmed the series is mechanically sound and explicitly approved it for queuing, though he questioned whether running `git index-pack` outside a repository is a legitimate use case or a user error. Tian clarified that the man page describes `index-pack` as operating on standalone `.pack` files, reinforcing the established behavior the patch preserves. No further changes are expected, and the series is now awaiting integration.

The patch touches 15 files, including `fetch-pack.c`, `fsck.c`, and `index-pack.c`, and resolves all remaining `the_repository` references for `fetch_if_missing`. Edge cases—like repository-less invocations and deferred operations in `fsck`/`index-pack`—are handled with fallbacks or explicit checks. While the broader architectural question of whether `fetch_if_missing` belongs in `struct repository` remains unresolved, Junio did not block the series on it. The only loose ends are untested multi-repository behavior and the unresolved design preference (Patrick Steinhardt had previously suggested moving the flag into the object database instead). The series coordinates with other in-flight topics, like `cf1687a41c`, to avoid conflicts.
