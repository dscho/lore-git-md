This thread is about a bugfix and hardening series for Git’s trace2 subsystem, authored by Derrick Stolee, aimed at preventing crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers like `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and `ALLOC_ARRAY()` under memory pressure or system call failures. The original crash—a "recursion detected in die handler" error—was traced to `xsnprintf()` failures in trace2 timestamp formatting on Git for Windows, but the series now seeks to eliminate *all* `die()`-prone functions from trace2 to ensure telemetry operations never crash Git, even at the cost of degraded telemetry.

Derrick has posted a complete seven-part v2 series that replaces banned functions with defensive fallbacks (e.g., `snprintf()` with hardcoded timestamps, `strdup()` with `NULL` returns, and manual string concatenation for redaction). The series introduces `banned-die.h`, a new enforcement mechanism to block `die()`-triggering functions in trace2 and potentially other subsystems. Junio C Hamano has endorsed the approach, while Taylor Blau’s MinGW-specific fix (disabling `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection for `xsnprintf()`) remains a planned follow-up.

Elijah Newren’s latest review of the fifth patch uncovered a critical one-byte off-by-one error in the manual string concatenation for redaction: the patch copies `redact_len - 1` bytes of the `:<REDACTED>` placeholder, omitting the trailing `>` and corrupting the output. This would break the redaction format and risk exposing sensitive data. The fix is simple, but the oversight highlights the fragility of replacing `xstrfmt()` with manual string operations. Elijah’s prior reviews also raised architectural concerns about `banned-die.h` (naming inconsistency, include-ordering risk) and an incomplete hardening in the config parsing path, where `xstrfmt()` still triggers `die()` in error paths.

The series is under active review, with no objections to its core goal. Open questions include the MinGW follow-up, broader trace2 audit needs, and the long-term impact of degraded telemetry on monitoring tools. The `banned-die.h` header is positioned as a reusable enforcement mechanism, but its future scope remains undecided. Derrick is likely to address Elijah’s feedback in a v3 iteration, though the error does not block the series’ progress.
