This thread is about a bugfix and hardening effort for Git’s trace2 subsystem, led by Derrick Stolee. The goal is to prevent crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers (like `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and memory allocators) under memory pressure or system call failures. The original issue—a "recursion detected in die handler" error—was traced to `xsnprintf()` failures in trace2 timestamp formatting, particularly on Git for Windows due to `libintl_vsnprintf()`’s allocation behavior.

Derrick has posted a complete seven-part series (v2) that replaces all banned functions in trace2 with defensive fallbacks, prioritizing process stability over telemetry completeness. The series introduces `banned-die.h`, a new header to enforce these restrictions, and touches 17 files within the trace2 subsystem. Junio Hamano’s feedback remains supportive, framing the header as a general-purpose mechanism for avoiding `die()`-prone functions, with potential applications beyond trace2 (e.g., banning `the_repository` or `the_hash_algo` in other subsystems). No objections or technical concerns have been raised, and the series is under active review.

Key changes include replacing `xsnprintf()` with `snprintf()` and hardcoded fallbacks, swapping `xstrdup()` for `strdup()` with error handling, and introducing a statically allocated fallback context for thread-local storage failures. The series also adds a `nr_skipped_regions` counter to gracefully degrade telemetry when memory reallocation fails. While the hardening applies to all platforms, the primary focus is Git for Windows, where the original crash occurred. A planned MinGW-specific follow-up (Taylor Blau’s alternative) will disable `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection for `xsnprintf()` in a platform-isolated file.

Open questions include how to test the fix (allocation failures are hard to reproduce), the long-term impact on trace consumers (who may see degraded data), and whether `banned-die.h` will be reused in other subsystems. Derrick, Taylor, and Junio are aligned on the two-part solution, and the series is on track for integration.
