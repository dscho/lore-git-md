This thread is about a seven-part patch series from Derrick Stolee that hardens Git’s trace2 telemetry subsystem to prevent crashes caused by memory allocation or system call failures. The original issue—a "recursion detected in die handler" error—occurred when `xsnprintf()` failed during trace2 timestamp formatting, particularly on Git for Windows due to `libintl_vsnprintf()`’s memory allocation behavior. The series expands this fix into a broader effort to eliminate all `die()`-triggering helpers (e.g., `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, `ALLOC_ARRAY()`) from trace2, replacing them with defensive fallbacks that prioritize process stability over telemetry completeness.

The second patch in the series directly addresses the root cause by replacing `xsnprintf()` with `snprintf()` in timestamp formatting, adding explicit error handling, and falling back to hardcoded timestamps (e.g., `"1900-00-00T00:00:00.000000Z"`) on failure. This change is self-contained, well-motivated, and aligns with the series’ goal of making trace2 robust against system call failures. The patch also extends the `banned-die.h` mechanism (introduced in the first patch) to block `xsnprintf()` in trace2, preventing future regressions. While the fix is technically sound and addresses a real-world crash, it lacks dynamic tests for failure cases—a minor limitation acknowledged in the thread.

The series is now at v3, with all prior feedback addressed, including Elijah Newren’s correctness concerns (e.g., off-by-one errors, include-ordering risks) and Jeff King’s broader architectural critique about indirect `die()` calls via helpers like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`. Derrick acknowledges these limitations but defends the series as a pragmatic first step, emphasizing that a complete solution would require significant architectural changes (e.g., a "safe strbuf" library or a ground-up rewrite of trace2). Junio Hamano’s feedback remains supportive, and Patrick Steinhardt has reinforced the potential for `banned-die.h` to be reused for other architectural constraints (e.g., `the_repository` removal).

Key open questions remain: whether the series should expand to address indirect `die()` dependencies, how to validate the fix without reproducible test cases, and the long-term impact on trace consumers. The discussion also touches on the feasibility of a ground-up rewrite of trace2 and the broader applicability of `banned-die.h` for other hardening efforts. For now, the series is under review and appears ready for integration, with the understanding that it represents incremental progress rather than a complete solution.
