This thread is about a bugfix and hardening series for Git’s trace2 subsystem, authored by Derrick Stolee, aimed at preventing crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers (like `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and memory allocators) under memory pressure or system call failures. The original crash—a "recursion detected in die handler" error—was traced to `xsnprintf()` failures in trace2 timestamp formatting on Git for Windows, but the series now seeks to eliminate *all* `die()`-prone functions from trace2 to prioritize process stability over telemetry completeness.

Derrick has posted a complete seven-patch series (v2) that replaces banned functions with defensive fallbacks (e.g., `snprintf()` instead of `xsnprintf()`, `calloc()` instead of `ALLOC_ARRAY()`) and introduces `banned-die.h`, a new header to enforce these prohibitions. The series touches 17 files, primarily within trace2, and is under active review. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has endorsed the approach, while Elijah Newren’s reviews have surfaced several technical concerns: a naming inconsistency in `banned-die.h`, an include-ordering risk, incomplete hardening in the config parsing path (where `xstrfmt()` still triggers `die()`), a one-byte off-by-one error in the redaction logic (omitting the trailing `>` in `:<REDACTED>`), and a splitting error in the sixth patch that introduces a regression when applied alone. Junio’s latest reply confirms the off-by-one error in the fifth patch and clarifies the fix—copying the full placeholder string, including its null terminator—without engaging broader architectural questions.

The series is still under review, with no major objections to its goals but several correctness and maintainability issues flagged for a v3 iteration. A MinGW-specific follow-up (Taylor Blau’s alternative to disable `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection) remains planned but separate. Open questions include how to test the fix (given the difficulty of reproducing allocation failures), the long-term impact of degraded telemetry on trace consumers, and whether `banned-die.h` will see broader adoption for other hardening efforts. Derrick and Junio appear aligned on the series’ direction, while Elijah’s feedback focuses on ensuring the mechanism’s reliability and correctness.
