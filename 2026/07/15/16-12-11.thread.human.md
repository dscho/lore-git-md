This thread is about hardening Git’s trace2 subsystem to prevent crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers like `xsnprintf()` or `xstrdup()` under memory pressure or system call failures. Derrick Stolee’s seven-part patch series replaces these helpers with defensive fallbacks, introduces `banned-die.h` to enforce the prohibition, and aims to make trace2 degrade gracefully rather than crash Git. The series is under active review, with key feedback from Junio C Hamano (maintainer), Elijah Newren, and Jeff King.

The core debate has shifted toward the *completeness* of the hardening. Jeff King argues the series blocks direct calls to banned functions but misses indirect calls via helpers like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`, which could still trigger `die()`. He suggests the current approach is a "tip of the iceberg" and may require a ground-up rewrite of trace2. Derrick Stolee acknowledges these limitations but defends the series as a pragmatic first step, sharing a CodeQL query that confirms trace2’s remaining `die()` exposure. Elijah Newren’s reviews identified technical issues—an off-by-one error in redaction logic, a regression in region stack handling, and incomplete hardening in config parsing—which Derrick has addressed or plans to fix in v3.

Junio and Patrick Steinhardt have discussed reusing `banned-die.h` for other architectural constraints (e.g., `the_repository`), framing it as a gradual hardening tool. However, no concrete plans for broader adoption have emerged. The series remains focused on trace2, with a MinGW-specific follow-up (Taylor Blau’s alternative) planned separately. Open questions include how to test the changes (given the difficulty of reproducing allocation failures), whether to expand the hardening to indirect dependencies, and whether the current approach is sufficient or needs a more radical rewrite. The thread’s tone is collaborative, with participants agreeing the series is a necessary but incomplete step toward making trace2 truly crash-resistant.
