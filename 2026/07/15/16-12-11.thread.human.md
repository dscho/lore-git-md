This thread is about hardening Git’s trace2 telemetry subsystem to prevent crashes caused by memory allocation or system call failures. Derrick Stolee posted a seven-part patch series (v2) that replaces all `die()`-triggering helpers—like `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and `ALLOC_ARRAY()`—with defensive fallbacks, ensuring trace2 degrades gracefully under failure instead of crashing Git. The series also introduces `banned-die.h`, a new enforcement mechanism to block these functions in trace2 and potentially other low-level subsystems.

The discussion has focused on whether this approach is sufficient. Junio Hamano and Elijah Newren have reviewed the series in detail, catching issues like an off-by-one error in redaction logic and a regression in region-stack handling. Derrick has addressed most of these, but Jeff King raised a broader architectural concern: the series only blocks *direct* calls to banned functions, not indirect ones via helpers like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`. Derrick acknowledged this limitation, sharing a CodeQL query that shows trace2 still depends on unsafe helpers, but defended the series as a pragmatic first step. He suggested future work could include a "safe strbuf" library or a ground-up rewrite of trace2, though neither is part of the current effort.

The series is under review, with no major objections but lingering questions about its completeness. The MinGW-specific fix (disabling `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection) remains a planned follow-up. Key open issues include whether the hardening should expand to address indirect dependencies, how to validate the fix without reproducible test cases, and whether `banned-die.h` is the right tool for the job. For now, the series is on track to land as a partial solution, with the understanding that further work may be needed to fully eliminate `die()`-triggering failures in trace2.
