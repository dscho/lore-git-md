This thread is about a bugfix and hardening effort for Git’s trace2 subsystem, led by Derrick Stolee. The goal is to prevent Git crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers (like `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and `ALLOC_ARRAY()`) during telemetry operations, particularly under memory pressure or system call failures. The original crash—a "recursion detected in die handler" error—was traced to `xsnprintf()` failures in trace2 timestamp formatting on Git for Windows, but the fix now targets all such helpers across the subsystem.

Derrick has posted a seven-part patch series (v2) that replaces these helpers with defensive fallbacks, prioritizing process stability over telemetry completeness. The first three patches introduced `banned-die.h` (a new enforcement mechanism), replaced `xsnprintf()` with `snprintf()` and hardcoded fallbacks, and swapped `xstrdup()` for `strdup()` with graceful degradation. The fourth patch, just posted, removes `ALLOC_ARRAY()` from `trace2.c`, using `calloc()` instead and returning `NULL` on failure, which calling functions now handle safely. The series touches 15 files within trace2 and introduces `banned-die.h` as a reusable tool for future hardening efforts.

Junio Hamano and Taylor Blau have both endorsed the approach, with Taylor’s MinGW-specific fix (disabling `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection for `xsnprintf()`) planned as a follow-up. No objections have been raised, though open questions remain about testing strategies, the broader impact on trace consumers, and whether other trace2 operations could still trigger `die()`. The series is under active review, with the remaining patches expected to follow the same pattern of replacing banned functions with defensive alternatives.
