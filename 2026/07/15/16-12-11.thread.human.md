This thread is about hardening Git’s trace2 telemetry subsystem to prevent crashes caused by memory allocation or system call failures. Derrick Stolee posted a seven-part patch series (v2) that replaces all `die()`-triggering helpers—like `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and `ALLOC_ARRAY()`—with defensive fallbacks, ensuring trace2 degrades gracefully (e.g., truncated messages, hardcoded timestamps, or dropped events) rather than crashing Git. The series also introduces `banned-die.h`, a new header to enforce these prohibitions at compile time, with Junio C Hamano and others viewing it as a general-purpose tool for future hardening efforts.

Key participants include Derrick (the author), Junio (the maintainer, who endorsed the approach), Elijah Newren (who raised technical concerns about naming, include-ordering, and correctness bugs), and Jeff King (Peff), whose recent critique questions whether the series goes far enough. Peff argues that compile-time bans miss indirect `die()` calls via helpers like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`, suggesting the current approach is a "tip of the iceberg" that may require a ground-up rewrite of trace2. Derrick’s latest reply acknowledges these limitations but defends the series as a pragmatic first step, emphasizing that the header provides *some* protection and can serve as a template for gradual improvement. The discussion leaves open whether future work will address indirect dependencies, but for now, the series is proceeding with `banned-die.h` as a documented-but-imperfect solution.

The series is under active review, with Elijah’s feedback already prompting fixes for a naming inconsistency and an off-by-one error in redaction logic. A MinGW-specific follow-up (to disable `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection) remains planned but separate. Open questions include how to test the fix (given the difficulty of reproducing allocation failures) and whether the hardening should expand to address indirect `die()`-prone dependencies. The thread also hints at broader architectural implications—Derrick and Junio see `banned-die.h` as reusable for other constraints (e.g., banning `the_repository`), while Peff cautions against over-reliance on compile-time enforcement. No objections to the series’ core goal have been raised, but the debate over its completeness continues.
