This is a **substantive review** of René Scharfe’s refactoring patch for `remote-curl.c`. The patch itself remains a clean, mechanical simplification of push specification handling, but Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback introduces a design-level question about the interface’s clarity and efficiency.

Patrick suggests passing a `const struct strvec *` directly to the protocol handlers (`push_dav` and `push_git`) instead of a raw `const char **`. This would make the NULL-termination guarantee explicit and align with the fact that all callers already have a `strvec` available. He also notes the absence of a hypothetical `strvec_pushvec()` function, which could optimize bulk appends by avoiding repeated reallocations—though this is speculative and not a blocker.

The review is substantive because it engages with the patch’s design choices rather than just its implementation. Patrick’s feedback is constructive and forward-looking, but it does not identify any bugs or regressions in the current patch. René’s original change (removing the redundant `nr_spec` count) is still valid and uncontroversial; Patrick’s suggestion is an optional refinement that could be pursued in a follow-up patch if desired.

No test failures, performance regressions, or edge cases are raised. The discussion is confined to `remote-curl.c` and does not affect other subsystems or user-visible behavior. The patch remains a low-risk refactoring, but Patrick’s comments may prompt a v2 if René chooses to adopt the `strvec *` interface.
