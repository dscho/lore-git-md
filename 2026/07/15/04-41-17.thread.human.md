This thread is about a small but thoughtful refactoring of Git’s HTTP transport code in `remote-curl.c`. René Scharfe proposed a patch to eliminate redundant tracking of the push specification array count, since the array is already NULL-terminated. The change is purely internal and doesn’t affect observable behavior, but Patrick Steinhardt suggested a further refinement: passing the entire `strvec` struct to the protocol handlers instead of a raw `const char **` to make the interface more self-documenting and safer.

The discussion has since shifted to a design-level question about interface clarity versus simplicity. René acknowledges the merits of Patrick’s suggestion but notes that the current simplicity of `strvec_pushv()` (which operates on NULL-terminated arrays) influenced his original approach. The conversation also touches on a hypothetical `strvec_pushvec()` function, which could optimize bulk appends, though René doubts the performance impact would be significant. No test failures or regressions have been reported, and the patch remains a low-risk refactoring.

The thread is now focused on design preferences rather than correctness, with no clear consensus yet on whether to adopt the `strvec *` interface. René hasn’t ruled out a v2, but the current patch is still under review and not blocked by this discussion. Key participants are René and Patrick, with both engaging constructively on the trade-offs involved.
