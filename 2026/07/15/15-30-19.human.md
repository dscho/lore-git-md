This is a **bugfix** for the sequencer subsystem, addressing a long-standing inconsistency in how `git rebase --autosquash` handles commits that become empty after a `fixup!` or `squash!` operation. The patch ensures that the `--empty` option (which controls whether empty commits are kept, dropped, or stopped at) is respected even when the emptiness results from autosquashing, not just from a regular `pick`.

The problem is straightforward: when a `fixup!` or `squash!` commit cancels out its target (e.g., reverting a previous change), the resulting empty commit currently *always* triggers a rebase halt, ignoring `--empty=drop` or `--empty=keep`. This forces manual intervention, breaking workflows like reverting a commit via `fixup!` and expecting `--empty=drop` to clean up both the original and its revert. The fix aligns autosquash behavior with the existing `--empty` handling for regular picks, making the tool more predictable and scriptable.

Phillip Wood’s review in this email is **substantive and thorough**, digging into edge cases that the patch must handle. He identifies several scenarios the current implementation misses:
- Conflicts during autosquash (e.g., `git reset HEAD^` vs. `git checkout HEAD^ :/`).
- Sequences of multiple `fixup!` commits where only the final result should be checked for emptiness.
- Cases where the original commit was already empty (e.g., `pick empty; fixup! commit-that-becomes-empty`).
- Situations where a `fixup!` follows a `reset`, `exec`, or `break` (not a `pick`), requiring careful tracking of whether the original commit was empty.
- The need to clear rewrite records for *all* dropped commits in a fixup sequence, not just the final one.
