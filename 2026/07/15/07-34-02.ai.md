This is a **substantive discussion** in the `git replay --linearize` thread, where Elijah Newren elaborates on his objections to the current implementation’s handling of multiple positive refs. The email is a detailed rebuttal to Junio C Hamano’s earlier defense of the status quo, and it reframes the debate around **usability, consistency, and recoverability** rather than just predictability.

Elijah’s core argument is that the current `--linearize` behavior **makes the rare use case easy at the expense of the common one**. Specifically:
- **Rare case**: Users who want to concatenate multiple independent branches into a single linear history (e.g., for an octopus merge) can do so with one command.
- **Common case**: Users who want to linearize *one* branch (flattening its merges) while keeping other branches independent must now manually split their work into separate invocations, which is error-prone and tedious. Worse, if they accidentally use `--linearize` with multiple branches, the result is a single concatenated chain that is difficult to untangle.

Elijah provides **concrete examples** to illustrate the problem, showing how the current implementation:
1. **Ignores the user’s command-line order** of branches (e.g., `branchA branchB branchC` becomes `B1 B2 A1 A2 C1 C2`).
2. **Turns multiple branches into one** while still updating all refs, creating a confusing topology where some branches are effectively "swallowed" by others.
3. **Behaves inconsistently** with `--advance` and `--revert`, which reject multiple positive refs to avoid ambiguity.

The email also highlights a **recoverability asymmetry**: if a user gets the "wrong" result (concatenated branches when they wanted independent linearization), fixing it requires meticulous manual work to split the chain back into branches. Conversely, if they get the "right" result (independent branches) but wanted concatenation, the fix is trivial (just rebase again).

Elijah proposes two paths forward:
1. **Minimal fix**: Make `--linearize` reject multiple positive refs (aligning with `--advance`/`--revert`), unblocking the series for merging while leaving per-branch linearization as future work.
2. **Complete fix**: Implement per-branch linearization now by tracking a `last_commit` per branch.

He strongly prefers the minimal fix, arguing that **erroring out now preserves future flexibility** (since turning an error into working behavior later is safe, but the reverse risks breaking users). This aligns with Git’s experimental status for `git replay`.

The email is **technically thorough**, with clear examples and a focus on **user experience** (e.g., the "disappointing" regression in multi-branch handling). It shifts the discussion from "is this behavior acceptable?" to "is this the *best* behavior for users?"—a higher bar that Junio’s earlier defense did not fully address. The ball is now back in Toon Claes’s court to respond, but Elijah’s framing makes a compelling case for restricting multiple refs, at least temporarily.
