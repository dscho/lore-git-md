This is a review comment on the fifth patch in Johannes Schindelin’s 11-patch series addressing unchecked return values reported by Coverity. The series as a whole is a straightforward hardening effort, adding missing error checks to prevent silent failures in various Git subsystems.

Junio’s feedback targets a specific edge case in `builtin/last-modified.c`. The patch currently skips unparsable commits in the `--not` queue by continuing to the next iteration, but Junio points out that this leaves the `active_paths` slab for the original commit `c` uncleaned. His suggestion (`goto cleanup`) ensures proper resource cleanup even when a commit in the `--not` queue fails to parse. The concern is subtle but valid: the slab tracks which paths have been processed, and failing to clean it up could lead to incorrect "last modified" results or resource leaks if the function is called repeatedly in the same process.

The review is substantive but narrow, focusing on a single control-flow issue rather than questioning the patch’s overall approach. The fix itself remains uncontroversial, and the discussion is unlikely to derail the series. If Johannes incorporates Junio’s suggestion, the patch will be stronger without introducing new complexity. This is a good example of how even mechanical hardening patches can benefit from careful review of edge cases.
