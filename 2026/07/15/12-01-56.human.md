This is a refactoring patch that converts `git show-branch` to use a commit-slab for per-commit state instead of the shared `object.flags` field. The patch is now in its fifth iteration (v5), addressing the final mechanical cleanups requested by Junio C Hamano in the previous round.

The core change eliminates the risk of flag-bit collisions with other Git subsystems by moving per-branch reachability bits into a dedicated commit-slab (`commit_rev_flags`) with a configurable stride of `uint64_t` words. This lifts the previous 27-branch limit to 64 branches, with infrastructure in place for dynamic stride to support even more branches in the future. The `UNINTERESTING` bit remains in `object.flags` for revision-walking compatibility, as it is used by other parts of Git and does not belong in the per-branch slab.

The patch is well-motivated and technically sound. It directly reuses the `init_commit_rev_flags_with_stride` infrastructure from the 2025 variable-length bitset slab series, demonstrating good alignment with ongoing efforts to eliminate misuse of `object.flags`. The commit message clearly explains the rationale, the behavioral changes, and the helper functions introduced to encapsulate slab access. Test coverage remains unchanged, but the existing tests continue to pass, indicating no regressions in functionality.

Junio’s feedback on v4 was narrow and actionable: retain the existing TODO comment about eventually storing ref names directly in the slab, and remove the local `#define UNINTERESTING` in favor of the common definition from `<revision.h>`. Both requests have been addressed in v5. The patch is now uncontested on its technical merits and appears ready for maintainer sign-off.
