This is the first patch in Paulius Zaleckas’s v5 series introducing configurable submodule fetch errors. It fixes a long-standing logic flaw in Git’s recursive submodule fetch: when a submodule’s refs fail to fetch (phase 1), the operation was marked as failed immediately, even though a subsequent OID-based fetch (phase 2) might still succeed. The patch defers error recording until all fetch attempts are exhausted, ensuring the outcome reflects whether the required commits were ultimately available.

The change is a prerequisite for the configurable policy in patch 2/2, but it also improves behavior on its own: submodule fetch errors are now consistently reported, and the NEEDSWORK comment added in 2019 is resolved. The implementation extracts the error-recording logic into a helper (`record_fetch_error()`) and updates the control flow in `fetch_finish()` to handle four cases: phase-2 success (no error), phase-2 failure (error), unconditional fetches (immediate error), and locally present commits with phase-1 failures (still an error). The test coverage is thorough, including a new test that verifies a failed submodule fetch is reported even when the gitlinked commits exist locally.

The patch touches only `submodule.c` and the test suite, with no user-facing changes. The commit message is clear and detailed, explaining the problem, the solution, and the edge cases. This is a well-motivated bugfix that addresses a real pain point—submodule fetches failing prematurely—and the implementation is clean and uncontroversial. It looks ready for `next`.
