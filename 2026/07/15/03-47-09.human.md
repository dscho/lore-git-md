This is a **contributor housekeeping** discussion about documentation terminology for the `git-subtree` safeguard and its forthcoming Rust rewrite. The email is a follow-up from Colin Stagner to Ian Jackson, offering recommendations on how to frame the "upstream" and "downstream" terminology in Git's documentation.

Colin suggests distinguishing between *command-level* documentation (e.g., `git-merge(1)`), which should focus on mechanical terms like "branch," "(sub)tree," and "merge-base," and *tutorial-level* documentation (e.g., `gitworkflows(7)`), where broader social terms like "upstream" and "downstream" are more appropriate. He argues that the dependency relationship implied by "upstream/downstream" may not always hold for `git-subtree` use cases—such as merging unrelated minirepos into a monorepo or splitting history for external build systems (e.g., AUR packages). His examples highlight that `git-subtree` can be used for technical convenience rather than dependency management, and he recommends keeping command documentation precise and mechanical.

The email also briefly revisits the earlier discussion about unmarked subtree merges (manual `git merge -X subtree` operations). Colin acknowledges Ian’s point that interleaving squash and non-squash merges could lead to unpredictable results, reinforcing the Rust rewrite’s documented limitation of not supporting such workflows.

This exchange is **purely advisory** and does not affect the patch series under review. The safeguard’s design (checking only the top-level config for `subtree.rust-rewrite`) and the Rust rewrite’s behavior remain unchanged. Colin’s recommendations are framed as optional suggestions for future documentation efforts, not as review feedback for the current patches. The thread remains procedurally ready for v2, with no new technical or conceptual objections.
