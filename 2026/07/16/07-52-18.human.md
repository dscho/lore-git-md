This is a performance-focused patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that reintroduces a `writev(3p)` wrapper for Git, aiming to reduce system call overhead when writing multiple buffers. The wrapper was originally merged in Git 2.54 but later reverted due to compatibility issues on the NonStop platform. This version includes fixes for NonStop and CMake, expands usage beyond the original single callsite, and adds benchmarks to demonstrate performance benefits.

The series consists of five patches. The first two introduce the wrapper in `compat/posix.h` and `wrapper.c`, while the third ensures proper handling of `MAX_IO_SIZE` limits. The final two patches apply the wrapper to `sideband.c` (for sending pktlines) and `builtin/fast-import.c` (for cat-blob responses), showing its utility in different contexts. The changes touch build system files (Makefile, CMakeLists.txt, meson.build) and core I/O plumbing.

Patrick frames this as a loose-end cleanup rather than a must-have feature, acknowledging the earlier controversy. The benchmarks are likely intended to address skepticism about the wrapper's value, though he leaves room for the community to reject it again. The NonStop fix (from a prior patch by him) and Johannes Schindelin's CMake adjustment suggest careful attention to platform compatibility this time around.

This is a well-scoped refactoring with clear performance motivations, but its history of being reverted means it may face renewed scrutiny. The inclusion of benchmarks and expanded usage examples strengthens the case for adoption. Developers interested in I/O performance or platform compatibility should pay attention.
