This thread is about a performance patch from first-time contributor Sahitya Chandra that addresses a quadratic-time inefficiency in Git’s `wt-status` subsystem, specifically in how untracked and ignored file paths are collected. The patch replaces repeated `string_list_insert()` calls—which maintain sorted order but can trigger O(n) array shifts per insertion—with a single `string_list_append()` pass followed by one sort and deduplicate operation per list, reducing the theoretical complexity from O(n²) to O(n log n).

The patch is small, touching only `wt-status.c`, and preserves the existing behavior of producing sorted, duplicate-free lists. Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the initial version, endorsing the approach but suggesting the use of `string_list_sort_u()` for simplicity and requesting benchmark results in the commit message. Jeff King (Peff) later joined the discussion, confirming that the quadratic behavior is likely theoretical rather than a real-world bottleneck, as `fill_directory()` may already return sorted entries. However, he still supports the patch for its improved code clarity and maintainability, leaving the core motivation intact.

The patch remains under review, with no major objections or new technical concerns raised. The next step is for Sahitya to post a v2 incorporating Patrick’s feedback (using `string_list_sort_u()` and adding benchmarks). The discussion also reaffirms the patch’s conservative design choice—explicit deduplication rather than relying on `fill_directory()`’s output—without revisiting the trade-off. No other participants have weighed in, and the patch’s scope remains narrow and uncontested.
