This is a performance-oriented patch series reintroducing a `writev(3p)` wrapper for Git, previously removed due to platform compatibility issues. The series targets low-level I/O operations, aiming to reduce system call overhead by batching writes.

The cover letter explains that the wrapper was originally introduced in Git 2.54 but reverted due to problems on the NonStop platform. This revival incorporates fixes for CMake (from Johannes Schindelin) and NonStop (with additional polish), plus adaptations to demonstrate broader applicability beyond a single callsite. Benchmarks are included to quantify performance gains, though Patrick Steinhardt notes he’s open to dropping the effort if the approach isn’t desired.

Key technical details:
- **Files touched**: `Makefile`, `compat/posix.h`, `compat/writev.c`, `wrapper.{c,h}`, `write-or-die.{c,h}`, `sideband.c`, `builtin/fast-import.c`, and build system files (`config.mak.uname`, `CMakeLists.txt`, `meson.build`).
- **New functions**: `git_writev()` and `git_writev_or_die()` wrappers, with platform-specific implementations in `compat/writev.c`.
- **Behavior change**: Replaces multiple `write()` calls with batched `writev()` in `sideband.c` (pktline transmission) and `fast-import.c` (cat-blob responses).
- **Edge case handling**: Explicit management of `MAX_IO_SIZE` to avoid partial writes.
- **Platform support**: Explicit NonStop compatibility fixes, with build system adjustments for CMake and Meson.

The series is self-contained and appears well-motivated, with clear ties to prior work (referenced commits) and attention to cross-platform concerns. The inclusion of benchmarks suggests measurable improvements, though their absence in the email leaves the magnitude unspecified. No prior discussion context is provided, but the cover letter’s tone implies this is a revival of a settled-but-reverted topic rather than a new controversy.
