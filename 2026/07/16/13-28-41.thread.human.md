This thread introduces a new `git repack --drop-filtered` option for partial clones, allowing users to safely reclaim disk space by removing locally cached promisor blobs exceeding a specified size threshold. The feature is implemented by first-time contributor Siddharth Shrimali and is now in its fourth iteration (v4), comprising seven patches that are functionally complete and production-ready.

The core functionality is stable: promisor objects are enumerated, filtered by size, and dropped by rebuilding the promisor pack while excluding them, with crash safety guaranteed by Git’s existing repack machinery. Junio’s feedback on earlier versions—including a bitmap validation fix and mechanical cleanups—has been incorporated, and his latest clarification confirms that three initially deferred items (remote verification, recency-based culling, and a drop log) are follow-up work, not blocking requirements. The author has acknowledged this and committed to final due diligence: running the full test suite and trial merges into `next` and `seen`. Documentation is thorough, covering the new `--drop-filtered` and `--dry-run` options, preconditions (e.g., `-a` required, promisor remote required), and safety optimizations like merge/rebase checks.

No maintainer decision has been made yet, but the series is well-scoped and addresses a practical need in partial clones. Key technical details include the new `list_objects_filter__filter_oidset()` helper (with a noted refactoring opportunity for duplicated logic) and the use of `ODB_FOR_EACH_OBJECT_PROMISOR_ONLY` for promisor-object enumeration. The implementation avoids lazy fetches and maintains backward compatibility, making it a strong candidate for integration once the final checks are complete.
