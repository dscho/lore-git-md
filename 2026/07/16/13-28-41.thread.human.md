The thread is about an RFC patch series introducing `git repack --drop-filtered`, a new option for partial clones that safely reclaims disk space by removing locally cached promisor blobs exceeding a user-specified size threshold (e.g., `--filter=blob:limit=10M`). The feature is designed to target only objects marked as promisor (ensuring they can be refetched from the remote), avoid locally created objects, and include safety checks to prevent drops during merges, rebases, or cherry-picks. The series is now in its second RFC version (7/7 patches), with all blocking issues from v1 addressed, including a validation flaw around `--write-bitmap-index` and the implementation of critical safety guards.

Key participants include Siddharth Shrimali (the first-time contributor and author), Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Christian Couder, and Siddharth Asthana (a reviewer). The series is functionally complete, with core logic, safety guards, and documentation in place, but its design is still under discussion. Junio recently proposed an alternative to the existing safety guards: a time-based heuristic that avoids culling recently fetched objects (e.g., those downloaded within the last 20 minutes) instead of relying on merge/rebase/cherry-pick checks. This would reduce redundant network operations while simplifying the design. Siddharth has already conceded that the current guards are optional optimizations rather than correctness measures, so this proposal may shape the next revision.

The series is not yet queued for integration, and no maintainer decision has been made. Open questions include whether to adopt Junio’s time-based heuristic, the reliability of promisor-object enumeration, and the scope of future filter extensions (e.g., object age or path). The drop log and remote verification remain deferred to follow-ups. The current implementation is limited to `blob:limit` filters and implies `-d` to reclaim disk space, with bitmaps silently disabled to avoid breaking pack closure.
