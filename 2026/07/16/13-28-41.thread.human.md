This thread is about an RFC patch series from first-time contributor Siddharth Shrimali that adds a `--drop-filtered` option to `git repack`, allowing partial clones to safely reclaim disk space by removing locally cached promisor blobs exceeding a size threshold. The v2 series is now complete (7 patches), addressing all blocking issues from v1: it fixed a validation flaw around bitmap handling, implemented critical safety guards to prevent drops during merges, rebases, or cherry-picks and to avoid dropping blobs referenced by the current index, and added comprehensive documentation.

Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has just raised three substantive technical questions about the v2 design. First, he wants confirmation that the promisor-object enumeration (`ODB_FOR_EACH_OBJECT_PROMISOR_ONLY`) is robust in practice, suggesting input from other partial-clone users would be valuable. Second, he seeks assurance that the repack machinery rebuilds promisor packs from locally available data only, without lazy fetches, and reuses the pre-enumerated object list. Third, and most critically, he questions the necessity of the safety guards (merge/rebase/cherry-pick checks and index validation), asking why they are needed if lazy-fetching can transparently restore dropped objects—his tone suggests skepticism about their added value.

The series is otherwise technically complete: it introduces the new CLI options (`--drop-filtered` and `--dry-run`), implements the core enumeration and removal logic, and documents the constraints and behavior. The drop log and remote verification are deferred to follow-ups, but the current implementation is functional. No maintainer decision on integration has been made yet, and the discussion now hinges on whether the safety guards are justified or if the promisor infrastructure alone is sufficient.
