This thread is about an RFC patch series introducing `git repack --drop-filtered`, a new option for partial clones that safely reclaims disk space by removing locally cached promisor blobs exceeding a user-specified size threshold. The series, now in its second iteration (RFC v2, 7/7 patches), is authored by first-time contributor Siddharth Shrimali and targets Git’s repack machinery to enable selective culling of promisor objects—those marked as recoverable from a promisor remote.

The core functionality is complete: the series implements `--drop-filtered` and `--dry-run` options, enforces safety constraints (e.g., requiring `-a`, rejecting `--filter-to`, and silently disabling bitmaps), and introduces critical safety guards to prevent drops during merges, rebases, or cherry-picks, or of blobs referenced by the current index. These guards, however, are now contested. Junio Hamano, the maintainer, has proposed replacing them with a time-based heuristic—avoiding culls of recently fetched objects (e.g., within the last 20 minutes)—to reduce redundant network operations. Siddharth has responded positively to this idea, framing it as a natural extension of the existing "enumerate-then-filter" framework. The discussion suggests the next revision will likely adopt this heuristic, though the current guards remain implemented as optional optimizations.

Key technical details include the use of `ODB_FOR_EACH_OBJECT_PROMISOR_ONLY` to enumerate promisor blobs, the repack machinery’s local-only construction of new promisor packs (avoiding lazy fetches), and the implied `-d` behavior to reclaim disk space. The series also introduces a drop log (deferred to a follow-up) and documents the feature in `git-repack.adoc`. Open questions revolve around the safety guard design, the reliability of promisor-object enumeration, and the deferred drop log and remote verification. The thread has seen substantive engagement from Junio and Siddharth Asthana, with the latter’s review in v1 driving the implementation of the current safety guards. No maintainer decision has been made yet, and the series remains under review.
