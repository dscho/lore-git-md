This thread introduces `git repack --drop-filtered`, an RFC patch series by first-time contributor Siddharth Shrimali that lets partial clones reclaim disk space by safely removing cached promisor blobs over a user-specified size (e.g., `--filter=blob:limit=10M`). The v2 series is now complete with seven patches, addressing all blocking feedback from v1: it fixes a validation flaw around bitmap handling, implements critical safety checks to block drops during merges or rebases and prevent removal of blobs referenced by the current index, and adds full documentation. The core logic enumerates promisor blobs via `ODB_FOR_EACH_OBJECT_PROMISOR_ONLY`, applies the size filter, and rebuilds promisor packs while excluding the dropped objects—implying `-d` to actually reclaim space.

The key participants are Siddharth (author), Junio C Hamano (maintainer), and reviewers Siddharth Asthana and Christian Couder. Junio’s latest feedback questions three aspects: whether the promisor-object enumeration is reliable in practice, whether the repack machinery avoids lazy fetches (it does, via `OBJECT_INFO_SKIP_FETCH_OBJECT`), and—most significantly—whether the safety guards (merge/rebase/cherry-pick checks and index validation) are necessary. Siddharth’s reply clarifies that these guards are conveniences to avoid redundant network operations, not correctness measures, since lazy-fetching would transparently restore dropped objects. He leaves it to the list to decide whether to retain them, which could simplify the next revision if they’re removed. The drop log and remote verification are deferred to follow-ups, but the core functionality is ready for review. No maintainer decision has been made yet, and the series remains in RFC status.
