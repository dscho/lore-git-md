This is a **substantive discussion** in the `git replay --linearize` thread, where Elijah Newren responds to Junio C Hamano’s defense of the current `--linearize` behavior with multiple branches. The email clarifies and reinforces Elijah’s position on the **multi-branch ambiguity**, arguing that the current implementation violates the principle of least astonishment and creates a **recoverability asymmetry** that favors rare use cases over common ones.

Elijah pushes back on Junio’s framing that users "ask for" concatenation by passing multiple branches. Instead, he argues that users are combining two intended features—updating multiple branches at once (`git replay`'s core functionality) and flattening a merge (`--linearize`)—and that the concatenation of unrelated branches is an **emergent, unintended behavior** that neither feature implies. He provides a concrete example: replaying three branches (`branchA`, `branchB`, `branchC`) where only `branchA` contains a merge. The current implementation would flatten all three branches into a single linear chain, whereas Elijah expects `branchA` to become linear while `branchB` and `branchC` remain independent. This distinction is critical: the user’s intent is to **flatten a merge**, not to **concatenate unrelated branches**.

The email also addresses Junio’s earlier suggestion that the problem could be solved by tracking replayed commits across invocations. Elijah agrees this is the right goal but points out that the current implementation lacks the persistent state to support it. He reiterates his preference for a **minimal fix**—restricting `--linearize` to a single positive ref—as an interim step to unblock the series, with **per-branch linearization** (the "complete fix") deferred to a later patch. This aligns with his earlier proposal and avoids the complexity of tracking state across invocations.

Elijah’s argument hinges on **usability and consistency**. He notes that the current behavior:
- Makes the **rare use case** (concatenating independent branches) easy at the expense of the **common one** (linearizing a single branch while keeping others independent).
- Ignores command-line order, creating a topology that is difficult to predict or recover from.
- Behaves inconsistently with `--advance` and `--revert`, which do not concatenate branches.
- Creates a **recoverability asymmetry**: untangling concatenated branches is harder than concatenating independent ones, so the design should favor the harder-to-recover-from operation.

The email also clarifies that Elijah’s proposed fixes (single-ref restriction or per-branch linearization) do not reintroduce the earlier bug about dropped lines of history, addressing Junio’s prior concern.
