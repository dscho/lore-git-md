This is a maintainer's reply in the ongoing discussion about adding `git rebase --abort` hints to exec-failure messages. Junio C Hamano agrees with Phillip Wood that aborting a rebase due to a failed exec command is rarely the right response, and he speculates that the workflow Harald Nordgren has in mind might not be a "normal" rebase at all. Instead, Junio suggests the user might be treating `git rebase` as a batch runner for tests or checks on each commit, where a failure should indeed halt the entire process. This framing implies that the `--abort` hint could be justified for such use cases, but it also raises the question of whether `git rebase` is the right tool for that job—or whether a more targeted command (like a hypothetical `git test-commits`) would be clearer. The reply does not propose a technical solution, but it shifts the discussion toward workflow intent and tool specialization, leaving the thread's central question—whether and when to show the `--abort` hint—still unresolved.
