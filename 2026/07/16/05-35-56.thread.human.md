This thread introduces a `--auto-reset` option for `git bisect`, aimed at automating the cleanup step in scripted or CI workflows. The feature is currently implemented to return the working tree to its original pre-bisect state after identifying the first bad commit, but Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has raised a fundamental objection: he expects the working tree to remain on the first bad commit (the culprit) instead. This disagreement has stalled progress, as the option name and behavior are now misaligned—`--auto-reset` implies a reset, but Junio’s preferred behavior would leave the working tree untouched.

The series itself consists of three patches: a preparatory refactoring to avoid reopening files by name, a quiet-checkout addition to suppress output during reset, and the core `--auto-reset` implementation. The first two patches are uncontroversial, but the third will need reworking depending on the outcome of the design discussion. Harald Nordgren, the author, has acknowledged the feedback and is open to changing the behavior, but no alternative names or approaches have been proposed yet. The thread is now at a decision point, with the maintainer’s preference likely to prevail unless other reviewers advocate for the original design. Until this is resolved, the feature’s utility and implementation details—such as the `BISECT_AUTO_RESET` state file—remain in flux.
