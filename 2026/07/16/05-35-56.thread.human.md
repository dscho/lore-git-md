This thread introduces a new `--auto-reset` option for `git bisect`, designed to streamline automated workflows by automatically returning the working tree to its original commit after identifying the first bad commit. The feature, proposed by Harald Nordgren, eliminates the need for an explicit `git bisect reset` in scripts or CI pipelines where manual intervention is impractical.

The series is currently under review in its first version (v1) and consists of three patches. The first refactors file descriptor handling to avoid reopening files by name, the second adds a quiet-checkout capability to suppress output during reset, and the third implements the `--auto-reset` option itself. The implementation touches `bisect.c`, `builtin/bisect.c`, the documentation, and adds new tests to `t/t6030-bisect-porcelain.sh`. The option is wired into both `git bisect start` and `git bisect run`, and the state is carefully managed to avoid persisting across sessions.

No review feedback has been received yet, so the series remains untested by the broader community. Open questions include how the feature behaves in edge cases like interrupted bisects, errors during `bisect run`, or descriptor failures. The patches are self-contained and do not depend on or conflict with other in-flight work. If accepted, this would be a small but practical improvement for users who rely on `git bisect` in automated environments.
