This is a review of the `git stash rename` feature patch by Emin Özata. Patrick Steinhardt raises three main concerns in his feedback, all targeting the design and implementation rather than surface-level issues.

First, he asks for clearer motivation in the commit message: why *this* implementation, and why *now*? The existing rationale references past discussions but doesn’t explain the author’s own use case or what makes this version different enough to reconsider the earlier rejection.

Second, he suggests renaming the subcommand from `rename` to `reword` to avoid confusion with the unrelated `git refs rename` command introduced recently. This is a naming bikeshed, but the point is valid—consistency matters in Git’s command vocabulary.

Third, and most substantively, he identifies performance risks in the current implementation. The patch drops and re-stores reflog entries one by one, which works fine with the traditional files backend but could be catastrophically slow with reftable. He recommends an atomic transaction that replaces the entire reflog in one operation instead of rewriting it entry-by-entry. This isn’t just a nit; it’s a fundamental design issue that could make the feature unusable at scale.

The review is substantive and tested—Patrick clearly ran the code in his head, spotted the reftable bottleneck, and proposed a concrete fix. The ball is now back to Emin to address these points before the patch can move forward.
