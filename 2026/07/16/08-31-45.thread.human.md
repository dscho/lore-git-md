This thread is about a proposed `git stash rename` (now renamed to `reword`) subcommand that lets users edit stash messages after creation. The feature was initially met with skepticism about its practicality, but user testimonials—including reports of 211 stashes with default messages in `git.git` and 100+ in another workflow—have convinced Junio C Hamano that the justification is sufficient. The patch is still under review, with no merge status reported.

The first version (`v1`) introduced `git stash rename` but faced criticism from Patrick Steinhardt over naming conflicts with `git refs rename` and concerns about the non-atomic, potentially quadratic implementation. Emin Özata has now committed to a `v2` that renames the subcommand to `reword` (aligning with Git’s existing terminology) and makes the reflog rewrite atomic using a single transaction. This should resolve the performance and correctness issues, particularly under the reftable backend. The selector constraints (index-based only) and safety checks remain unchanged.

The broader design question—Junio’s suggestion that stashes could be tied to branches—remains unaddressed but is now separate from the immediate implementation. The next step is the `v2` submission, which will focus on the transactional reflog rewrite and the renamed subcommand. Key participants include Junio (who now accepts the feature’s justification but hasn’t reviewed the implementation), Patrick (who raised the critical design concerns), and brian m. carlson and erik88 (who provided the user testimonials).
