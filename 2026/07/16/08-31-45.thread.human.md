The `git stash rename` patch series, introduced by Emin Özata, aims to let users edit stash messages after creation—a long-requested usability improvement. The initial version (`v1`) adds a `git stash rename <stash@{n}> <new-message>` subcommand, implemented as a drop-and-re-store sequence to avoid in-place reflog rewrites. It touches `builtin/stash.c`, documentation, bash completion, and tests, with careful handling of edge cases like invalid selectors and mid-operation failures.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review raises three major concerns that could shape the series’ future. First, he asks for a clearer justification: the commit message cites historical discussions but doesn’t explain why this feature is needed *now* or what concrete pain point it solves beyond existing workflows (e.g., `drop` + `store`). Second, he flags a naming conflict with the recent `git refs rename` command, suggesting `reword` as a less ambiguous alternative. Third, and most critically, he highlights performance and atomicity issues: the current implementation’s non-atomic drop-and-re-store approach risks quadratic runtime with large reflogs (especially under reftable) and could leave the reflog inconsistent if interrupted. Steinhardt advocates for a transactional design to address these risks.

The series remains under review, with no merge status reported. The next steps hinge on how the author responds to these design challenges—particularly the atomicity and naming questions—before the patch can move forward.
