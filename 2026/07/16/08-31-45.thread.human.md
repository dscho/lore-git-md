This thread is about a proposed `git stash rename` subcommand (with `reword` as a suggested alternative name) that would allow users to edit stash messages after creation. The series, authored by Emin Özata, is currently at version 1 and under review, but its future is uncertain after maintainer feedback.

The core idea is to address a long-standing usability gap, but the patch’s motivation has come under scrutiny. Patrick Steinhardt raised concerns about unclear use cases, naming conflicts with `git refs rename`, and a non-atomic implementation that could perform poorly with large reflogs, especially under the reftable backend. Junio C Hamano has now weighed in, questioning whether the feature is even necessary. He argues that the current justification—editing messages shown in `git stash list`—is weak, as users can already achieve the same result by unstashing to a branch and committing with a corrected message. Junio’s feedback shifts the focus from *how* the feature works to *whether* it should exist at all, leaving the patch’s fate dependent on the author’s ability to articulate a concrete, real-world scenario where existing workflows fall short.

The technical implementation touches `builtin/stash.c`, adds tests, and handles edge cases like invalid selectors and non-stash reflog entries. However, the drop-and-re-store approach is non-atomic and risks quadratic runtime, a concern that hasn’t been addressed in this round of feedback. The naming debate (`rename` vs. `reword`) also remains unresolved. For now, the thread is stalled on the motivation question, with no clear path forward unless the author can provide a stronger rationale.
