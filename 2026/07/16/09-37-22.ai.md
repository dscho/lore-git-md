This is a substantive review reply in the ongoing `git rebase --abort` advice thread. Phillip Wood pushes back against Harald Nordgren’s framing of `--abort` as a universal "escape hatch" for exec failures, arguing that aborting is rarely the right response to a failed exec step. Instead, he proposes two concrete alternatives: teaching `git rebase --skip` to handle rescheduled exec commands (a feature gap he identifies), and adding a hint that guides users toward editing the todo list when the failure stems from a bad command. His critique is grounded in workflow realism—he contends that aborting discards work unnecessarily, while continuing (with fixes) or skipping (for transient issues) are more constructive paths. The reply leaves the thread’s central tension unresolved: whether the `--abort` hint should be shown for *all* exec failures (Harald’s position) or suppressed for recoverable ones (Phillip’s implicit preference). No new code or tests are proposed here, but the discussion now hinges on competing visions of user guidance.
