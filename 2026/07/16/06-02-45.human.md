This is a small but user-facing improvement to `git rebase` messaging. The patch (still at v1) aims to make the `--abort` option more discoverable by adding it to two places where only `--continue` was previously mentioned: when an "exec" step fails during rebase, and in the status help text shown during interactive rebase operations like commit splitting or editing.

The series has already reached consensus on the status-message additions and on the design of the exec-failure message (a failure-specific warning followed by a neutral `--abort` hint, with the hint suppressed if the exec failure is due to a conflict). The only remaining open question is whether the `--abort` hint should be shown for *all* exec failures or only for irrecoverable ones (e.g., broken scripts). Junio’s latest review suggests that transient failures (like missing files) may not warrant the hint, as users can often `--continue` after resolving the issue.

Harald’s reply here is brief but reaffirms his position: when a user is stuck in the middle of a rebase, offering both a way forward (`--continue`) and a way back (`--abort`) is the least surprising behavior. He frames it as a matter of reducing unnecessary obscurity, not as a workflow recommendation. The tone is pragmatic, and the argument is user-focused rather than technical. This keeps the discussion alive but doesn’t resolve the conditional-suppression question Junio raised. The patch is well-motivated and uncontroversial in its current form, but the exec-failure hint may need further refinement before it’s ready to merge.
