This is a performance patch targeting the sideband protocol implementation. It reduces syscall overhead in `send_sideband()` by switching from two `write()` calls per pktline (one for the header, one for the payload) to a single `writev()` call that sends both parts at once.

The patch is the fourth in a five-patch series that reintroduces `writev()` support to Git’s I/O layer. The commit message provides a clear before-and-after benchmark: under a synthetic workload that eliminates packfile generation overhead, `git-upload-pack` becomes about 1.37× faster (193 ms → 141 ms). The real-world motivation is GitLab’s observation that under high cache-hit loads, the kernel’s pipe-write semaphore becomes a bottleneck; halving the syscall count pushes that bottleneck further out.

Code changes are minimal and mechanical: the loop in `sideband.c` now builds an `iovec` array for the header and payload and calls `writev_or_die()` instead of two separate `write_or_die()` calls. The patch is well-motivated, includes reproducible benchmarking instructions, and is authored by Patrick Steinhardt, who has both the domain knowledge and the production context to justify the change.
