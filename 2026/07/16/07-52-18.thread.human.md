The thread is about reviving a `writev(3p)`-like wrapper to reduce system call overhead in Git’s low-level I/O operations, specifically for pktline transmission in `sideband.c` and cat-blob responses in `fast-import.c`. Originally merged in Git 2.54, the wrapper was reverted due to platform compatibility issues (NonStop, CMake), but this v2 series incorporates fixes for those issues, resolves all prior technical objections, and demonstrates measurable performance improvements.

Patrick Steinhardt, the author, has addressed every concern raised in the v1 review. The integer-overflow issue identified by Simon Richter was resolved by Junio C Hamano’s confirmation of existing bounds-checking safety and a simplification of the compatibility wrapper. The API naming dispute was settled in favor of `git_write_gather()` to avoid implying POSIX semantics, and the architectural question about atomicity was resolved by Johannes Sixt and Junio’s agreement that Git’s pktline and cat-blob use cases don’t require it. The wrapper now writes only the first `iovec` in the loop, restoring the non-interleaving property of `write(3p)` and avoiding interleaving even on platforms without `writev(2)`. This change also simplifies the wrapper by removing most loop logic, though Patrick proposes retaining the overflow check for POSIX compliance.
