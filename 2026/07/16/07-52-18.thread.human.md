This thread is about reviving a performance optimization that was previously merged and then reverted—Patrick Steinhardt’s patch series reintroducing a `writev(3p)`-like wrapper to reduce system call overhead in Git’s low-level I/O operations. The series, now at version 2, targets pktline transmission in `sideband.c` and cat-blob responses in `fast-import.c`, aiming to batch writes for better performance. All technical objections from the first version have been resolved: the integer-overflow concern was addressed by Junio C Hamano’s confirmation of existing safety checks, the API was renamed to `git_write_gather()` to avoid implying POSIX semantics, and the architectural question about atomicity was settled after Johannes Sixt and Junio confirmed that Git’s use cases don’t require it.

The latest email covers patch 3 of 5 in the v2 series, which tackles a platform-specific limitation on systems like NonStop where overly large `iovec` entries can cause `writev(2)` to fail. The patch introduces `xwritev()`, a new wrapper that splits large requests into smaller chunks to stay within `MAX_IO_SIZE`, mirroring the behavior of Git’s existing `xwrite()`. This ensures compatibility while preserving the performance benefits of batched writes. The change is confined to `wrapper.c` and `wrapper.h` and integrates cleanly with the existing I/O infrastructure, though like the rest of the series, it lacks test coverage.

The series is technically complete and under review, with all substantive feedback incorporated. Junio C Hamano has accepted Patrick’s proposed tweaks to the compatibility wrapper, simplifying it and resolving the last technical hurdles. The remaining open question is whether Junio will merge the series—his appetite for the performance gains versus the added complexity hasn’t been stated yet. The participants, including Simon Richter and Johannes Sixt, have all weighed in with substantive reviews, and the discussion has been constructive, with no unresolved objections. The only minor loose end is the lack of a range-diff to the original reverted version, which would have made it easier to track changes, but the v2 range-diff provided here covers the key simplifications.
