This is a **refactoring patch** in Tian Yuchen’s v9 series migrating Git’s global configuration variables into `struct repo_config_values` as part of the ongoing libification effort. The thread is a **design discussion** between Tian and Pablo Sabater about the destructor function `repo_config_values_clear()`, specifically its signature and error-handling philosophy.

Pablo’s latest reply **reiterates his preference for robustness** over fail-fast behavior. He argues that `repo_config_values_clear()` should take a `struct repo_config_values *` directly (matching `_init()`) and access the private field without going through `repo_config_values()`, which currently BUG()s for uninitialized repositories or submodules. This would eliminate the NEEDSWORK workaround, avoid silent submodule leaks, and ensure the destructor works for any repository that has undergone `memset()`—regardless of initialization state. Pablo sees the destructor’s role as purely mechanical cleanup, leaving validation to the rest of the codebase.

Tian, while agreeing to align the signatures of `_clear()` and `_init()`, **still favors the assertion guardrail** in `repo_config_values()`. He views uninitialized repositories as a misuse that should be surfaced early, not silently tolerated. The discussion reveals a **philosophical divide**: Pablo prioritizes leak-free operation, while Tian prioritizes catching misuse. Neither has conceded, but Tian’s next reroll (v10) will likely adopt the aligned signature while retaining the assertion, leaving the submodule leak risk unresolved.

The broader series (migrating 9 variables) is technically complete and CI-clean, but this design debate could delay merging. If you care about libification or repository lifecycle management, this thread is worth following—expect a v10 reroll soon.
