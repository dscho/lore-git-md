This is a **design discussion** in the ongoing `git log --graph` visual root indentation series, now at v9. Pablo Sabater responds to Mirko Faina’s bounded wrapping refinement with a counter-proposal: **capping indentation at four levels instead of two** before resetting to the left margin. His argument is that a four-column wrap creates a more abrupt visual break, reinforcing the unrelatedness of commits while avoiding the "pyramid effect" that Mirko’s two-column version could produce in edge cases. Pablo illustrates both approaches with ASCII examples, showing how the four-column version handles ambiguity (when the number of visual roots is a multiple of the cap) by forcing an extra level of indentation if the next commit is not a visual root.

The email also reopens the question of whether an opt-out flag (`--no-graph-indent`) is still necessary, regardless of the final indentation design. Pablo’s tone suggests he remains open to alternatives but prefers the four-column wrap for its clarity. The discussion is now focused on **fine-tuning the visual rule**—how many levels to indent before wrapping—rather than the underlying architecture, which was settled in earlier iterations. The series is otherwise technically complete and ready for final review; this is the last unresolved design decision before the maintainers can merge it.
