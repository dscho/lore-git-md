This is a design discussion about Git's default behavior for displaying commit metadata. Oswald Buddenhagen challenges the long-standing rationale for why `git log` and similar commands default to showing the *author date* instead of the *committer date*. He argues that for most users exploring branch histories, the committer date—when the commit actually landed in the target branch—is more meaningful than the "meta" details of when the patch was submitted. While he acknowledges that the author's name should remain the default (as it identifies who did the work), he suggests that pairing the author name with the committer date would be more useful in practice.

Buddenhagen proposes a concrete change: defaulting to showing the committer date instead of the author date, with a visual distinction to avoid confusion. He suggests renaming the field from "Date:" to "Stamp:" in the output, finding the latter term more intuitive for this purpose. He also floats the idea of introducing a new format name (e.g., "compact") for this behavior, though he dismisses backward compatibility concerns for users parsing porcelain output without specifying a format. The tone is direct and opinionated, framing the current default as a historical quirk rather than a deliberate design choice aligned with user needs.

The email does not engage with the technical implications of such a change (e.g., impact on `git blame`, `git bisect`, or scripts relying on the current output), nor does it address how other workflows (e.g., CI-driven or rebase-heavy) might be affected. It also does not propose a configuration option to let users choose their preferred default, which could be a middle ground. The discussion remains open-ended, but Buddenhagen’s argument shifts the focus from Git’s mailing-list origins to broader usability concerns. Whether this will gain traction depends on how other contributors weigh the trade-offs between historical consistency and practical utility.
