This is a refactoring series (v10, 9 patches) that advances the `the_repository` removal effort by migrating more global configuration variables into `struct repo_config_values`. The series is now **technically complete**, addressing all prior feedback: the memory leak in `core_pager_config()` is fixed, the destructor signature mismatch is resolved, and redundant function calls in getters are eliminated. The patches are split into two logical groups: infrastructure and string variables (patches 1-6) and enum migrations (patches 7-9).

The key changes since v9 are mechanical but important: the destructor `repo_config_values_clear()` now takes a `struct repo_config_values*` (enabling submodule support and avoiding initialization-order crashes), redundant calls to `repo_config_values()` are cached in `repo_excludes_file()` and `git_editor()`, and minor typos are fixed. Pablo Sabater’s review feedback is fully incorporated, removing the last technical blockers. The series remains **CI-clean** and **memory-leak-free**, with all heap-allocated fields (`excludes_file`, `editor_program`, `pager_program`, `askpass_program`, `attributes_file`, `apply_default_whitespace`, `apply_default_ignorewhitespace`) properly managed.

Files touched span `environment.c`/`environment.h` (primary), `apply.c`, `branch.c`, `builtin/push.c`, `pager.c`, and others. New symbols include the updated destructor and direct field access for migrated variables (e.g., `repo_config_values(the_repository)->editor_program`). The RFC questions from earlier versions—whether migrating program paths is worthwhile and how this relates to `git_branch_track`—remain open but do not affect correctness. The author has promised follow-up patches to clean up comments and variable classification in `repo_config_values`, though these are not blockers.

The series is **ready for `next`** and represents a solid step in the libification effort. The remaining `NEEDSWORK` items (circular dependency for `push_default` enum placement, `object_creation_mode` relocation) are documented and deferred. Reviewers should focus on the updated destructor signature and the cached getter calls, as these were the last substantive changes. The patches are well-motivated, with clear commit messages and no user-visible behavior changes.
