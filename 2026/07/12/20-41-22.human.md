This is the sixth patch in Junio C Hamano’s six-part documentation series clarifying contributor guidance. The series aims to reduce ambiguity in Git’s patch submission and integration process by codifying existing practices in `Documentation/MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`.

This specific email is a surface-level review from Michael Montalbo on the sixth patch, which standardizes the writing style for "What's cooking" report entries. The patch itself adds explicit guidance that these entries should use past or present perfect tense in passive voice (e.g., "The XYZ subsystem has been updated to..."), contrasting with the present-tense, imperative-mood style of commit messages. Montalbo’s feedback is minor: he notes that the patch introduces a "TIP" section, while the rest of `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` consistently uses "NOTE" for similar annotations. This is a purely editorial suggestion with no bearing on the patch’s substance, and Junio will likely incorporate it in the next reroll.

The series as a whole is uncontroversial and well-motivated, addressing recurring pain points for new contributors. Earlier patches in the series have already clarified the role of the `seen` branch, the meaning of "Will queue" and "Will merge to `next`" in maintainer communications, and the proper use of `Reviewed-by:` and `Acked-by:` trailers. The feedback so far has been light and focused on wording, formatting, and presentation, with no objections to the core goals. A reroll is expected soon to address these minor editorial tweaks.
