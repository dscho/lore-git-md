This is the latest update to patch 5/7 in Harald Nordgren’s long-running `git branch --delete-merged` series, now in its 18th iteration. The series is feature-complete and ready for merging, with all design questions resolved and only minor mechanical fixes remaining.

This specific email addresses a test-case nit in the integration suite: the `on-local` branch was originally set to track `mainline` (a copy of `main`) rather than `main` itself, which confused Phillip Wood during review. Harald explains that the indirection was meant to protect `mainline` from deletion, but after experimenting, he proposes a cleaner fix—dropping `mainline` entirely and instead configuring `branch.main.pushRemote origin` to shield `main` from being treated as a forked branch. This change makes the test setup more intuitive while preserving the intended safety behavior.

The broader series introduces a new `--delete-merged` subcommand that safely deletes local branches merged into their upstream, with multiple safety mechanisms: per-branch opt-out via `branch.<name>.deleteMerged=false`, protection for branches used as upstreams (now implemented as "abort-and-clear"), `--dry-run` preview, and the foundational `--forked` filter. This patch (5/7) implements the core deletion logic, including the `<branch>` argument support added in response to Phillip’s usability feedback. All substantive review points have been addressed, and the remaining work is purely mechanical—applying test improvements and a pre-merge flags cleanup in patches 2–4/7.
