This is a **contributor housekeeping** follow-up in the `git-subtree` safeguard thread. D. Ben Knoble seeks clarification on the Rust rewrite’s stance toward manual `-X subtree` merges. Ian Jackson’s earlier message (quoted in the email) explains that the Rust rewrite will **not** support unmarked subtree merges (those performed manually with `-X subtree`), but will not actively prevent or detect them at runtime. The Rust rewrite will treat such merges as if they were downstream branches predating the subtree addition, which may result in missing parent relationships during `split` operations—undesirable but not catastrophic. However, interleaving manual `-X subtree` merges with `git-subtree --squash` operations could produce unpredictable and incorrect tree states, a scenario the Rust rewrite cannot reliably detect or prevent.

Knoble’s question distills to whether the Rust rewrite intends to **discourage** the use of `-X subtree` merges entirely, or merely discourage **mixing** them with the new implementation. The distinction matters for users who rely on `-X subtree` for workflows outside `git-subtree`’s scope. The thread context confirms that the safeguard itself (the subject of the patch series) only prevents mixing the two implementations by detecting the `subtree.rust-rewrite` config key, not by policing manual merge strategies. The Rust rewrite’s design intent is to avoid guessing or heuristics, so it will not attempt to parse or validate unmarked merges, but it will not block users from performing them. The documentation will advise against the practice, but enforcement is limited to the safeguard’s runtime check.
