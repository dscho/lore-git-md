This is the **final patch (10/10)** in a **fully reviewed, ready-to-merge performance optimization series** for Git’s commit-reach subsystem. The patch removes the commit-date ordering fallback in `paint_down_to_common()`, completing the series’ core goal of enabling **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric merge-base queries by relying exclusively on generation-number ordering.

The fallback, introduced in 2018 to mitigate performance regressions with v1 commit-graphs, is now obsolete. The series’ earlier patches implemented **side-exhaustion termination**—halting the walk when one side’s commit queue empties—which eliminates the deep traversals the fallback was designed to prevent. Benchmarks on the Linux kernel repo show dramatic improvements: `git merge-base --all v4.8 v4.9` now takes **5,725 steps (0.02s)** with v1 graphs (vs. 167,413 steps/0.25s pre-optimization) and **3,887 steps (0.01s)** with v2 graphs.

The patch also deletes the unused `corrected_commit_dates_enabled()` function and updates the technical documentation (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`) to reflect the removal of the fallback. Tests in `t6600-test-reach.sh` are adjusted to account for reduced step counts under the new logic, with deterministic assertions ensuring functional equivalence. The changes are **low-risk** (no on-disk format impact) and **self-verifying**, with exhaustive edge-case coverage for clock-skewed commits and mixed finite/INFINITY generation numbers.
