This is the **final patch (10/10)** in a **fully reviewed, ready-to-merge performance optimization series** for Git’s commit-reach subsystem. The patch **removes the commit-date ordering fallback** in `paint_down_to_common()`, completing the series’ core goal of enabling **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric merge-base queries by relying solely on generation-number ordering.

### What the patch does
- **Removes the fallback** introduced in 2018 (commit `091f4cf3`) that switched from generation-number ordering to commit-date ordering when the commit-graph lacked corrected commit dates (v1 graphs). This fallback was a workaround for performance regressions in repositories like the Linux kernel, where v1 topo levels caused deep traversals (e.g., 636k commits for `git merge-base v4.8 v4.9`).
- **Deletes `corrected_commit_dates_enabled()`** from `commit-graph.c` and `commit-graph.h`, as it has no remaining callers.
- **Updates the technical documentation** (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`) to remove all references to the fallback, simplifying the explanation of termination conditions and side-exhaustion logic.
- **Adjusts tests** in `t6600-test-reach.sh` to reflect reduced step counts (e.g., `merge-base --all commit-9-9 commit-9-1` drops from 81 steps to 9 in full commit-graph mode) and removes outdated comments about clock-skew workarounds.

### Why this is safe
The fallback is no longer needed because **side-exhaustion** (introduced in earlier patches) terminates the walk as soon as one side’s commit queue empties, preventing deep traversals regardless of queue ordering. Benchmarks on the Linux kernel repo show the optimization reduces steps from 167k to **5.7k** (v1 graph) and **3.9k** (v2 graph), with corresponding wall-clock speedups (e.g., 3.25s → 0.02s).

The patch also **fixes a correctness issue**: the fallback’s commit-date ordering could violate topological guarantees, causing premature termination or missed merge bases. With generation ordering always active, the `min_generation` check in `paint_queue_get()` correctly identifies when the walk has entered the finite-generation region, ensuring all merge bases are found.
