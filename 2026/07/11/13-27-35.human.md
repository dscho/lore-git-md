This is the **final, ready-to-merge version (v6)** of a performance optimization series targeting Git’s `paint_down_to_common()` function in the commit-reach subsystem. The series is **fully reviewed, technically complete, and addresses a long-standing bottleneck** in merge-base calculations for asymmetric histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). It delivers **100-1000x speedups** in pathological cases while maintaining correctness.

The core change teaches `paint_down_to_common()` to terminate early when one side of the history is exhausted, eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided commit queues. The optimization is gated by generation ordering to ensure correctness, and the series includes thorough test coverage, documentation, and instrumentation to verify the new behavior. The final patch removes the obsolete commit-date ordering fallback, simplifying the code and ensuring consistent queue ordering.

**Performance impact**: Benchmarks show dramatic improvements. For example, `merge-base --all` across a 2.6M-commit monorepo drops from **3.67s to 5ms**, and step counts in git.git are reduced by **30-60%** for common queries. The series is **self-verifying**, with deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes (none, full, half, no-gdat) and edge cases (clock skew, redundant ancestors, infinity-both-sides).
