Core problem or goal
Clarify and streamline contributor guidance in `Documentation/MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, addressing recurring questions and ambiguities about the patch submission, review, and integration process. The series codifies existing practices rather than introducing new ones, focusing on practical aspects such as commit message structure (now formalized as **Observation**, **Design (The Approach)**, **Implementation (The Changes)**), handling unresponsive patches, the meaning of `Reviewed-by:`/`Acked-by:` trailers, the role of the `seen` branch, and how to draft "What's cooking" report descriptions. The fourth and fifth patches explicitly disambiguate the role of the `seen` branch—emphasizing that its use is not an acceptance signal but an early integration step—and clarify the meaning of "Will queue" and "Will merge to `next`" annotations in maintainer communications. The sixth patch adds explicit style guidance for "What's cooking" report entries, specifying past or present perfect tense in passive voice (e.g., "The XYZ subsystem has been updated to...") to contrast with the present-tense, imperative-mood style of commit messages. The second patch now also acknowledges that reviewer silence may reflect disinterest, not just busyness or oversight, following Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback.

Key technical details
- Files touched: `Documentation/MyFirstContribution.adoc`, `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`
- No new commands, config keys, CLI options, or code behavior introduced
- Clarifies the meaning of:
  - `seen` branch: explicitly described as an early integration step for testing and exposure, not a signal of acceptance; patches may land here even before review
  - "Will queue" in maintainer replies: a promise to add the topic to `seen`, carrying no implication of acceptance or approval
  - "Will merge to `next`" in "What's cooking" reports: the actual signal of maintainer intent to merge
  - `Reviewed-by:` and `Acked-by:` trailers: social signals of approval, with specific rules for carry-over between patch versions
- Introduces explicit style guidance for:
  - Commit message structure: **Observation** (problem), **Design (The Approach)** (solution rationale), **Implementation (The Changes)** (concrete changes, written in imperative mood)
  - Formatting: 50-character subject lines, lowercase after “area:” prefix, 68–72 column wrapping (pragmatic guideline to ensure readability in quoted email threads), present-tense imperative mood
  - Trailer carry-over: `Acked-by:` may be retained for minor changes (e.g., typo fixes or style tweaks) that do not affect reviewed logic; `Reviewed-by:` should generally *not* be carried over unless the patch is resent unchanged. Contributors must explicitly note significant changes in the cover letter.
  - "What's cooking" report entries: past or present perfect tense in passive voice (e.g., "The XYZ subsystem has been updated to..."), contrasting with the present-tense, imperative-mood style of commit messages
  - Handling unresponsive patches: contributors may send a polite "ping" after one to two weeks of silence, and silence does not imply approval. The guidance now explicitly acknowledges that silence may reflect reviewer disinterest, not just busyness or oversight, and encourages reviewers to communicate disinterest rather than leaving contributors in limbo. Recommends using the existing `git-contacts` script to identify additional reviewers.
