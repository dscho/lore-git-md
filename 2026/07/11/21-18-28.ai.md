This is a **post-merge stylistic discussion** continuing the exchange between Junio C Hamano and Taylor Blau about how to structure patches that introduce a failing test followed by its fix.

Junio responds to Taylor’s defense of splitting the test and fix into separate commits by clarifying his earlier concern: the issue is not with reading the history after the fact (where `git log -W -p` works fine) but with the *initial review experience* on the mailing list. When a patch email shows only a few lines of context, a reviewer seeing a multi-line test body with `test_expect_failure` in the first patch and then a flip to `test_expect_success` in the second may struggle to understand what behavior is actually changing, especially if the test setup is complex. Junio frames this as a trade-off: while splitting the commits may make the author’s life easier, it can make the reviewer’s job harder when they lack full context in the email. He does not demand a change but suggests that the project’s imbalance between patch volume and reviewer capacity makes this a relevant consideration. The discussion remains focused on workflow ergonomics rather than code correctness.
