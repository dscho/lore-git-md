This is a **post-merge stylistic discussion** about patch presentation in the Git project. Junio C Hamano responds to Taylor Blau’s defense of splitting failing tests and fixes into separate commits, clarifying his earlier caution. He acknowledges that the two-commit structure is not problematic when viewing history with `git log -W -p` but argues that it makes pre-review harder for mailing-list reviewers who see only the patch email, not the full context. Junio suggests that the trade-off—easier patch writing versus harder pre-review—may not favor the two-commit approach, given the project’s imbalance between patch volume and reviewer capacity. The discussion remains a matter of preference and project workflow, not a technical objection, and no changes to the recently merged commit-graph fix are proposed or needed.
