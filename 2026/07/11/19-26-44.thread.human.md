This thread is about a six-patch documentation series from Junio C Hamano aimed at clarifying and streamlining contributor guidance in `Documentation/MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`. The series codifies existing practices, addressing recurring questions about patch submission, review, and integration, including commit message structure, trailer semantics, and the roles of Git’s integration branches.

The series is under review, with minor editorial revisions pending in response to feedback from Weijie Yuan, Matt Hunter, Michael Montalbo, and D. Ben Knoble. These revisions focus on phrasing, AsciiDoc formatting, and structural clarity in the commit-message template, though a lingering ambiguity in the template’s numbering (whether **Observation**, **Design**, and **Implementation** are parallel to **Title** and **Body** or subparts of the body) remains unresolved. Junio has accepted most suggestions, including rephrasing the **Design (The Approach)** section and consolidating redundant formatting guidance, but has not yet addressed the numbering issue or the idea of including concrete commit-message examples.

Patrick Steinhardt’s recent review adds a substantive nuance to the second patch, which guides contributors on handling unresponsive patches. He points out that the current text omits a key reason for silence: reviewers may simply not be interested in the patch. While the patch already acknowledges that reviewers may be busy or overlook contributions, Patrick suggests explicitly noting disinterest as a common reality, framing it as a harsh but necessary truth. His feedback is narrowly scoped—adding a single sentence—but raises a broader question about whether the documentation should encourage reviewers to proactively signal disinterest. The review does not challenge the patch’s core recommendation (sending a polite ping) but refines its social context.

No technical changes or merge status updates have been reported. The series remains focused on documentation-only improvements, with no dependencies on other in-flight work. The unresolved editorial issues and Patrick’s suggestion are the main loose ends, but the series’ overarching goals—clarifying contributor expectations and codifying existing practices—remain uncontested.
