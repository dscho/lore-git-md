This thread is a six-patch documentation series from Junio C Hamano aimed at clarifying and streamlining contributor guidance in Git’s documentation. The series updates `Documentation/MyFirstContribution.adoc` and `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to codify existing practices around patch submission, review, and integration, addressing common ambiguities such as commit message structure, the role of the `seen` branch, and the meaning of maintainer annotations like "Will queue" and "Will merge to `next`."

The latest patch, the sixth in the series, focuses on the writing style for "What's cooking" report entries. It formalizes the use of past or present perfect tense in passive voice for these summaries (e.g., "The XYZ subsystem has been updated to..."), contrasting with the present-tense, imperative-mood style used in commit messages. This change replaces a generic instruction with concrete guidance, improving readability and reducing confusion for contributors who draft these reports. The patch is uncontroversial, as it merely documents the style Junio has long used in his reports.
