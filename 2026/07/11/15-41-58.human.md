This is a **design discussion** in the ongoing thread about Pablo Sabater’s `git log --graph` cascading indentation feature, now at v9. Pablo responds to Mirko Faina’s usability objection with three concrete proposals, shifting the conversation from whether the feature should exist to how it should behave by default.

Mirko’s concern—that the always-on indentation clutters output for simple cases like sequential one-patch series—is now acknowledged as a real issue, not just a hypothetical. Pablo does not dismiss it; instead, he offers alternatives. His first suggestion is to keep the current behavior as the default but add a `--no-graph-indent` flag for users who prefer the traditional flat layout. This preserves the feature’s intended clarity for complex histories while allowing opt-out for simpler ones.

The second proposal is more ambitious: a “zig-zag” indentation pattern that alternates left and right instead of cascading ever deeper. Pablo sketches an example (A → B → C → D) and notes the trade-off—zig-zag could reintroduce ambiguity when multiple commits share the same parent, forcing a choice between continuing the cascade or resetting indentation. This idea is exploratory, not a polished design, but it shows willingness to reconsider the visualization rules if the community prefers it.

The third implicit option is to do nothing now and merge as-is, addressing usability later. Pablo’s closing line—“I think we need some more opinions about the design”—invites maintainers to weigh in, signaling that the series is not yet ready for `next` despite its technical completeness.

This reply is **high-weight**: it moves the thread from “is this correct?” to “is this the right user experience?” and provides concrete alternatives. The next step is likely a maintainer ruling on whether to (a) merge with `--no-graph-indent`, (b) adopt zig-zag or another heuristic, or (c) postpone the decision. Until then, the series remains in limbo.
