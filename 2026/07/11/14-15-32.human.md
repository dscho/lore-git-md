This is a review comment on Pablo Sabater’s v9 patch series implementing cascading indentation for visual roots in `git log --graph`. The series is now technically complete, addressing all known edge cases, but Mirko Faina raises a **user-experience concern**: the new indentation behavior may feel excessive or unnecessary when viewing simple histories, such as sequences of one-patch series, where the visual distinction between roots and unrelated commits is less critical.

Mirko suggests a **two-mode approach**: keep the traditional flat graph rendering as the default, and enable cascading indentation only when using `--oneline` (or `--format=reference`). This would preserve backward compatibility while still offering the improved clarity for users who explicitly opt into a compact format. The proposal is framed as a design preference rather than a technical flaw, but it touches on a recurring tension in Git’s UI: balancing visual consistency with user control over output density.

The timing of this feedback is notable. The series has undergone nine revisions, with the architectural foundation (lookahead buffer, unified commit retrieval) now stable and well-reviewed. Junio Hamano and Phillip Wood’s final reviews are pending, but the core logic is unlikely to change unless a major issue emerges. Mirko’s suggestion, while reasonable, would require a non-trivial redesign—adding a configuration knob or command-line flag to toggle indentation, and potentially duplicating or conditionally branching the graph-rendering logic. Given the series’ maturity, such a change would likely be deferred to a follow-up effort unless other reviewers echo the concern.

For now, this is a **surface-level design critique** rather than a blocker. The series remains ready for `next` once the final reviews are complete, but Mirko’s comment may prompt discussion about whether the indentation behavior should be opt-in or configurable in the future.
