This is the **eighth patch in the v6 series** optimizing Git’s merge-base computation, and it implements the **core performance optimization**: early termination of `paint_down_to_common()` when one side of the walk is exhausted. The patch is part of a **fully reviewed, ready-to-merge series** that delivers **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric merge-base queries (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories).

The change adds a termination condition to `paint_queue_get()`: once the walk enters the finite-generation region (where topological ordering holds), it stops early if either `parent1_count` or `parent2_count` drops to zero and no pending merge-base candidates remain. This avoids unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories. The patch also updates the technical documentation (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`) to explain the new "side-exhaustion" condition and its interaction with generation-number regions.

### Key details

- **Files touched**: `commit-reach.c` (logic), `Documentation/technical/paint-down-to-common.adoc` (docs), and `t/t6600-test-reach.sh` (test adjustments).
- **Behavior change**: The walk now terminates when `(!state->parent1_count || !state->parent2_count) && !state->mb_candidate_count && last_gen < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`.
- **Performance impact**: Step counts drop dramatically (e.g., from 72,264 to 44,589 steps for `merge-base --all v2.0.0 v2.55.0-rc1` in git.git).
- **Safety**: The INFINITY gate ensures correctness; commits without generation numbers (e.g., those not in the commit-graph) are still ordered by commit date, which is not topologically reliable, so the optimization only activates in the finite-generation region.
