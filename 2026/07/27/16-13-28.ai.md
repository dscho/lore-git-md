This is the **thread conclusion** for Hardik Kumar’s refactoring patch series changing the return type of `utf8_strwidth()` and `utf8_strnwidth()` from `int` to `size_t`. The email marks the **withdrawal of the patch** after sustained reviewer opposition, particularly from Junio C Hamano, Pablo Sabater, and Phillip Wood.

Hardik acknowledges that the patch’s approach—reintroducing explicit casts to `int` at call sites—fails to deliver meaningful type-system improvements and instead creates unnecessary complexity and risk. He concedes that the change is "not worth" pursuing, citing Phillip Wood’s critique that the `size_t` return type is impractical for functions whose results are almost always used in contexts requiring `int` (e.g., `printf()` padding calculations). The patch’s withdrawal resolves the blocking feedback from reviewers, who argued that the status quo (with improved documentation) is preferable to a disruptive and incomplete refactoring.

Key technical outcomes:
- The TODO comment in `utf8.c` (suggesting a return type change) will be **removed** in a follow-up patch, replacing it with documentation explaining (a) why the functions return `int` and (b) the necessity of `cast_size_t_to_int()` to prevent overflows. This aligns with Junio’s and Phillip’s recommendations.
- The patch’s regressions (mixed-sign comparisons, underflow risks, struct field mismatches) and incomplete conversions (e.g., overlooked callers in `builtin/worktree.c`) will **not be addressed**, as the effort is abandoned.
- The `cast_size_t_to_int()` helper, which included an overflow check, will **remain in place** despite its removal in v3; its rationale will be documented to clarify its role in preventing undefined behavior.

The thread’s resolution reflects a broader consensus that the patch’s theoretical benefits (type consistency) do not justify its practical costs (disruption, risk, and pervasive explicit casts). Hardik’s withdrawal is a pragmatic response to the weight of reviewer feedback, particularly Phillip Wood’s substantive critique and Junio’s alignment with it. The focus now shifts to documenting the existing design rather than refactoring it. No further iterations of this patch are expected.
