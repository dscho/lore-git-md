This is a follow-up in the ongoing refactoring thread changing the return type of UTF-8 string-width functions (`utf8_strwidth()` and `utf8_strnwidth()`) from `int` to `size_t`. The author, Hardik Kumar, acknowledges Junio C Hamano’s skepticism about the patch’s practical value but remains committed to the effort, arguing that using `size_t` (an unsigned type) is more appropriate for functions that inherently compute non-negative display widths. He plans to send an updated patch addressing the regressions identified in v2, particularly those related to unsigned integer underflow and mixed-sign comparisons, though he does not outline specific fixes in this email.

The thread remains in a state of uncertainty. Junio’s latest email strongly questions whether the patch is worth pursuing at all, given the high cost of careful review and the risk of regressions relative to the minimal practical benefit. Hardik’s response does not engage with Junio’s broader concerns about the patch’s viability, focusing instead on the technical merits of using `size_t`. The unresolved regressions—including underflow in `diff.c`, mixed-sign comparisons in `pretty.c` and `blame.c`, and incomplete conditional compilation updates in `gettext.h`—remain blocking issues. Additionally, the removal of the overflow check in the `cast_size_t_to_int()` helper (which previously protected against undefined behavior) has not been justified, and Pablo Sabater’s high-weight review from v2 remains unaddressed.

Key technical details to track in future discussion include:

- The `occupied` variable in `pretty.c` (now `size_t`, causing mixed-sign comparison issues with `int padding`).
- The `num` variable in `blame.c` (proposed to cast `utf8_strwidth()` results back to `int` for assignments).
- The `gettext.h` conditional compilation path (incomplete signature update when `NO_GETTEXT` is defined).
- The `stats_table` struct fields in `repo.c` (remaining `int`, requiring casts where `utf8_strwidth()` results are assigned).
- The removed overflow check in `cast_size_t_to_int()` (no rationale provided for its removal).

The patch is not ready for `next` and will require either a v3 with substantial fixes or withdrawal. The thread’s future hinges on whether Hardik can address the regressions without reintroducing implicit conversions or whether Junio’s skepticism prevails, leading to the effort’s abandonment.
