This is a **follow-up in an ongoing refactoring thread** where Hardik Kumar responds to Pablo Sabater’s substantive review of v2 of his patch changing UTF-8 string-width functions (`utf8_strwidth()` and `utf8_strnwidth()`) from `int` to `size_t`. The thread is now focused on **resolving type-safety regressions** introduced in v2, with Hardik proposing targeted fixes for specific issues raised by Pablo.

Hardik addresses three of Pablo’s review points with proposed solutions:

1. **`builtin/blame.c`**: Instead of splitting the `num` variable (now `size_t`) into separate variables for `strlen()`, `utf8_strwidth()`, and line counts, Hardik suggests casting the return value of `utf8_strwidth()` back to `int` when assigning to `num`. This avoids mixed-sign comparisons with the `longest_*` variables (still `int`) but retains the patch’s goal of using `size_t` for string-width calculations where possible.

2. **`builtin/repo.c`**: Hardik resists promoting the `stats_table` struct fields (`name_col_width`, `value_col_width`, `unit_col_width`) from `int` to `size_t`, arguing that the change would generate unnecessary signed/unsigned warnings and that the fields’ values will never realistically require the range of `size_t`. Instead, he proposes keeping the fields as `int` and casting the return values of `utf8_strwidth()` where needed. Pablo had noted that this approach would still involve narrowing conversions and mixed-sign comparisons, but Hardik’s response implies a preference for minimal changes over strict type consistency.

3. **`diff.c`**: Hardik proposes reverting `len` (used in padding calculations) back to `int` to resolve unsigned underflow risks in `len -= 3` and mixed-sign comparisons with `padding`. This would restore the original behavior but reintroduce implicit conversions from `size_t` to `int` in the `utf8_strwidth()` call. Pablo had suggested a safer alternative (checking for underflow before subtraction), but Hardik’s response prioritizes simplicity over robustness.

Hardik also acknowledges the **`gettext.h` conditional compilation issue** (the `gettext_width()` signature change was not mirrored in the `NO_GETTEXT` branch) and proposes casting the result back to `int` at the single call site, noting that both implementations (`utf8_strwidth()` and `strlen()`) return `size_t`.

The responses are **pragmatic but incomplete**: Hardik’s proposals address the immediate issues but do not fully resolve the underlying type-safety concerns. For example, casting `utf8_strwidth()` results back to `int` in `blame.c` and `gettext.c` reintroduces the implicit conversions the patch aimed to eliminate, while reverting `len` to `int` in `diff.c` sidesteps the unsigned underflow problem without fixing it. The thread remains **blocked on these regressions**, and a v3 will likely be needed to either adopt Pablo’s more thorough fixes or justify the trade-offs Hardik proposes.

Junio’s earlier skepticism about the patch’s overall value (citing minimal practical benefit and high regression risk) looms over the discussion, but this email does not engage with that broader question. The focus here is purely on addressing the technical regressions identified in v2.
