This is a follow-up from Junio C Hamano in the thread reviewing Hardik Kumar’s v2 patch that changes the return type of UTF-8 string-width functions (`utf8_strwidth()` and `utf8_strnwidth()`) from `int` to `size_t`. The patch is a type-system cleanup addressing a `//TODO` comment, but Junio now expresses **strong skepticism about the effort’s overall value** given the high cost of careful coding and review required to avoid regressions.

Junio acknowledges the patch’s surface-level appeal and the goal of clearing the existing TODO, but he highlights that the functions are only used for measuring display widths of strings that fit on a single line (e.g., for vertical alignment in `git diff --stat` or commit messages). He argues that the practical benefit of this change is minimal, as the functions do not need to support extremely wide displays (e.g., 2 billion columns), and the risk of regressions—such as unsigned integer underflow and mixed-sign comparison bugs—outweighs the theoretical type-safety improvement.

His key point is that the patch’s cost (in terms of review effort and introduced bugs) is disproportionate to its benefit. He suggests that the project might be better off **abandoning the effort entirely** and instead relying on the existing "safe cast" helper (`cast_size_t_to_int`), which includes an overflow check to prevent undefined behavior. This would avoid the need to update all callers and the associated risk of regressions.

The email does not propose a specific path forward but signals that the patch’s current trajectory is unlikely to succeed without a compelling justification for the effort. The thread remains open, with unresolved questions about the overflow check’s necessity and the patch’s overall merit. Hardik Kumar will need to either address Junio’s concerns in a v3 or reconsider whether the patch is worth pursuing.
