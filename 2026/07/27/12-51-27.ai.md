This is a **substantive review** of Hardik Kumar’s v3 patch changing the return type of UTF-8 string-width functions from `int` to `size_t`. Phillip Wood’s reply **strongly opposes the patch** on both technical and practical grounds, arguing that the change introduces unnecessary complexity and risk without meaningful benefit.

Phillip’s core objection is that the patch forces callers to explicitly cast the `size_t` return value back to `int` for use with `printf()` and other APIs that expect signed integers. He illustrates this with the `builtin/blame.c` example, where `utf8_strwidth()`’s result is immediately cast to `int` to compute padding for `printf()`. This pattern, he argues, is pervasive: nearly every caller of these functions uses the result in contexts requiring `int`, making the `size_t` return type impractical and error-prone. His position is that the patch **creates more problems than it solves**, turning what was previously an implicit conversion into an explicit one that callers must manage, increasing the risk of bugs.

Phillip also questions whether the patch is complete, noting that a grep for `utf8_strn?width` reveals callers (e.g., in `builtin/worktree.c`) that appear to be incorrectly converted or overlooked. This suggests the patch may not have been thoroughly audited, further undermining its viability.

His proposed alternative is to **abandon the patch entirely**: remove the TODO comment in `utf8.c` (which originally suggested changing the return type) and instead document why the functions return `int` and why `cast_size_t_to_int()` is necessary to prevent overflows. This approach would preserve the status quo while making the existing design’s rationale explicit, avoiding the disruption and risk introduced by the patch.

The review is **high-weight and likely blocking**. Phillip’s feedback aligns with Junio C Hamano’s prior skepticism about the patch’s value, and his technical critique (e.g., the impracticality of `size_t` for padding calculations) directly addresses the patch’s stated goal. Hardik’s v3 does not engage with these objections, instead prioritizing minimal disruption by reintroducing casts at call sites. Phillip’s reply makes it clear that this approach is insufficient and that the patch’s fundamental premise is flawed. The patch is **not ready for `next`** and may face withdrawal unless Hardik can justify the change’s benefits against the costs identified by reviewers.
