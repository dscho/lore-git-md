This thread reports and analyzes a performance regression in `git receive-pack` introduced by the ODB abstraction rework, specifically commit `a593373b09`. The regression, confirmed by Wolfgang Kritzinger (Atlassian) and quantified by Jeff King (Peff), causes a 12% slowdown on fast filesystems and significantly worse performance on NFS-backed deployments due to excessive failing `open()` calls for loose objects. The root cause, diagnosed by Taylor Blau, is a change in object lookup order: the ODB rework shifted from a "pack-first, loose-second" strategy *per backend* to a "pack-loose, pack-loose" strategy *across backends*, leading to redundant loose object checks even when objects exist in packs.

The thread has reached a critical juncture with Junio C Hamano’s recent input, which reframes the problem as an opportunity to optimize the ODB abstraction for specialized use cases like connectivity checks. Junio suggests two potential directions: distinguishing "fast" and "slow" lookups within each backend to preserve the abstraction while enabling cross-backend optimizations, or pre-loading loose object names into an in-core table (or Bloom filter) to short-circuit expensive `lstat()` calls during connectivity checks. These ideas shift the discussion from a narrow fix to a broader design question: how can the ODB abstraction accommodate both general-purpose lookups and the unique requirements of operations like `git receive-pack`? The thread now awaits Patrick Steinhardt’s response to evaluate these proposals and determine whether the abstraction can evolve to address the regression without compromising its design goals. Taylor Blau’s proposed two-phase lookup remains on the table, but Junio’s input suggests a preference for solutions that align with the abstraction’s principles.
