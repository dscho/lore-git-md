This is a **procedural clarification** from Junio C Hamano about the `Cc:` trailer in Git commit messages, responding to Weijie Yuan’s question in the thread for Jamie Magee’s test fix for Trace2 ancestry tracking.

Junio explains that while the `Cc:` trailer is not part of Git’s formal conventions (and in his view, belongs in email headers rather than commit messages), it is used by GitGitGadget to automatically copy recipients when sending patches. This aligns with the Linux kernel’s practice, which Weijie noted, but Junio makes clear that Git does not treat the `Cc:` trailer as a way to shift responsibility for a commit’s correctness to reviewers. The tone is lighthearted but firm, emphasizing that the trailer’s role is purely mechanical—facilitating email distribution—rather than a formal review or approval mechanism.

The patch itself remains unchanged and its status is unaffected by this exchange. It is still pending a substantive review from a Trace2 domain expert, as Junio previously requested, despite his initial approval in principle. The fix—skipping ancestry tests when process names are unreliable under emulation—continues to look uncontroversial and well-scoped, but this reply shifts the thread’s focus to a tangential documentation question rather than advancing the patch toward integration. For readers tracking the patch, the key takeaway is that the `Cc:` trailer is a convenience for email routing, not a project requirement or a substitute for thorough review.
