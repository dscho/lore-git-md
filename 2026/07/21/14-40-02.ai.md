This is a **maintainer's perspective on the ODB performance regression**, offering a high-level design discussion rather than a code-level fix. Junio C Hamano steps back from the immediate lookup-order debate and re-examines the broader context: the `git rev-list` connectivity check is a non-standard use of the revision traversal machinery, optimized for a single bit of information (whether the traversal succeeds) rather than the full object list. He suggests that this peculiar use case might justify application-specific hints or optimizations that would not make sense for general-purpose object lookups.

Junio proposes two potential directions. First, he endorses the idea of distinguishing "fast" and "slow" lookups within each ODB backend, preserving the abstraction while allowing cross-backend optimizations (e.g., checking all packs before any loose objects). This aligns with Taylor Blau’s proposed two-phase lookup but frames it as a backend capability rather than a violation of the abstraction. Second, he floats a more radical idea: pre-loading loose object names into an in-core table (or Bloom filter) to short-circuit expensive `lstat()` calls during connectivity checks. This would be a targeted optimization for the specific needs of `git receive-pack`, where false positives (claiming an object is missing when it isn’t) are tolerable as long as false negatives are avoided.

The email does not resolve the regression but reframes it as an opportunity to exploit the unique requirements of connectivity checks. Junio’s tone is exploratory, and he explicitly avoids prescribing a solution, instead inviting further discussion about whether such optimizations are worth the complexity. This shifts the thread’s focus from "how do we fix this regression?" to "how can we design the ODB abstraction to accommodate both general-purpose lookups and specialized use cases like connectivity checks?" Expect follow-up from Patrick Steinhardt (ODB rework author) and possibly Peff or Taylor Blau on the feasibility of these ideas.
