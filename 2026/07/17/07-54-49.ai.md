This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing performance patch thread targeting the `wt-status` subsystem. Jeff King (Peff) joins the conversation to weigh in on the theoretical versus real-world nature of the inefficiency addressed by Sahitya Chandra’s patch.

Peff confirms Patrick Steinhardt’s suspicion: the quadratic-time behavior is likely theoretical rather than a practical bottleneck. He explains that if `fill_directory()` already returns sorted entries, each `string_list_insert()` call would be O(1), making the current implementation effectively O(n) in the best case. However, he still endorses the patch’s approach, arguing that replacing the insertion loop with a single append-and-sort operation makes the code’s complexity more transparent and easier to reason about. He even speculates that the final sort could be O(n) if the implementation detects pre-sorted input, though this is not guaranteed.

The exchange leaves the patch’s motivation intact but shifts the framing: the change is now justified more by code clarity and maintainability than by measurable performance gains. No new technical objections or suggestions are raised, so the path to a v2 incorporating Patrick’s earlier feedback (`string_list_sort_u()` and benchmarks) remains straightforward. The discussion also implicitly reaffirms the patch’s conservative design choice—explicit deduplication rather than relying on `fill_directory()`’s output—without revisiting the trade-off.
