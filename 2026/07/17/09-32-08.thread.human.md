This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s nine-patch series refactoring Git’s loose object backend to make it fully self-contained as part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to move all loose-object writing logic from `object-file.c` into `odb/source-loose.c`, eliminating direct calls to `write_loose_object()` and consolidating loose backend internals under the generic `odb_source_write_object()` interface. The series also centralizes object hash computation, compatibility ID handling, object-existence checks, and mtime support in the generic ODB layer, and relocates `force_object_loose()` to its sole caller in `builtin/pack-objects.c`.

The series is now under substantive review after a build-breaking issue—caused by a missing evil merge resolution—was identified by SZEDER Gábor and fixed by Junio C Hamano. All nine patches have been posted, completing the refactoring. The final patch (9/9) moves the remaining loose-object writing helpers into `odb/source-loose.c`, leaving only `odb_source_loose_write_stream` in `object-file.c` with a TODO comment for future relocation. Toon Claes reviewed the final patch and confirmed its correctness, noting that the loose backend is now fully self-contained and praising the removal of `the_repository` in favor of the source’s repository handle. His only mild hesitation was about bundling the exposure of transaction-related helpers into the same commit, but this was a stylistic observation rather than a technical objection.

The series touches 12 files, primarily moving code from `object-file.c` (removing ~823 lines) to `odb/source-loose.c` (adding ~757 lines), with adjustments to ODB transaction interfaces and all four ODB source backends. Key changes include centralizing object hash computation, lifting object-existence checks into the generic ODB layer, and adding mtime support to the ODB write interface. No behavior changes or on-disk format changes are introduced, and the series fixes a memory leak in `force_object_loose()`. Open questions remain about the transaction interface changes, the impact on other ODB backends, and the performance implications of the new mtime parameter plumbing, but no major objections have surfaced so far. The series is built on top of `jt/receive-pack-use-odb-transaction` and `jk/git-hash-cleanups`, and is now awaiting broader review of the earlier patches.
