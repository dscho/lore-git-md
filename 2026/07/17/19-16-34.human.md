This is the final patch in Toon Claes’s four-part series to speed up the experimental `git last-modified` command by reusing Git’s Bloom filters. The series as a whole cuts runtime from 52 ms to 14 ms on the ziglang/zig repository, matching the speed of `git log`.

This patch specifically ensures the optimization works for wildcard pathspecs like `*.c`, which the Bloom-filter machinery cannot directly convert into a single key. The fix is simple: after `prepare_revision_walk()` clears the `bloom_filter_settings` field (because it cannot generate a Bloom key for wildcards), the patch restores it so per-path Bloom-filter lookups can continue. The change is confined to `builtin/last-modified.c` and adds just eight lines of straightforward plumbing.

Toon clarifies in the email that this approach differs slightly from Jeff King’s earlier suggestion—he restores the settings directly rather than re-running `prepare_to_use_bloom_filter()`, since that function is already called by `prepare_revision_walk()`. The patch is well-motivated and addresses a clear edge case without complicating the code.

The series looks like a clean, focused performance win with no obvious downsides. The benchmarks in the cover letter are compelling, and the implementation is incremental and testable. If the rest of the series holds up under review, this patch should be uncontroversial.
