This is a **substantive review** of the third patch in Toon Claes’s four-patch series optimizing the experimental `git last-modified` command. The series aims to reuse Git’s Bloom filters to speed up queries about the last commit modifying files matching a pathspec, including wildcards.

Taylor Blau identifies a **regression in `--show-trees` mode**: the new Bloom-filter pre-filter incorrectly skips commits that modify ancestor directories of the pathspec, causing `git last-modified -t` to miss changes. The test case he provides demonstrates the bug: without commit-graph, `d` is attributed to `touch-b` and `d/a` to `touch-a`, but with commit-graph (and Bloom filters), both are incorrectly attributed to `touch-a`. His proposed fix—guarding the pre-filter with `!lm->show_trees`—restores correctness while preserving the speedup for the common case.

The review is **tested**: Taylor includes a minimal reproduction case for `t8020` and verifies the issue with and without commit-graph. He also notes that the cover-letter benchmark (which uses `--show-trees`) should be rechecked, as the reported speedup may be misleading.

This patch is part of a well-motivated performance optimization, but the regression shows the Bloom-filter reuse needs careful handling of edge cases. The fix is straightforward, but the issue underscores that the series’ correctness depends on subtle interactions between pathspec expansion, Bloom filters, and tree-walking modes.
