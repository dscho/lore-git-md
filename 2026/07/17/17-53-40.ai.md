This is Junio C Hamano’s follow-up in the `git show-branch` commit-slab refactoring thread, escalating his earlier critique of the patch’s internal inconsistencies into a broader statement about the risks of over-reliance on AI-generated contributions. The email does not introduce new technical objections to the patch itself—those remain the two inconsistencies already identified in v7 (the commit message’s claim to have removed the `max_revs` limit while documentation still mentions a 64-branch cap, and the removal of the local `UNINTERESTING` definition while leaving a TODO comment about migrating to the shared definition). Instead, Junio frames these issues as symptoms of a deeper problem: the patch appears to have been generated and submitted without meaningful human oversight, undermining confidence in its coherence.

The tone is deliberately blunt, distinguishing between acceptable uses of AI tools (e.g., proofreading a human-written draft) and unacceptable ones (relaying machine-generated output without substantive review). Junio’s position is that the latter approach—where a human acts as a mere proxy for an AI—degrades the quality of contributions and imposes unnecessary review burden on the community. He emphasizes that contributors must demonstrate deeper understanding than the tools they use, and that the “machine generates, human reviews” model is inherently flawed when the human lacks the domain knowledge to critically assess the output. The email serves as both a technical reset (the patch cannot be merged until the inconsistencies are fixed) and a cultural signal to the broader community about the project’s expectations for contributions.
