This is a review comment on the second patch in Junio’s six-part documentation series, which adds guidance for contributors on what to do if their patch receives no response. Patrick Steinhardt suggests expanding the explanation of why patches might go unreviewed to include a third reason: reviewers may simply not be interested in the patch. He acknowledges this is a blunt point but argues it reflects reality and could help manage contributor expectations. He also notes that, ideally, reviewers would explicitly communicate disinterest rather than leaving contributors in limbo.

The comment is substantive but narrowly scoped, focusing on a single paragraph in the patch. It does not challenge the patch’s core advice (that pinging is acceptable) or its broader goals, but rather seeks to make the guidance more complete and honest. The tone is pragmatic and constructive, and the suggestion is unlikely to be controversial. Junio has not yet responded, so the patch remains unchanged on this point. The rest of the patch—including the recommendation to use `git-contacts` and the clarification that silence does not imply approval—is unaffected.
