This is a performance-minded refactoring patch in the `wt-status` subsystem, now at v2. The author, a first-time contributor, addresses a theoretical quadratic-time inefficiency in how untracked and ignored paths are collected. The patch replaces repeated `string_list_insert()` calls—which maintain sorted order but can trigger O(n) array shifts per insertion—with a single `string_list_append()` pass followed by one sort and deduplicate operation per list. This reduces the complexity to O(n log n) while preserving the existing behavior of producing sorted, duplicate-free lists.

The v2 revision incorporates feedback from Patrick Steinhardt and Jeff King, who both endorsed the approach but suggested minor improvements. The patch now uses `string_list_sort_u()` for simplicity, and the commit message has been reworded to avoid overstating the performance impact, acknowledging that the input from `fill_directory()` may already be sorted. The change is small (6 insertions, 2 deletions) and touches only `wt-status.c`, specifically the `wt_status_collect_untracked()` function.

The patch is well-motivated and clearly explained. The author considered but rejected a more aggressive optimization that would rely on `fill_directory()`'s pre-sorted output, opting instead for the safer approach that explicitly removes duplicates. This conservative design choice aligns with the feedback from reviewers, who agreed that benchmarks were unnecessary given the likely negligible performance impact. The patch appears ready for further review or integration, though the unresolved question about whether duplicate removal is strictly necessary could still surface in discussion.

Overall, this is a clean, focused contribution that improves code clarity and maintainability without changing behavior. It’s a good example of a first-time patch that addresses a real (if theoretical) inefficiency while engaging thoughtfully with reviewer feedback. Worth a quick look if you care about `wt-status` or string-list performance, but unlikely to be controversial.
