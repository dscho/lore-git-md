This thread introduces a four-part patch series by Toon Claes to speed up the experimental `git last-modified` command by reusing Git’s Bloom filters. The goal is to match the speed of `git log` when querying the last commit that modified files in a directory—currently, `git last-modified` is about 3.5 times slower (52 ms vs. 14 ms on the ziglang/zig repository).

The series is under initial review, with no prior versions or objections raised so far. The key changes involve refactoring Bloom filter logic in `revision.c` and `revision.h` to avoid redundant lookups, then wiring this into `git last-modified` to skip unnecessary tree diffs for commits where Bloom filters confirm no changes. The latest patch (4/4) ensures wildcard pathspecs (like `*.c`) also benefit from these optimizations by restoring a cleared field after `prepare_revision_walk()`.

The work is self-contained, touching only `revision.c`, `revision.h`, and `builtin/last-modified.c`, with no on-disk format changes. Benchmarks in the cover letter demonstrate the series achieves its performance goal, and the author has addressed the last technical gap (wildcard pathspecs) in this final patch. No reviewers have weighed in yet, so the series remains uncontested.
