This is a **bugfix follow-up** in the trace2 recursion crash thread. Derrick Stolee acknowledges Taylor Blau’s analysis that the root cause lies in Git for Windows’s `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection for `xsnprintf()`, which allocates memory for format strings with more than six placeholders (trace2 uses seven). The allocation failure triggers `xsnprintf()`’s `die()`, causing the observed recursion.

Derrick agrees that Taylor’s proposed alternative—disabling `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection for `xsnprintf()` in `wrapper.c`—is architecturally cleaner and plans to explore it. However, he also defends his original patch’s removal of `xsnprintf()` from trace2, arguing that trace2 should avoid `die()` entirely where possible. This suggests the thread may converge on a two-part fix: Taylor’s alternative to address the `xsnprintf()`/`libintl` boundary, and Derrick’s patch to harden trace2 against reentrancy.

Key technical details for future discussion:
- **Files**: `wrapper.c` (Taylor’s alternative), `trace2/tr2_tbuf.c` (Derrick’s patch).
- **Subsystems**: trace2, gettext/libintl, MinGW compatibility.
- **Behavior**: Taylor’s approach preserves `xsnprintf()` but avoids libintl’s allocation; Derrick’s removes `xsnprintf()` from trace2 entirely.
- **Open questions**: Why the libintl allocation fails (OOM? other constraints?) and whether both fixes are needed for robustness.
- **Platform focus**: Git for Windows, where `libintl_vsnprintf()`’s behavior differs from POSIX.

The tone is collaborative, with both contributors agreeing on the broader issue while leaving room for further testing. Future replies may clarify whether Taylor’s alternative alone suffices or if Derrick’s patch remains necessary as a belt-and-braces measure.
