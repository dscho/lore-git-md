This is a **design discussion follow-up** in the ongoing `no-ref-delta` capability thread, where Taylor Blau responds to Jeff King’s latest questions about the technical rationale behind the feature.

Taylor clarifies that the write-through proxy *does* index incoming packs during validation, but the durable index for the stored pack is built asynchronously after the push is accepted. The key distinction is that `OFS_DELTA` objects can be resolved in a single forward pass over the pack, with random access to already-processed bases, avoiding the need to hold the entire pack in memory. In contrast, `REF_DELTA` objects risk pipeline stalls if a long delta chain depends on a base that appears late in the pack, forcing sequential resolution and potentially requiring a pending work queue or OID-to-position mapping.

Jeff’s suggestion of a narrower “bases-before-deltas” capability—enforcing Git’s default ordering—would mitigate the worst-case stall but doesn’t fully address the proxy’s design goal of offset-based reconstruction metadata. Taylor acknowledges the trade-off: while the tighter capability would preserve broader protocol compatibility, it still requires OID lookups for `REF_DELTA` bases, complicating the proxy’s indexing logic. The discussion remains open on whether `no-ref-delta` is the optimal solution or if a middle ground could satisfy both performance and compatibility concerns. The thread’s next steps will likely hinge on whether the project prioritizes the proxy’s specific needs or seeks a more generalizable approach.
