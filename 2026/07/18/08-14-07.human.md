This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) on the fourth patch in Toon Claes’s four-part series optimizing `git last-modified` with Bloom filters. The patch itself is small and narrowly scoped—it restores Bloom filter settings after `prepare_revision_walk()` clears them for wildcard pathspecs—but Peff’s feedback raises a deeper design question about whether `last-modified` should be reusing `rev_info.bloom_filter_settings` at all.

Peff argues that `bloom_filter_settings` is an internal implementation detail of `revision.c` and suggests a cleaner alternative: `last-modified` should maintain its own copy of the settings pointer. His proposed patch moves the settings into the `last_modified` struct, avoiding any potential conflict with `revision.c`’s use of the same field. While both approaches would work (since the pointer ultimately comes from the repository object), Peff’s version makes it clearer that `last-modified` is responsible for its own Bloom filter state, reducing the risk of subtle bugs if `revision.c` ever changes how it uses the field.

The review is substantive—it engages with the code’s design rather than just its surface—and includes a concrete alternative implementation. Peff also notes a minor improvement opportunity (moving the settings lookup into the repository API), though he acknowledges it’s not urgent. The tone is constructive, framing the feedback as a matter of long-term maintainability rather than a correctness issue. This is the kind of review that could shape the final version of the patch, even if the immediate fix is functionally sound.
