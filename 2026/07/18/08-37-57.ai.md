This is a **substantive review** of patch 3/4 in Toon Claes’s series optimizing `git last-modified` with Bloom filters. Jeff King (Peff) engages with Taylor Blau’s earlier correctness concern about `--show-trees` and proposes a design alternative.

The core issue remains: when `--show-trees` is used, `populate_paths_from_revs()` adds ancestor tree entries (e.g., `d/`) to `lm->paths`, but the Bloom filter only covers the original pathspec (`d/a`). Peff confirms Taylor’s observation that this makes the Bloom-filter pre-filter unsafe, as it may incorrectly prune commits that modified `d/` but not `d/a`. He also notes a documentation inconsistency: the man page claims `-t` has no effect without `-r`, but the test case shows it does.

Peff’s key suggestion is to **remove ancestor tree entries from the output** when they are not covered by the original pathspec, rather than disabling the Bloom-filter optimization entirely. This would preserve the performance benefit while avoiding misleading results. He argues this is an acceptable trade-off for an experimental command, as it simplifies the output and aligns with the likely intent of most callers (who care about files, not trees). The proposal involves double-checking pathspecs in `add_path_from_diff()` to filter out such entries.

The review also touches on the broader design question of whether `--show-trees` should behave this way at all, given that `git log` does not include ancestor trees in its output for similar queries. Peff’s feedback is technical and forward-looking, focusing on how to reconcile correctness with the optimization’s goals. No code is provided, but the discussion implies a potential reroll of patch 3/4 to address the issue.
