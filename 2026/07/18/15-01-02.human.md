This is a bugfix patch targeting the trace2 subsystem, part of an ongoing discussion about how best to prevent a "recursion detected in die handler" crash. The crash occurs when timestamp formatting in trace2 telemetry fails, triggering `die()` during `atexit` events and causing infinite recursion.

Derrick Stolee’s original patch replaces `xsnprintf()` with `snprintf()` in `trace2/tr2_tbuf.c` and adds defensive initialization to avoid crashes, falling back to a hardcoded timestamp on failure. Taylor Blau’s review proposed an alternative root cause: Git for Windows’s redirection of `vsnprintf()` to `libintl_vsnprintf()`, which allocates memory for format strings with more than six placeholders (trace2 uses seven), potentially causing `xsnprintf()` to `die()` and trigger recursion. This email is Derrick’s reply, where he acknowledges Taylor’s analysis as plausible and agrees the `xsnprintf()`/`libintl` boundary should be addressed separately.

The discussion now splits into two complementary goals: fixing the immediate crash (Derrick’s patch) and addressing the broader architectural issue (Taylor’s proposed change to `wrapper.c`). Derrick emphasizes that trace2 should avoid `die()` entirely where possible, reinforcing his patch’s defensive approach. The transient nature of the issue remains unexplained—why it’s surfacing now despite no recent changes—but the focus is on robustness rather than root-cause diagnosis.

This patch looks well-crafted and addresses a real problem. The lack of test coverage is understandable given the difficulty of reproducing system call failures, and the discussion suggests both short-term and long-term fixes are in play. For readers tracking Git’s telemetry subsystem or Windows compatibility, this thread is worth following to see how the two approaches converge.
