This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s 13-patch refactoring series to separate repository discovery from setup. Justin Tobler raises a technical question about the patch’s behavior change: whether deferring the "bogus worktree" check from `setup_explicit_git_dir()` to `apply_repository_format()` could silently ignore misconfigurations when the `GIT_WORK_TREE` environment variable is set.

The review focuses on the edge case where `core.worktree` and `core.bare` are both set but overridden by `GIT_WORK_TREE`. The patch explicitly unsets `core.worktree` in this scenario to avoid warnings, which Justin flags as potentially masking misconfigurations. He asks whether this deferral could have unintended consequences, such as delaying the warning until after other setup steps have already run. The question is well-targeted, as it probes the architectural trade-off between consolidating logic in `apply_repository_format()` and preserving early validation.

Justin’s feedback is constructive and grounded in the code’s behavior (e.g., referencing test cases like `#22.2` and `#30`). While he doesn’t oppose the change outright, his concern about silent overrides could spark discussion about whether the environment variable should suppress warnings entirely. The patch itself is otherwise uncontroversial, but this review highlights a potential point of contention for the series’ broader goal of decoupling discovery and setup.
