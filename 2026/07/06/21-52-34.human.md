This is a performance optimization patch for Git's priority queue (`prio-queue`) implementation, specifically the cascade-down optimization for `extract-min` operations. The patch is now a follow-up to the recently merged `lazy-fold` series (`kk/prio-queue-get-put-fusion`), which fused `get` and `put` operations to reduce overhead.

Kristofer Karlsson reports that after rebasing the cascade-down patch on top of the `lazy-fold` series, the previously identified regression in `prio_queue_replace()` is resolved. The remaining optimization applies only to `flush_get()`, where unfused `get` operations now use cascade-down instead of sift-down. Benchmarking on the Linux kernel repository and a large merge-heavy repository shows a modest but consistent 1-2% end-to-end improvement in real-world commands. While a microbenchmark would likely show a larger gain, the priority queue is only a small part of Git's overall workload.

The author is uncertain whether the 1-2% improvement justifies the added complexity of introducing another sift function (`sift_up_rebalance()`). He defers to René Scharfe and Junio C Hamano on whether to proceed with the patch or drop it. If the decision is to move forward, Kristofer will submit a reroll based on the current `next` branch.

This patch is part of an ongoing effort to optimize Git's priority queue, which is used in operations like merge base finding and revision walking. The discussion has already reached consensus on the technical approach (a hybrid of sift-down and cascade-down), and the maintainer has signaled approval of the direction. The remaining question is purely about the cost-benefit tradeoff of merging this incremental improvement.
