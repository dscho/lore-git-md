This is a **performance optimization discussion** for Git’s reftable backend, specifically about Kristofer Karlsson’s proposed fix for quadratic-time behavior during bulk ref deletion and re-creation. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Kristofer’s hesitation about submitting the patch, suggesting a clearer benchmark scenario to demonstrate its value.

The patch in question (draft at [GitGitGadget PR #2166](https://github.com/gitgitgadget/git/pull/2166)) addresses a known bottleneck: tombstone iteration inefficiency in `refs_verify_refname_available()` and `reftable_be_transaction_prepare()`. While earlier benchmarks showed dramatic improvements (e.g., 8,000 refs: **14.93s → 0.145s**), Kristofer struggled to reproduce these gains in realistic scenarios, particularly when compaction was enabled. His concern is that the patch’s benefits may be limited to edge cases, making its code complexity (tombstone-skipping loops at call sites) unjustified.

Patrick’s reply reframes the discussion by proposing a **more targeted benchmark**: creating and then deleting *X* references without compaction. This scenario directly exercises the tombstone-heavy code paths the patch optimizes, avoiding the noise of compaction. He implies that if the patch delivers measurable gains here, it would be worth submitting despite its narrow scope. The underlying question remains whether the reftable backend’s design—prioritizing compaction avoidance—should tolerate quadratic-time behavior in edge cases, or if the patch’s safeguards are worth the added complexity.

For a busy reader, this email signals that the optimization effort is **not yet abandoned** but hinges on clearer benchmarking. If Patrick’s suggested test confirms the patch’s value, expect a formal submission; otherwise, the fix may be deferred or dropped. The thread’s outcome will likely influence whether similar low-level optimizations are pursued in the future.
