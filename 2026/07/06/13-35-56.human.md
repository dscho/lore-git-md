This is the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s two-part bugfix series for the reftable backend, now authored by Kristofer Karlsson. It addresses the quadratic performance regression when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned), which the thread summary already outlined. The patch removes the `suppress_deletions` flag from the merged iterator and pushes tombstone handling to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are available. This allows early termination of iteration when tombstones exceed the relevant bound, reducing runtime from ~14s to ~0.2s in the perf test’s 8000-ref scenarios.

The change touches four files: `refs/reftable-backend.c` (core logic), `reftable/merged.{c,h}` (removing `suppress_deletions`), and `reftable/stack.c` (minor cleanup). The diff adds deletion checks to log iteration paths and updates ref-reading logic to skip tombstones after bounds checks. The patch is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend and avoids broader refactoring, focusing on the specific performance pathology. The motivation is clear, the test coverage (added in patch 1/2) is thorough, and the performance improvement is dramatic.

This patch is a direct implementation of the fix described in the series’ cover letter and thread summary. The removal of `suppress_deletions` could theoretically affect other callers, but none are mentioned in the commit message, and the change appears well-justified by the performance data. The patch looks ready for review, with no obvious loose ends or controversial design choices. If you care about reftable performance or are tracking the backend’s stability, this is worth a closer look.
