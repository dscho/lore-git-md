Thread identity: reftable backend performance regression fix (2-patch series, v1) by Patrick Steinhardt. Thread type: bugfix patch series.

Core problem or goal: fix a quadratic performance regression in the reftable backend when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned). The regression occurs in `update-ref` operations where the merged iterator’s `suppress_deletions` flag forces a full scan of all tombstone records before higher-level bounds checks can terminate iteration, making `refs_verify_refnames_available()` and `reftable_backend_read_ref()` O(n) per call in tombstone-heavy scenarios.

Current status: v1 posted; under review. The series moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are available, enabling early termination when tombstones exceed the relevant bound. Performance improves from ~14s to ~0.2s in the perf test’s 8000-ref scenarios. The fix is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, avoiding broader refactoring.

Key technical details: files touched: `refs/reftable-backend.c` (core logic), `reftable/merged.{c,h}` (removing `suppress_deletions`), `reftable/stack.c` (minor cleanup). New symbols: none introduced; behavior change: tombstone suppression logic removed from merged iterator and handled explicitly at call sites after bounds checks. Test coverage: new perf test `p1401-ref-store-tombstones.sh` and correctness test `t0610-reftable-basics.sh` added, covering tombstone behavior and edge cases for deleted-then-recreated refs. The performance test exercises two scenarios: recreating the same refs after deletion (quadratic behavior) and creating new, differently-named refs after deletion (worst-case tombstone sorting). The correctness test verifies ref visibility after deletion and recreation. On-disk format: unchanged. Open questions and loose ends: none identified. The suppression logic’s removal is now explicitly handled at all call sites, and no other callers are known to rely on the merged iterator’s suppression behavior.
