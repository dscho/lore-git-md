This is a **performance optimization patch** for Git’s reftable backend, specifically targeting a long-standing quadratic-time scalability issue in bulk ref deletion and re-creation workflows. Kristofer Karlsson confirms that his proposed fix—exposing tombstones to iterator bounds checks—delivers **dramatic speedups** in realistic scenarios, not just artificial benchmarks. For example, deleting 8,000 "old-*" refs and creating 8,000 "new-*" refs (a lexicographical worst-case) improves from **27 seconds to 0.09 seconds** (a ~300x speedup). Even with compaction enabled, the "recreate-same-refs" case shows a **100x improvement** for 8,000 refs.

The patch addresses a deliberate trade-off in reftable’s design: tombstones (markers for deleted refs) avoid compaction overhead but force subsequent operations to scan all tombstones to confirm refname availability, creating O(n²) behavior during bulk ref re-creation. Kristofer’s fix allows iterators to terminate early when a tombstone’s name sorts after the target ref, eliminating the bottleneck. While the patch adds complexity (tombstone-skipping loops at call sites), the performance gains in edge cases—now validated in realistic workflows—justify the change. Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier suggestion to test bulk ref deletion without compaction proved pivotal, shifting the discussion from theoretical benchmarks to measurable improvements.
