This is a two-patch bugfix series targeting the reftable backend, addressing a quadratic performance regression when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned). The issue manifests in `update-ref` operations where the merged iterator’s `suppress_deletions` flag forces a full scan of all tombstone records before higher-level bounds checks can terminate iteration, making `refs_verify_refnames_available()` and `reftable_backend_read_ref()` O(n) per call in tombstone-heavy scenarios.

The fix moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are available, allowing early termination when tombstones exceed the relevant bound. The series includes both a test patch (adding perf test `p1401` and correctness test `t0610`) and the core optimization, which reduces runtime from ~14s to ~0.2s in the perf test’s 8000-ref scenarios. While auto-compaction typically mitigates tombstone accumulation, the fix guarantees correct time complexity regardless of compaction state.

Key technical details: the change touches `refs/reftable-backend.c` (core logic), `reftable/merged.{c,h}` (removing `suppress_deletions`), and `reftable/stack.c` (minor cleanup). The test files introduce new scenarios for tombstone behavior, including edge cases for deleted-then-recreated refs.
