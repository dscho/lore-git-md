This is a **substantive review** from Phillip Wood on Trevor Gross’s `git rebase -i -x` patch series, engaging directly with the design objections raised by Jeff King (Peff) and Junio C Hamano.

Phillip endorses Peff’s critique of the feature’s conceptual misalignment: `git rebase` and `git cherry-pick` serve fundamentally different workflows (moving vs. duplicating history), and the `-x` flag’s purpose—recording commit origin—loses meaning when the original commits are discarded. He explicitly agrees that extending `git cherry-pick` with an interactive mode (`-i`) would be a cleaner solution, asking Trevor what specific gaps in `cherry-pick`’s functionality drive the use of `rebase` instead. This frames the discussion as a *requirements* problem rather than an implementation one, pressing the author to justify the rebase-centric approach.

On the technical side, Phillip confirms that generalizing the rebase todo-list editor for `cherry-pick -i` would require minimal effort, though he raises edge-case concerns about supporting commands like `edit -x` or `pick -x` followed by `fixup`. These scenarios complicate the semantics of the "cherry picked from" trailer, as the commit’s content may diverge from the original. He also notes a latent inconsistency in the current patch: the per-commit `-x` flag (introduced by Trevor) and the global `record-origin` option (used by `cherry-pick -x`) could be harmonized by having `cherry-pick -x` automatically add `-x` to each todo line, avoiding redundant checks in the sequencer.

Phillip’s review highlights two implementation quirks in Trevor’s patch: (1) the fast-forward behavior (where `-x` is skipped) stems from unchanged conditions in `do_pick_commit()` and `skip_unnecessary_picks()`, and (2) the patch’s per-commit flag approach would require awkward conditional logic (`if (opt->record_origin || (item->flags & TODO_RECORD_ORIGIN))`) to check for trailers. These observations reinforce the argument for a `cherry-pick -i` solution, which would sidestep such inconsistencies.

The review also includes a tangential but practical suggestion: adding a `--exec` option to `git cherry-pick` (analogous to `git rebase --exec`) to run commands like `make test` after each pick. This hints at broader usability gaps in `cherry-pick` that could be addressed alongside an interactive mode.

Phillip’s feedback is high-weight and constructive, aligning with Peff’s objections while offering concrete technical paths forward.
