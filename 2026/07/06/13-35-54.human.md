This is a performance bugfix targeting the reftable backend, part of a two-patch series. The issue is quadratic behavior in `update-ref` when many refs are deleted (leaving tombstones) and then recreated. The root cause is the merged iterator’s `suppress_deletions` flag, which forces a full scan of tombstones before higher-level code can apply bounds checks. The fix moves tombstone handling to the call sites, allowing early termination and restoring linear time complexity.

The series includes both tests and the fix. Patch 1 adds a perf test (`p1401`) with two scenarios (8,000 refs) and a correctness test (`t0610`) to verify deleted-then-recreated refs remain visible. Patch 2 is the optimization itself, reducing runtime from ~14s to ~0.2s in the perf tests. The commit message notes that auto-compaction usually prevents tombstone buildup, but the fix ensures correct behavior regardless of compaction state.

The change is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, touching `refs/reftable-backend.c`, `reftable/merged.c`, and related files. Previous discussion (linked in the email) suggests the issue was identified by Jeff King, lending credibility to the problem’s significance. The series looks well-motivated and self-contained, with clear before/after metrics and test coverage. Worth attention if you care about reftable performance or large-scale ref operations.
