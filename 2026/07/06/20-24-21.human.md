This is a brief but significant reply from Junio C Hamano in the ongoing discussion about Trevor Gross’s `git rebase -i -x` patch series. Junio wholeheartedly endorses Phillip Wood’s critique of the feature’s conceptual misalignment: `git rebase` and `git cherry-pick` serve fundamentally different purposes—rebase moves history, while cherry-pick duplicates it—and the `-x` flag’s "cherry picked from" annotation loses its meaning when the original commits are discarded. His agreement with Phillip (and by extension, Jeff King) reinforces the high-weight substantive objections already raised, making it clear that the patch’s current design is unlikely to gain traction without a major rethink.

Junio also expresses relief that he isn’t alone in his concerns, noting that `rebase` and `cherry-pick` should behave differently (e.g., in how they handle notes). His tone suggests the objections are not merely technical quibbles but reflect a deeper philosophical divide about Git’s workflow semantics. The thread’s future now hinges on whether Trevor can justify the rebase-centric approach or pivot to the proposed `cherry-pick -i` alternative.

On a tangential note, Junio agrees with Phillip’s suggestion to add a `--exec` option to `git cherry-pick`, calling it "quite handy." This hints at broader usability improvements that could accompany an interactive cherry-pick mode, though it’s not the focus of the current discussion. The key takeaway: the design objections are now firmly settled among the project’s most influential voices, and the ball is in Trevor’s court to address them.
