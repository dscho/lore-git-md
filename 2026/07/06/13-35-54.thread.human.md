This thread is about a two-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt and Kristofer Karlsson that fixes a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend. The bug surfaces when re-creating refs that were previously deleted, forcing unnecessary scans of tombstone records. The fix moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the backend’s call sites, where bounds checks can terminate early, cutting runtime from ~14s to ~0.2s in the perf test. The series is now at v1, with a compatibility-preserving revision proposed to avoid breaking libgit2.

The only remaining loose end is test placement: Patrick suggests moving the correctness test from `t0610-reftable-basics.sh` to `t1400-update-ref.sh`, arguing that the delete-then-recreate flow is already implicitly covered there and isn’t backend-specific. Kristofer had defended the test’s necessity for tombstone edge cases, but Patrick’s feedback is purely organizational—no objections to the test’s substance. The next revision will likely relocate it. The perf test will also be split into separate benchmarks for clearer measurement. No other open questions remain, and the series is under review with no substantive objections.
