This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series (v2) by Kristofer Karlsson that addresses a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend. The issue occurs when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned), causing operations like `refs_verify_refnames_available()` and `reftable_backend_read_ref()` to scan all tombstone records before bounds checks can terminate iteration. The fix moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are available, enabling early termination and restoring linear performance. In testing, this reduces runtime from ~14 seconds to ~0.2 seconds in scenarios with 8,000 refs.

The series has seen substantive review from Patrick Steinhardt, who confirmed the logic and advocated for clearer test isolation, and real-world validation from brian m. carlson, who observed the regression in production-scale repositories. The v2 revision addressed compatibility concerns by preserving the `suppress_deletions` flag in the reftable library API for downstream users like libgit2 while removing its use from Git’s internal code. It also improved the perf test (`p1401-ref-store-tombstones.sh`) by using fresh repositories for each scenario to prevent tombstone carryover and dropped a redundant correctness test (`t0610-reftable-basics.sh`) after consensus that its coverage was already implicit in `t1400-update-ref.sh`.

The fix is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, with no changes to the on-disk format or other ref backends. The patches touch `refs/reftable-backend.c` and `reftable/stack.c`, adding explicit deletion checks to ref and log iteration paths. No open questions remain, and the series appears ready for integration, likely to land in the near future.
