This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series (now at v3) that addresses a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend. The issue occurs when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned), causing operations like `refs_verify_refnames_available()` and `reftable_backend_read_ref()` to scan all tombstone records before bounds checks can terminate, leading to O(n) per-call behavior in tombstone-heavy scenarios. The fix moves the `suppress_deletions` flag from the merged iterator to `struct reftable_stack_options`, allowing callers to control tombstone suppression at stack creation time while preserving libgit2’s current behavior.

Kristofer Karlsson, the author, finalized the series in v3 after incorporating feedback from Patrick Steinhardt, who proposed the API-compatibility refinement to ensure libgit2 can continue working without relying on internal implementation details. The fix explicitly handles tombstones at call sites after bounds checks, enabling early termination and improving performance from ~14s to ~0.2s in the perf test’s 8000-ref scenarios. brian m. carlson endorsed the fix, noting the regression is observed in real-world benchmarks, not just theoretical scenarios.

The series is now ready for review and integration, with no open questions remaining. Downstream users like libgit2 will eventually need to update to use the new `reftable_stack_options` field, but the break is already inevitable due to libgit2’s use of pre-options-stack code. The patch touches `refs/reftable-backend.c`, `reftable/reftable-stack.h`, and `reftable/stack.c`, with no changes to the on-disk format. A new perf test (`p1401-ref-store-tombstones.sh`) measures the regression and fix.
