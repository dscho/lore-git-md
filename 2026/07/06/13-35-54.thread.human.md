This thread is about a two-patch series fixing a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend. The issue surfaces when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned): the merged iterator’s `suppress_deletions` flag forces a full scan of all tombstone records before higher-level bounds checks can terminate, making operations like `refs_verify_refnames_available()` and `reftable_backend_read_ref()` O(n) per call in tombstone-heavy scenarios.

The first patch, just posted, adds test coverage—both a performance test (`p1401`) and a correctness test (`t0610`)—to demonstrate the regression and verify the fix. The performance test uses 8,000 refs to show the problem clearly, including a worst-case scenario where tombstones and new refs sort in opposite order. The second patch, not yet reviewed, moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are available, enabling early termination. This narrow fix improves performance from ~14s to ~0.2s in the test scenarios.

The series is under review, with no substantive feedback yet. The tests are uncontroversial and directly target the regression, providing a solid baseline for evaluating the performance fix. The fix itself is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, avoiding broader refactoring. No open questions or objections have surfaced so far.
