This thread is about a two-patch series from Kristofer Karlsson that fixes a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend. The issue occurs when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned), causing operations like `refs_verify_refnames_available()` to scan all tombstone records before terminating, leading to O(n) performance per call in tombstone-heavy scenarios. The fix moves the `suppress_deletions` flag from the merged iterator to `struct reftable_stack_options`, allowing downstream users like libgit2 to control tombstone suppression at stack creation time while preserving their current behavior. Performance improves dramatically—from ~14 seconds to ~0.2 seconds in the perf test’s 8000-ref scenarios.

The series has now reached its final form (v3) after incorporating all feedback. Patrick Steinhardt, who had previously suggested API-compatibility refinements and test improvements, has reviewed and acked the latest version, noting only minor cosmetic issues (a typo in the commit message and a missing comment) that don’t warrant a reroll. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, has confirmed the design preserves libgit2’s current usage while future-proofing the API, though libgit2 will eventually need updates due to its reliance on pre-options-stack code. Brian m. carlson also endorsed the fix, citing real-world performance regressions observed in production-scale repositories.

With all substantive feedback addressed and no remaining open questions, the series is ready for Junio to merge. The changes are narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, with no on-disk format modifications, and include a perf test (`t/perf/p1401-ref-store-tombstones.sh`) to measure the regression and fix.
