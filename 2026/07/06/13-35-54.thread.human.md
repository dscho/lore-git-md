This thread is a two-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that fixes a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend. The issue surfaces when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned): the merged iterator’s `suppress_deletions` flag forces a full scan of all tombstone records before higher-level bounds checks can terminate, making operations like `update-ref` painfully slow in tombstone-heavy scenarios. The fix moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are known, allowing early termination and restoring O(1) performance. In the perf test’s 8,000-ref scenario, runtime drops from ~14 seconds to ~0.2 seconds.

The series is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, touching only `refs/reftable-backend.c`, `reftable/merged.{c,h}`, and `reftable/stack.c`. No new symbols or on-disk format changes are introduced. Test coverage includes a new perf test (`p1401-ref-store-tombstones.sh`) and correctness test (`t0610-reftable-basics.sh`), which verify tombstone behavior and edge cases for deleted-then-recreated refs. The first patch in the series already added these tests, and this second patch completes the fix by removing the problematic `suppress_deletions` flag and updating call sites to handle tombstones explicitly.

The series is under review, with no open questions or objections raised so far. Jeff King (Peff) is referenced in the commit context, but no explicit review or ack has been recorded yet. The fix is motivated by prior discussion and a linked lore thread, and it avoids broader refactoring to keep the change focused and low-risk.
