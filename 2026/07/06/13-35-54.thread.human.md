This thread is about a two-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that fixes a quadratic performance regression in Git’s reftable backend when recreating refs that were previously deleted (tombstoned). The issue surfaced in `update-ref` operations where the merged iterator’s `suppress_deletions` flag forced a full scan of tombstone records, making operations like `refs_verify_refnames_available()` and `reftable_backend_read_ref()` unnecessarily slow in tombstone-heavy scenarios.

The fix moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are available, allowing early termination and improving performance from ~14 seconds to ~0.2 seconds in the perf test’s 8,000-ref scenarios. The series is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, and a compatibility-preserving revision ensures libgit2 can continue using the `suppress_deletions` flag while Git’s internal callers bypass it.

The v1 patches are under review, with all technical objections resolved. The only remaining discussion is about test placement: Kristofer Karlsson (the patch author) and Patrick Steinhardt agreed to drop the newly added correctness test (`t0610-reftable-basics.sh`) rather than relocate it to `t1400-update-ref.sh`, since the delete-then-recreate flow is already implicitly covered by existing tests. The perf test (`p1401-ref-store-tombstones.sh`) will be revised to split benchmarks and use fresh repositories for each scenario to ensure accurate measurements. The series is now ready for a v2 iteration, pending final adjustments to the perf test.
