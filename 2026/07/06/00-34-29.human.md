This is a **follow-up discussion in the memory-leak cleanup thread**, specifically about CI infrastructure and compiler testing. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Junio C Hamano’s endorsement of a minimalist CI approach by clarifying his rationale for the existing `clang/gcc` job matrix.

Peff acknowledges that the primary goal isn’t to find bugs in the compilers themselves but to catch undefined behavior (UB) that might manifest differently under different compilers or optimization levels. He cites a past example (from 2025) where UB caused inconsistent test results between compilers, though he notes the details are vague. His position is that while such cases are rare, tools like UBSan (Undefined Behavior Sanitizer) are more effective for detecting UB than relying on compiler differences. The tone is pragmatic—he doesn’t push to retain the `clang/gcc` matrix but explains why it was originally considered.

This exchange doesn’t alter the status of the original patch series (which fixes leaks in `git format-patch` and the test harness) or the agreed-upon CI changes (consolidating jobs into `linux-TEST-vars-leaks`). It’s a minor clarification of historical context, unlikely to change the outcome. The thread remains converged on the CI streamlining plan.
