This is the latest review in the `git history squash` feature thread, now in its seventh iteration. Phillip Wood raises several substantive concerns about the `--reedit-message` flag and input validation, building on prior discussion.

The key points in this review are about usability and consistency. Phillip questions why `--reedit-message` isn’t the default behavior, noting that users might expect to review the squashed commit message (similar to `git rebase -i`). He also critiques the current template format, which mimics `git rebase -i`’s squash template but may be overly verbose for this use case—especially when dealing with `fixup!` or `amend!` commits. His preference is for a more compact template that omits redundant messages, rather than the current approach of including all folded messages (with some commented out).

On input validation, Phillip flags two issues: (1) the lack of explicit checks for `rev-list` options like `--reverse`, which could lead to unexpected range resolution, and (2) the handling of `fixup!`/`squash!` commits. He argues that squashing a range containing `fixup!` commits should either include their targets or reject the operation outright, rather than silently folding them. He also reiterates that the current implementation doesn’t reorder commits to group `fixup!` commits with their targets, which could make the template harder to parse.

The review is substantive, focusing on edge cases and user experience rather than surface-level nitpicks. Phillip’s feedback is actionable—he links to prior discussion threads and suggests specific improvements—but it also highlights lingering design questions that may require broader consensus. The series is otherwise complete and well-tested, with all prior feedback addressed in v7.
