This is a performance optimization patch discussion targeting Git's priority queue implementation. Kristofer Karlsson updates the thread with new benchmark results after rebasing his cascade-down optimization on top of the recently merged `kk/prio-queue-get-put-fusion` series (which fuses `prio_queue_get()` and `prio_queue_put()` operations).

The key development is that the earlier performance regression René Scharfe identified in `prio_queue_replace()` is now resolved by the lazy-fold series, leaving only a small 1-2% end-to-end improvement from the cascade optimization in the remaining unfused `prio_queue_get()` calls. Kristofer notes that while cascade-down is still theoretically better for these cases, the practical benefit is diminished because the lazy-fold optimization has reduced the number of unfused operations. He expresses uncertainty about whether the small performance gain justifies adding another sift function (`sift_up_rebalance()`) to the codebase and defers to René and Junio for a final decision.

The patch itself is now minimal: it replaces `sift_down_root()` with `sift_up_rebalance()` in `flush_get()` and adds the ~20-line implementation of the new function. The discussion reflects a mature optimization effort where the marginal gains are being weighed against code complexity. The maintainers' input will likely determine whether this patch proceeds or is dropped in favor of the already-merged lazy-fold work.
