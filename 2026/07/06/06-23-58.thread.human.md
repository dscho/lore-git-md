This thread is about a performance fix for a slow test in Git’s test suite. The test, `t4141-apply-too-large.sh`, verifies that `git apply` correctly rejects patches exceeding Git’s size limits, but it was artificially slow—taking up to 40 minutes in CI—because it generated a 1 GB patch by copying one byte at a time. Patrick Steinhardt’s patch (part 3 of a 9-patch series) replaces this inefficient method with a direct call to `test-tool genzeros`, which can generate the required data in one step, reducing the test’s runtime to under a second. The fix is purely mechanical, touching only the test script, and preserves the test’s behavior and its `EXPENSIVE` prerequisite since `git apply` still consumes 1 GB of memory.

The change is uncontroversial and focused on performance, with no objections or alternative approaches raised so far. The patch is part of a larger series, but this specific change stands alone and doesn’t depend on the rest of the series to be useful. If the series progresses, this patch should be a straightforward candidate for merging.
