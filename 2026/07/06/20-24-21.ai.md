This is a brief reply from Junio C Hamano in the ongoing `git rebase -i -x` thread, weighing in on the design debate. Junio explicitly endorses Phillip Wood’s and Jeff King’s (Peff) substantive objection to the feature’s conceptual alignment: `git rebase` and `git cherry-pick` serve fundamentally different workflows—rebase moves history (discarding old commits), while cherry-pick duplicates it. The "cherry picked from commit ..." annotation, he argues, loses its meaning in a rebase workflow where the original commits are no longer available. This reinforces the core design tension that has stalled the patch series, making it clear that the objection is not merely technical but philosophical.

Junio also briefly acknowledges Phillip’s tangential suggestion of adding a `--exec` option to `git cherry-pick` (analogous to `git rebase --exec`), calling it "quite handy." This hints at broader usability gaps in `cherry-pick` that could be addressed if the effort pivots to Peff’s proposed `cherry-pick -i` mode. The reply is short but high-weight, as it signals Junio’s agreement with the two most vocal critics of the current approach. The thread’s trajectory now hinges on whether Trevor Gross can justify the rebase-centric design or will shift focus to extending `cherry-pick` instead. Key context: the sequencer backend already supports interactive cherry-picking (per Peff’s earlier review), so the technical lift for `cherry-pick -i` may be minimal.
