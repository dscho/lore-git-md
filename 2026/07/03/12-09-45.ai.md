This is a **performance optimization follow-up for the reftable backend**, specifically Kristofer Karlsson’s proposed fix for the quadratic-time behavior in bulk ref deletion and re-creation. The patch, which exposes tombstones to iterator bounds checks to enable early termination, was previously shown to reduce runtime from **O(n²) to O(n)** in artificial benchmarks (e.g., 8,000 refs: **14.93s → 0.145s**). However, Kristofer now reports difficulty reproducing the performance improvement in realistic scenarios, particularly when compaction is enabled, and questions whether the patch is worth formal submission.

The email centers on the **practical value of the fix** and the **trade-offs of its implementation**. While the patch addresses a known bottleneck (tombstone iteration inefficiency in `refs_verify_refname_available()` and `reftable_be_transaction_prepare()`), its real-world impact appears limited to edge cases where compaction is disabled. Kristofer’s draft patch ([GitGitGadget PR #2166](https://github.com/gitgitgadget/git/pull/2166)) spreads changes across multiple call sites (e.g., `reftable_be_reflog_exists()`), adding tombstone-skipping loops that could clutter the codebase. The lack of measurable improvement in realistic workflows—combined with the reftable backend’s design emphasis on avoiding compaction overhead—raises doubts about whether the patch’s benefits justify its complexity.

Key technical details remain unchanged from the parent email:
- **Files touched**: `reftable/merged.c`, `reftable/refs.c`, and `reftable/stack.c`.
- **Root cause**: The `merged_iter`’s `suppress_deletions` flag hid tombstones from bounds checks, forcing full scans.
- **New behavior**: Tombstones are now visible to iterator bounds logic, enabling early termination.
- **Benchmark caveat**: Improvements are only observable when compaction is disabled, suggesting the fix may not address the most common use cases.
