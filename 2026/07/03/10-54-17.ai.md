This is a **surface-level review** of the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s merged five-patch series adding reference-writing subcommands to `git refs`. The reviewer, Toon Claes, raises minor stylistic and consistency questions about the `git refs delete` implementation, none of which challenge the patch’s core design or correctness.

Toon’s feedback focuses on three areas:
1. **CLI ergonomics**: Suggests allowing both `--deref` and `--no-deref` flags (with `--deref` as the default) for symmetry with other Git commands, though the current implementation already matches `git update-ref -d`’s behavior.
2. **Test consistency**: Notes a mix of `git -C repo` and `cd repo` in the test script and questions why `git refs exists` isn’t used uniformly (it *is* used in most cases, but `show-ref` appears once for legacy reasons).
3. **Edge case curiosity**: Asks what happens when deleting a symbolic reference with an `<old-value>` (the answer is implicit in the code: `--no-deref` is required to avoid dereferencing, and `<old-value>` applies to the symref itself, not its target).

The review is purely cosmetic—no bugs or behavioral issues are identified. The questions are framed as "would it make sense" rather than "this is wrong," and the patch’s functionality (mirroring `git update-ref -d` exactly) is not disputed. Given the series is already merged, this feedback is likely intended for future reference or follow-up patches.
