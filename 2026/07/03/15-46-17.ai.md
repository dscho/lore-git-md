This is a **build system** and **platform compatibility** follow-up in the ongoing `USE_NSEC` discussion. D. Ben Knoble responds to Jeff King’s recent testing by confirming that nanosecond timestamps are preserved on XFS as well, reinforcing Peff’s conclusion that modern Linux filesystems (ext4, ext2, XFS, and even vfat) handle sub-second timestamps reliably. Knoble’s observation strengthens the case that the historical "racy Git" concerns—where filesystem timestamp granularity could cause spurious stat mismatches—are obsolete on Linux, at least for common filesystems.

The email does not propose new patches or technical changes, but it shifts the burden of proof in the thread. Knoble’s confirmation aligns with Peff’s findings, suggesting that the `USE_NSEC` knob is *not* subtly broken on modern Linux, contrary to Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier skepticism. This undermines the argument for removing the knob entirely and bolsters Brian M. Carlson’s proposal to flip the default to `true` for most users. However, Knoble does not engage with the broader questions raised in the thread: whether the knob should be runtime-configurable (Patrick’s proposal), whether it should be deprecated (Patrick’s earlier position), or whether the Meson option should proceed as-is (Knoble’s original patch).

Key technical details to track: the list of filesystems now confirmed to preserve nanoseconds (ext4, ext2, XFS, vfat), the test methodology (dropping caches and re-reading timestamps), and the implication that Linux VFS behavior has improved since the original `USE_NSEC` concerns were documented. The email does not address interoperability risks (e.g., mixing `USE_NSEC` and non-`USE_NSEC` implementations like Git and JGit) or platform coverage beyond Linux, leaving those questions unresolved. No new action is proposed, but the confirmation of XFS support adds weight to the argument that the original patch (adding a Meson `nanosec` option) is justified for build-time parity.
