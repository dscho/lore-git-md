This is a **test infrastructure** follow-up in the ongoing series to make `GIT_TEST_LONG` tests reliable and efficient. The email is a brief but substantive review reply from Jeff King (Peff) to Patrick Steinhardt, closing the loop on a design question about the `test_copy_bytes` helper.

Peff’s original review raised a "Chesterton’s fence" concern: the `test_copy_bytes` helper exists because `head -c` was not portable (specifically on IRIX), but modern systems may no longer need this workaround. He suggested either modernizing the helper or replacing it with a more efficient implementation (e.g., a `test-tool` helper). Patrick’s reply argued that the helper’s inefficiency is not worth fixing—most callers copy small amounts of data, and even the largest case (65 KB in `t0021-conversion.sh`) completes in seconds. Peff now concedes the point, agreeing that further optimization is unnecessary.

This exchange does not alter the immediate fix in patch 3 (replacing `dd` with `genzeros` in `t4141-apply-too-large.sh`), nor does it reopen the earlier debate about retaining the `EXPENSIVE` prerequisite for memory usage. The broader question about how Git defines "expensive" tests (runtime vs. resource consumption) remains a policy matter, but this thread’s technical scope is now settled. No new objections or technical concerns are introduced. The patch’s current design—runtime improvement at the cost of unchanged memory usage—stands as the agreed trade-off.
