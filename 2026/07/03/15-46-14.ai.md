This is a **build system** and **platform compatibility** follow-up in the ongoing `USE_NSEC` discussion. D. Ben Knoble responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier skepticism about the `nanosec` Meson option, reaffirming his support for the original patch while acknowledging the broader debate about whether `USE_NSEC` should exist at all.

Knoble argues that the patch remains valuable for build system parity, particularly for source distributions like Gentoo that want to offer the knob to users who know their filesystem supports nanosecond timestamps. He suggests that if the option is problematic, it should be removed from the Makefile entirely rather than just omitted from Meson. This framing shifts the burden of proof: the patch’s existence is justified unless the entire `USE_NSEC` mechanism is deprecated.

The email also signals openness to a more ambitious change—converting `USE_NSEC` to a runtime-configurable flag (e.g., `core.useNsec`). Knoble acknowledges this would require "more surgery" than the original patch but sees potential value in the dynamism. However, he raises a practical concern: testing such a change would be non-trivial, as it would need to verify behavior across filesystems with varying timestamp granularity. No concrete implementation or patch is proposed yet, leaving the runtime-configuration idea as a speculative direction rather than an immediate action item.

Key technical details to track: the patch’s current status (queued for merging), the unresolved tension between build-time and runtime configuration, and the lack of a clear path forward for testing or auto-detection. The thread’s focus remains on whether to proceed with the original patch, pivot to runtime configuration, or deprecate `USE_NSEC` entirely—with no consensus in sight.
