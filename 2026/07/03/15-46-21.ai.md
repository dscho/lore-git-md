This is a **build system and platform compatibility** follow-up in the `USE_NSEC` discussion. D. Ben Knoble responds to Jeff King’s feedback on Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to convert `USE_NSEC` from a build-time option to a runtime-configurable setting (e.g., `core.useNsec`). Knoble **reaffirms his support for the original Meson parity patch** while acknowledging the broader debate about the knob’s viability. He confirms that **XFS preserves nanosecond timestamps**, reinforcing Peff’s findings that modern Linux filesystems (ext4, ext2, vfat, XFS) no longer suffer from the "racy Git" issues that originally motivated `USE_NSEC`. However, he notes that **runtime configuration would require "more surgery"** and non-trivial testing, particularly for edge cases across filesystems, and does not propose a concrete implementation.

The email shifts the burden of proof: Knoble argues the original patch should proceed unless `USE_NSEC` is deprecated entirely, as it provides parity for source distributions (e.g., Gentoo) that want to expose the knob to users who know their filesystem supports nanosecond timestamps. He signals openness to runtime configuration but does not engage with the interoperability risks (e.g., Git/JGit mixing) or the unresolved question of whether the default should be `true` or `false`. The discussion remains stuck between three competing proposals: (1) Knoble’s build-time parity patch (now queued by Junio), (2) Brian M. Carlson’s proposal to flip the default to `true`, and (3) Patrick Steinhardt’s runtime-configuration idea, which Peff’s feedback suggests is desirable but technically challenging. No consensus has emerged on which path to prioritize.
