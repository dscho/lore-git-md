This is a follow-up in the ongoing reftable performance discussion, specifically about Kristofer Karlsson’s proposed fix for quadratic-time behavior in bulk ref deletion and re-creation. Kristofer has a draft patch (linked as a GitGitGadget PR) that exposes tombstones to iterator bounds checks, reducing runtime from O(n²) to O(n) in artificial benchmarks. However, he’s uncertain whether the change is worth submitting formally, citing two concerns: the fix is small but spread across multiple call sites, and the performance improvement only manifests when compaction is disabled—meaning it may not help real-world workflows.

The patch itself is technically sound and addresses a known bottleneck (tombstone iteration inefficiency in `merged_iter_next_void()`), but its practical impact is unclear. Kristofer’s hesitation reflects a broader tension in the thread: whether to optimize for edge cases (like bulk ref churn in CI or `git fast-import` scenarios) or prioritize code simplicity. Patrick Steinhardt previously encouraged submission, but Kristofer’s new data—showing the fix only helps when compaction is off—might weaken the case for merging. If this were sent as a formal patch, expect discussion about whether the added complexity (tombstone-skipping loops at call sites) is justified for a niche use case. For now, it remains a draft, and the decision likely hinges on whether others can reproduce the performance win in realistic scenarios.
