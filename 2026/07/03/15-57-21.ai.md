This is a follow-up in the ongoing worktree diff performance thread, focused on D. Ben Knoble’s response to Jeff King’s (Peff) proposed patch. The email reflects Knoble’s practical concerns as a user (shell prompt performance) and his interest in further investigating two areas: the slow first-diff regression introduced by Peff’s patch, and the broader issue of limiting racy entries at initial index write.

Knoble acknowledges the tradeoff Peff’s patch introduces—slowing the first `git diff` (~1800ms vs ~500ms) to speed up subsequent ones (~30ms vs ~500ms)—but frames it as acceptable for his use case (shell prompts), where consistency matters more than the initial delay. He expresses willingness to tolerate the first-diff slowdown if it eliminates the need to manually run `git status` to clear racy entries, a workaround he finds cumbersome to document for others.

The email highlights two potential areas for further work:
1. **Optimizing the index refresh diff**: Knoble notes the unexplained 800ms gap in Peff’s measurements (1800ms vs an expected 1000ms) and suggests investigating whether `diffcore_skip_stat_unmatch()`’s content check (`diff_filespec_check_stat_unmatch()`) is inherently slower than `ie_match_stat()`’s (`ce_modified_check_fs()`). This is framed as an "interesting puzzle" rather than a blocker, but Knoble lacks the time to dig into the index and diff machinery himself.
2. **Reducing racy entries at initial write**: Knoble references down-thread discussions (likely Peff’s `USE_NSEC` proposal) where the idea of stalling to avoid raciness was dismissed in favor of leveraging nanosecond precision. He ties this to the separate Meson thread about enabling `USE_NSEC` more broadly, implying that if nanosecond support were unconditionally available, the need to optimize the first-diff slowdown might diminish.

Knoble’s tone is collaborative and pragmatic. He defers to Peff and Junio’s technical judgment but signals his priorities as a user: predictable performance (even if slower initially) and reduced friction (no manual workarounds). His email does not propose new solutions but clarifies the user-facing impact of the tradeoffs under discussion, which may help guide the thread toward a solution that balances correctness, performance, and usability. The mention of the Meson thread also subtly links this effort to broader platform compatibility concerns.
