This is a follow-up in the ongoing worktree diff performance thread. D. Ben Knoble chimes in to share his perspective as the original reporter of the slowdown (noticed while debugging a slow shell prompt). He expresses a strong preference for a solution that trades a slower first `git diff` for consistently fast subsequent operations, rather than requiring users to manually run `git status` to clear racy entries. This aligns with the patch Jeff King (Peff) proposed earlier, which forces index refreshes by treating racy entries as stat-dirty.

Ben identifies two potential areas for further investigation: (1) optimizing the index refresh triggered by Peff’s patch (currently adding ~800ms to the first diff), and (2) reducing the number of racy entries written to the index in the first place. He notes that the latter was effectively deprioritized in favor of Peff’s `USE_NSEC` approach (which eliminates racy entries at the source by leveraging nanosecond timestamps), but remains curious about whether the refresh overhead can be mitigated. His email is more of a stakeholder perspective than a technical deep dive—he’s not proposing code changes, but rather advocating for a user-friendly tradeoff and signaling his intent to explore the refresh optimization when time permits.

The thread remains focused on Peff’s patch as the most promising path forward, though Ben’s feedback underscores the importance of addressing the first-diff slowdown. No new technical proposals or measurements are introduced here, but his framing may influence how the final solution is evaluated. If you care about worktree performance or racy-git edge cases, this email is worth noting for its user-centric priorities, but it doesn’t advance the technical discussion on its own.
