This is a follow-up to a bug report about leftover `REBASE_HEAD` state after a successful rebase. Matt Hunter shares his own experience with the issue, noting that the behavior seems tied to how the *last* item in the rebase todo list is handled. Specifically, he observed that `REBASE_HEAD` persists if the final action was a `squash` or `edit`, but not for `reword`. He also points out an inconsistency: if rebase stops on a `break` command, `REBASE_HEAD` is missing entirely, even though the rebase is still in progress.

Matt questions whether this is even a bug, suggesting it might be intentional or at least not clearly problematic. For his own use case (detecting an in-progress rebase), he worked around the issue by checking for the presence of the `rebase-merge` or `rebase-apply` directories in `$GIT_DIR` instead of relying on `REBASE_HEAD`.

This reply adds useful context to the original report, narrowing the scope of when the issue occurs and introducing a potential workaround. It doesn’t propose a fix or confirm the root cause, but it does suggest the behavior might not be as straightforward as a simple cleanup failure. The discussion is still early, and no patches have been submitted yet. If you’re scripting around rebase state, Matt’s workaround might be worth noting, but the underlying issue remains unresolved.
