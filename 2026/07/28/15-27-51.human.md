This is a follow-up in an ongoing bug report about leftover `REBASE_HEAD` state after a successful rebase. The discussion remains in the exploratory phase, with no patch proposed yet.

Long 76 pushes back on Matt Hunter’s skepticism about whether the behavior is even a bug. He argues that Git’s handling of `REBASE_HEAD` should mirror its behavior with other temporary HEAD files (like those for `MERGE_HEAD` or `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`), which are cleaned up after the operation completes. He also cites the documentation for `git rebase --show-current-patch`, which implies that `REBASE_HEAD` should exist *only* when a rebase is actively in progress (e.g., stopped due to conflicts), not after it finishes successfully. This reinforces his view that the leftover file is unintended and misleading.

Hunter’s workaround—checking for `rebase-merge` or `rebase-apply` directories instead of `REBASE_HEAD`—is acknowledged by Long 76, but he treats it as a temporary fix rather than a justification for the status quo. The exchange clarifies that the issue isn’t just about scripts breaking; it’s about Git violating its own documented expectations for how rebase state should be managed. The thread now has a stronger case for treating this as a bug, but the root cause (and whether it’s a regression or a long-standing oversight) is still unresolved. Phillip Wood’s earlier confirmation of the bug remains the most authoritative take so far.
