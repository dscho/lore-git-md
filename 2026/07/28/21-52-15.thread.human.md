The `git add --resolved` patch series, now at v3, introduces a new option to stage only paths whose conflict markers have been removed, streamlining merge conflict resolution. Junio C Hamano is the author, and the series has addressed all prior technical feedback, including fixes for option incompatibility logic and test script portability.

The core debate remains whether `--resolved` should be a monolithic, workflow-oriented operation (Junio’s position) or split into a more general `--unmerged` selector with policy flags (Michael Montalbo’s proposal). Jeff King (Peff) recently weighed in with a substantive review, raising two usability concerns. The first—accidentally staging unrelated local changes—he dismissed as unlikely due to merge strategy safeguards. The second, however, is more pressing: `--resolved` might give users a false sense of completeness by staging only files with resolved conflict markers, ignoring logically related changes (e.g., updates to callers of a modified function). Peff suggests this risk could be mitigated with clearer documentation or a warning in the command’s output.

The series is technically complete and under review, with no new blocking issues. The remaining discussion centers on design philosophy and usability, not implementation flaws. Reviewers should ensure the documentation and test suite address Peff’s edge cases, particularly scenarios where related changes exist outside conflicted files. The feature is otherwise ready for integration, pending resolution of the interface debate.
