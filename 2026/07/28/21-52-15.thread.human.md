The `git add --resolved` feature, introduced by Junio C Hamano in a four-patch series, aims to simplify conflict resolution by staging only files where conflict markers have been removed. The v2 update addressed early technical feedback, including a logic fix for option incompatibility and a missing test script entry, while adding a performance tweak for binary files. The series is now under review for its design choices, particularly the naming and scope of the `--resolved` option.

Michael Montalbo’s recent review challenges the current interface, arguing that `--resolved` conflates two distinct operations: selecting unmerged paths (an index-level property) and enforcing a conflict-marker check (a content-level policy). He proposes splitting the functionality into a more general `--unmerged` option, which would stage all unmerged paths by default, with optional flags like `--allow-conflict-markers` or `--skip-conflict-markers` to control safety checks. This approach would decouple the selection mechanism from the policy, making the command more flexible and the naming more precise. While Montalbo acknowledges the workflow appeal of `--resolved`, his critique centers on whether the interface accurately reflects the implementation’s behavior.

The series itself is technically complete, with robust test coverage and no known bugs. The conflict-marker detection logic has been consolidated and made stricter, and the new helpers (`is_conflict_marker_line()` and `remove_file_from_index_with_flags()`) are well-documented. However, the naming debate could influence the feature’s long-term adoption, as Git’s command-line interface tends to prioritize clarity and extensibility. No other reviewers have weighed in yet, so the discussion remains open. If the current design stands, `--resolved` will provide a targeted solution for conflict resolution, but Montalbo’s alternative could offer a more adaptable tool for advanced workflows.
