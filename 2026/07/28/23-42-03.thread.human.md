This thread investigates why `fetch.bundleURI`-based clones of the Linux kernel repository are unexpectedly slow—sometimes slower than standard clones—despite bundles being designed for performance. The issue was raised by Ryszard Knop, who observed that `git index-pack` was the bottleneck, consuming 100% CPU on a single thread for most of a 4–5 minute clone operation in their CI workflow.

The key breakthrough came when Brian M. Carlson clarified that multi-ref bundles force Git to process *all* objects in the bundle’s pack, not just those reachable from the requested ref, and suggested enabling threaded `index-pack` via `pack.threads`. Ryszard confirmed this resolved the immediate bottleneck: setting `pack.threads=0` halved the clone time to ~2 minutes, proving the single-threaded behavior was a misconfiguration rather than a fundamental limitation. The discussion also ruled out bare repositories as a viable alternative due to security risks, reinforcing `fetch.bundleURI` as the safer solution for CI environments.

With the threading issue resolved, the thread now shifts to exploring further optimizations. Open questions include why some packing sections remain single-threaded, whether bundle generation parameters (e.g., single-ref bundles, compression levels) could improve performance, and whether `index-pack` could skip unreachable objects during processing. No objections or alternative explanations have been raised, and the conversation is now focused on refining best practices for `fetch.bundleURI` in large-repository workflows.
