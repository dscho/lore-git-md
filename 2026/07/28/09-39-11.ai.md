This is a follow-up to a bug report about leftover `REBASE_HEAD` state after a successful rebase. The new email adds observational data from Matt Hunter, who encountered the same issue while writing a script. Hunter notes that the behavior appears tied to the *last action* in the rebase todo list: if the final step is a `squash` or `edit` (but not `reword`), `REBASE_HEAD` is left behind. He also reports an inconsistency where `REBASE_HEAD` is *missing* if the rebase stops on a `break` command, despite the rebase still being in progress.

Hunter’s feedback suggests the issue may not be a straightforward cleanup failure but rather a quirk in how the sequencer handles different rebase actions. He questions whether the behavior is even a bug, as he worked around it by checking for the presence of `rebase-merge` or `rebase-apply` directories instead of relying on `REBASE_HEAD`. His input expands the scope of the problem: it’s not just about conflict resolution (as the original report suggested) but also about specific rebase commands and edge cases like `break`.

The thread now has two data points: Long 76’s conflict-resolution scenario and Hunter’s squash/edit scenario. Neither has proposed a patch, and the root cause remains unclear. The sequencer’s state cleanup logic is likely involved, but whether this is a regression, a design oversight, or an intentional (if undocumented) behavior is still an open question. The discussion is still in the exploratory phase, with no clear consensus on whether or how to fix it.
