This thread is about a bug in Git’s rebase cleanup logic that leaves behind the `.git/REBASE_HEAD` file and `REBASE_HEAD` ref after a successful rebase in certain scenarios, such as after conflict resolution or specific rebase actions like `squash`, `edit`, or `reword`. The leftover state can cause scripts to misbehave or require unnecessary force-pushes. The issue was confirmed by Phillip Wood, a rebase/sequencer expert, who pinpointed the root cause: the sequencer’s cleanup logic fails to remove `REBASE_HEAD` after the final rebase command completes, regardless of the command type.

The expected behavior, as clarified by Wood, is that `REBASE_HEAD` should exist only during active rebase operations (e.g., conflicts or editor pauses) and must not persist after completion. Long 76, the original reporter, reinforced this by citing Git’s consistent cleanup of other temporary HEAD files (like those for `merge` or `cherry-pick`) and the `--show-current-patch` documentation, which implies `REBASE_HEAD` should only exist during an active rebase. Matt Hunter, another reporter, identified that the issue is tied to specific rebase actions and noted an inconsistency with the `break` command, which does not trigger the bug. Hunter also proposed a workaround: checking for `rebase-merge` or `rebase-apply` directories instead of `REBASE_HEAD` to detect an in-progress rebase.

The thread remains focused on confirming the bug’s existence and expected behavior, with no patch yet proposed. Open questions include whether this is a regression or a long-standing oversight, and whether other sequencer state files might also be inconsistently cleaned up. The discussion has not revealed any version-specific behavior or `.gitconfig` dependencies. For now, the workaround endorsed by Wood is the recommended way to handle the issue in scripts.
