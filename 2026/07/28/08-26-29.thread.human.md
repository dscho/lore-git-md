This thread is about a bug where Git fails to clean up the `.git/REBASE_HEAD` file and `REBASE_HEAD` ref after a successful rebase, particularly when the rebase involved conflict resolution or certain commands like `squash` or `edit`. The issue was first reported by Long 76, who noted that the leftover state could cause spurious force-push requirements and mislead scripts checking for `REBASE_HEAD`.

Matt Hunter followed up with additional observations, showing that the problem isn’t limited to conflict resolution. Instead, it appears tied to the *last action* in the rebase todo list: if the final step is a `squash` or `edit` (but not `reword`), `REBASE_HEAD` is left behind. Hunter also noted an inconsistency where `REBASE_HEAD` is missing if the rebase stops on a `break` command, despite the rebase still being in progress. He worked around the issue by checking for `rebase-merge` or `rebase-apply` directories instead of relying on `REBASE_HEAD`, but the root cause remains unclear. The discussion is still exploratory, with no patches proposed or consensus on whether this is a bug, a regression, or an intentional (if undocumented) behavior. The sequencer’s state cleanup logic is likely involved, but further investigation is needed.
