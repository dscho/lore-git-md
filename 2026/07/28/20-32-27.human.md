This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s five-part series making Git’s on-disk ODB structures pluggable. The patch refactors loose-object-map loading to remove hard-coded assumptions about the ODB backend and initialization order, a key step in the ongoing ODB abstraction effort.

Justin Tobler’s review engages directly with the patch’s design and motivation. He confirms the problem (eager loading of compatibility object maps and direct access to the loose backend) and agrees with the solution (deferring map loading to the loose backend itself). His questions—whether each ODB source might answer the `should_use_loose_object_map()` check differently, and why eager loading was needed in the first place—are technical and forward-looking, probing edge cases rather than nitpicking. The review is concise but substantive, reinforcing the patch’s correctness and architectural soundness.

The discussion leaves one minor open question: whether the `should_use_loose_object_map()` check should remain centralized or be delegated to individual backends. This is not a blocker, but it hints at a possible follow-up refinement. The patch itself is well-motivated, narrowly scoped, and aligns cleanly with the series’ goal of decoupling backend-specific logic from core setup. It looks ready for integration once the commit message is tweaked for clarity (as Junio requested in the parent review).
