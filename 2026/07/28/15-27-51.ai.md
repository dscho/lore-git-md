This is a follow-up in an ongoing bug report about leftover `REBASE_HEAD` state after a successful rebase. The email is a reply from the original reporter, Long 76, to Matt Hunter’s earlier observations.

Long 76 reinforces the position that the behavior is a bug, not an intentional design choice. He points to Git’s handling of other temporary HEAD files (like those for `merge` or `cherry-pick`) as precedent: those are reliably cleaned up after the operation completes, and `REBASE_HEAD` should behave the same way. He also cites the documentation for `--show-current-patch`, which implies that `REBASE_HEAD` should exist *only* when a rebase is actively in progress (e.g., stopped due to conflicts), not after it has finished. This aligns with Phillip Wood’s earlier assessment that the file should not persist.

The email does not introduce new technical details or reproduction steps, but it clarifies the reporter’s expectations and strengthens the case for treating this as a bug rather than an edge case. Long 76 also acknowledges Hunter’s workaround (checking for `rebase-merge` or `rebase-apply` directories instead of `REBASE_HEAD`) but does not engage with the inconsistency Hunter noted around the `break` command or the specific rebase actions that trigger the issue. The thread remains in the exploratory phase, with no patch proposed yet. The key unresolved question is still whether this is a regression or a long-standing oversight in the sequencer’s cleanup logic.
