This is a minor follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the optional fourth patch in Jeff King’s four-patch series fixing and refactoring a segfault in `git diff --relative --cached`. The patch adds a `BUG()` assertion to `oneway_diff()` in `diff-lib.c` to enforce an invariant that at least one of `idx` or `tree` must be non-NULL. The assertion is already approved and technically uncontroversial; the only remaining question is the wording of its message.

Jeff King clarifies his original intent: the assertion is not primarily for static analyzers (though it may help them), but to document the invariant for human readers and future contributors. He acknowledges the awkwardness of including a comment without the `BUG()` check, which is why he initially hesitated to send the patch at all. Junio C Hamano playfully suggests that if the invariant is hard for static analyzers to understand, it might also challenge AI-assisted contributors—though neither treats this as a serious obstacle.

The exchange remains lighthearted and confirms that the assertion’s purpose is purely defensive and documentary. The patch is optional and not blocking integration; the entire series (original bugfix plus three refactorings) is already queued for `next`. The only loose end is whether to merge the assertion with Jeff’s neutral message (`"oneway_diff with neither idx nor tree"`), Junio’s humorous alternative (`"Hey, Coverity, this does not happen"`), or omit it entirely. No technical objections exist, and the discussion is now purely about code clarity.
