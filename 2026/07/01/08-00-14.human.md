This is a **performance analysis follow-up** to the reftable backend's quadratic-time behavior during bulk ref deletion and re-creation. Jeff King (Peff) expands on Patrick Steinhardt's earlier diagnosis, confirming that the issue stems from reftable's tombstone-based deletion design. The discussion now splits the bottleneck into two roughly equal parts: half the runtime is consumed by `refs_verify_refname_available()` (scanning tombstones to confirm refname availability), and the other half by `reftable_be_transaction_prepare()` (reading each ref individually during transaction preparation, which becomes expensive due to tombstone overhead).

Peff suggests a potential optimization for the transaction phase—merging the sorted list of transaction refs with the reftable contents to avoid redundant reads—but does not propose a concrete fix. Both contributors agree this is an edge case tied to artificial benchmarks (e.g., `git for-each-ref | git update-ref --stdin`) rather than real-world workflows, so it remains deprioritized. The thread leaves the door open for future optimizations (like early iterator termination or transaction-level batching) but does not advance the issue further.

This exchange is **substantive but low-urgency**, offering a clearer breakdown of the quadratic-time problem for future reference. The key takeaway: reftable's tombstoning trade-off (avoiding compaction overhead) creates performance cliffs for bulk operations, but the impact is currently limited to niche scenarios. No immediate action is expected, but the analysis provides a roadmap for later improvements.
