This thread is a 13-patch series from Johannes Schindelin (Dscho) that fixes resource leaks and error-path bugs identified by Coverity Scan after its recent outage. The series targets memory, file-descriptor, and process-handle leaks in core Git, with no user-visible changes—purely internal correctness improvements. Junio C Hamano has just given the series a green light, calling it "looking good" and indicating he will queue it for merging, despite two unresolved correctness concerns.

The most critical issue remains in **patch 1/13**, where Junio previously identified a latent bug: the patch checks `errno` to detect file-reading errors, but `errno` may have been clobbered by intervening function calls (`strbuf_release`, `insert_loose_map`). He recommended replacing the `errno` check with an explicit `ferror(fp)` call for reliability. **Patch 5/13** is also problematic—Patrick Steinhardt pointed out that the fix doesn’t actually plug the leak it claims to address, as the `dpath` variable is never assigned a non-NULL value before being freed. Both issues will likely require follow-up patches, as Junio’s approval suggests he views them as fixable post-merge rather than blockers.

Other patches have seen positive feedback: **patches 2/13, 4/13, and 6/13** carry Reviewed-by from Patrick Steinhardt and Jeff King (Peff), with Peff independently reproducing the leak fixed in patch 6/13. **Patch 7/13**’s completeness concern was resolved after Johannes clarified ownership transfer, and **patch 8/13**’s maintainability caveat (two separate `free(cwd)` calls) was noted but not deemed a blocker. The remaining patches (3/13, 9–13/13) address leaks in areas like bundle URIs, worktree creation, IMAP uploads, reftable, fsmonitor, and Windows process handling, with no objections raised.

The series is now on track for merging, with the expectation that the two correctness issues will be addressed in follow-ups. The tone of the discussion is collaborative, treating these as mechanical cleanups rather than contentious changes.
