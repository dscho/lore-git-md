This is the **fifth iteration (v5) of a performance optimization series** for Git’s commit-reach subsystem, specifically targeting `paint_down_to_common()`. The series is now **fully reviewed, regression-free, and ready to merge**, with all feedback incorporated. The core goal is to **terminate merge-base walks early when one side of the history is exhausted**, eliminating unnecessary traversal of large one-sided histories (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories). This yields **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric queries, such as `merge-base --all` across a 2.6M-commit monorepo (3.67s → 5ms).

The series is **collaborative and well-tested**, with contributions from Kristofer Karlsson (author), Elijah Newren (test cases), and reviewers Derrick Stolee and SZEDER Gábor. Key updates in v5 include:
- **New infrastructure**: A `test_trace2_data_singular` helper (patch 2/10) improves diagnostic output for trace2 assertions, making it easier to iterate on step counts.
- **Regression tests**: Added clock-skew topologies (patch 6/10) and step-count assertions for edge cases, ensuring the optimization works correctly with v1 commit-graphs.
- **Mechanical cleanup**: Squashed the removal of unused `nonstale_queue` dedup wrappers into the `paint_state` introduction (patch 7/10).
- **Dead-code removal**: The commit-date ordering fallback (introduced in 2018) is **removed entirely** (patch 10/10), as the side-exhaustion optimization renders it obsolete. This simplifies the codebase and ensures the queue is always generation-ordered.

The series touches **`commit-reach.c`**, **documentation** (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`), and **test scripts** (`t6099-merge-base-side-exhaustion.sh`, `t6600-test-reach.sh`). New symbols include the `paint_state` struct (with counters for `parent1_count`, `parent2_count`, and `mb_candidate_count`), `paint_queue_put()`/`paint_queue_get()`, and trace2 instrumentation. The behavior change is **internal-only**: the walk now terminates early when `(!state->parent1_count || !state->parent2_count) && state->gen_ordered && generation < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`.

Benchmarks show **no performance regressions**, and the series is **uncontroversial**.
