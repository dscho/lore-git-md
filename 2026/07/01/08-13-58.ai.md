This is a **bugfix follow-up discussion** in the ongoing memory-leak cleanup thread. Jeff King (Peff) replies to Patrick Steinhardt’s review of the `format-patch` leak fix (patch 2/2 of the series) with two main topics: CI infrastructure for leak detection and additional leak fixes he has prepared.

Peff agrees with Patrick’s suggestion to enable LeakSanitizer (LSan) for the `linux-TEST-vars` CI job but proposes a more aggressive consolidation: merge the existing `linux-reftables-leaks` job into a single job that runs LSan, reftable-specific tests, and the `TEST-vars` suite. He sees little risk of code-path interference and suggests this would simplify CI maintenance. The proposal is a process tweak, not a blocker for the current series.

On the technical side, Peff reports he has already sent patches for the bloom-filter leaks Patrick observed in `t0095`, `t4013`, and `t4211`. He then expands the scope to OpenSSL SHA-1 leaks: when Git is built with `OPENSSL_SHA1_UNSAFE`, recent OpenSSL versions allocate memory for SHA-1 contexts that must be freed via a new `git_hash_discard()` function. These leaks surface in `csum-file.c` during aborted hash operations. Peff has draft patches but notes they need polishing—specifically, exposing a discard primitive in each hash implementation’s API to avoid inefficiency. He also hints that the same issue exists for SHA-256, though he hasn’t yet tested it under LSan.

The email is **not a new patch** but a status update on related work. It keeps the thread focused on leak detection as a broader effort while confirming the original `format-patch` fix remains correct and self-contained. Key technical details for follow-up: the proposed CI job name (`linux-reftables-leaks-TEST-vars`), the new `git_hash_discard()` function, and the OpenSSL SHA-1/SHA-256 context leaks.
