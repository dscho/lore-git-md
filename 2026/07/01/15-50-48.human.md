This is the first patch in Jeff King’s three-part bugfix series addressing memory leaks in Git’s commit-graph Bloom filter code. Junio C Hamano, the project maintainer, replies to Peff’s patch with a brief but positive review, calling it "trivially correct." Junio’s response focuses on the patch’s design choices—particularly the decision to use an explicit static flag (`bloom_filter_slab_initialized`) to make slab initialization idempotent rather than relying on internal commit-slab details or skipping re-initialization after `deinit_bloom_filters()`. He endorses the approach as straightforward and safe, noting that the flag-based solution avoids surprises by keeping the init/deinit pairing explicit.

The patch itself is minimal (five lines added to `bloom.c`) and fixes a leak that surfaces when `init_bloom_filters()` is called multiple times—such as during split commit-graph parsing or in the `test-tool bloom` helper—without an intervening `deinit_bloom_filters()` call. The leak occurs because repeated initialization overwrites the existing slab pointers, losing track of previously allocated memory. Junio highlights an additional benefit of the fix: it ensures the test-tool’s explicit initialization doesn’t mask bugs in the production code’s lazy initialization logic. The patch is part of a broader effort to eliminate leaks in the `linux-TEST-vars` CI jobs when leak-checking is enabled, and it has already received positive feedback from Patrick Steinhardt and Derrick Stolee in earlier reviews.

This patch is uncontroversial, well-motivated, and clearly correct. It’s a typical example of Peff’s precise, low-level bugfix work, and Junio’s approval suggests it’s likely to be merged soon.
