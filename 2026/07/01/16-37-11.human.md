This is the **final patch in a fully reviewed, ready-to-merge performance series** targeting Git’s commit-reach subsystem. The patch removes the commit-date ordering fallback in `paint_down_to_common()`, a legacy heuristic that was introduced in 2018 to avoid performance regressions with v1 commit-graph data. The fallback is now obsolete because the series’ earlier patches implement **side-exhaustion termination**, which stops the walk as soon as one side of the history is exhausted—eliminating the deep traversals that the fallback was designed to prevent.

The patch deletes the `corrected_commit_dates_enabled()` function (no remaining callers) and updates `paint_down_to_common()` to **always use generation ordering**, simplifying the code and restoring invariants that the fallback broke. A comment at queue initialization documents the requirement for topological ordering, and the `gen_ordered` flag is removed from the `paint_state` struct. Benchmarks on the Linux kernel repo show the side-exhaustion optimization outperforms the fallback by orders of magnitude: `git merge-base --all v4.8 v4.9` now completes in **17 ms** (v1 graph) and **5 ms** (v2 graph), down from 250 ms and 290 ms respectively, with step counts reduced from 167k to **3.9k–5.7k**.

The documentation (`paint-down-to-common.adoc`) is updated to remove all references to the fallback, including the section explaining its limitations (e.g., broken single-result optimizations and premature termination risks). Test assertions in `t6600-test-reach.sh` are adjusted to reflect the new step counts, which drop by one in some topologies due to earlier loop exits—behavior documented as equivalent.

This patch is **uncontroversial** and approved by Derrick Stolee, who confirmed the side-exhaustion logic renders the fallback unnecessary. The series is **technically complete**, with no open questions or expected rerolls. It is **ready for merging** and will land as part of the broader `paint_down_to_common()` optimization effort.
