This is the second patch in Jeff King’s three-patch bugfix series targeting memory leaks in Git’s commit-graph Bloom filter code. Junio C Hamano replies to Jeff’s earlier email to explicitly endorse the design choice behind the fix: avoiding reuse of existing Bloom-filter "keyvecs" in favor of generating fresh ones for each traversal of a `rev_info` structure, even though the efficiency impact is negligible. Junio praises the conservative approach and the clarity of the commit message, which explains why reusing keyvecs could introduce subtle bugs if the pruning pathspec changes between traversals. The patch itself is minimal—just two lines added to `prepare_to_use_bloom_filter()` in `revision.c`—and directly addresses the leak by explicitly freeing old keyvecs before generating new ones. No behavior changes or on-disk format modifications are introduced, and the leak is only detectable under `GIT_TEST_COMMIT_GRAPH_CHANGED_PATHS=1` with leak sanitizers enabled.

The series as a whole is now fully reviewed, with all three patches receiving positive feedback from Patrick Steinhardt, Derrick Stolee, and Junio. This particular patch is a straightforward, well-motivated bugfix that aligns with Git’s existing patterns, and Junio’s endorsement reinforces its readiness for merging. If you care about memory leaks in the commit-graph Bloom filter code—or about the broader effort to enable leak-checking in CI—this patch is worth noting, but it’s unlikely to be controversial or require further discussion.
