This email is part of Patrick Steinhardt’s response to **substantive pushback** on his RFC v2 patch (2/2) to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. The focus here is on addressing SZEDER Gábor’s objection about the disruption to `git log --follow` and historical tracking, which SZEDER framed as a zero-sum trade-off between root directory clutter and usability.

Patrick acknowledges the subjectivity of the change and argues that the **one-time cost** of the reorganization is justified by a **permanent improvement in discoverability**, particularly for newcomers. He directly engages with SZEDER’s concern about `git log --follow` stopping at the "rename barrier," framing it as a **deficiency in Git’s tooling** that the project is uniquely positioned to address. By subjecting Git itself to the pain of large-scale renames, Patrick suggests the reorganization could **incentivize improvements** to `git log --follow` and related tools, turning a drawback into a catalyst for better tooling. He commits to amending the commit message to include this rationale.

The response does not propose a technical solution to the `git log --follow` issue but instead shifts the debate toward whether the **structural benefits** (e.g., onboarding, codebase clarity) outweigh the **temporary disruption** to historical tracking. This argument may resonate with contributors who prioritize long-term maintainability, but it leaves the door open for further discussion about whether the tooling improvements will materialize or if the trade-off remains unacceptable. The tone is collaborative, treating SZEDER’s feedback as constructive rather than dismissive. Key files/subsystems mentioned: `lib/`, `git log --follow`. Related prior work: none explicitly cited. Likely points of contention: whether the tooling argument is persuasive, and whether the reorganization’s benefits justify the disruption to downstream workflows.
