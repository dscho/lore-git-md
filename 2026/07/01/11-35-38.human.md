This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s 11-part series implementing the `git history drop` subcommand, which removes a commit from history and replays its descendants on top of its parent. The series is now technically complete, with all prior review feedback—including the critical ref-resolution dispute in v7—fully addressed in earlier patches.

The patch adds the user-facing `drop` subcommand to the experimental `git history` command. It handles the full lifecycle of dropping a commit: detecting conflicts, preserving local changes, updating the index and working tree when HEAD moves, and supporting bare repositories. The implementation reuses the ref-update and replay machinery introduced in earlier patches, avoiding code duplication. Key behaviors include refusing to drop root or merge commits, aborting on conflicts, and providing `--dry-run` and `--update-refs` options for safety and control.

The accompanying test file (`t/t3454-history-drop.sh`) is thorough, covering 561 lines of edge cases: detached HEAD, dirty indexes, staged and unstaged changes, empty descendants, and branch updates. The documentation update adds clear usage examples and limitations. The patch is well-motivated, well-tested, and integrates cleanly with the rest of the series.
