This is Junio’s “What’s cooking” report for early July 2026, the first status update of the Git 2.56 cycle.
It lists 47 topics currently in flight, grouped by integration branch and maturity.

The most notable new arrivals are Patrick Steinhardt’s **ps/setup-split-discovery-and-setup** (16 patches), which finally disentangles repository discovery from repository setup, and Phillip Wood’s **pw/rebase-drop-notes-with-commit** (15 patches), which stops the rebase machinery from copying notes to unrelated commits when a commit is dropped. Both are large, well-motivated series that have already seen several rounds of review; expect them to land in ‘next’ soon.

On the performance front, Karthik K’s **kk/prio-queue-cascade-sift** (1 patch) and Karthik Nayak’s **kk/merge-base-exhaustion** (10 patches) offer measurable speed-ups for commit-graph traversals and merge-base queries, respectively. The latter is still marked as “passes t6600 standalone, breaks when merged to ‘seen’,” so it will need another reroll before it graduates.

Several smaller but important fixes are also queued: Jeff King’s **jk/bloom-leak-fixes** (3 patches) plugs memory leaks in the changed-path Bloom filter code, and Johannes Schindelin’s **js/coverity-fixes** (13 patches) addresses a variety of resource leaks and invalid file-descriptor closures flagged by Coverity.

The stalled column still holds three long-running topics: **ap/http-redirect-wwwauth-fix**, **jt/config-lock-timeout**, and **js/parseopt-subcommand-autocorrection**, all of which have been waiting for author responses for more than a month. Junio’s tone suggests they may be dropped unless fresh interest appears.

Overall, the report paints a picture of a healthy development cycle: a mix of architectural refactoring, performance work, and bug fixes, with no single topic dominating the queue. The absence of any “brown paper bag” regressions in the freshly tagged 2.55 release means the tree is in good shape to start merging topics to ‘master’ next week.
