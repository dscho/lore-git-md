This is the **fifth iteration (v5) of a performance optimization series** in Git’s commit-reach subsystem, targeting `paint_down_to_common()`—the core function behind merge-base calculations. The series is now **fully reviewed, regression-free, and ready to merge**, with all feedback incorporated. It delivers **100-1000x speedups** for asymmetric queries (e.g., repositories with import grafts or shallow histories) by terminating early when one side of the history is exhausted.

The series touches **`commit-reach.c`**, **documentation**, and **tests**, introducing a new `paint_state` struct with per-side counters (`parent1_count`, `parent2_count`, `mb_candidate_count`) to track exclusive commits. The optimization is gated by generation ordering (`gen_ordered` flag) and finite-generation checks, ensuring correctness. Key symbols removed include the obsolete `corrected_commit_dates_enabled()` fallback and unused dedup wrappers (`nonstale_queue_put_dedup`, `nonstale_queue_get_dedup`). The walk now terminates when `(!state->parent1_count || !state->parent2_count) && state->gen_ordered && generation < GENERATION_NUMBER_INFINITY`.

**New in v5**:
- **Patch 2/10**: Adds `test_trace2_data_singular` to `test-lib-functions.sh`, improving diagnostic output for trace2 assertions (e.g., "expected X, got Y" instead of silent grep failures).
- **Patch 6/10**: Introduces clock-skew topologies (`se-`, `se2-`) to test side-exhaustion edge cases with v1 commit graphs, including step-count assertions.
- **Patch 10/10**: Removes the commit-date ordering fallback entirely, superseding the separate "commit-reach: fix !FIND_ALL early exit with v1 commit graph" topic. This ensures the queue is always generation-ordered, simplifying termination conditions.
- **Mechanical squash**: Folds the unused dedup wrapper removal into patch 7/10.
- **Documentation updates**: Clarifies that infinite-to-finite generation transitions do not hold under the date-ordering fallback (now removed).

**Performance impact**: Benchmarks show dramatic improvements. For example, `merge-base --all` across a 2.6M-commit monorepo drops from **3.67s to 5ms**, and step counts in git.git are reduced by **30-60%** for common queries. The series is **self-verifying**, with deterministic step-count assertions for all commit-graph modes (none, full, half, no-gdat) and edge cases (self/duplicate/pending-stale/infinity-both-sides, criss-cross merges, grafts, shallow histories).
