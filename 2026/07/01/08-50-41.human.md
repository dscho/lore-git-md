This is a follow-up in the already-merged `--linearize` feature for `git replay`. Toon Claes responds to Junio C Hamano’s review of the edge case where `git replay --linearize` drops one side of a divergent merge commit, leaving the other branch dangling.

Toon acknowledges the issue and clarifies the intended design: `--linearize` should produce a single linear sequence, even when multiple input branches are provided. This means all commits—regardless of their original branch structure—are flattened into one line, which may duplicate shared commits if branches are replayed separately. He now leans toward restoring the v1-v4 behavior, where `--linearize` always collapses everything into a single tip, rather than trying to preserve branch independence.

He also explores two workarounds for users who want to replay multiple branches without rewriting unrelated history: using `--ref` to limit which ref is updated, or peeling the revision to a commit (e.g., `Z^{commit}`). These suggestions highlight the tension between predictability and flexibility in the feature’s design.

The email is part of a larger post-merge discussion about three critical issues in the series: the silent commit-dropping regression (highest priority), the CLI design inconsistency with `git rebase`, and this merge-commit divergence edge case. Toon’s response here focuses on the third issue, but his broader stance—prioritizing predictable linearization over branch preservation—also informs the regression debate. A follow-up patch and documentation update are likely needed to align the implementation with the clarified design intent.
