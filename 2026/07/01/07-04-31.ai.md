This is the final patch in Johannes Schindelin’s 13-patch Coverity-driven cleanup series, targeting Windows-specific process-handle leaks. The patch fixes an inconsistency in handle ownership between `exit_process()` and `terminate_process_tree()`: previously, `exit_process()` leaked the process handle on early returns (when `GetExitCodeProcess()` fails or the process is still active), while `terminate_process_tree()` always closed it. The mismatch led to a double-close bug in `mingw_kill()`, where the caller tried to close the handle again on error paths, triggering a use-after-free that Coverity flagged as CID 1437238.

The fix establishes a clear invariant: both functions now own the handle from the call onward and close it on every return path. The change is minimal: `exit_process()` gains a single `CloseHandle(process)` call before returning, and the redundant `CloseHandle(h)` in `mingw_kill()` is removed. The patch touches only two files (`compat/mingw.c` and `compat/win32/exit-process.h`) and makes no behavior changes beyond plugging the leak.

This is a platform-compatibility patch with no cross-platform impact. The fix is mechanical and aligns with the rest of the series—small, focused, and uncontroversial. Eric Sunshine’s prior reviews of the series (including this patch) suggest the changes are correct and well-scoped. The patch is ready for merging and fits cleanly into the larger Coverity cleanup effort. Key names to track in follow-up discussion: `exit_process`, `terminate_process_tree`, and `mingw_kill`.
