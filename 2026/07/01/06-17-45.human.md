This is a **performance analysis follow-up** in the ongoing reftable backend discussion. Patrick Steinhardt confirms Jeff King’s observation of quadratic-time behavior during bulk ref deletion and re-creation, identifying the root cause as **tombstone handling inefficiency** in the reftable backend. When refs are deleted, they are not immediately removed but instead marked as tombstones, forcing subsequent operations to process all tombstones to determine if a ref exists. This leads to non-linear runtime growth (e.g., 1.8s for 4,000 refs vs. 7.1s for 8,000 refs).

Patrick notes this issue was previously discussed (linking to an earlier thread) and partially addressed via optimizations to `refs_verify_refname_available()`, but further improvements are possible. He suggests that the merged iterator could abort early when searching for a specific refname if the tombstone name sorts lexicographically after the target, avoiding full iteration. However, he also acknowledges this is an edge case in artificial benchmarks, deprioritizing it relative to other reftable work.

The email is **substantive and technical**, providing clear context for future optimizations. It does not propose a patch but clarifies the mechanism behind the quadratic behavior, making it easier for others to tackle. The tone is pragmatic—Patrick frames the issue as known but low-priority, while leaving room for incremental fixes. This is **not a call to action** but a useful reference for anyone working on reftable performance.
