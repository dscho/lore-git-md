This is a **performance optimization for the reftable backend**, addressing a previously diagnosed quadratic-time scalability issue in bulk ref deletion and re-creation. The patch targets a specific inefficiency in how Git’s reftable backend handles tombstones (deleted refs that are marked but not purged), which forced redundant full scans during operations like `refs_verify_refname_available()` and `reftable_be_transaction_prepare()`.

Kristofer Karlsson proposes a fix that exposes tombstones to iterator bounds checks, allowing early termination when a tombstone’s name sorts after the target ref. This reduces the benchmark from **O(n²) to O(n)**—for example, runtime for 8,000 refs drops from **14.93s to 0.145s**. The change touches core reftable internals (`merged_iter_next_void()`, `reftable_ref_iterator_advance()`, and log iteration paths) and requires adding tombstone-skipping loops at call sites (e.g., `reftable_be_reflog_exists()`). Patrick Steinhardt endorses the approach, calling the gains "nice" and encouraging a polished patch.

The optimization is well-motivated and data-driven, but its adoption may hinge on whether the added complexity (per-call-site tombstone filtering) is justified for what the thread previously framed as an edge case. The fix aligns with Jeff King’s earlier suggestion of iterator-based merging but implements it at a lower level, addressing both halves of the bottleneck simultaneously. If sent as a patch, it will likely spark discussion about code clarity vs. performance trade-offs, especially since the quadratic behavior primarily affects artificial benchmarks rather than real-world workflows.
