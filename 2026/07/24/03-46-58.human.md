This is a **performance regression follow-up** from Patrick Steinhardt, the author of the ODB rework that introduced the slowdown. He responds to Peff’s observation that the regression is not quarantine-specific but a systemic alternates performance issue affecting all operations using `GIT_ALTERNATE_OBJECT_DIRECTORIES`.

Patrick proposes a **medium-term architectural course correction**: moving alternates handling from the ODB layer into the "files" backend itself. This would allow the backend to treat alternates as an implementation detail, enabling optimizations like pack-first-loose-second lookups *across all alternates* (fixing the regression) while also addressing other awkwardnesses: dropping the `OBJECT_INFO_SECOND_READ` flag, unifying commit-graph/bitmap loading, simplifying the object storage extension, and improving ODB maintenance. He acknowledges the change is non-trivial but argues it’s feasible and would clean up several long-standing design tensions. A patch series removing `odb_prepare_alternates()` calls is already in progress and will be sent in two weeks, with follow-up work to complete the transition.

The email is **substantive and forward-looking**, positioning the alternates redesign as a principled fix for both the immediate performance regression and broader ODB abstraction issues. It does not conflict with the near-term lookup-order fix Peff and Junio favor, but it may supersede or subsume it if successful. The tone is constructive and collaborative, framing the redesign as a natural evolution of the ODB work rather than a defensive reaction.
