This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s six-patch series (v4) to make Git’s on-disk object database (ODB) structures pluggable, part of the larger ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to decouple backend-specific directory creation (like `objects/pack/`) from the core repository setup code, allowing future ODB backends to define their own on-disk layouts without modifying Git’s initialization logic.

The first patch, a correctness fix for loose-object map loading in repositories using compatibility hash algorithms (e.g., SHA-256 with SHA-1), is now marked as "will merge to 'next'" in Junio’s latest "What's cooking" report (2025-06-10). The remaining five patches are uncontroversial and ready for merging. The series has evolved significantly: the fourth patch abandoned its original design (deferred ODB creation) in favor of a simpler, unified approach proposed by Toon Claes, making ODB creation explicit at every call site. The sixth and final patch, just posted, implements the `create_on_disk` callback in the "files" backend, replacing generic directory-creation logic with backend-specific handling. This completes the architectural shift, though a few loose ends remain, such as whether the callback should be renamed for non-filesystem backends (deferred for now) and the safety of downcasting macros in multi-backend setups.

Key participants include Junio (who reviewed the series and signaled intent to merge the first patch independently), Justin Tobler (who approved the changes after technical questions were addressed), and Toon Claes (who provided critical feedback and proposed the alternative initialization design now adopted). The series touches core files like `odb.c`, `setup.c`, and `repository.c`, and introduces new symbols like the `create_on_disk` callback and `safe_create_dir()`. No major objections remain, and the series is poised to land in the current development cycle.
