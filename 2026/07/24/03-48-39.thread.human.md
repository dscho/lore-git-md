This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s five-patch series making Git’s on-disk object database (ODB) structures pluggable, a key step in the broader ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to decouple backend-specific directory creation (like `objects/pack` or `objects/info`) from the core repository setup code, allowing future ODB backends to define their own layouts without modifying Git’s internals. The series also fixes latent bugs in loose-object map loading and removes hardcoded assumptions about initialization order.

The patches are under active review, with Junio C Hamano and Justin Tobler providing the most substantive feedback. Junio’s concerns focus on architectural robustness—particularly the safety of downcasting in multi-backend scenarios and the extensibility of the new `create_on_disk` callback—while Justin has questioned the design of the initialization sequence and the consistency of loose-object map checks. The fifth and final patch, which introduces the `create_on_disk` callback, received surface-level approval from Justin, who noted its alignment with the current files-based layout but mused about whether the callback should be invoked differently. No merge status has been reported, and the series remains in the review phase, with open questions about initialization clarity and multi-backend safety still unresolved. The changes are internal-only, with no on-disk format or user-visible behavior changes.
