This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s six-patch series (v3) to make Git’s on-disk object database (ODB) structures pluggable, part of the larger ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to decouple backend-specific directory creation (like `objects/pack/`) from core repository setup, allowing future ODB backends to define their own layouts without modifying Git’s core logic. The first patch in the series is a correctness fix for loose-object map loading in multi-backend setups (e.g., SHA-256 with SHA-1 compatibility), and Junio has marked it as "will merge to 'next'" in his latest "What's cooking" report. The remaining patches are uncontroversial and ready for merging, with no outstanding technical objections.

The series has seen broad review from Junio, Justin Tobler, and Toon Claes, with most feedback already addressed in v3. However, Toon’s latest reply reopens a key architectural question: whether the initialization sequence should split repository configuration and ODB creation into two explicit steps (his proposed design) or keep them unified (the current approach). His proof-of-concept diff simplifies the API by removing the `APPLY_REPOSITORY_FORMAT_SKIP_ODB_CREATION` flag and making `odb_new()` calls explicit at call sites. This directly challenges the fourth patch in the series, which defers ODB creation to `create_object_database()` to ensure on-disk structures stay in sync. While the current patches are otherwise ready, this design debate could lead to a v4 if Patrick adopts Toon’s simpler API.

The thread remains under active review, with the first patch poised for early graduation to `next` due to its correctness fix, while the rest await resolution of this initialization sequence question. Key participants—Patrick, Junio, Justin, and Toon—have all weighed in, with Patrick’s pragmatic choices (e.g., retaining eager loading of compatibility maps) largely accepted. The only loose ends are minor cleanups and forward-looking design questions, like the future of `repo_read_loose_object_map()` and the `create_on_disk` callback’s extensibility for non-filesystem backends.
