This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s five-patch series (v2) to make Git’s on-disk object database (ODB) structures pluggable, part of the broader ODB abstraction effort. The goal is to decouple backend-specific directory creation (like `objects/pack` and `objects/info`) from the core repository setup code, enabling future ODB backends with custom layouts. The first patch in the series is now a confirmed correctness fix, addressing a latent bug in loose-object map loading that could trigger a BUG in repositories with compatibility hash algorithms and alternates—Toon Claes provided a test case that Patrick incorporated, elevating its priority.

The series is under active review, with v2 addressing feedback from Junio C Hamano and Justin Tobler. The most substantive discussions revolved around the second patch (latent BUG fix) and the third (deferred ODB creation), while the fourth and fifth patches were largely uncontroversial. Junio’s review highlighted architectural concerns, such as the safety of downcasting ODB sources and the naming of the new `create_on_disk` callback, though Patrick deferred the latter for consistency with the ref subsystem. Justin Tobler raised questions about multi-backend consistency and initialization sequence design, but Patrick clarified the pragmatic choices behind the current approach. The fifth patch, just summarized, introduces the `create_on_disk` callback to let backends handle their own directory setup, moving `pack/` and `info/` creation into the "files" backend. It’s ready for merging, with no new objections in v2.

Key participants include Patrick (author), Junio (maintainer), Justin Tobler (substantive reviewer), and Toon Claes (who contributed the test case for the first patch). The series remains under review, with the first patch a candidate for early graduation due to its correctness fix. The rest of the series is a prerequisite for the planned `objectStorage` extension, which will enable non-filesystem ODB backends. Open questions include the long-term design of the initialization sequence and whether the `create_on_disk` callback should be renamed for broader applicability.
