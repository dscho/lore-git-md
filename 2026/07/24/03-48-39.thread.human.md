This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s five-patch series (`v2`) to make Git’s on-disk object database (ODB) structures pluggable, a key step in the broader ODB abstraction effort. The series decouples backend-specific directory creation (like `objects/pack` or `objects/info`) from the core repository setup code, allowing future ODB backends to define their own layouts. The first patch fixes a latent correctness bug in loose-object map loading, ensuring it works correctly in repositories with compatibility hash algorithms (e.g., SHA-256 with SHA-1) and alternates—a fix now validated by a test case from Toon Claes. The rest of the series refactors initialization logic, defers ODB creation, and introduces a `create_on_disk` callback to handle backend-specific setup.

The series has gained momentum in its second iteration. Justin Tobler, who previously raised substantive concerns about downcast safety and initialization design, now calls the updated version "looks good to me," covering all five patches. This endorsement follows Patrick’s incorporation of feedback: the test case for the loose-object map bug, a spelling fix ("in-memory" instead of "inmemory"), and clearer commit messages. Junio Hamano’s earlier review also approved the fourth and fifth patches, though design questions about callback naming and multi-backend consistency remain open. The first patch, now confirmed as a correctness fix, is a strong candidate for early graduation, while the rest of the series is uncontroversial and ready for merging. The series is under active review, with no merge status reported yet.
