This is Junio C Hamano raising a **substantive design concern** about the stacked-branch protection logic in Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series. The patch (v19 5/7) implements an "abort-and-clear" safety mechanism to prevent deleting branches that are used as upstreams for unmerged branches, while clearing stale upstream configs for kept branches. Junio’s question cuts to the heart of whether this logic is **deterministic and name-order-independent**.

The issue: the current implementation uses `refs_for_each_branch_ref()` to iterate over branches in alphabetical order. If three branches form a chain (`a_tip → b_mid → c_lower`), the outcome depends on their names. With names like `a_tip`, `b_mid`, `c_lower`, both `b_mid` and `c_lower` are spared. With names like `tip`, `mid`, `lower`, only `mid` is spared and `lower` is deleted (with `mid`’s upstream cleared). The relationships are identical, but the result differs — a violation of the principle that Git’s behavior should not depend on branch naming.

Junio is not rejecting the patch, but he is flagging a **potential design flaw** that could lead to user confusion or subtle bugs. The concern is serious enough to warrant a rethink before merging, even though the series is otherwise complete and ready. This is a **substantive review** that engages with the algorithmic correctness of the code, not a surface-level nit.
