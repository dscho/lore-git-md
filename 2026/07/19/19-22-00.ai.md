This is Junio C Hamano's reply to Harald Nordgren's proposed fix for the **order dependency bug** in the stacked-branch protection logic of `git branch --delete-merged`. The patch under discussion is part of the v19 series (5/7), which implements the core command.

Junio acknowledges Harald's minimal fix (adding a check for `data->spared` in `spare_stacked_base()`) but expresses skepticism about its completeness. He notes that the example he provided (two equivalent dependency chains with different branch names) may not cover all edge cases arising from the sequential, order-dependent traversal of the dependency graph. His concern is that the current approach—processing branches one by one and marking their upstreams as "spared"—could still produce inconsistent results for more complex dependency structures, even with the fix.

Junio speculates that a **multi-pass algorithm** (iteratively identifying all branches transitively needed by surviving branches) might be necessary to fully eliminate order dependency, though he admits this is an unproven hunch. He does not demand such a rewrite, but his tone suggests the current fix may be insufficient for a robust solution.

This email does not reject the fix outright but signals that Junio remains unconvinced. The series remains blocked until he is satisfied with the correctness of the stacked-branch protection logic. The earlier agreement to extend this protection to `git branch -d` as a follow-up is unaffected, but the immediate priority is resolving the order dependency issue. If Harald can demonstrate the fix works for broader test cases (or Junio is persuaded by further analysis), the series could proceed; otherwise, a more invasive redesign may be required.
