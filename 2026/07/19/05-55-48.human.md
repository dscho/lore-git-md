This is a minor but substantive follow-up in the ongoing discussion about René Scharfe’s patch to improve `git branch -d` error messages. The patch itself aims to clarify why deleting the current branch is rejected by naming the specific operation (e.g., "for bisect" or "for rebase") instead of a generic "used by worktree" message.

The email focuses on a narrow implementation detail: whether the `kind` variable in `branch.c` should be declared as an `enum branch_checkout_kind` or an `int`. Junio initially questioned René’s use of `int`, noting that enums provide symbolic output in debuggers like GDB, which aids troubleshooting. René responds that he chose `int` for simplicity, as it made printing easier without requiring explicit type conversion. However, he concedes that the conversion rule for enums is straightforward and that Junio’s example—where both `int` and `enum` can be printed identically—demonstrates that the choice doesn’t affect functionality.

This exchange doesn’t resolve the larger open questions in the thread, such as whether to merge the `BRANCH_CHECKOUT_KIND_UPDATE_REF` case (used during rebase with merges) into the "rebase" category for simpler error messages or to keep it distinct for precision. Nor does it address the missing test coverage for all checkout kinds. The discussion remains technical and collaborative, with no clear winner yet—just a shared understanding that the enum vs. `int` choice is less consequential than initially thought. A v2 patch may incorporate this detail, but the broader design decisions are still pending.
