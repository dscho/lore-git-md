This is a review reply from Jeff King (Peff) on brian m. carlson’s two-patch Rust hash cleanup series. The series fixes memory leaks and uninitialized memory risks in the Rust bindings for Git’s C hash functions, specifically when non-default block algorithms (like OpenSSL) are used.

Peff confirms both patches look good to him, though he notes his limited familiarity with Rust. He initially worried his recent C-side leak fixes might have introduced new issues, but concludes these are pre-existing problems that mirror the C-side cleanup he already did. For the first patch, he briefly considers an alternative approach (making `git_hash_clone()` not require initialization) but dismisses it as more effort than it’s worth and potentially riskier, given the tiny performance gain. He endorses the current approach as sensible and consistent with the C code’s design.

This is a substantive review: Peff engages with the technical trade-offs and confirms the fixes address real, pre-existing issues. His feedback is positive and the series now has a clear path to being merged once any minor Rust-specific adjustments are made. The patches remain confined to `src/hash.rs` and introduce no behavior changes, keeping the scope narrow and uncontroversial.
