This is a **substantive review** from Junio C Hamano on the core stacked-branch protection logic in Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series (patch 5/7 of v19). Junio identifies a **potential correctness issue** in the `spare_stacked_bases()` implementation: the algorithm’s behavior depends on the **alphabetical order** in which branches are visited by `refs_for_each_branch_ref()`, rather than the logical dependency chain between branches.

The concern is illustrated with two equivalent dependency chains (`a_tip→b_mid→c_lower` vs. `tip→mid→lower`), where the outcome (which branches are spared or have their upstream cleared) differs solely due to branch naming. This violates the principle that identical branch relationships should produce identical results, regardless of naming. While the order is deterministic (alphabetical), it is arbitrary with respect to the actual dependency structure, making the behavior unintuitive and potentially confusing for users.

The review does not propose a fix but flags the issue as a **design flaw** that could undermine the safety guarantees of the stacked-branch protection mechanism. This is a **blocking concern** for the series, as the protection logic is central to the feature’s safety claims. The thread’s earlier consensus on the "abort-and-clear" design (spare branches used as upstreams for unmerged branches, clear stale upstreams for kept branches) remains valid, but the implementation must now be revised to ensure **order-independent results**.

Key technical details to track in follow-up discussion:
- The `spare_stacked_bases()` function and its callback `spare_stacked_base()`.
- The use of `refs_for_each_branch_ref()` (alphabetical iteration) vs. a dependency-aware traversal.
- The `deletable` and `spared` strsets, which track branches to delete and those to spare/clean.
- The interaction with the rest of the deletion pipeline (e.g., `delete_merged_branches()`).

This is a **high-priority issue** for the series, as it affects the core safety mechanism. The fix will likely require reworking the traversal logic to process branches in dependency order (e.g., topological sort) rather than alphabetical order.
