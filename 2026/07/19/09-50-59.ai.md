This is a substantive review reply in the ongoing discussion about improving `git branch -d` error messages. Phillip Wood weighs in on the unresolved design question: whether to merge the `BRANCH_CHECKOUT_KIND_UPDATE_REF` case (used during rebase with merges) into the generic "rebase" category for simplicity, or to keep it distinct for clarity.

Phillip raises a concrete usability concern: if the error message says the branch is "being rebased" but `git status` in the worktree shows a different branch name, users may be confused. He proposes two solutions: either enhance `git status` to show all branches being rewritten (preferred but more work), or craft a distinct error message for the `UPDATE_REF` case that clarifies multiple branches are being updated. His example message ("because it is being updated by a rebase running in '../feature' which is updating multiple branches") aims to bridge the gap between technical accuracy and user comprehension.

The reply also includes a minor endorsement of Junio’s earlier suggestion to tweak the `BRANCH_CHECKOUT_KIND_CHECKOUT` message to say "checked out" instead of "used by worktree," which René had not yet addressed. Phillip’s tone is constructive, framing the discussion as a usability improvement rather than a technical debate. No new implementation is proposed here, but the feedback adds a user-experience perspective that was missing from the prior exchange between René and Junio. The thread remains open, with the `UPDATE_REF` message design still unresolved.
