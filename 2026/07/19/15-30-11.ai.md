This is a **bugfix follow-up** to Harald Nordgren’s `git branch --delete-merged` series, addressing the **blocking correctness issue** in the stacked-branch protection logic identified by Junio C Hamano. The patch targets the `spare_stacked_bases()` function in patch 5/7 of v19, where the algorithm’s behavior was shown to depend on the alphabetical order of branch names rather than their logical dependency chain.

Harald proposes a **minimal fix** to the `spare_stacked_base()` callback: checking both the `deletable` and `spared` strsets before deciding whether to spare a branch. The change ensures that a branch already marked as "spared" (because it is used as an upstream for an unmerged branch) is not incorrectly re-evaluated based on its name’s alphabetical position. This should eliminate the order dependency Junio highlighted, as branches spared earlier in the traversal will now remain spared regardless of subsequent alphabetical ordering.

The accompanying test (not shown in the email but implied by the context) verifies the fix using Junio’s example of two equivalent dependency chains (`a_tip→b_mid→c_lower` vs. `tip→mid→lower`). The test likely confirms that the outcome is now consistent across naming variations, though the exact assertions are not detailed here.

This is a **critical correctness fix** for the series, as the stacked-branch protection is central to the feature’s safety guarantees. The fix is narrowly scoped to the protection logic and does not affect other aspects of the series (e.g., per-branch opt-out, `--dry-run`, or the `--forked` filter). If this change resolves the order dependency, it will unblock the series for merging, though Junio’s review will be needed to confirm the fix’s sufficiency. The earlier agreement to extend this protection to `git branch -d` as a follow-up remains unchanged.
