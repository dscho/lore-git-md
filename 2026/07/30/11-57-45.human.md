This is a **build system** follow-up in the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES=1` test-failure thread. Jeff King (Peff) replies to Junio C Hamano’s review of brian m. carlson’s recently merged patch, which moved the inclusion of `config.mak` earlier in the Makefile to ensure `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` is respected during initial build target generation.

Peff acknowledges Junio’s concern that the patch exposes many Makefile symbols (like `PROGRAM_OBJS`, `BUILT_INS`, and platform-specific variables) to `config.mak` earlier than before, but notes that some of these variables are already manipulable after the fact. More critically, he identifies a **serious flaw** in the current Makefile logic: the `NO_RUST` and `uname_S` checks (which determine `RUST_LIB_NAME` and `RUST_BUILD_CONFIG`) appear *before* `config.mak` is included, meaning they cannot be overridden by `config.mak` settings. This is the same class of bug that brian’s patch fixed for `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES`, but the fix does not address it. Peff suggests that the `NO_RUST` check may be redundant (since unused flags do no harm), but the `uname_S` issue could cause real problems on Windows builds if `config.mak` tries to override the platform-specific library name.

The tone is diagnostic and solution-oriented, but the observation is sharp: the Makefile’s current structure is inconsistent and fragile. The patch that was merged to fix `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` does not resolve these related issues, and Peff’s reply implies that a more comprehensive or targeted fix may be needed. This is not a call to revert brian’s patch—it is already in `master`—but a warning that the build system still has latent bugs that could surface as `config.mak` usage grows, especially in the run-up to Git 3.0. The thread is no longer about test failures, but about build system correctness.
