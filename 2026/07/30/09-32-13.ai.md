The first issue is **`branch_pushes_to_upstream()` correctness**. The current implementation checks whether the branch’s *fetch* refspec maps to its upstream, but it should instead **apply the *push* refspec to the local branch, then apply the *fetch* refspec to the result, and compare that to the upstream branch**. Phillip clarifies the correct sequence (push → fetch, not fetch in reverse) and provides a concrete example: if `origin` uses `main` locally but `master` remotely, the push refspec `refs/heads/main:refs/heads/master` and fetch refspec `refs/heads/master:refs/remotes/origin/main` must both be applied to determine whether pushing the branch would update its upstream. The current logic risks **false negatives** (deleting branches like `main` that track `origin/main`) or **false positives** (keeping branches that should be deleted). Harald acknowledges this will require re-adding some of the push logic removed earlier for simplicity, but the fix is critical for safety.

The second issue is a **test discrepancy in the stacked-branch protection logic**. The test for "keeps the upstream chain of a surviving branch" expects the upstream config of `lower` (a kept merged branch) to remain intact, but the design goal is to **clear stale configs for kept (merged) branches whose own upstream is being deleted**. Phillip questions why the whole chain is preserved rather than just the upstream of the unmerged branch, noting this is a **behavior change from earlier versions** that wasn’t clearly justified in the cover letter. Harald’s reply confirms the intent is to preserve the upstream chain of surviving *tips*, but the test expectation contradicts this. Resolution requires either **fixing the logic to match the test** (preserving upstream configs for kept merged branches) or **updating the test to match the design** (clearing stale configs).
