This thread introduces a new `--date=elapsed` (and `--date=elapsed-pad`) date format for Git, proposed by Israel Roldan. The feature fills a gap between Git's existing `--date=human` (which gives imprecise phrases like "2 days ago") and raw timestamps, offering a precise, script-friendly breakdown of elapsed time (e.g., "1y 11M 5d 13h 5m 13s") modeled after `uptime(1)`. The patch is well-structured, adding the new format to `date.c`, exposing it via pretty-format placeholders (`%aX` and `%cX`), and including thorough documentation and tests.

The implementation is clean and handles edge cases like future timestamps (clamped to "0s"). The author has flagged four minor open questions—placeholder letter choice, format naming, future timestamp behavior, and symmetry of padded placeholders—but none block the patch's core functionality. The patch is ready for review and appears to be a strong candidate for merging, as it is self-contained and unlikely to introduce regressions.
