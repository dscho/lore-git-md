The series aims to reject time-based stash selectors (e.g., `stash@{2.days.ago}`) in those subcommands to prevent accidental removal of more stash entries than the user expects.

Junio clarifies the purpose of the hard-coded limit (`100000000`, or 1e8) in the `strtoul` conversion of the `@{n}` index. The limit mirrors the behavior of Git’s `approxidate` function, which interprets very large integers as timestamps (number-of-seconds-since-epoch) rather than positional indices. By capping the valid range for `stash@{n}`, the patch ensures that users cannot accidentally trigger time-based resolution when they intend to reference a stash by its numeric position. This aligns with the series’ goal of making `drop` and `pop` safer by rejecting ambiguous selectors.

The limit is not an arbitrary constraint but a deliberate design choice to avoid edge-case confusion between positional and time-based references.
