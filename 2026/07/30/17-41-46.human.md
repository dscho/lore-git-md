This is the second iteration of a feature patch series introducing `git repack --drop-filtered` to reclaim disk space in partial clones. The series is now ready for serious review after addressing the blocking issues identified in the RFC v1.

The core idea remains unchanged: partial clones accumulate locally cached promisor blobs over time, and there is no safe built-in way to remove them. This series adds that capability by letting users specify a size threshold (e.g., `--filter=blob:limit=10M`) and safely dropping blobs that exceed it. The implementation guarantees safety by only targeting objects marked as promisor (ensuring they can be re-fetched) and by adding explicit safety guards to prevent drops during merges, rebases, or when blobs are referenced by the current index.

Key improvements since v1 include fixing the critical validation flaw around `--write-bitmap-index` (now distinguishing between user-requested bitmaps and config-driven ones), implementing the promised safety guards, reorganizing function signatures, and distributing tests across the relevant commits. The drop log is still deferred to a follow-up, but the core functionality is now complete and well-tested. The series touches the repack machinery, promisor pack rebuilding logic, and the list-objects-filter API, and introduces a new test script (`t/t7706-repack-drop-filtered.sh`).

The author has clearly engaged with the feedback from Junio and Siddharth Asthana, and the result is a series that looks production-ready in terms of both design and implementation. The commit messages are thorough, the test coverage is comprehensive, and the safety mechanisms appear robust. This is a well-motivated feature for partial clone users and a solid contribution from a first-time contributor.
