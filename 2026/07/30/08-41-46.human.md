This is a **feature addition** introducing `git add --resolved`, part of a four-patch series that consolidates conflict-marker detection logic and adds helpers for safer index manipulation. The series is technically complete and addresses all prior review feedback, but the discussion has now shifted to the user-facing interface.

Junio C Hamano responds to Michael Montalbo’s critique of the `--resolved` option name, defending it as intentional and workflow-oriented. The name mirrors `git am --resolved`, framing the command as a way to *record* that conflicts have been dealt with, not merely to stage unmerged paths. Junio acknowledges that the current implementation could later support policy overrides like `--allow-conflict-markers` but argues that such cases would be rare and better handled by manually staging exceptions with `git add $path`. He dismisses the alternative `--unmerged` name as a regression, emphasizing that the command’s purpose is to mark conflicts as resolved, not to select unmerged paths in general.

The exchange highlights a design tension: Montalbo’s proposal decouples selection (unmerged paths) from policy (conflict-marker checks), while Junio’s approach treats them as a single, workflow-aligned operation. Neither side has budged, but the discussion remains civil and focused on user experience. The series itself is well-crafted, with thorough test coverage and performance optimizations, but the naming debate could delay final acceptance if reviewers remain divided.
