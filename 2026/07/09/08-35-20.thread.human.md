This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s eight-patch series (v2) to refactor `git cat-file --batch-all-objects` so it can work with pluggable object databases, a key step in Git’s ongoing ODB abstraction effort. The series removes the command’s tight coupling to the "packed" backend by introducing object filters to `odb_for_each_object()`, allowing backends to optimize enumeration (e.g., via bitmaps) while keeping the filtering logic abstracted.

The final patch (8/8) landed in this update, completing the series by replacing 69 lines of backend-specific bitmap handling in `git cat-file` with 7 lines of ODB-mediated filtering. Junio C Hamano reviewed the prior version (7/7 in v1) and praised the implementation, requesting only a clearer commit message to explain the problem being solved—specifically, that the old code reached into ODB internals and manually managed bitmaps. Patrick incorporated this feedback in v2, along with all other review comments from Justin Tobler, who had raised questions about edge cases and design choices in earlier patches. All open questions have been resolved, including a design discussion about whether backends should be required to support a subset of filters (the resolution: they may ignore filters, consistent with Git’s existing patterns).

The series is now under final review, with no remaining technical objections. The changes touch the ODB, packed backend, and pack-bitmap subsystems, introducing new functions like `open_bitmap_for_source()` and `can_filter_bitmap()`, while dropping redundant helper functions and simplifying the codebase. The on-disk format is unchanged, and no end-user behavioral changes are expected. The series builds on Patrick’s earlier `ps/odb-drop-whence` topic and is part of his broader ODB abstraction work. With Junio’s supportive review and all feedback addressed, the series appears ready for merging once minor adjustments to the final patch are made.
