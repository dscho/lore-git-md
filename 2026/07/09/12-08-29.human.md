This is the second iteration of a two-patch bugfix series targeting a quadratic performance regression in the reftable backend. The issue occurs when re-creating refs that were previously deleted (leaving tombstones), causing operations like `update-ref` to scan all tombstones before bounds checks can terminate iteration. The fix moves tombstone handling from the merged iterator to the reftable backend’s call sites, where prefix and refname bounds are available, enabling early termination and reducing runtime from ~14s to ~0.2s in the perf test’s 8000-ref scenarios.

The v2 revision addresses downstream compatibility with libgit2 by preserving the `suppress_deletions` flag in the reftable library API while removing its use from Git’s internal `stack.c`. This ensures libgit2 can continue using the flag without affecting Git’s performance. The series also broadens the scope description to clarify that the regression affects all readers (not just ref creation) and improves the perf test by using separate repositories to avoid cross-scenario state contamination. The correctness test from v1 was dropped after consensus that its coverage is already implicit in `t1400-update-ref.sh`.

The core logic remains unchanged: tombstones are now returned to callers, which skip them after bounds checks, allowing iteration to terminate early. The change is narrowly scoped to the reftable backend, touching `refs/reftable-backend.c`, `reftable/merged.{c,h}`, and `reftable/stack.c`. The perf test (`p1401-ref-store-tombstones.sh`) exercises two scenarios: recreating the same refs after deletion and creating new, differently-named refs after deletion (the latter being the worst-case for tombstone sorting).

The series is well-motivated, with real-world relevance confirmed by brian m. carlson’s report of encountering the regression in production-scale repositories. The fix is technically sound, preserving the on-disk format and avoiding broader refactoring. Review feedback from v1 has been incorporated, and the patch appears ready for further review. No controversial design choices are evident, though the suppression logic’s removal could theoretically affect other callers (none are mentioned beyond libgit2). The performance improvement is dramatic and the test coverage is thorough.
