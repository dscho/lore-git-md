This thread is about a patch series by Domen Kožar that introduces three new Git hooks—`post-worktree-add`, `post-worktree-remove`, and `post-worktree-move`—to automate actions triggered by worktree lifecycle events. The goal is to support high-velocity tooling, particularly AI-driven development environments that frequently create and destroy worktrees. The series is now at version 2, with changes including unconditional execution of `post-worktree-add` (even if `post-checkout` fails), simplified hook arguments, and the addition of `post-worktree-move`.

The series has been under review, with Phillip Wood providing substantive feedback on design consistency and execution order, while Caleb White offered surface-level suggestions. However, Junio C Hamano has now raised a fundamental question: whether these hooks are necessary at all. He argues that most of the proposed functionality can already be achieved by wrapping `git worktree` commands in shell scripts, and that hooks are only justified for use cases that require Git’s internal state, locks, or remote access—none of which apply here. This critique challenges the series’ premise and may shift the discussion toward whether the feature belongs in Git at all, rather than its implementation details. The thread is now at a crossroads, with the maintainer’s skepticism casting doubt on the series’ future.
