This thread introduces a patch series by Domen Kožar that adds `post-worktree-add` and `post-worktree-remove` hooks to Git, aimed at supporting automation tools—especially AI-driven development environments—that frequently create and destroy worktrees. The series is in its first version and currently under review.

Phillip Wood’s review is the first substantive feedback on the series. He agrees with the core motivation, noting that the new hooks fill a real gap for tooling managing ephemeral worktrees. On design specifics, he endorses the decision to skip `post-worktree-add` if `post-checkout` fails (since Git removes the worktree in that case), but questions the usefulness of passing the worktree’s absolute directory and ID to the hook when it already runs inside the worktree. For `post-worktree-remove`, he points out that the hook receives an empty string when the gitdir file is missing or corrupt, leaving it unaware of which worktree was removed. He also flags an undocumented gap: the relative order of `post-worktree-add` and `post-checkout` should be clarified in the documentation. His feedback is technical and constructive, leaving room for Domen to refine the approach. No decisions or approvals have been made yet.
