This is a **contributor housekeeping** patch series follow-up, specifically the author’s response to Colin Stagner’s surface-level review of the `git-subtree` safeguard (v1 1/2 and 2/2). Ian Jackson accepts the scoping fix (`$1` instead of global `rev`) and the error-message formatting decision from Junio, but pushes back on two of Colin’s minor points and engages with the test-style suggestions.

The substantive news is that Ian will adopt the `test_commit` helper and orphan-branch pattern for the test script, making the new test case (`t/t7900-subtree.sh`) consistent with the rest of the subtree test suite. On the portability front, he declines to remove the existing `local` declarations, arguing that the risk of shell incompatibility is negligible and that globally unique variable names would be more error-prone. He also confirms that the detection logic should **not** look for `.git-subtree/config` inside the subtree being split—only at the top level of the downstream repository—because the decision to use the new algorithm depends on the downstream’s adoption of the Rust rewrite, not on any upstream metadata.

Finally, Ian clarifies the design intent of the forthcoming Rust rewrite: it will **not** support unmarked subtree merges (those performed manually with `git merge -X subtree`), which he considers unreliable. He plans to document this limitation and add a test case for the scenario, but does not intend to prevent or detect it at runtime.
