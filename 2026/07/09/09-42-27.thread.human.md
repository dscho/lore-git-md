This thread is a bugfix series from Johannes Schindelin (Dscho) addressing eleven NULL-dereference vulnerabilities identified by Coverity across various Git subsystems. The series is uncontroversial in its goal—preventing crashes in edge cases—but the third patch, which guards against a NULL `remote` in `remote_tracking()`, has sparked a philosophical debate.

Dscho’s approach treats the NULL case as a recoverable error, returning `-1` to signal "no tracking ref available." Junio C Hamano, however, argues that the function’s contract forbids NULL inputs entirely, and silently returning `-1` masks a programming error. He proposes using `BUG()` instead, making violations immediately visible. The disagreement is narrow but significant: it hinges on whether defensive programming should prioritize robustness (Dscho) or fail-fast clarity (Junio). The patch will need a v3 update to resolve this.

The first two patches (fixing `diffcore-break.c` and `diff.c`) have already been approved by Junio, who called them "obviously correct" and noted the former addressed a latent bug present for over two decades. The final patch (shallow.c) also requires a v3 to incorporate Junio’s suggested fix for a separate latent issue: the unsafe use of `oid_to_hex()`’s static buffer in verbose logging. No other objections or open questions remain, and the series is otherwise ready for integration once these adjustments are made.
