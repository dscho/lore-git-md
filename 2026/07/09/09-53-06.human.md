This is a **follow-up discussion** in the now-merged `git refs` series, focusing on the usability of the `--no-deref` option in the new `git refs create` subcommand. The exchange between Toon Claes and Patrick Steinhardt clarifies how `--no-deref` behaves and why it exists, though it leaves open questions about its discoverability and intuitiveness.

Toon raises concerns about `--no-deref`'s obscurity, noting that its purpose—failing if the target reference exists as a symbolic ref (symref)—is not obvious from the documentation or option name. For example, if `refs/heads/symref` points to `refs/heads/target`, `git refs create refs/heads/symref $OID` would create `refs/heads/target` (dereferencing the symref), while `--no-deref` would fail instead. Patrick confirms this behavior, emphasizing that the option enforces atomicity (preventing accidental overwrites) rather than enabling symref creation. He also acknowledges the option’s awkwardness but defends its consistency with other `git refs` subcommands, suggesting that changing the default would require a broader overhaul.

The discussion highlights a tension between atomicity guarantees and CLI ergonomics. Toon’s proposed test for symref creation with `--no-deref` is rejected, as the option’s role is to *prevent* creation when a symref exists, not to create symrefs. The exchange also clarifies that `git refs create` intentionally rejects duplicate creations, even with the same value, to enforce "must not exist" semantics. While the series is already merged, the feedback may inform future refinements to the `git refs` interface, particularly around `--no-deref`’s discoverability and symref handling. No code changes are proposed or expected.
