This is the second patch in a two-patch bugfix series that restores performance in split commit-graph chains. The series targets a regression introduced in September 2025 by commit `199d452758` (`ps/commit-graph-via-source`), which caused incremental commit-graph writes to slow down from 233ms to 4.1 seconds on large repositories. The root cause is a one-character loop variable mismatch (`g` instead of `chain`) that prevented the `topo_levels` slab from being propagated to all layers of the split commit-graph chain, forcing unnecessary DFS re-walks of commit ancestry.

Patrick Steinhardt’s reply is a brief, substantive review: he confirms the bug is real ("embarrassing"), approves the fix, and implicitly endorses the test update. His feedback is minimal but carries weight—he is a domain expert in the commit-graph subsystem and a frequent contributor to its refactoring. The absence of further questions or caveats suggests the patch is technically sound and ready for integration.

The patch itself is a one-line change in `commit-graph.c` that aligns the loop variable with the rest of the refactoring, ensuring the `topo_levels` slab is attached to every layer in the chain. The accompanying test update in `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh` removes a BUG comment and flips the expected DFS step count from 7 to 1, verifying the fix. The commit message (in the parent email) is thorough, quantifying the performance impact and explaining the mechanism clearly.

This is a well-scoped, uncontroversial fix for a real-world regression. The series is marked as "Expecting a reroll" by Junio C Hamano solely due to a minor commit-message date correction (April → September 2025); once that is addressed, the topic is ready for merge. Users with `fetch.writeCommitGraph` enabled or those running `git maintenance` after fetch will see immediate performance benefits.
