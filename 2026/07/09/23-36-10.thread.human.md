This thread introduces a new `post-worktree-remove` hook, proposed by Domen Kožar (with AI co-authorship) as the second patch in a three-patch series. The hook addresses a gap in Git's worktree management: external tools currently have no reliable way to detect when a worktree is removed. It runs after `git worktree remove` deletes the worktree's administrative entry, providing the removed worktree's path and identifier as arguments, and its exit status is reflected in the command's overall exit code—though it cannot alter the removal outcome.

The implementation is straightforward, touching `builtin/worktree.c` to invoke the hook, updating the hooks documentation, and adding thorough tests covering basic invocation, edge cases (like missing worktrees), and failure scenarios. The design follows Git's established pattern for post-command hooks, such as `post-checkout` and `post-worktree-add`, and the patch is narrowly scoped to avoid side effects. No prior discussion of this specific hook is mentioned, but it fits naturally into Git's existing hook ecosystem. The series is at its first version (v1), and no reviews or objections have surfaced yet.
