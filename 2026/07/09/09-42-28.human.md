This is the first patch in Johannes Schindelin’s eleven-patch bugfix series addressing NULL-dereference issues identified by Coverity. It targets the `diffcore-break.c` file, specifically the `diffcore_merge_broken()` function, where an inner loop can dereference a NULL pointer if it encounters a queue entry that was set to NULL by a previous iteration of the outer loop.

The fix is minimal and surgical: it adds a NULL check (`if (!pp) continue`) to the inner loop, mirroring an existing guard in the outer loop. The scenario that triggers this issue is rare but plausible—requiring at least two broken pairs whose peers survive rename/copy detection and appear later in the queue. The commit message explains the mechanics clearly, and the patch itself is just two lines of code, making it easy to verify.

This is a classic defensive programming fix: no new behavior is introduced, and the change is unlikely to break existing functionality. Given Dscho’s track record and the straightforward nature of the fix, this patch looks uncontroversial and well-justified. It’s part of a broader effort to harden Git’s codebase against NULL-dereference vulnerabilities, and the series as a whole appears to be a direct follow-up to previously merged Coverity fixes.
