This is a **substantive review** of the ongoing bugfix series for a critical memory-safety issue in `git worktree add`. René Scharfe joins the thread to analyze the root cause and propose a broader refactoring of `worktree_basename()` to eliminate the out-of-bounds read risk entirely.

René’s review confirms Junio’s earlier observation that the current patch (v1 1/2) is insufficient: it still allows pointer underflow when `path` consists only of separators (e.g., `"///"`). He goes further by mapping how the bug manifests differently across the function’s three callers. In `add_worktree()`, the underflow leads to `strbuf_add()` attempting to allocate an enormous buffer (due to negative length wrapping to `SIZE_MAX`), while in `dwim_branch()` and `add()`, `xstrndup()` with a zero length is technically safe but still relies on undefined behavior. René suggests that **patch 2 alone (rejecting empty strings) would prevent the immediate out-of-bounds read**, but argues the function’s design is fundamentally flawed: it returns a pointer and a length that don’t match, forcing callers to perform error-prone pointer arithmetic.

His proposed solution is to **refactor `worktree_basename()` to return the basename’s length directly**, eliminating the need for callers to compute `path + len - name`. This would make the function harder to misuse and remove the risk of underflow entirely. He also questions whether trailing separators should be preserved at all, noting that `xstrndup("bar/", 7)` could theoretically read out of bounds (though in practice, `memchr()` stops at the first separator). The review is technically precise, referencing specific callers, edge cases, and the internal behavior of `xstrndup()` and `memchr()`.

The feedback implies that the series may need a **v2 with a more comprehensive fix** rather than a minimal band-aid. While René doesn’t explicitly reject the current approach, his analysis suggests that the function’s interface is the real problem, and a deeper refactoring would be more robust.
