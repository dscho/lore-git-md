This is Junio’s reply to the v2 RFC series introducing `git repack --drop-filtered` for partial clones. The series, now complete at seven patches, aims to reclaim disk space by safely removing locally cached promisor blobs that exceed a size threshold.

Junio’s questions focus on three areas. First, he probes the reliability of the promisor-object enumeration (`ODB_FOR_EACH_OBJECT_PROMISOR_ONLY`), asking whether the “by construction” guarantee holds in practice and suggesting that feedback from other partial-clone users would be valuable. Second, he confirms that the promisor-pack rebuild step uses only locally available data, avoiding lazy fetches that would undermine the space-reclamation goal. Finally, he raises a design question about future extensibility: while the current implementation filters only by size, he wonders what other criteria (e.g., age, access frequency) might be useful and whether the framework can accommodate them without requiring a rewrite.

On the safety guards—blocking drops during merges, rebases, or when blobs are referenced by the current index—Junio seeks clarification. He distinguishes between a race condition (which he thinks is unlikely) and a scenario where a user pauses an operation, runs `git repack --drop-filtered`, and then resumes. In either case, he notes that lazy refetching should automatically restore any dropped blobs, making the safeguards seem redundant. His tone is curious rather than dismissive, leaving room for the author to explain the rationale behind the current design.
