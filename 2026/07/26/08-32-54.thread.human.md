This thread is about a patch series from Jeff King (Peff) that proposes bumping the Ubuntu image used in Git’s static-analysis CI jobs from `ubuntu-22.04` to `ubuntu-latest` (now `ubuntu-24.04`) to pick up a newer Coccinelle version (1.3.0+), with the expectation of improved performance. The series also includes a preparatory cleanup in `bloom.c` to silence a Coccinelle false positive triggered by an assertion macro under GCC 15.

The patches are under review, with Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt initially signaling approval in principle, and Elijah Newren endorsing the change. However, SZEDER Gábor’s review introduced a major caveat: benchmarks show Coccinelle 1.3.1 is **4.5× slower** than 1.1.1, directly contradicting the performance rationale for the image bump. This has led to broader questions about the value of Coccinelle rules in the project. Patrick Steinhardt later questioned whether the project should continue writing Coccinelle rules for API migrations, while Junio countered by pointing to their utility in enforcing coding conventions (e.g., catching `if (a == NULL)` instead of `if (!a)`). The thread remains unresolved, with open questions about whether to proceed with the image bump despite the performance regression, how to address inefficiencies in the CI job setup, and whether to audit or prune Coccinelle rules. No merge status has been reported.
