This thread is about a small CI update proposed by Jeff King (Peff) to bump the Ubuntu image used in Git’s static-analysis jobs from `ubuntu-22.04` to `ubuntu-latest` (resolving to `ubuntu-24.04`), with the goal of picking up a newer Coccinelle version for better performance. The series also includes a preparatory cleanup in `bloom.c` to silence a Coccinelle false positive triggered by an assertion macro under GCC 15.

The proposal has broad approval in principle from Junio C Hamano, Patrick Steinhardt, and Elijah Newren, but its performance rationale is now contested. SZEDER Gábor’s benchmarks show Coccinelle 1.3.1 as 4.5× slower than 1.1.1, directly contradicting the expected improvement. The discussion has since shifted to clarifying Coccinelle’s role in the project. Peff and Patrick Steinhardt now agree that Coccinelle should focus on *permanent* coding convention enforcement (e.g., catching `if (a == NULL)`) rather than *temporary* API migrations, which they view as not worth the maintenance overhead. Junio supports this distinction, while Patrick has also highlighted inefficiencies in the CI setup—unnecessary build dependencies in static-analysis and documentation jobs—that could be addressed separately.

The series remains under review, with no merge status reported. Open questions include whether to proceed with the Ubuntu image bump despite the performance regression, how to address the CI inefficiencies, and whether to audit and prune existing Coccinelle rules to reflect the new consensus on their scope. The thread has also surfaced broader questions about Coccinelle’s long-term utility and how to manage its operational overhead.
