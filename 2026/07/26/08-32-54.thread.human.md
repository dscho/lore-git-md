This thread is about updating the Ubuntu image used in Git’s static-analysis CI jobs from `ubuntu-22.04` to `ubuntu-latest` (resolving to `ubuntu-24.04`), primarily to pick up a newer Coccinelle version (1.3.0+) for improved performance. The series, posted by Jeff King (Peff), also includes a preparatory cleanup in `bloom.c` to silence a Coccinelle false positive triggered by an assertion macro under GCC 15.

The original rationale for the image bump—better Coccinelle performance—has been **challenged by SZEDER Gábor’s benchmarks**, which show Coccinelle 1.3.1 as **4.5× slower** than 1.1.1 on the same Git revision. Despite this regression, Peff has accepted the trade-off, arguing that **long-term maintainability** (avoiding obsolete or unsupported toolchain versions) outweighs the performance cost. The project has also clarified its stance on Coccinelle’s role: **temporary API-migration rules** (e.g., helping in-flight patches adapt to interface changes) are no longer considered worth the effort, while **permanent antipattern-enforcement rules** (e.g., catching `if (a == NULL)` or enforcing `oidclr()` over `hashclr()`) remain supported by Junio, Patrick Steinhardt, and Peff.

Key participants include Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt, who have signaled approval in principle for the series, and Elijah Newren, who endorsed the change. Gábor’s substantive review also identified an inefficiency in the CI setup—static-analysis and documentation jobs unnecessarily install full build dependencies (compiler, Apache, p4, jgit, etc.)—which Peff acknowledged as "a little nuts" but left unaddressed for now. Open questions remain about whether to proceed with the image bump as-is, optimize CI workflows, or audit and prune Coccinelle rules to separate temporary migration aids from permanent enforcement rules. The series is still under review, with no merge status reported.
