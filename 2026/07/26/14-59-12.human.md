This is Junio C Hamano's follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the `git mv` bugfix series. The focus remains on the second patch, which adds an early check for the destination directory's existence. Junio clarifies his earlier objection to using `stat()` (which follows symlinks) instead of `lstat()` (which does not). He argues that Git should never follow symlinks in the working tree, regardless of whether they point outside the working tree, because Git tracks symlinks as first-class objects. The example he gives—`git mv x y` where `y` is a symlink—should fail just as it would if `y` were a regular file, since the destination is "taken." He also raises a more complex scenario: `git mv x a/b/c` where `a` is a symlink pointing to `.` (the current directory). Here, he insists the command should refuse to follow the symlink and error out, rather than treating `a/b/c` as equivalent to `b/c`. This reinforces his position that `lstat()` is the correct choice, as it avoids traversing symlinks entirely. The patch is not yet ready for integration, and the symlink-handling strategy remains unresolved.
