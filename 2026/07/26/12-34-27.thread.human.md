This thread began as an attempt by Hardik Kumar to change the return type of Git’s UTF-8 string-width functions (`utf8_strwidth()` and `utf8_strnwidth()`) from `int` to `size_t`, arguing that the functions compute inherently non-negative values. After three iterations (v1–v3), the series was withdrawn in the face of sustained opposition from Junio C Hamano, Pablo Sabater, and Phillip Wood, who all concluded that the risks (regressions, complexity, and pervasive casts) outweighed the theoretical benefits. The thread has now concluded with a documentation patch that replaces the existing TODO comment in `utf8.c` with a clear rationale for the status quo.

The final patch, authored by Hardik Kumar, explains why the functions return `int`—to align with APIs like `printf()` that require `int` for padding calculations—and why the `cast_size_t_to_int()` helper is necessary to prevent overflows. Phillip Wood’s latest review is the last substantive feedback: he approves the direction but asks for two refinements. First, the commit message should explicitly state that `cast_size_t_to_int()` was introduced to fix a crash caused by implicit `size_t`→`int` conversion (citing commit 937b7c8b). Second, the in-code comment should make this overflow-prevention role unmistakable. Once these tweaks are made, the patch will be ready to merge, closing the thread with no further technical discussion expected.
