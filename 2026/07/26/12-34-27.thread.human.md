This thread began as a refactoring effort by Hardik Kumar to change the return type of Git’s UTF-8 string-width functions (`utf8_strwidth()` and `utf8_strnwidth()`) from `int` to `size_t`, aiming for type-system consistency. After three iterations (v1–v3), the series was withdrawn following sustained opposition from Junio C Hamano, Pablo Sabater, and Phillip Wood. Reviewers unanimously concluded that the theoretical benefits of the change—eliminating a type mismatch for inherently non-negative display widths—were outweighed by practical risks, including regressions (underflow in `diff.c`, mixed-sign comparisons in `pretty.c`, struct field mismatches in `builtin/repo.c`), pervasive explicit casts, and increased complexity. The v3 patch attempted to address compilation errors but reintroduced casts to `int` at call sites, undermining the original goal and failing to resolve core issues.

The withdrawal resolves the debate in favor of the status quo. Hardik’s final email acknowledges that the v3 approach, which prioritized minimal disruption over type safety, did not deliver meaningful improvements. The functions will retain their `int` return type, and the `cast_size_t_to_int()` helper—removed in v3 but criticized for its role in overflow prevention—will remain in place. A follow-up patch will document the rationale for the existing design: alignment with `printf()`-style APIs (which require `int` for padding calculations) and the necessity of `cast_size_t_to_int()` to prevent overflows. The documentation will replace the TODO comment in `utf8.c` and clarify why the functions return `int` despite measuring non-negative widths.

The thread’s key participants—Junio, Pablo, and Phillip—all raised substantive objections, with Phillip Wood’s v3 review strongly opposing the patch on technical and practical grounds. His argument—that the change forced callers to cast `size_t` results back to `int` for use with `printf()` and other APIs—became the consensus view. Hardik’s withdrawal email confirms the patch is "not worth" pursuing, and the only remaining action is the documentation update. The discussion is now procedural: Hardik seeks guidance on whether the documentation should live in a new `.adoc` file in `Documentation/technical/` or elsewhere. No further technical changes are expected unless Junio or Phillip reply with placement preferences.
