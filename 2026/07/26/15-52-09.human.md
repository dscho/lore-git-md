This is a refactoring patch in progress, part of a type-system cleanup in Git’s UTF-8 string-width calculation code. The author, Hardik Kumar, is responding to substantive review feedback from Pablo Sabater and René Scharfe about his patch that changes the return type of `utf8_strwidth()` and `utf8_strnwidth()` from `int` to `size_t`.

The patch itself is a clean, mechanical change that addresses a `//TODO` comment and removes a helper function (`cast_size_t_to_int`) used for type conversion. However, the header-level change affects *all* callers of these functions, not just the updated internal caller (`strbuf_utf8_align()`). The discussion has centered on whether the patch should rely on implicit conversion at call sites or explicitly update all callers to use `size_t`. Pablo’s audit found callers in `builtin/repo.c` and elsewhere, and he argues the patch should either update all callers for consistency or explain why it is safe to leave them as-is.

Hardik’s latest reply acknowledges the feedback and plans to address the style nit (unnecessary parentheses around `string` in a ternary) in the next version. He also notes he will revise the commit message as suggested by Pablo. However, the core technical question—whether to rely on implicit conversion or explicitly update callers—remains unresolved. The removal of the overflow check in `cast_size_t_to_int()` is another open question, as Pablo explicitly asked whether any caller relied on it.

This patch is well-motivated and looks like a sensible cleanup, but the compatibility risk is real and the discussion is still active. If you care about type-system hygiene or the UTF-8 formatting code, this thread is worth following. The next version (v2) should clarify the scope and rationale, but until then, the patch is not ready for merging.
