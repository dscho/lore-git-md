This is a **tested, substantive review** of the second patch in Ted Nyman’s v5 series fixing packfile URI download race conditions. Jeff King (Peff) engages deeply with the core logic, edge cases, and Windows-specific workarounds, raising no blocking concerns but expressing discomfort with the subtlety of the solution.

Peff’s review focuses on three areas: the POSIX-ish read-write/open-seek design, the Windows/MINGW workaround, and a latent bug in the cleanup logic. He confirms the POSIX approach is sound—each downloader’s seek establishes an atomic offset, preventing corruption even if another process advances the file. For Windows, he flags a race in the O_EXCL loop but accepts the workaround as necessary; he suggests splitting the Windows bits into a separate commit for clarity, though he does not demand it. Most critically, he identifies that the patch accidentally fixes a long-standing bug: the `tmpfile_fd` descriptor was always leaked because `run_command()` closes it, making the explicit `close()` redundant. Peff recommends extracting this fix into a preparatory commit to reduce cognitive load.

The review is **tested**: Peff’s earlier `--stress` runs confirmed the patch resolves the intermittent test failure, and he does not report new issues. His tone is candid—he calls the solution “horrible and gross” but concedes it works with minimal regression risk. The only open question is whether HTTP 416 handling is still needed; Peff speculates servers might reject “N-” range requests for files of length N, but he does not insist on a change.

The patch remains in Junio’s `next` branch, production code unchanged since v3. Peff’s feedback is purely advisory; the series is technically complete and ready for `master`.
