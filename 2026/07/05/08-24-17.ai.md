This is the v2 revision of a 12-patch series from Johannes Schindelin that fixes resource leaks and error-path bugs identified by Coverity in Git's codebase. The series is part of a broader Coverity-driven cleanup effort and focuses on plugging memory, file-descriptor, and process-handle leaks in core Git.

The v2 revision addresses feedback from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt on the initial submission. Key changes include: replacing an unsafe `errno`-based error detection in `load_one_loose_object_map()` with an explicit `ferror(fp)` check; withdrawing an obsolete patch (previously 5/13) that was already fixed upstream; clarifying ownership semantics in `read_one_dir()`; and rewriting `get_superproject_working_tree()` to consolidate cleanup into a single exit path, eliminating a fragility risk and plugging a latent leak of `one_up`’s backing buffer. The commit messages have also been edited for consistency, with function names now in backticks.

The patches touch a wide range of subsystems: loose-object handling (`loose.c`), bundle URI downloads (`bundle-uri.c`), run-command API (`run-command.c`), diff machinery (`diff-lib.c`, `line-log.c`), directory traversal (`dir.c`), submodules (`submodule.c`), worktree creation (`builtin/worktree.c`), IMAP sending (`imap-send.c`), reftable (`reftable/table.c`), fsmonitor daemon (`builtin/fsmonitor--daemon.c`), and Windows-specific code (`compat/mingw.c`). The fixes are mechanical and focused on internal correctness, with no user-visible behavior changes. Junio has stated he will queue the series for merging, indicating strong maintainer approval.

The series carries Reviewed-by tags from Patrick Steinhardt and Jeff King (Peff) for several patches, and Peff independently reproduced the leak fixed in patch 5/12 under `GIT_TEST_COMMIT_GRAPH_CHANGED_PATHS=1`. An orthogonal bug in `read_one_dir()`—uninitialized slots in `->dirs` and `->untracked` on failure paths—is noted but will be addressed separately. The patches are small, well-scoped, and unlikely to introduce regressions, making them suitable for early-cycle merging.
