Trevor Gross’s patch series proposing a `-x` flag for `git rebase -i` to append "(cherry picked from commit ...)" lines remains stuck on fundamental design objections. Junio C Hamano and Jeff King (Peff) immediately challenged the feature’s conceptual fit: `rebase` moves history, while `cherry-pick` duplicates it, making the `-x` annotation’s purpose questionable when original commits are discarded. Peff suggested an alternative—extending `cherry-pick` with an interactive mode (`-i`)—arguing it would align better with Git’s semantics and require minimal changes, since `cherry-pick` already uses the sequencer backend.

Phillip Wood’s recent review deepens the divide. He endorses Peff’s critique, pressing Trevor to justify why `rebase` is the right tool for this job rather than addressing gaps in `cherry-pick`. He also suggests harmonizing the two approaches by having `cherry-pick -x` automatically add `-x` to each todo line, simplifying the sequencer’s logic. The thread now hinges on Trevor’s response: whether to defend the rebase-centric design, pivot to Peff’s `cherry-pick -i` proposal, or abandon the effort.
