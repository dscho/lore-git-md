This is Junio C Hamano’s latest reply in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a `lib/` directory. The email is a **substantive critique** that challenges the structural rationale behind the reorganization and proposes an alternative approach.

Junio acknowledges brian m. carlson’s support for improving Git’s rename-following tools (e.g., `git log --follow`) but dismisses the idea that the `lib/` reorganization itself is a necessary or sufficient step toward that goal. He argues that moving files en masse without a concrete plan or strategy is arbitrary and disruptive, and he directly counters the claim that the reorganization reduces clutter. Instead, he observes that the `lib/` directory merely shifts the problem one level down, with most files still sitting directly in `lib/` alongside a few subsystem-specific subdirectories like `odb/` and `refs/`. This, he suggests, fails to achieve the promised structural improvement.

His preferred alternative is incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem reorganization: rather than moving everything into `lib/` and then potentially regrouping it further, he proposes migrating each subsystem directly to its own root-level directory (e.g., `refs/`, `odb/`). This approach, he argues, would be less disruptive, avoid multiple rounds of churn, and result in a cleaner structure without the intermediate `lib/` layer. He does not object to the idea of grouping related code—only to the specific implementation in the RFC patch.

The reply reinforces Junio’s earlier skepticism about the reorganization’s merits and his preference for incremental, motivated changes over sweeping mechanical moves. It also signals that the current patch is unlikely to gain his support without a significant rethink of its structural goals. The debate remains deadlocked, with proponents prioritizing codebase hygiene and critics emphasizing disruption and lack of concrete benefits.
