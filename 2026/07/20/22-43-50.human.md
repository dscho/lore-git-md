This is Junio C Hamano’s latest reply in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a `lib/` directory. Junio directly challenges the premise of the reorganization, arguing that the current patch merely shifts clutter one level down without resolving it. He suggests an alternative approach: **incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem moves** directly to new root-level directories (e.g., `refs/`, `odb/`) rather than a single sweeping change to `lib/`. This would avoid multiple rounds of disruption and align with past reorganizations that were tied to substantive subsystem updates.

Junio’s critique is **substantive and grounded in maintainer experience**. He dismisses the idea of moving files "simply because we want to move them" without a concrete strategy, reinforcing his preference for **motivated, incremental changes** over mechanical cleanup. His proposal—migrating subsystems one at a time—addresses the disruption concerns raised by critics (e.g., SZEDER Gábor, Phillip Wood) while still allowing for structural improvements. However, it would require a more coordinated, long-term effort than Patrick’s current patch.

The reply does not engage with tooling proposals (e.g., Kaartic Sivaraam’s `git show` hint) or the pro-reorganization camp’s arguments about codebase hygiene. Instead, it **reframes the debate** around the practicality of the approach, making it clear that Junio is unlikely to support the current patch without significant revision. This positions the discussion for a potential pivot toward smaller, subsystem-focused moves—or a stalemate if the pro-reorganization camp remains committed to the `lib/` approach. The timing (close to the upcoming release) suggests this debate will continue into the next development cycle.
