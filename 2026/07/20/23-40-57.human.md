This is Taylor Blau’s reply in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a `lib/` directory. The email is a **substantive critique** that questions the patch’s motivation and structural rationale, aligning closely with Junio C Hamano’s earlier objections.

Taylor agrees with Junio’s preference for an incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem reorganization over a single large move, arguing that piecemeal changes would avoid subjecting any subsystem to multiple disruptive relocations. However, he goes further by directly challenging the patch’s stated motivation: he questions whether moving ~700 files into `lib/` actually improves newcomer navigability, noting that the distinction between `libgit.a` and non-library files is unlikely to be meaningful to beginners. He also expresses skepticism about the `lib/` directory’s name, pointing out that many of the moved files are not currently suitable for external linking, which could mislead contributors about their intended use.

The email reinforces the deadlock in the debate, with Taylor’s concerns mirroring those of Junio and SZEDER Gábor—namely, that the reorganization imposes significant disruption without clear, tangible benefits. His critique focuses on the **practical value** of the change for newcomers and the **semantic clarity** of the `lib/` directory, rather than the technical mechanics of the move. While he leaves the door open to being convinced, his current stance suggests the patch would need a stronger, more concrete rationale to gain his support. The discussion remains polarized, with no clear path forward.
