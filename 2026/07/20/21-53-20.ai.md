This is a brief supportive reply from brian m. carlson to Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC v3 patch moving `libgit.a` sources into a `lib/` directory. The email is **surface-level** in depth—it offers no technical critique, testing, or new ideas—but it carries weight due to the author’s long-standing involvement in the project.

brian endorses the reorganization, calling the current root-directory clutter "odd" and framing the change as a long-overdue cleanup that will benefit both existing and new contributors. He echoes Patrick’s argument that the one-time disruption is justified by permanent improvements in code discoverability, and he explicitly welcomes the idea that this mass rename might spur improvements to Git’s rename-following tools (e.g., `git log --follow`). The reply does not engage with the practical concerns raised by critics (e.g., rebasing pain, mental overhead of file moves, or the feasibility of tooling improvements), nor does it propose concrete steps to address them. Instead, it reinforces the pro-reorganization camp’s framing of the trade-off as a necessary step for codebase hygiene.
