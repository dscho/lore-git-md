This is a **substantive review follow-up** in the `no-ref-delta` capability thread, where Jeff King (Peff) engages deeply with the technical trade-offs of Taylor Blau’s proposed design.

Peff now acknowledges that restricting packs to `OFS_DELTA` entries could enable meaningful optimizations for the write-through proxy’s asynchronous delta resolution pipeline, particularly around worker coordination and intermediate-state caching. He highlights a key challenge: with `REF_DELTA`, the proxy cannot immediately determine which worker is processing a base object, complicating load balancing and cache locality. This dependency tracking is simpler with `OFS_DELTA` because base offsets are known upfront. However, he notes that even `OFS_DELTA` does not fully solve the problem of predicting which intermediate objects will be reused later in the pack, as delta families may not be grouped contiguously.

Despite these insights, Peff remains hesitant about merging a protocol capability that Git itself does not use, citing concerns about long-term maintainability and the risk of the feature becoming a mismatch for future optimizations. He suggests that the proxy’s optimizations might be more compelling if they were also applicable to Git’s native receiving code, though he does not propose a concrete path for that integration. The discussion leaves open whether the proxy’s specific needs justify a niche protocol addition or if a more general solution (e.g., Peff’s earlier “bases-before-deltas” idea) would better align with Git’s broader goals. No new code or tests are introduced here, but the technical depth of the review clarifies the design’s implications for parallel delta resolution.
