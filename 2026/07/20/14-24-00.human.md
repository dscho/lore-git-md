This is a reply in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch series to move Git’s core library sources into a new `lib/` directory. Johannes Schindelin (Dscho) pushes back against SZEDER Gábor’s argument that the reorganization imposes an **ongoing burden**, framing the disruption as a **one-time cost** that pales next to the current, persistent burden of a disorganized source tree.

Dscho’s response is concise and direct. He dismisses Gábor’s claim that the pain will be recurring, pointing out the obvious: Git is not going to repeatedly shuffle files into `lib/`. More importantly, he reframes the debate by highlighting the **current, ongoing cost** of the root directory’s clutter—something the patch aims to fix. His tone is unapologetic, even drawing a parallel to past resistance against migrating repositories to Git itself, implying that skepticism about tooling improvements is shortsighted.

The reply also serves as a clear **vote of support** for the patch. Dscho aligns with Patrick’s view that the structural benefits (better code organization, clearer library boundaries) justify the temporary disruption, and he explicitly endorses the idea that Git should improve its tooling to handle mass renames—something the patch could help catalyze. His position is pragmatic: the pain is temporary, the gain is permanent, and the project should not shy away from necessary cleanup out of fear of short-term friction.

This exchange sharpens the thread’s central tension: **structural hygiene versus workflow disruption**. Dscho’s stance is unlikely to sway Gábor, who remains focused on the practical headaches for contributors, but it reinforces the patch’s rationale for those who prioritize long-term codebase health. The debate is now clearly split between those who see the reorganization as a net win (Patrick, Dscho, Oswald Buddenhagen) and those who view it as a net loss (Gábor, Junio, Phillip Wood), with no clear path to consensus yet.
