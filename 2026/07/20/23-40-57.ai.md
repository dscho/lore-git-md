This is a **substantive review** from Taylor Blau in the ongoing debate over Patrick Steinhardt’s RFC patch to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a `lib/` directory. Taylor aligns with Junio C Hamano’s critique of the patch’s structural rationale, agreeing that an **incremental, subsystem-by-subsystem reorganization** (e.g., moving `refs/` or `odb/` directly to root-level directories) would be less disruptive than a single mass move. He questions the patch’s core motivation—improving newcomer discoverability—arguing that relocating ~700 files into `lib/` does little to clarify the project’s structure for contributors who are not yet thinking in terms of `libgit.a` boundaries. Taylor also expresses skepticism about the `lib/` directory’s name, noting that many moved files are not currently suitable for external linking, which could mislead developers about their intended use.

The review reinforces the **deadlock** in the debate, with Taylor’s position mirroring Junio’s preference for incremental, motivated changes over sweeping mechanical moves. His concerns about the patch’s **discoverability claims** and the **semantic ambiguity** of the `lib/` directory add weight to the argument that the reorganization’s benefits do not justify its disruption. While he leaves the door open to being convinced, his current stance suggests the patch would need a **fundamental rethink**—either in its structural approach or its justification—to gain his support. The timing of this feedback, near the upcoming release, may further reduce the likelihood of the patch landing in its current form.
