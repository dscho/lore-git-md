This is a refactoring patch under active review, part of the ongoing effort to eliminate misuse of Git’s shared `object.flags` field. Junio C Hamano’s reply to Gatla Vishweshwar Reddy’s v2 patch surfaces two technical concerns that go beyond the mechanical slab conversion.

First, Junio questions the choice of a fixed `uint64_t` slab element. He argues that a truly scalable design should use a variable-length slab stride (e.g., `uint64_t[n]`) so that the command can handle hundreds of branches without hitting a hard limit. He also suggests keeping the `UNINTERESTING` bit in the shared `object.flags` rather than mixing it into the slab, since it is only used for revision walking and does not need per-branch state.

Second, Junio points out two implementation nits: a mis-indented line in the diff and a potential type mismatch in `omit_in_dense()`, where the local variable `flag` may still be declared as `int` instead of `uint64_t`, risking truncation on 32-bit platforms. These are straightforward to fix but need to be addressed before the patch can graduate.

The patch itself remains well-scoped and well-motivated. It touches only `builtin/show-branch.c`, preserves existing behavior, and lifts the previous 27-branch limit to 62. The open questions are now about design trade-offs (fixed vs. variable slab stride) and mechanical correctness, not about the core goal of removing `object.flags` misuse. If the author incorporates the type-safety fix and clarifies the design rationale, this should be ready for `next`.
