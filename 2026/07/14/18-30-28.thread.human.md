This thread is about a refactoring patch to `git show-branch` that replaces its use of the shared `object.flags` field with a dedicated commit-slab, eliminating the risk of flag collisions and lifting the previous 27-branch limit. The patch is now in its third iteration (v3), submitted by Gatla Vishweshwar Reddy, and addresses all the conceptual feedback from Junio C Hamano and Jeff King on the earlier version.

The key change in v3 is a more principled design: the `UNINTERESTING` bit (used for revision walking) remains in `object.flags`, while only the per-branch reachability bits are moved to a new commit-slab (`commit_rev_flags`) with a configurable stride of 64-bit words. This lifts the branch limit to 64, with room for future expansion via dynamic strides. The patch also fixes a type mismatch and an indentation nit, and adds clean helper functions to encapsulate slab access. All existing tests continue to pass.

Junio and Peff had previously raised concerns about the fixed-width slab design in v2, but v3 now reuses infrastructure from a 2025 variable-length bitset slab series, aligning with their feedback. No other reviewers have weighed in, and the patch appears ready for merge unless new objections surface. The work is part of a broader effort to eliminate misuse of `object.flags` across Git’s codebase.
