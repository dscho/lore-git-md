This thread is about a refactoring patch to `git show-branch` that replaces its use of the shared `object.flags` field with a dedicated commit-slab, removing a long-standing 27-branch limit. The patch, now at version 10, is authored by Gatla Vishweshwar Reddy and has been under review since an earlier RFC by Meet Soni.

The key change is technical: the new slab (`commit_rev_flags`) dynamically grows to accommodate any number of branches, using a runtime-computed stride of `uint64_t` words. The patch also introduces helper functions for flag management and removes hardcoded limits from both the code and documentation. Earlier versions addressed behavioral regressions in branch independence checks, `UNINTERESTING` flag propagation, and redundant traversal avoidance, while version 10 fixes a critical crash (division-by-zero due to an uninitialized slab) that Junio C Hamano identified in version 9.

Junio’s feedback has been thorough, covering everything from mechanical cleanups to subtle behavioral and performance regressions. Jeff King (Peff) and Patrick Steinhardt also contributed reviews, with Peff pointing to prior art for the slab design and Patrick emphasizing the importance of human communication in mailing list interactions. Gatla has addressed all feedback, including the latest regression, and the patch now appears technically sound. The full test suite passes, and Gatla’s manual testing across all `show-branch` modes suggests the fix is robust. The patch is likely ready for integration once Junio confirms the regression fix.
