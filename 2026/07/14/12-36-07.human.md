This is a follow-up discussion in the `git history squash` thread, now in its ninth iteration. Ben Knoble chimes in to echo Matt Hunter’s praise for the feature’s handling of `fixup!`/`squash!`/`amend!` commits and speculates about performance. He suggests that combining `git history squash` with `git replay` could emulate `git rebase --autosquash` more efficiently than the current rebase implementation, since both commands avoid the repeated conflict stops of a traditional rebase. Knoble acknowledges edge cases (e.g., `fixup!` commits targeting upstream commits or conflict scenarios) where delegation might not work, but sees potential for a performance win in compatible cases. His comment is forward-looking and exploratory, not a request for immediate changes to the series. The tone is enthusiastic and constructive, reinforcing the feature’s value while opening a new avenue for future optimization.
