This thread is about René Scharfe’s 5-patch series (v1) introducing repository-aware variants of Git’s tempfile and lockfile APIs (`repo_create_tempfile`, `repo_hold_lock_file_for_update`, etc.). The goal is to eliminate implicit reliance on the global `the_repository` variable as part of the ongoing effort to remove this global state. The series touches 38 files across core subsystems like apply, builtins, bundle, commit-graph, config, and ref backends, but the changes are mechanical and confined to plumbing—no user-visible behavior changes are expected.

The patches add new repository-aware functions (e.g., `repo_create_tempfile_mode`) and migrate existing callers to use them, ensuring that `core.sharedRepository` is read from the correct repository context. Initial adopters include `commit-graph.c`, `midx-write.c`, and ref backends, which already work with non-default repositories. The series is incremental, with future patches expected to convert additional callers. Junio Hamano queued the series in his `seen` branch on July 10, indicating it’s under review but not yet ready for merging. Patrick Steinhardt, who leads the ODB abstraction effort, welcomed the change and provided substantive feedback on the design, particularly questioning whether passing a full `struct repository *` is necessary when only the `core.sharedRepository` setting is used. René responded by clarifying that while a repository-less API *could* be cleaner in theory, every caller already has access to a repository, making the current approach pragmatic. No changes to the series are expected from this discussion.

Patrick also suggested two minor improvements in his review of the final patch: adding a brief rationale for the selected callers in the commit message and introducing a compile-time guard (e.g., `USE_THE_REPOSITORY_VARIABLE`) to prevent new callers from reintroducing reliance on `the_repository`. These are follow-up considerations rather than blockers. The series is aligned with the broader `the_repository` removal effort, and no objections or additional substantive reviews have been raised. The main loose ends are whether future patches will convert more callers and whether the proposed compile-time guard will be implemented. For now, the series remains in `seen`, and René’s response to Patrick’s feedback suggests no v2 is needed.
