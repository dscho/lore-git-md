This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) of Taylor Blau’s `no-ref-delta` capability series. Peff raises fundamental questions about the motivation and design choices behind the feature.

The core of his critique is the apparent contradiction in the stated use case: if a receiver (`receive-pack`) wants to retain an incoming pack without building an object ID index, how can it safely update refs without first verifying the pack’s contents? Peff argues that indexing the pack is unavoidable for safety, making the avoidance of `REF_DELTA` entries seem unnecessary or misaligned with the stated goal. He suspects there may be an unstated or larger system context driving this feature and suggests that without clearer motivation, it’s hard to assess whether the approach is sensible.

Peff also probes technical edge cases. He acknowledges the need to handle bitmap pack reuse correctly (which the series addresses) but questions how thin packs are handled. Thin packs can introduce `REF_DELTA` entries on the server side after the pack is completed and indexed, and Peff notes that Git’s `receive-pack` doesn’t currently support the `no-thin` capability, which could complicate enforcement of `no-ref-delta`. He also asks what a server should do if it receives a `REF_DELTA` despite requesting `no-ref-delta`, tying this back to the broader question of why `REF_DELTA` entries are undesirable in the first place.

The review is substantive and technical, focusing on both the high-level rationale and low-level implementation details. Peff’s questions suggest that the series may need to either clarify its motivation or adjust its design to better align with the stated goals. This is likely to prompt further discussion or revisions from Taylor Blau before the series can proceed.
