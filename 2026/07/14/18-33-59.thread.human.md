The thread is about improving the performance of `git last-modified`, a command Toon Claes wrote to show when files were last modified. Originally, it was found to be 2.5× slower than a hand-rolled `git log` pipeline when checking a single directory in the ziglang/zig repository, even with commit-graph and Bloom filters enabled. Jeff King (Peff) identified the bottleneck—`last-modified` was checking every path in the target directory against every commit’s Bloom filter—and posted a proof-of-concept patch that reuses Bloom filter logic from `revision.c` to cut runtime from 64 ms to 16 ms.

Toon has now chimed in to clarify GitLab’s perspective: their real-world use case batches 25 files at once, where `last-modified` already outperforms the naive loop, so they hadn’t prioritized the single-file case. He’s coordinating with Peff to combine their efforts into a single patch series. The patch itself is still under review, with open questions about edge cases (non-literal pathspecs, potential regressions in frequently modified directories), whether GitHub’s internal `blame-tree` has a reusable solution, and whether exposing internal `revision.c` helpers is acceptable. No other reviews have landed yet, but Taylor Blau has been cc’d, suggesting GitHub’s team may help drive the patch forward.
