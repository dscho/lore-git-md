This is a **substantive review** of the `no-ref-delta` capability series from Jeff King (Peff). He raises fundamental questions about the motivation and design of the feature, signaling that the series may not yet be ready for merging without clarification.

Peff starts by questioning the core premise: why would a receiver want to retain an incoming pack without first indexing it? He points out that updating refs without knowing the pack’s contents would allow a malicious client to corrupt the repository, implying that some form of indexing is unavoidable. This casts doubt on the practical value of the `no-ref-delta` capability as described, suggesting that either the use case is missing context or the approach needs rethinking.

On the technical side, Peff acknowledges the need to handle bitmap pack reuse correctly but expresses concern about thin packs, which can introduce `REF_DELTA` entries on the server side after the pack is completed. He notes that Git’s `receive-pack` does not currently support the `no-thin` capability (which would prevent thin packs), and that even if it did, not all clients might respect it. This raises a potential interoperability issue: if a server requests `no-ref-delta` but receives a thin pack anyway, what should it do? Peff’s questions imply that the series may need to address fallback behavior or error handling for such cases.

The review is **not a rejection**, but it is a strong signal that the series lacks sufficient justification. Peff’s tone suggests skepticism about whether the problem being solved is real or whether the solution aligns with Git’s design principles. The absence of a clear, compelling use case in the cover letter or commit messages appears to be the primary sticking point. Future discussion will likely focus on clarifying the motivation—whether this is part of a larger system (e.g., a custom `receive-pack` implementation with unique constraints) or an optimization for a specific workflow.
