This is a substantive review in the ongoing bugfix series for packfile URI download race conditions in Git's HTTP transport. Jeff King (Peff) engages deeply with the design and implementation of Patch 1/2, which uses unique tempfiles to prevent corruption when multiple processes fetch the same packfile URI concurrently.

Peff starts by proposing an alternative technical approach: instead of using unique tempfiles, he suggests dropping down to POSIX file descriptors with `O_WRONLY` (without `O_APPEND`) to avoid the corruption caused by concurrent append operations. His proof-of-concept patch passes the existing test, but he acknowledges potential portability issues and the risk of sending malformed HTTP range requests to the server. This is presented as a discussion point rather than a counter-proposal, signaling openness to the current design while raising technical trade-offs.

The review then pivots to the broader design question of whether abandoning resumable downloads for packfile URIs is justified. Peff argues that resumption is a valuable feature, especially for packfile URIs, and suggests two potential improvements if resumption is dropped: (1) allowing users to opt into resumable fetches via configuration, and (2) piping the download directly to `index-pack --stdin` to reduce disk I/O and enable CPU/network pipelining. These suggestions are framed as follow-up opportunities rather than blockers, but they highlight a tension between correctness and efficiency in the current patch.

Peff's code-level feedback is thorough but largely approving. He notes minor quirks (e.g., the redundant `preq->packfile = NULL` assignment after `delete_tempfile()`, the confusing naming of `tempfile` vs. `tmpfile` fields) and suggests clarifications or mechanical cleanups. His comments on the test coverage are pragmatic: while he doesn’t deeply scrutinize the concurrent download test, he proposes an alternative test design (writing garbage to the tempfile) to more robustly verify isolation between processes.

The review implicitly endorses the series' direction ("fix first, optimize later") but surfaces important questions about long-term consistency between packfile URI and dumb HTTP behaviors. Peff’s engagement with the patch’s core logic—without outright rejection—suggests the series is on track for eventual merging, pending resolution of the mechanical cleanups and Junio’s final verdict on the design trade-offs. The discussion here is likely to shape follow-up work, particularly around resumption and pipelining.
