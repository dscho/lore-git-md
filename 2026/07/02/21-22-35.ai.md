This is a substantive review comment on patch 5/9 of Toon Claes’s series making `GIT_TEST_LONG` tests reliable for CI. Jeff King (Peff) questions the patch’s premise: he reports that the `linux32` CI job has been passing for him even after `7a094d68a2` enabled expensive tests, contradicting the commit message’s claim that the test "blows up" and hangs. Peff is puzzled why his CI runs succeed when the patch implies they should always fail, and asks whether the analysis is incomplete or if there’s an unaccounted factor suppressing the expensive tests in his environment.

The review targets the technical accuracy of the patch’s motivation rather than its implementation. The change itself (skipping the test on 32-bit systems) is uncontroversial, but Peff’s observation suggests the problem may be intermittent or dependent on specific CI runner configurations. This could indicate that the test’s failure mode is not as deterministic as the commit message implies, or that the `SIZE_T_IS_64BIT` prerequisite alone does not fully capture the conditions under which the test fails. No alternative fix is proposed, but the comment flags a potential gap in the patch’s reasoning. The author (Patrick Steinhardt) or series maintainer (Toon Claes) will need to clarify whether the issue is indeed universal or if additional factors (e.g., runner memory, Git build flags) influence the test’s behavior.
