This is the third patch in Jeff King’s nine-patch **bugfix series** plugging memory leaks in Git’s hash implementations when non-default backends (OpenSSL, libgcrypt) are used. The series was introduced earlier in the thread and targets leaks that only surface under `SANITIZE=leak` with `OPENSSL_SHA256=1` or similar configurations.

This patch ensures the `git_hash_ctx` inside a `hashfile` struct is always cleaned up, either by `git_hash_final()` or the new `git_hash_discard()` primitive introduced in patch 2. The problem arises in two scenarios: when `free_hashfile()` is called directly (bypassing `finalize_hashfile()`), and when the `skip_hash` flag is set (preventing `git_hash_final()` from being called). The fix splits `free_hashfile()` into a public interface (which now calls `git_hash_discard()`) and an internal `free_hashfile_memory()` helper used by `finalize_hashfile()` to avoid double-cleanup. The change is minimal—just 12 lines added to `csum-file.c`—and resolves leaks in several test scripts (t1600, t1601, t2107, t7008, t9210, t9211) when tested with OpenSSL’s unsafe SHA-1 implementation.

The patch is narrowly scoped and well-motivated, addressing a correctness issue without altering behavior. It’s part of a larger, uncontroversial effort to harden Git’s hash handling, and the commit message clearly explains the leak’s cause and fix. No reviews have been posted yet, but the series as a whole looks like a straightforward cleanup that should attract little debate. If you’re tracking memory-safety improvements or non-default hash backends, this patch is worth a quick skim; otherwise, it’s safe to assume it’s in good shape and will land as-is.
