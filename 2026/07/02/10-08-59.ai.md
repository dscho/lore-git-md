This is a **CI infrastructure discussion** in the memory-leak cleanup thread, specifically a follow-up to Jeff King’s (Peff) proposal to consolidate Git’s CI jobs. Patrick Steinhardt replies to Peff’s skepticism about expanding the CI matrix, agreeing that the current `clang/gcc` and potential `macos-TEST-vars` jobs are likely unnecessary. Both contributors converge on a minimalist approach: reducing redundant jobs while maintaining coverage, and renaming the `linux-TEST-vars` job to something clearer like `linux-exotic-flags` or `linux-changed-defaults`.

The discussion is procedural and uncontroversial. Patrick endorses Peff’s suggestion to fold the `linux-reftable` job into `linux-TEST-vars` and create a combined `linux-TEST-vars-leaks` job, while dropping the redundant non-leak variant. He also agrees that compiler-specific test jobs (clang/gcc) rarely produce different results and that a `macos-TEST-vars` job would likely be low-value, given that most `TEST-vars` are feature-selection flags. The naming debate is resolved in favor of `linux-exotic-flags`, which both find more intuitive than `linux-TEST-vars` or `linux-changed-defaults`.

Key technical details for future reference: the proposed job names (`linux-exotic-flags`, `linux-TEST-vars-leaks`), the decision to drop the `linux-reftable` job, and the consensus that SHA-256/OpenSSL-specific leaks are too niche for CI expansion. The original patch series (fixing `format-patch` and test harness leaks) remains unaffected and ready for merging. No new code changes, CLI options, or on-disk formats are introduced.
