This is the latest reply in Patrick Steinhardt’s ongoing RFC series to reorganize Git’s source tree by moving `libgit.a` components into a new `lib/` directory. The email responds directly to Junio C Hamano’s and Phillip Wood’s critiques about the discoverability rationale and workflow disruption caused by the rename.

Patrick clarifies that the reorganization’s primary goal is **not** to make library files easier to find (as Junio and Phillip assumed) but to improve discoverability of *non-library* files in the root directory by reducing clutter. He acknowledges that the change does not help locate library files themselves, conceding the point to Phillip’s argument. However, he doubles down on the structural benefit of grouping library code, arguing that the current layout—where library and non-library files are mixed across directories—makes it harder to enforce coding conventions (e.g., avoiding `the_repository` or `die()` in library code). He cites inconsistencies like `scalar.c` (non-library) and `compat/` (library) as evidence that the current structure lacks clarity.

On the workflow disruption front, Patrick offers no concrete mitigation for the recurring pain of remembering file moves (e.g., `wt-status.c` → `lib/wt-status.c`), calling his own defense "hand-wavy." This leaves the debate’s core tension unresolved: the trade-off between long-term structural benefits and ongoing friction for contributors. The email does not propose new technical changes or address Junio’s suggestion of a documentation-based "map" of the codebase, keeping the focus on the reorganization’s original goals.

Key technical details referenced: `lib/wt-status.c`, `scalar.c`, `compat/`, `the_repository`, `die()`. The reply advances the discussion by refining the motivation but does not shift the balance of arguments. The series remains a **refactoring-only** effort with no functional changes, and the debate hinges on whether the structural benefits justify the disruption.
