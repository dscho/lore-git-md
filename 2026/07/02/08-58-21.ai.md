This is a **CI infrastructure discussion** in the ongoing memory-leak cleanup thread. Jeff King (Peff) replies to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to consolidate Git’s CI jobs, focusing on the practicality of extending the pattern to macOS and the naming of the `linux-TEST-vars` job.

Peff is skeptical about creating a `macos-TEST-vars` job, arguing that most `TEST-vars` are about feature selection and unlikely to reveal macOS-specific issues. He also questions the value of running redundant compiler jobs (clang/gcc), noting that while different compilers catch different warnings, he hasn’t seen cases where test results differ between them. His stance leans toward CI minimalism, cautioning that excessive jobs incur costs without clear benefits. However, he concedes that folding the `linux-reftable` job into `linux-TEST-vars` is reasonable for broader coverage without increasing CI overhead.

On naming, Peff strongly agrees that `linux-TEST-vars` is unclear and suggests alternatives like `linux-changed-defaults` or `linux-exotic-flags` for better readability. He also mentions discovering leaks specific to the SHA-256 backend with OpenSSL, but considers this too niche for CI expansion. The discussion remains procedural and uncontroversial, with no impact on the original patch series (fixing `format-patch` and test harness leaks), which is still ready for merging. Key technical details for future reference: the proposed job names (`macos-TEST-vars`, `linux-changed-defaults`, `linux-exotic-flags`), the redundancy of compiler-specific test jobs, and the niche nature of SHA-256/OpenSSL leak detection.
