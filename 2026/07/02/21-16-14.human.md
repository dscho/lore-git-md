This is a **test infrastructure refactoring** discussion, specifically a review of patch 3/9 in Toon Claes’s series to make `GIT_TEST_LONG` tests reliable and efficient in CI. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s patch that replaces the slow `test_copy_bytes` pipeline in `t4141-apply-too-large.sh` with direct `genzeros` generation, eliminating a 6-minute bottleneck.

Peff endorses the fix for this specific case but questions whether `test_copy_bytes` itself should be improved more broadly. He notes that the function was originally added because `head -c` wasn’t portable (citing IRIX as the problem), but wonders if that limitation still holds today. He also shares an amusing history of the function: it was originally implemented with `dd`, switched to Perl for speed, then reverted back to `dd` in 2025, reintroducing the slowness. His comment is more of a musing on technical debt than a request for action—he doesn’t ask Patrick or Toon to change anything, but the observation could inspire future cleanup.

The review is **surface-level** in the context of this patch (it doesn’t engage with the test’s behavior or resource usage), but it’s **substantive** for Git’s test infrastructure. It highlights a recurring inefficiency and suggests that `test_copy_bytes` might be worth revisiting in a separate effort. For now, the patch itself remains uncontroversial and well-motivated.
