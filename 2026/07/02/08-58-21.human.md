This is a **CI infrastructure discussion** in the middle of a memory-leak cleanup thread. Jeff King (Peff) replies to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to consolidate Git’s CI jobs and rename the `linux-TEST-vars` job.

Peff agrees with Patrick’s plan to fold the `linux-reftable` job into `linux-TEST-vars` and create a new `linux-TEST-vars-leaks` job, avoiding any increase in CI cost. He is skeptical about extending the pattern to macOS (`macos-TEST-vars`), arguing that most `TEST-vars` are about feature selection and unlikely to reveal platform-specific issues. He also questions the value of running redundant compiler jobs (clang vs. gcc), noting that while different compilers catch different warnings, he hasn’t seen cases where test results differ between them. His stance is pragmatic: CI should be useful, not just maximalist, and the cost of extra jobs (CPU minutes) should be justified by real failures.

On naming, Peff wholeheartedly endorses renaming `linux-TEST-vars` to something clearer, joking that the current name gives him a "stroke" every time he sees it. He suggests `linux-exotic-flags` as a playful alternative to Patrick’s `linux-changed-defaults`, though neither is perfect. The discussion remains lighthearted and uncontroversial, with no objections to the technical direction. The original patch series (fixing leaks in `git format-patch` and the test harness) is unaffected and still considered ready for merging.
