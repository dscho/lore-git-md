This thread is about a nine-patch series that makes Git’s long-running tests (`GIT_TEST_LONG`) reliable and efficient enough to run in CI without hanging or consuming excessive resources. The series, now at its final version (v3), was originally authored by Toon Claes and later revised by Patrick Steinhardt, who addressed all feedback from earlier iterations.

The patches fix broken or hanging tests in GitHub and GitLab CI, improve test efficiency by reducing disk and CPU usage, and ensure GitLab CI now runs these expensive tests on integration branches, matching GitHub Actions’ behavior. Key changes include skipping tests on platforms where they’re known to fail due to `size_t` limitations, replacing slow `dd` pipelines with faster alternatives, and isolating expensive test data to reduce disk footprint. The series also introduces a consistent way to parse `GIT_TEST_LONG` and removes a macOS RAM-disk workaround in GitLab CI to improve stability, even at the cost of slightly longer runtimes.

All feedback has been addressed, and the series is uncontroversial. Junio C Hamano reviewed the final interdiff and confirmed it’s ready for merging, while Jeff King (Peff) has now endorsed the series, signaling no remaining technical concerns. The patches are queued for `next` and should graduate to `master` in the current cycle. No open questions or loose ends remain, though a broader policy discussion about how Git defines "expensive" tests (runtime vs. resource usage) continues separately.
