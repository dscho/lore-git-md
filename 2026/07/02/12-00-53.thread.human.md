This thread is a nine-patch series from Toon Claes aimed at making Git’s long-running tests (those gated by `GIT_TEST_LONG`) both reliable and efficient enough to run in CI. The series tackles two main problems: broken or hanging tests in GitHub and GitLab CI, and the lack of visibility for these expensive tests in GitLab’s pipeline.

The first eight patches address test correctness and efficiency. They fix broken tests (like one in `t0021-conversion.sh` that hangs on 64-bit systems), improve performance (e.g., replacing a slow `dd` copy in `t4141-apply-too-large.sh` with direct `genzeros` generation), and reduce resource usage (e.g., cutting peak disk usage in `t5608-clone-2gb.sh` from 16 GB to 8 GB). The series also standardizes how `GIT_TEST_LONG` is parsed across the test suite and CI scripts, ensuring consistent behavior. A notable change is disabling the macOS RAM disk workaround in GitLab CI to avoid out-of-memory errors when running large tests, accepting a modest runtime increase for stability.

The final patch, just summarized, enables `GIT_TEST_LONG` in GitLab CI for pushes to integration branches (like `next` and `master`), matching the behavior already in place for GitHub Actions. It also disables these tests on Windows runners due to their limited RAM (7.5 GB), preventing out-of-memory failures. The patch is uncontroversial and completes the series’ goal of making long-running tests visible and reliable in GitLab CI.

No substantive reviews or objections have been recorded yet, and the series appears self-contained with no dependencies on other in-flight work. The changes are pragmatic, addressing observed CI failures and resource constraints while improving test coverage. If the series lands as-is, it will significantly enhance the robustness of Git’s CI infrastructure for long-running tests.
