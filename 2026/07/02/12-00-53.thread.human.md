This patch series from Toon Claes aims to make Git's long-running tests (`GIT_TEST_LONG`) more reliable and efficient in CI environments, particularly addressing broken or hanging tests in GitHub and GitLab pipelines. The series is now at its first version, with most patches uncontroversial, but two key discussions have emerged.

The first point of contention is patch 3, which refactors `t4141-apply-too-large.sh` to reduce runtime by replacing a slow `dd` pipeline with direct `genzeros` generation. While this change cuts execution time dramatically, SZEDER Gábor argues the test should retain its `EXPENSIVE` label because it still consumes over 1 GiB of memory—a concern not addressed by the patch. Jeff King (Peff) endorsed the immediate fix but raised broader questions about the portability of the `test_copy_bytes` helper used in the test suite.

The second discussion centers on patch 5, which skips `t7508-status.sh` on 32-bit systems due to reported hangs. Peff challenged this change after observing that the test passes in his `linux32` CI environment, suggesting the issue may be intermittent or dependent on specific runner configurations. This leaves the patch's motivation in question, though no alternative solution has been proposed yet.

Other patches in the series have seen smoother reception. Patch 6, for example, was praised by Peff for its straightforward improvement to `t7900-maintenance.sh`, where it isolates a 2 GB repository into a dedicated sub-repo and deletes it immediately after the test, reducing peak disk usage without altering test logic. The series also includes CI configuration changes, such as enabling `GIT_TEST_LONG` in GitLab CI for integration branches and disabling it on Windows runners due to hardware limitations.

The series remains in early review, with no maintainer feedback yet. Key open questions include whether the `EXPENSIVE` label should be retained for memory-intensive tests (not just slow ones) and whether patch 5's 32-bit skip is justified or needs refinement. The broader goal—making long-running tests CI-friendly—is widely supported, but the specifics of how to define and handle "expensive" tests may evolve as the discussion continues.
