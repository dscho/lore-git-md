This is a **CI infrastructure discussion** in the ongoing memory-leak cleanup thread. Patrick Steinhardt and Jeff King (Peff) agree on consolidating Git’s CI jobs to reduce redundancy while maintaining leak detection coverage.

They confirm the plan to merge the `linux-reftable` job into `linux-TEST-vars` and replace `linux-reftable-leaks` with a new `linux-TEST-vars-leaks` job, avoiding any increase in CI overhead. The naming of `linux-TEST-vars` is unanimously disliked; Patrick suggests `linux-changed-defaults`, while Peff prefers `linux-exotic-flags` for its clarity and slight humor. Both names are improvements, and the final choice will likely be a matter of preference rather than technical debate.

Peff remains skeptical about extending this pattern to macOS (`macos-TEST-vars`), arguing that most `TEST-vars` are feature-selection flags unlikely to reveal macOS-specific issues. He also questions the value of running redundant compiler-specific jobs (clang/gcc), noting that while different compilers catch different warnings, he hasn’t seen cases where test results differ between them. Patrick agrees that the current test matrix could be streamlined without losing coverage, though no specific reductions are proposed here.

The discussion is procedural and uncontroversial, with no impact on the original patch series (fixing `format-patch` and test harness leaks), which remains ready for merging. The broader takeaway is a shared preference for CI minimalism, balancing thoroughness with practicality.
