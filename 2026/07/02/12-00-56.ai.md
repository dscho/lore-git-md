This is a performance-focused test refactoring patch, part 3 of 9 in Toon Claes’s series to make `GIT_TEST_LONG` tests reliable and efficient in CI. The patch targets `t4141-apply-too-large.sh`, which verifies that `git apply` rejects patches exceeding Git’s size limits.

The test previously generated a 1 GiB patch by piping an infinite stream of zeros from `test-tool genzeros` through `test_copy_bytes`, which uses `dd ibs=1` to copy one byte at a time. This approach was extremely slow—taking 6 minutes locally and up to 40 minutes in CI—due to the byte-by-byte I/O. Patrick Steinhardt’s change leverages `genzeros`’ built-in limit parameter to generate exactly 1 GiB of data directly, eliminating the need for `test_copy_bytes` and reducing runtime to under a second. The patch also removes the `EXPENSIVE` prerequisite, making the test runnable in standard CI pipelines without special configuration.

The change is mechanical and self-contained, touching only the test script. It preserves the test’s original intent while dramatically improving efficiency. The commit message clearly explains the performance bottleneck and the rationale for the fix, and the diff is minimal and focused. This patch is uncontroversial and aligns with the series’ goal of making `GIT_TEST_LONG` tests practical to run in CI. No new behavior, edge cases, or dependencies are introduced.
