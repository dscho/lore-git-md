This is a **brief design discussion** in the `git history squash` thread, where Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s oplog proposal by clarifying the reflog’s intended purpose and its limitations for undo operations.

Junio **acknowledges Patrick’s critique**—that the reflog is "way too hard to reason about" for undoing complex operations—but **pushes back on the framing**: the reflog was never designed for undo. Its primary role is to record *what a ref pointed to historically*, not *why* it changed or how to reverse an operation. He cites `@{-N}` notation and `git push --force-if-includes` as examples of features that successfully use the reflog for *programmatic* history inspection, but these are read-only operations, not undo mechanisms.

**Key points**:
- The reflog’s strength is its simplicity: it logs ref updates without context, making it lightweight but ill-suited for high-level undo.
- Junio’s examples show the reflog *can* support limited undo-like functionality (e.g., recovering a lost branch), but it lacks the metadata needed for atomic reversal of multi-ref operations (like `git history squash --update-refs`).
- His response implies that Patrick’s oplog idea—while ambitious—would require a separate system (e.g., an operations log) rather than overloading the reflog.

**Implications for the `squash` series**:
- The recoverability gap remains unresolved, but Junio’s stance suggests the reflog is not the right place to fix it.
- The discussion reinforces that the `squash` feature’s `--update-refs` behavior is correct, but users need better tooling (e.g., `--dry-run --verbose` or an oplog) to manage its side effects.
- No immediate impact on the series, which is otherwise ready for final review.

**Technical context**:
- Junio’s mention of `@{-N}` (e.g., `git checkout @{-1}` to return to the previous branch) and `--force-if-includes` (which checks if a remote branch was force-pushed) highlights the reflog’s utility for *state recovery*, not *operation reversal*.
- The oplog would need to track *operations* (e.g., "squash commits A..B") alongside ref updates, which is beyond the reflog’s scope.

**Status**:
- This is a **philosophical clarification**, not a technical objection. The thread’s focus remains on the `squash` series’ readiness, with the oplog as a potential future direction.
- Junio’s tone is neutral but firm: the reflog’s design is intentional, and undo capabilities would require a new system.
