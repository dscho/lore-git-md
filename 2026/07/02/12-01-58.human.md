This is the second iteration of Patrick Steinhardt’s six-patch series refactoring `struct object_info` in Git’s object database (ODB) subsystem. The series replaces the coarse `whence` field with a new `struct odb_source_info` that carries both the source type and backend-specific data, enabling future multi-source object resolution and pluggable ODB backends.

The v2 update addresses two surface-level concerns raised in the first iteration: the `struct object_info_source` is now renamed to `odb_source_info`, and the `sourcep` pointer is renamed to `source_infop` to avoid confusion with the actual ODB source. These changes are mechanical and do not alter the series’ core design or behavior. The rest of the series remains structurally identical to v1, with patches 1–2 and 5–6 unchanged except for the renamed symbols. Patches 3 and 4 (previously 3 and 4 in v1) are split but functionally equivalent, reflecting the same backend population and treewide conversion logic.

The series is conceptually approved by Junio C Hamano, who called the design “everything makes sense” in the v1 thread. Justin Tobler’s substantive review of the architectural rationale (opt-in `source` parameter vs. centralized tracking) is now settled, though he also suggested the naming improvements adopted in v2. The only remaining open question from v1—the readability of the implicit upcast in `oi->source_infop->source = &source->base`—persists but is not blocking.

This is a well-crafted, incremental step in Patrick’s ongoing ODB abstraction effort. The changes are confined to the ODB and packfile subsystems, with no on-disk format or user-visible behavior changes. If you care about Git’s object storage layer or the long-term goal of pluggable backends, this series is worth tracking—it’s close to merge-ready and sets the stage for future work like multi-source object resolution.
