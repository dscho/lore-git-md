This is a performance bugfix targeting `unpack-trees.c`. The patch resolves a quadratic-time index scan in `next_cache_entry()` that occurs when running `` `git diff` `` with a pathspec matching a subtree whose entries appear early in the index. The fix is now in its final stages of review after addressing Junio’s critical correctness concern about the interaction between forward and backward scan logic.

The solution introduces a `cache_bottom` field in `o->internal` to track forward-scan progress, allowing subsequent calls to skip already-unpacked entries. This optimization is minimal—just four lines in `unpack-trees.c`—and preserves existing ordering invariants by leveraging how `find_cache_pos()` already advances `.cache_bottom` past unpacked entries (as established in commit `e53e6b4433`). The performance improvement is dramatic: on a Chromium-scale repository with ~500k index entries, `` `git diff HEAD -- <pathspec>` `` drops from 8 minutes to 0.07 seconds (a 99.7% speedup).

The patch includes a new perf test (`t/perf/p0009-diff-pathspec.sh`) that reproduces the issue by creating a 100k-entry index with a problematic layout (the pathspec `aaa/file` as the first entry). Henrique has now adjusted the test to scale its entry count via the `EXPENSIVE` prerequisite, addressing Junio’s last remaining suggestion. The test’s initial count is set to 1,000 to ensure the improvement remains noticeable even on less powerful hardware.

Junio has explicitly acknowledged that Henrique’s argument about the safety of the optimization is "sensible," resolving the primary correctness concern raised in his earlier review. The patch is well-motivated, thoroughly tested, and appears ready to land once the `EXPENSIVE` test tweak is incorporated.
