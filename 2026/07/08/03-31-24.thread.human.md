This thread is about clarifying how contributors should abandon a patch series by notifying the mailing list, rather than leaving it to the maintainer to track stale topics. Junio C Hamano proposed a patch to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` adding explicit guidance to send a retraction message, and the discussion has since expanded into broader questions about documentation hygiene and mailing list workflows.

The patch itself is straightforward: it advises contributors to send a retraction message to the list when abandoning a series. Junio has accepted editorial feedback from Michael Montalbo (including a typo fix) but hasn’t yet posted a revised version. Meanwhile, two meta-discussions have emerged. First, Weijie Yuan and Michael Montalbo noted a recurring issue where replies sent via lore.kernel.org’s "mailto:" links in Gmail lose their `In-Reply-To` headers, breaking threading and complicating tooling like `b4`. Michael acknowledged the problem as a workflow flaw and plans to investigate. Second, Weijie suggested that updates to `SubmittingPatches` should also be reflected in `MyFirstContribution` to maintain consistency, citing Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier observation about the burden of keeping the two documents in sync. Junio declined this suggestion, arguing that the maintainer’s internal workflow details aren’t contributor-facing and that synchronizing the documents is beyond the scope of this patch.

The thread remains open, with the patch still awaiting a v2. No decisions have been made about whether to codify Junio’s workflow details (e.g., 7-day soak in `next`, 4-week staleness threshold) in the documentation, and the question of updating `MyFirstContribution` in parallel is unresolved. The discussion has been observational rather than prescriptive, with no one demanding changes—just raising points for future consideration.
