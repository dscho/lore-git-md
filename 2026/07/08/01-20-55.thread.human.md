This thread is about a small but useful documentation update from Junio C Hamano that formalizes how contributors should signal when they are abandoning a patch series. The patch adds a new section to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` explaining that contributors should explicitly retract a series if they no longer intend to pursue it—whether due to technical issues, time constraints, or other reasons. This helps the maintainer and community avoid wasted effort and keeps the list of in-flight topics accurate.

The guidance is straightforward: use the term "retract" (not "abandon"), note that Junio may discard inactive topics after four weeks of silence, and clarify that retracted topics can be resurrected later if circumstances change. The patch is now in its second iteration (v2) and remains uncontroversial, reflecting existing informal practices rather than introducing new ones. Michael Montalbo reviewed it and gave a brief "LGTM," noting that the phrasing and four-week threshold are clear and helpful, but his feedback was purely surface-level and didn’t engage with the substance of the change.

No substantive objections or open questions remain, and the patch is ready for merging. Minor wording tweaks might still come up, but they won’t alter the core guidance. This update is a small but practical improvement to the contributor experience, ensuring that abandoned work doesn’t linger unnecessarily in the review queue.
