This is the second patch in a two-patch bugfix series that Junio has just marked for `next`. It fixes a regression in `git merge-base` (without `--all`) introduced in April 2025 by commit 93e5b1680e. The bug surfaces when a repository uses a v1 commit-graph (no corrected commit dates), the commit topology has clock skew, and the correct merge base has a lower committer date than one of its ancestors.

The patch adds a simple `gen_ordered` flag to track whether the priority queue is generation-ordered. When the fallback to commit-date ordering occurs (for v1 commit graphs), the flag is cleared, and the early-exit optimization in `paint_down_to_common()` is now gated on this flag. This ensures the optimization only fires when the queue is generation-ordered, preventing incorrect early exits when clock skew is present. The change is minimal: ten lines in `commit-reach.c` and a one-line test flip in `t6600-test-reach.sh`.

Junio’s review is brief but positive, calling the problem description “excellent” and the solution “simple and straight-forward.” The series is now queued for integration into `next`, meaning it will cook for roughly a week before graduating to `master` for the 2.55 release. No further objections or review feedback have been raised, and the fix appears ready to land. The accompanying test (added in patch 1) constructs a clock-skew topology to verify the fix, making the regression scenario easy to reproduce and confirm.
