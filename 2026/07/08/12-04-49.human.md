This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about feedback output for `git history reword`. Patrick Steinhardt responds to D. Ben Knoble’s review, agreeing in principle with the need for user feedback but emphasizing flexibility and usability.

The exchange centers on how to represent successful rewrites to users. Steinhardt is open to printing old and new commit hashes—echoing Knoble’s suggestion to borrow the `--update-refs=print` format from `git replay`—but only if the output can be made "nice and consistent." He stresses that any feedback mechanism must handle not just HEAD but any reference that might be updated, including detached HEAD states.

On the question of safety checks, Steinhardt pushes back against mandatory reachability verification. While he sees value in a "strict" mode (as proposed by Knoble) that would enforce reachability, he insists it should not be the default. Instead, he prefers a permissive design where users can opt into stricter behavior, preserving the ability to rewrite commits that are not reachable from HEAD. This aligns with the tool’s experimental, user-focused nature.

The discussion leaves open whether strict mode should be opt-in or opt-out, but the direction is clear: feedback will be added, and safety checks will be configurable rather than mandatory. The patch series is still in flux, but this reply narrows the design space for the next iteration.
