This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing ODB performance regression thread, specifically Junio’s reply to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposed transaction-aware ODB write redesign. Junio acknowledges Patrick’s architectural direction but emphasizes that it is largely independent of the immediate performance fix needed for the regression. He points out that Peff’s reproduction case—using the alternates mechanism to set up one object store borrowing from another—demonstrates the core issue without any transaction or temporary directory manipulation: the regression stems from the ODB rework’s shift from a "pack-first, loose-second" lookup *across backends* to a "pack-loose, pack-loose" strategy, causing redundant loose object checks even when objects exist in packs. Junio’s comment suggests that while Patrick’s long-term redesign may eventually resolve the issue, a targeted fix addressing the lookup order change is still needed to restore performance in the short term. The email is concise and substantive, clarifying that the architectural and immediate performance goals are separable.
