This is the second patch in Son Luong Ngoc’s two-patch bugfix series for `git rebase --update-refs` handling of symbolic references (symrefs). The series targets edge cases where symrefs—particularly those pointing outside `refs/heads/`—cause rebase failures due to duplicate or cross-worktree ref updates.

This patch specifically guards against unsafe updates to non-branch symrefs (e.g., a branch symref pointing to a tag). It ensures that if a symref’s resolved target is checked out in another worktree, or if multiple symrefs resolve to the same target, the rebase will skip updating those refs rather than risking a failed compare-and-swap operation. The implementation checks both the literal symref and its resolved target against a list of checked-out reservations, deduplicating updates by target ref. It also extends the reservation logic to include symref targets when loading another worktree’s update-refs state, preventing cross-worktree conflicts.

The changes are minimal and focused, touching `branch.c` (to track symref targets during checked-out branch preparation) and `sequencer.c` (to enforce the new safeguards during rebase). The test coverage in `t3404-rebase-interactive.sh` is thorough, verifying behavior for checked-out targets, deduplication, and reservation handling across worktrees. The tests confirm that symrefs remain intact after rebase and that the correct refs are skipped or updated.

This patch directly addresses feedback from Phillip Wood’s substantive review of earlier versions, particularly around cross-worktree safety and deduplication. The series is now technically complete, with clear commit messages and test coverage demonstrating the fixes for all identified edge cases. It looks ready for inclusion, complementing the first patch’s handling of branch-to-branch symrefs.
