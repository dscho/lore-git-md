This thread introduces a `no-ref-delta` capability for Git’s pack protocol, allowing receivers to instruct senders to omit `REF_DELTA` entries from generated packs. The goal is to simplify pack processing for receivers that don’t want to build an object ID index upfront. Taylor Blau posted the initial v1 series, but a malformed Message-ID caused the patches to appear as a separate thread, complicating review.

The series has hit a major roadblock with Jeff King’s (Peff) substantive review. Peff questions the fundamental motivation behind the feature, asking why a receiver would want to retain a pack without first indexing it—especially since updating refs without knowing the pack’s contents could allow repository corruption. He also raises concerns about thin packs, which can introduce `REF_DELTA` entries server-side after the pack is completed, and notes that Git’s `receive-pack` doesn’t currently support the `no-thin` capability. This suggests the series may need to address fallback behavior or error handling if a server requests `no-ref-delta` but receives a thin pack anyway.

No other reviews have been posted yet, and the series remains under active discussion. The next steps will likely involve Taylor clarifying the use case—whether this is part of a larger system (e.g., a custom `receive-pack` implementation) or an optimization for a specific workflow—or revising the approach to address Peff’s concerns. Until then, the series is unlikely to move forward.
