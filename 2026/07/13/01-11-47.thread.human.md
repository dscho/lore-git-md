This thread is about a new `no-ref-delta` capability for Git’s pack protocol, proposed by Taylor Blau to enable write-through proxies to process packs asynchronously without building an object ID index upfront. The feature would allow receivers to request packs containing only `OFS_DELTA` entries, avoiding the complications of `REF_DELTA` lookups in the proxy’s pipeline.

The series is at v1 and under active review. Jeff King (Peff) has raised fundamental questions about the design’s motivation and trade-offs. Initially, Peff questioned whether the feature was necessary at all, but Taylor clarified that the use case—a write-through proxy that spools packs byte-for-byte before replaying them upstream—requires `OFS_DELTA`-only packs to avoid pipeline stalls caused by `REF_DELTA` entries with late-appearing bases. Peff’s latest response digs deeper into the technical rationale, suggesting that the real issue isn’t the general overhead of `REF_DELTA` indexing but the worst-case scenario where a long delta chain depends on a base that appears late in the pack. He proposes an alternative: a tighter capability enforcing “bases before deltas” ordering, which Git already generates by default, as a way to prevent stalls without banning `REF_DELTA` outright.

The open questions now center on whether `no-ref-delta` is the right solution or if a narrower capability could achieve the same performance goals with less protocol disruption. Taylor’s next response will likely determine whether the series proceeds as-is, pivots to Peff’s suggested alternative, or requires further refinement. The discussion remains technical and focused on the proxy’s specific needs, with no broader consensus yet on the feature’s long-term role in Git’s protocol.
