Unlike a one-time rebase, this isn’t something that "goes away" after the initial merge—it’s a recurring annoyance that forces contributors to remember the new file locations (e.g., `wt-status.c` → `lib/wt-status.c`) or manually adjust their workflows.

Gábor questions whether such improvements are even feasible ("I'm uncertain how that should work") and doubts the project has the bandwidth to implement them. He implies that Patrick, as the author of the disruptive change, should bear the responsibility for delivering those tooling fixes *before* the reorganization lands, rather than using the patch as an "incentive" for future work. His tone suggests this feels like an unfunded mandate: the pain is immediate, but the promised relief is speculative.

Patrick views the reorganization as a net win for code organization, even if it requires temporary discomfort and spurs tooling improvements. Gábor, however, sees it as trading one set of annoyances (root-directory clutter) for another (broken history tracking and mental overhead) with no clear path to resolution. His closing line—"this just trades one annoyance for the other, and it's not worth it"—makes it clear he remains unconvinced by the series’ rationale.
