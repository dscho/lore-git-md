Harald Nordgren's recently merged enhancement to `git status` now shows divergence information for both upstream and push tracking branches, after 25 iterations of careful review. The implementation maintains all existing behavior while cleanly adding the new push branch status on a separate line, with comprehensive test coverage for various remote configurations and edge cases. Junio Hamano approved the series for merging after all feedback was addressed, including Phillip Wood's input on push.default handling and advice display.

However, Jeff King (Peff) has raised a practical concern post-merge. In his triangular workflow - where he works on a 'jch' branch and uses the remote as a simple mirror - the new push branch status appears as unnecessary noise. While the output can be disabled globally via `status.aheadbehind`, this would also hide the upstream branch information he finds valuable. Peff notes this information also appears unconditionally in `git checkout` output. His feedback suggests that even after extensive pre-merge testing, real-world usage reveals workflow variations that might benefit from more granular control over the status display - perhaps a configuration to suppress just the push branch status while retaining upstream information. This may prompt follow-up discussion about making the push status display more context-sensitive.
