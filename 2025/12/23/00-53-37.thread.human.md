Harald Nordgren's long-running effort to enhance `git status` with push tracking information has reached its final form and been approved for merging into `next`. After 24 iterations of review, the series now cleanly shows divergence information for both upstream and push branches when they differ, while maintaining all existing output formats and behaviors. The implementation consists of two patches: a core refactoring that centralizes branch comparison logic, and the push tracking addition that handles remapped refspecs and maintains quick-mode behavior. Key refinements in the final version include renamed flags for clearer advice-display control and simplified push branch resolution.

Junio Hamano has given his approval after the last round of feedback was addressed, particularly around the advice-display logic where Phillip Wood pushed for consistency. The series includes extensive test coverage and preserves all edge case handling from previous versions. While the current implementation assumes `push.default=current`, any follow-up work to handle other push configurations can be addressed separately. This represents a significant quality-of-life improvement for users who work with multiple remotes or complex push configurations.
