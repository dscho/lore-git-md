This is a technical discussion about performance implications of the reftable backend versus the traditional packed-refs approach, specifically regarding reference verification and peeling overhead during `git pack-refs`.

Jeff King responds to brian m. carlson's concerns about optimizing ref verification by noting that while reftable performs similar peeling operations, it distributes the cost differently. The reftable backend amortizes the peeling overhead across ref updates rather than concentrating it during packing operations. King explains that reftable may actually reduce total object accesses since it can leverage already-loaded objects from previous operations, and it avoids re-peeling during compaction. The discussion highlights how reftable's design inherently changes the performance characteristics compared to the files backend, though both ultimately perform the same verification work.

Key technical details include reftable's ability to maintain peeled references during compaction and its potential to cache object information from prior operations. The exchange reinforces that reftable doesn't eliminate verification needs but reorganizes when and how often the work occurs.
