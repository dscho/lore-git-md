This thread explores a proposal to extend commit signing configuration by generalizing the `commit.signOff` option beyond just `git format-patch` to cover other commands like `commit`, `merge`, and `revert`. The discussion has revealed deep historical and legal context around Signed-off-by (SOB) trailers, with key insights from Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón and Junio C Hamano. They've emphasized that SOB carries specific legal weight in open source, referencing a 2020 rejection of config-based SOB due to concerns it could weaken the Developer Certificate of Origin's standing by enabling "inertia" sign-offs.

The core tension remains between workflow convenience (particularly for tools like lazygit) and preserving the intentionality behind SOB certification. Junio has proposed resurrecting a 2020 documentation patch to clearly explain Git's position against automatic SOB trailers. Collin Funk recently contributed a thoughtful documentation refinement, suggesting spelling out "Signed-off-by" instead of using "SoB" for consistency and clarity. This shows the discussion shifting from debating the feature's merits to carefully crafting how to communicate the rationale, particularly the legal concerns, to future contributors. The thread remains open to exploring whether alternative approaches might satisfy tooling needs without compromising certification integrity.
