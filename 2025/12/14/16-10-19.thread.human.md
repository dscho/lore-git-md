This thread has reached a clear resolution regarding Git's stance on automatic commit sign-offs. The discussion began with a proposal to extend the `commit.signOff` configuration option beyond `format-patch` to other commands like `commit` and `merge`, motivated by lazygit's workflow. However, maintainers including Junio C Hamano and Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón emphasized the legal significance of Signed-off-by (SOB) trailers in open source contribution workflows, explaining that they represent a Developer Certificate of Origin that requires explicit action.

After thorough discussion about the project's historical position (dating back to a 2020 rejection of similar functionality), the consensus has solidified around maintaining the status quo where sign-off must remain an explicit action. Brian m. carlson contributed refinements to the legal rationale text, which Junio has now formalized in a patch adding documentation to both the Git FAQ and signoff option help text. The documentation makes clear that while `format.signoff` exists as a historical exception, the project will not expand automatic sign-off functionality to other commands, as this could undermine the legal standing of the certification in court.

The patch serves as a definitive statement of Git project policy, with all participants agreeing that SOB must remain an intentional act rather than a configuration-driven automation. This closes the discussion with clear guidance for both users and tool authors, while preserving the legal integrity of the contribution certification process. No further technical changes are expected on this topic.
