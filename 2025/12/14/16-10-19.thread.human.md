This thread has been a thorough examination of Git's stance on automatic sign-offs and the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO). It began with a feature request to expand automatic sign-off support beyond `git format-patch`, which led to a deeper discussion about the legal implications of the DCO and why Git maintains that sign-offs must be intentional acts.

Junio C Hamano and Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón provided crucial insights about the legal standing of the DCO, establishing that automatic sign-offs would weaken its certification value. Junio then contributed documentation patches to formalize Git's position, with identical legal rationale appearing in both the FAQ and signoff option sections. Brian m. carlson confirmed maintainer consensus on this approach.

The discussion has since focused on refining the documentation's clarity while preserving the established policy. Elijah Newren suggested improved wording, and now Johannes Sixt has proposed further streamlining of the text while maintaining all key legal reasoning. The only remaining question is whether to add cross-references between the FAQ and command documentation to help users find the full explanation.

All technical matters are settled: `format.signoff` remains the sole exception where automatic sign-offs are allowed, with all other commands requiring explicit flags. The thread demonstrates Git's careful balance between user convenience and legal requirements, with maintainers united in preserving the intentionality of DCO certification. The documentation is now receiving its final polish before this long-running discussion concludes.
