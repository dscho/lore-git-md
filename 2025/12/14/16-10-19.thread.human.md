This thread began as a feature request to expand automatic sign-off functionality beyond `git format-patch` to commands like `commit`, `merge`, and `revert`. However, it evolved into a deeper discussion about the legal implications of the Signed-off-by (SOB) trailer and the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO). Junio C Hamano and Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón provided key insights, emphasizing that automatic sign-offs could weaken the legal standing of the DCO by making it too easy to add without proper consideration.

The discussion reached consensus that no technical changes would be made, and instead, the focus shifted to improving documentation to clearly communicate Git's position. Junio contributed a documentation patch that adds authoritative guidance explaining why automatic sign-offs (except for the historical `format.signoff` exception) are intentionally not supported. The patch includes identical legal rationale text in both the FAQ and signoff option documentation sections, warning that making sign-off too easy could undermine its legal validity. Brian m. carlson co-authored the legal phrasing and has now confirmed their approval of the final documentation.

With this acknowledgment, the thread concludes, having successfully transformed a recurring discussion into permanent reference material that should prevent future debates on this topic. The key takeaway is that Git maintains a clear stance: sign-offs must remain explicit actions through command-line flags to preserve the legal intentionality of the DCO.
