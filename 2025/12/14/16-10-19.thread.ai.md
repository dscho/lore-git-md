Thread summary:

Feature proposal and subsequent documentation patch regarding commit signing configuration, focusing on the `commit.signOff` option and its potential expansion beyond `git format-patch`. The discussion has evolved from a technical proposal to a documentation effort formalizing Git's stance against automatic sign-offs.

The thread originated with a feature request to generalize sign-off behavior to commands like `commit`, `merge`, and `revert`, motivated by lazygit's needs. This uncovered deep historical and legal context about Signed-off-by (SOB) trailer semantics, with Junio C Hamano and Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón providing key insights about the Developer Certificate of Origin's legal standing. The core tension between user convenience and maintaining SOB's legal intentionality was identified, with maintainers emphasizing the need for explicit `--signoff` flags.

Junio now contributes a documentation patch that codifies Git's position, adding two authoritative sections (FAQ and signoff option documentation) with identical legal rationale text. The documentation explains why automatic sign-offs weaken the Developer Certificate of Origin by enabling "inertia" sign-offs, while acknowledging `format.signoff` as a historical exception that won't be expanded. The text warns that making sign-off too easy could undermine its legal validity in court. Brian m. carlson is credited as co-author for their input on the legal argument's phrasing.

Collin Funk previously contributed documentation refinements (spelling out "Signed-off-by" instead of "SoB"), and the current patch maintains this consistency. The discussion has reached consensus that no technical changes will be made, with all energy focused on improving documentation to clearly communicate the project's position. The historical context remains relevant - automatic sign-offs were rejected in 2020 due to concerns about weakening contribution certification, and this patch serves to prevent repeated discussions by providing definitive reference material.

Key technical details remain unchanged: the `format.signoff` configuration exists for historical reasons but won't be extended to other commands, and all new sign-offs must remain explicit actions through command-line flags. The documentation patch represents the thread's conclusion, providing authoritative guidance for users and tool authors while maintaining the legal integrity of the contribution certification process.
