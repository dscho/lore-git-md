This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about modifying `git reset --hard`'s behavior to prevent accidental loss of staged-but-uncommitted content. The original proposal to warn users has evolved into a debate about whether Git should refuse the operation entirely when the destination tree is empty (covering pre-first-commit and post-empty-commit scenarios).

Stefanos Koutsouflakis responds to maintainer Junio Hamano's skepticism about special-casing empty trees, arguing that scripts relying on the current behavior are likely buggy anyway. He suggests two alternative mitigation approaches: (1) failing with a clear error message suggesting `git rm --cached` instead, or (2) automatically stashing the changes with a warning.

The exchange highlights the tension between protecting users from data loss and maintaining Git's principle of predictable destructive operations. While technical solutions exist, the core philosophical disagreement remains unresolved, with Hamano previously rejecting even the narrowed empty-tree prevention approach. The thread appears to be exploring whether any compromise solution could satisfy both safety concerns and Git's design principles.
