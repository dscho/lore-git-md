This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about modifying `git reset --hard`'s behavior to prevent accidental loss of staged-but-uncommitted content. The thread has reached an impasse with maintainer Junio Hamano firmly rejecting even the narrowed proposal to prevent `--hard` operations when the destination tree is empty (covering pre-first-commit and post-empty-commit scenarios).

Original author Koutsouflakis Stefanos seeks clarification on Hamano's objection to the empty-tree special case, arguing that scripts relying on this behavior would likely be buggy anyway. He proposes two alternative mitigation approaches: (1) failing with a fatal error and suggesting `git rm --cached -r .` as an alternative, or (2) implementing an autostash mechanism with warnings.

The email highlights the core tension between practical protection needs and Git's design principles, with Hamano having previously dismissed similar safety mechanisms as philosophically inconsistent (comparing `--hard --force` to meaningless constructs like `rm -f -i`). Stefanos' suggestions represent attempts to find middle ground, though the maintainer's strong stance suggests these may face similar rejection.

Technical context remains focused on `builtin/reset.c`, with the empty-tree check implementation having been technically viable but philosophically unacceptable. The discussion appears to be shifting toward exploring non-behavioral solutions like improved recovery documentation, as evidenced by D. Ben Knoble's recent contribution about `git fsck --lost-found` techniques.
