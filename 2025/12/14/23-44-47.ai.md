This is a continuation of the discussion about commit signing configuration, specifically addressing the cultural and legal implications of automatically adding Signed-off-by (SOB) trailers. Junio C Hamano references a 2020 patch proposal that explicitly rejected adding a `commit.signOff` config option due to concerns about the legal weight of SOB trailers when added automatically.

The key new information is the quoted documentation patch that explains Git's position: automatic SOB addition could weaken the legal standing of the Developer Certificate of Origin by making it easier to argue in court that the sign-off was done by "inertia" rather than intentional certification. This reinforces the earlier point about SOB trailers needing explicit, conscious application.

The email suggests this historical context could help potential contributors understand why Git maintains its current stance, preventing wasted effort on proposals that would violate this principle. The discussion continues to highlight the tension between user convenience (particularly for tools like lazygit) and the need to preserve the legal and cultural significance of SOB trailers in open source contribution workflows.
