This is a maintainer response in the ongoing debate about modifying `git reset --hard` behavior to prevent accidental data loss. Junio C Hamano reiterates his opposition to any changes that would make `reset --hard` non-destructive, even in the specific case of empty trees (pre-first-commit or post-empty-commit scenarios).

Hamano argues that special-casing empty trees would create inconsistent behavior, and maintains that `reset --hard` must remain fully destructive as per its documented purpose. He pushes back against the idea that scripts relying on this behavior are buggy, noting his own reliance on `reset --hard && clean -f -x` as a common workflow to completely clean a working tree.

The response suggests the discussion may have reached an impasse, with Hamano indicating that any change making `reset --hard` fail in these cases would be seen as a regression rather than an improvement. This reinforces the thread's trajectory toward documentation improvements rather than behavior changes as the likely resolution path.
