This is a maintainer response in the ongoing discussion about making `git reset --hard` safer for uncommitted work. Junio Hamano weighs in on Johannes Sixt's proposal to have `reset --hard` refuse operation when the destination tree is empty (covering both pre-first-commit and post-empty-commit scenarios).

Hamano expresses skepticism about special-casing empty trees, noting that data loss more commonly occurs in non-empty scenarios when developers reset after adding new files or making modifications. He acknowledges the problem but sees no ideal solution - interactive prompts are unacceptable, and a simple failure would likely lead to awkward `--hard --force` workarounds.

The response maintains Hamano's previously stated philosophical position against changing `--hard`'s destructive nature, though he concedes he has no better alternatives to offer. This keeps the thread's core tension intact: between the desire to prevent common data loss scenarios and the maintainer's reluctance to modify Git's explicit-destruction design principle. The discussion remains focused on implementation details (empty tree checks) while the fundamental disagreement persists.
