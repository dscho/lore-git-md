Maintainer response in the `git reset --hard` safety discussion. Junio C Hamano remains skeptical about special-casing empty-tree cases for `reset --hard` protection, despite acknowledging the common pain point of losing uncommitted work. He explicitly doubts the palatability of Johannes Sixt's proposed solution to refuse operations when the destination tree is empty, calling it the least bad option among problematic alternatives.

The email highlights two key tensions in the discussion: (1) the maintainer's philosophical resistance to changing `--hard`'s destructive semantics, even for narrowly scoped cases, and (2) the practical challenge of implementing protections without creating awkward workarounds like `--hard --force`. Hamano draws a parallel to the meaningless combination of `rm -f -i`, suggesting any bypass mechanism would be similarly nonsensical.

Notably, while Hamano recognizes the frequency of the workflow where users lose significant uncommitted work (particularly when new files outweigh modifications), he maintains that special-casing either empty trees or new file removal would be inconsistent with Git's design principles. The response leaves the thread at an impasse - while technical solutions have converged on empty-tree checks, core maintainer objections remain regarding any deviation from `--hard`'s current behavior.
