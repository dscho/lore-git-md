New participant perspective in the git-history command discussion. Kristoffer Haugsbakk weighs in on the ongoing debate about multi-branch commit handling in the proposed `git-history` command, responding to Matthias Beyer's preference for explicit opt-in behavior.

Haugsbakk challenges the characterization of multi-branch updates as "magic," arguing that both commit creation and branch pointer updates are conceptually similar operations. He suggests that updating all affected branches (the second step) is no more complex or magical than creating new commits (the first step), and that neither operation should be considered inherently more advanced.

This continues the thread's central tension between those who favor automatic multi-branch handling (like Junio Hamano) and those who prefer explicit opt-in (like Beyer). Haugsbakk's perspective leans toward the former camp by questioning the distinction between what's considered "simple" versus "magic" in the operation sequence. The email doesn't propose new technical solutions but rather adds philosophical weight to the automatic-update side of the debate.
