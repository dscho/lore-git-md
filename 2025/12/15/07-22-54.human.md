This is a thread conclusion in the `git reset --hard` behavior discussion. Koutsouflakis Stefanos formally withdraws their RFC proposal to modify `--hard`'s behavior after maintainer Junio Hamano's strong objections. The proposal had sought to prevent or warn about destruction of staged-but-uncommitted content in empty-tree scenarios (like pre-first-commit states).

Hamano's decisive counterarguments centered on two points: the inconsistency of special-casing empty trees, and the established reliance of workflows (like his own `reset --hard && clean -f -x` pattern) on the current destructive behavior. He maintained that users would perceive any safety checks as bugs in `reset --hard` rather than improvements.

Stefanos acknowledges the maintainer's broader perspective on Git's design philosophy and usage patterns, conceding that their view was too narrow. The withdrawal marks the end of this discussion thread, with no changes planned to `reset --hard`'s behavior. The outcome preserves Git's current documented semantics while validating recovery tools like `git fsck --lost-found` as the recommended solution for accidental data loss.
