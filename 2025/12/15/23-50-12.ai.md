This is a discussion email in the ongoing `git-history` command RFC thread, focusing on the debate around multi-branch commit handling behavior. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Matthias Beyer's concerns about "magic" in automatic branch rewriting, arguing that both commit rewriting and branch updates are conceptually similar operations that shouldn't be treated differently in terms of user expectations.

The email challenges Beyer's position that explicit opt-in behavior should be the default, suggesting that updating all affected branches when rewriting commits is a natural extension of the operation rather than "magic". Haugsbakk makes the case that both parts of the operation (creating new commits and updating branch pointers) are equally fundamental to history editing, implying the current-branch-only approach creates artificial limitations.

This continues the core philosophical debate in the thread between automatic multi-branch handling (favored by Junio Hamano and Phillip Wood) versus explicit opt-in behavior (advocated by Beyer and initially by Patrick Steinhardt). The exchange highlights differing perspectives on what constitutes "simple" behavior, with Haugsbakk positioning branch updates as a logical consequence of commit rewriting rather than a separate magical operation.

Key technical context remains the same as in previous thread updates: the proposed `git-history` command with `reword` and `split` subcommands, ongoing discussions about safety mechanisms (immutable refs vs flags), and the tension between workflow flexibility and protection against accidental history rewrites. The email doesn't introduce new technical proposals but sharpens the conceptual argument for multi-branch handling.
