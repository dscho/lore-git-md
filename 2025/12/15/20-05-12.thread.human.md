This thread documents and discusses the behavior of `git rev-list --filter` when objects are explicitly provided. The initial patch from Justin Tobler clarifies in the documentation that such objects bypass filter restrictions by default, only respecting filters when `--filter-provided-objects` is specified. While the documentation change is uncontroversial and has been queued by Junio Hamano, it has sparked a deeper discussion about the design.

Patrick Steinhardt, who originally implemented `--filter-provided-objects`, explains that the current behavior was a backward compatibility compromise rather than an intentional design choice. While acknowledging the behavior is surprising, he took a conservative approach during implementation due to concerns about potential impact on partial clone users, even though no positive use cases for the current default behavior are known. Justin Tobler's probing questions have highlighted that the behavior may lack clear utility beyond maintaining compatibility.

All agree the documentation should accurately reflect the current implementation, but the discussion leaves open whether this behavior should be preserved long-term. Changing it would require research to assess potential breakage, particularly for complex systems like partial clones. The thread provides important context about why this surprising behavior exists while preserving the option to revisit the design in future if safety can be established.
