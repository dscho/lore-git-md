This is a discussion about the multi-branch handling behavior in the proposed `git-history` command, specifically around safety considerations for history rewriting operations. Martin von Zweigbergk responds to Matthias Beyer's concerns about rewriting commits that affect multiple branches by default.

Martin suggests taking inspiration from Jujutsu's (jj) approach of having configurable immutable ref patterns, which provides a more flexible way to protect certain branches from being rewritten accidentally. He notes this has worked well in Jujutsu's implementation, where immutable revisions are respected across all commands. This offers a middle ground between the current debate about whether to rewrite all branches by default (as Junio Hamano prefers) or require explicit opt-in (as Matthias Beyer advocates).

The discussion highlights an important design tension in the tradeoff between safety and convenience for history editing operations. Martin's proposal would allow users to define their own branches that should never be rewritten, rather than having the tool make assumptions about which branches are "safe" to modify. This builds on the thread's ongoing theme of learning from Jujutsu's implementation while adapting those concepts to Git's workflows.
