This is a discussion about safety mechanisms for Git's proposed `git-history` command, specifically around Martin von Zweigbergk's proposal for configurable immutable ref patterns inspired by Jujutsu's model.

Martin responds to Elijah Newren's question about how immutable revisions would handle a commit that appears in multiple branches. He clarifies that if any branch containing commit B is marked as immutable, attempting to rewrite B would be blocked entirely - not just for that branch, but for all branches where it appears. This maintains consistency by preventing partial rewrites of shared history.

The proposal draws directly from Jujutsu's established immutable commits system, where certain refs (like published branches) can be marked as immutable to prevent accidental rewrites. Martin argues this is the right behavior since rewriting a shared commit like B in some branches but not others would create inconsistency.

This continues the thread's central debate about how to balance safety with flexibility in history rewriting. Martin's position favors prevention (via immutability) over opt-in controls, seeing it as a more robust solution than the current flag-based compromises being discussed. The exchange highlights the philosophical divide between those who want granular control over single-branch operations and those who prioritize preventing history inconsistencies across branches.
