This is a discussion thread about the behavior of the proposed `git-history` command's multi-branch commit handling, part of the ongoing RFC series introducing new history editing capabilities.

Phillip Wood responds to Matthias Beyer's preference for conservative, single-branch rewriting behavior by default in the `git-history` command. Matthias had expressed that he expects the tool to only affect the current branch unless explicitly told otherwise, valuing explicitness over automatic multi-branch operations. Phillip acknowledges this perspective as valuable feedback, noting that he had previously assumed rewriting all descendant branches would be the natural default behavior.

The exchange highlights a key design tension in the feature: whether to prioritize safety through conservative single-branch operations (Matthias's view) or convenience through automatic multi-branch synchronization (Phillip's initial assumption). This discussion feeds into the broader debate in the thread about how the command should handle commits that are ancestors of multiple branches, with various participants advocating different approaches ranging from strict current-branch-only behavior to automatic multi-branch rewriting with safety checks.

The email doesn't propose new technical solutions but rather captures an important user perspective that informs the ongoing design discussion. Phillip's response shows openness to reconsidering default behaviors based on user expectations, which may influence the eventual compromise solution being discussed in the series.
