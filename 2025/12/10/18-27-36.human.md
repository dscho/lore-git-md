This is a discussion about the proposed `git-history` command's behavior regarding multi-branch commit rewriting, specifically focusing on safety mechanisms for history editing.

Elijah Newren responds to Martin von Zweigbergk's suggestion of using configurable immutable ref patterns (inspired by Jujutsu's implementation) as a safety mechanism for history rewriting operations. While Newren finds the immutable revisions concept appealing, he questions whether it would actually solve the immediate use case being discussed - pointing out that in the example graph where commit B is being dropped, an immutable revisions approach would likely error out rather than allow rewriting just one branch.

The exchange highlights the ongoing tension in the design between:
1) Automatic multi-branch rewriting (preferred by Junio Hamano and others)
2) Conservative single-branch operations (advocated by Matthias Beyer)
3) New middle-ground approaches like immutable ref patterns (proposed by Martin von Zweigbergk)

Newren's question suggests the immutable revisions approach might not fully address the workflow concerns raised earlier in the thread, as it would prevent rather than selectively allow certain rewrites. This keeps open the fundamental debate about whether the tool should err on the side of safety (potentially requiring more user intervention) or convenience (automatically handling complex cases but with more potential for mistakes).
