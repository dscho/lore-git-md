Phillip Wood weighs in to support Martin von Zweigbergk and Elijah Newren's preference for option (c) - rewriting all branches containing the commit by default. The discussion references Jujutsu's implementation (where Martin is a maintainer) as evidence that the multi-branch rewrite approach works well in practice, with `jj duplicate` serving as an escape hatch for rare cases where users want more control. This email doesn't introduce new technical details about the implementation, but reinforces the ongoing philosophical debate about the command's default behavior. The key tension remains between:
1. The Git tradition of conservative defaults (favored by Patrick and Matthias)
2. Avoiding repeating `git-rebase`'s limitations (Elijah's concern)
3. The practical experience from Jujutsu showing multi-branch rewriting works well (Martin's position)

Phillip's suggestion of a configurable flag attempts to bridge these positions while keeping the discussion moving forward. The thread shows no clear consensus yet, with maintainer Junio Hamano still advocating for experimental inclusion in 'next' despite the unresolved debate.
