This is a maintainer comment in the ongoing `git-history` command RFC discussion, specifically addressing the multi-branch rewrite behavior debate. Junio Hamano weighs in on the philosophical divide between explicit control (Matthias Beyer's position) and automatic multi-branch handling (Phillip Wood's current proposal).

Hamano acknowledges the validity of both perspectives - viewing the tool as either a low-level building block (favoring explicit control) or a complete workflow solution (where automatic behavior may be preferable). While personally leaning toward the "building block" approach in general, he expresses that for this specific case of history rewriting, he finds it less confusing to automatically move all branch refs away from obsoleted commits during rewrites.

This positions Hamano closer to Phillip Wood's current stance (multi-branch rewrite by default with opt-out) than Matthias Beyer's preference for single-branch operations by default. The comment reinforces the fundamental tension in the discussion between Git's traditional conservatism and the desire to avoid `git-rebase`'s limitations, while suggesting the history editing context may warrant more automatic behavior than usual.

Key technical context referenced includes:
- Ongoing debate about default behavior (single-branch vs multi-branch)
- The concept of "obsoleted commits" during rewriting
- The distinction between tool-as-building-block vs complete-workflow-solution perspectives

This moves the thread toward resolution by indicating the maintainer's leaning on the core philosophical question, though without final decision or technical implementation direction. The comment leaves room for either a flag-based compromise or documentation-based solution to address the user experience concerns raised by Beyer.
