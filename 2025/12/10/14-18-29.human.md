This is a maintainer comment on the ongoing `git-history` command design discussion, specifically addressing the debate around multi-branch commit handling behavior.

Junio Hamano weighs in on the philosophical divide between those who want the new `git-history` command to automatically update all branches containing rewritten commits (Phillip Wood, Elijah Newren) versus those preferring explicit opt-in behavior (Matthias Beyer, Patrick Steinhardt). Hamano reveals his own leaning toward automatically moving all branch refs away from obsoleted commits, suggesting this would be less confusing for users. This aligns with Phillip Wood's current compromise proposal (default to rewriting all branches with a `--single-branch` opt-out flag) while also recognizing the validity of Matthias Beyer's user experience arguments about explicit control.

The comment doesn't introduce new technical details but provides important maintainer perspective on the core philosophical question that has dominated the thread. Hamano's position as Git's maintainer makes this a significant signal about the likely resolution direction, though he doesn't issue a final ruling here.
