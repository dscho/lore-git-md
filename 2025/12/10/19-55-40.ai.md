This is a discussion about safety mechanisms for the proposed `git-history` command, specifically around the handling of immutable ref patterns. The exchange between Elijah Newren and Martin von Zweigbergk clarifies how Jujutsu's immutable commit model would apply to Git's history editing operations.

Elijah initially expresses confusion about whether immutable refs would allow single-branch operations (like rewriting just branch2 in the example), but realizes he misremembered Matthias Beyer's original position. Martin confirms that immutable refs would indeed block all operations on protected branches as designed - if any branch containing commit B is marked immutable, attempting to drop B would fail entirely rather than allowing partial rewrites.

The discussion highlights that Jujutsu's immutable ref patterns provide stronger safety guarantees than flag-based approaches by preventing any modifications to protected history, but at the cost of workflow flexibility. This creates a clear philosophical divide between prevention (immutability) and opt-in (flags) approaches to safety. The immutable ref model would intentionally block single-branch operations on protected branches, which Martin defends as the desired behavior for preventing accidental history rewrites.

This exchange helps clarify that the immutability proposal isn't a middle ground between single-branch and multi-branch behaviors, but rather an orthogonal safety mechanism that would enforce stricter protections than either approach. The conversation also surfaces that this model comes from Jujutsu's established configuration system where immutable revisions are respected by all commands.
