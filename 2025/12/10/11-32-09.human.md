This is a discussion about the multi-branch commit handling behavior in the proposed `git-history` command, part of Patrick Steinhardt's v6 series. Phillip Wood weighs in on the ongoing debate between three approaches for handling commits that exist in multiple branches.

Phillip agrees with Martin von Zweigbergk and Elijah Newren that option (c) - rewriting all branches containing the commit by default - is the ideal behavior, noting this matches Jujutsu's successful implementation. However, he acknowledges that some users find this behavior surprising and suggests adding a flag to control the behavior as a compromise.

The email references prior discussion where Patrick Steinhardt had proposed a hybrid of options (a) and (b) - erroring on multi-branch commits by default with a flag to override - citing workflow and performance concerns. Martin's response provides Jujutsu's real-world experience, noting that the "rewrite some descendants but not others" use case is rare enough that their users just use workarounds when needed.

This exchange highlights the fundamental design tension between Git's traditional conservative defaults and the desire to avoid repeating `git-rebase`'s limitations. The discussion remains unresolved but appears to be moving toward some form of configurable behavior rather than a strict single approach.
