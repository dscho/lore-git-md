This is a discussion about safety mechanisms for Git's proposed `git-history` command, specifically around handling multi-branch rewrites. Martin von Zweigbergk suggests adopting Jujutsu's configurable immutable ref patterns as a way to prevent accidental rewrites of shared history. Elijah Newren engages with the idea but questions whether it would actually address the core concern raised earlier by Matthias Beyer about single-branch operations.

The key technical point is whether immutable ref patterns would provide the right granularity of control. While Jujutsu's model prevents rewrites of marked branches entirely (resulting in errors), Matthias and others want the ability to rewrite just the current branch by default, with explicit opt-in for multi-branch operations. Elijah's question highlights that immutable refs might be too coarse-grained, as they would block all rewrites of shared commits rather than allowing selective single-branch modifications.

This exchange represents an important philosophical fork in the design: whether to prevent dangerous operations entirely (via immutability) versus making them opt-in (via flags). The discussion connects to earlier debates about whether `git-history` should default to single-branch or multi-branch behavior, with Junio Hamano having recently expressed preference for the latter. Martin's proposal offers a third way via configuration, but as Elijah notes, it may not fully align with the workflow concerns that motivated Matthias's position.
