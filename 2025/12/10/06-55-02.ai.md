This is a continuation of the debate about multi-branch commit handling in the `git-history` command series. Elijah Newren strongly reiterates his opposition to Patrick Steinhardt's current-branch-only approach, framing it as repeating what he considers a fundamental design mistake in `git-rebase`. He presents three alternative approaches (a-c) for handling commits that exist in multiple branches, with a clear preference for option (c) which would rewrite all containing branches by default.

The exchange reveals key technical context: `git-replay` was designed from the start with multi-branch operations in mind (option c), while Patrick's git-history implementation currently takes the opposite approach. Elijah states this difference in design goals so strongly that he would actively recommend against using git-history command if merged with its current behavior, though he acknowledges the final call isn't his to make.

Patrick appears open to considering a hybrid of options (a) and (b) - checking for single-branch commits and requiring flags for multi-branch cases - but raises concerns about implementation efficiency. The discussion remains unresolved but has clarified the fundamental philosophical divide between the approaches, with Elijah drawing a direct line to his past frustrations with `git-rebase`'s limitations.
