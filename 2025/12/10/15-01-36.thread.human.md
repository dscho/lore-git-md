This thread discusses a proposal to make `git reset --hard` safer in edge cases where it could destroy uncommitted work, particularly before the first commit in a repository. The original author, Koutsouflakis Stefanos, focused on the high-risk scenario where `reset --hard` could erase an entire project's worth of staged files with no recovery path. Maintainer Junio C Hamano has been skeptical, maintaining that `--hard` is explicitly designed to be destructive.

Johannes Sixt (Hannes) has now joined the discussion, identifying another dangerous case where users might destroy staged work after creating an empty initial commit. He suggests a technical solution: having `git reset --hard` refuse to operate when the destination tree is empty, which would cover both scenarios. This represents a new direction in the discussion, though it still faces Junio's philosophical resistance to changing `--hard`'s behavior.

The core disagreement remains between those advocating for safety in these edge cases and those who believe destructive operations should remain explicit. Hannes's proposal offers a potential compromise, but the thread remains at an impasse with no clear resolution yet. The discussion has narrowed from general staged content protection to these specific high-risk scenarios while maintaining focus on the technical implementation in `builtin/reset.c`.
