This thread introduces a safety enhancement proposal for `git reset --hard` to prevent accidental loss of staged but uncommitted changes. The author identifies a common foot-gun scenario where users stage new files in a fresh repository, then permanently lose that work with an unforced reset before ever committing. The proposed solution would require `--force` in such cases, accompanied by a warning listing the affected files.

The core argument is that this aligns with Git's philosophy of protecting users from destructive operations by default. The author provides a clear reproduction case and outlines the warning message format. Key discussion points include whether the safety benefit justifies the added complexity, and whether any legitimate workflows would be disrupted by the new requirement. The proposal appears well-considered, touching only reset's safety checks while preserving the ability to force through when needed. Documentation updates would be required if accepted. The author is prepared to implement the change pending community feedback.
