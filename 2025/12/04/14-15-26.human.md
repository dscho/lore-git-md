This is part 2/11 of a hook subsystem refactoring series, focusing on parallel process infrastructure. The patch adds stdin handling capabilities to `run_processes_parallel()`, enabling efficient streaming of large inputs to child processes without buffering everything in memory.

The change introduces a new `feed_pipe_fn` callback that gets called repeatedly to write data to child processes' stdin pipes in chunks. This solves a practical problem where hook implementations need to process large inputs (like the pre-push hook receiving many ref updates) without hitting pipe buffer limits or memory constraints. The implementation carefully manages pipe state with new helper functions like `child_is_receiving_input()` and handles SIGPIPE gracefully.

The patch includes comprehensive test coverage demonstrating the stdin feeding mechanism works correctly, including proper handling of EPIPE when children close their stdin early. This infrastructure will be used by subsequent patches in the series to modernize Git's hook implementations.

As part of a well-reviewed series that has already addressed technical concerns in prior iterations, this change appears solid with clear motivation and thorough testing.
