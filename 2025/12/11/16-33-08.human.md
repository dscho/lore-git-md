This is a discussion about modifying `git reset --hard` behavior to prevent accidental data loss in edge cases. The thread has evolved from Stefanos Koutsouflakis's original RFC proposing warnings for pre-first-commit scenarios to Johannes Sixt's suggestion of refusing the operation when the destination tree is empty, which would cover both pre-first-commit and post-empty-commit cases.

Stefanos agrees with Sixt's technical approach of preventing the operation rather than just warning, noting that checking for an empty tree seems more robust. He also observes that adding a `--force` override would be redundant for this case, comparing it to `rm -rf --really-delete`. The discussion highlights the tension between Git's philosophy of explicit destructive operations and the practical reality of users following potentially dangerous tutorials.

The exchange represents a narrowing of scope from the original proposal, focusing specifically on empty-tree scenarios where data loss would be most catastrophic. While maintainer skepticism about changing `--hard`'s behavior remains, this refined approach may be more palatable as it targets only the highest-risk cases without fundamentally altering Git's destructive-by-design philosophy for normal usage.
