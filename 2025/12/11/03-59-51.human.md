This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing RFC thread about making `git reset --hard` safer when discarding uncommitted staged changes. Eric Sunshine points out a small typo in Junio Hamano's response where he likely meant to say "there is *no* need" rather than "there is need" when describing why `--force` shouldn't be required for `--hard` operations.

The exchange is brief but notable as it shows the maintainer's strong position that `--hard` should remain explicitly destructive by design, without additional safety checks. This aligns with the thread's broader discussion about whether Git's philosophy of explicit destructive operations should be modified for safety in this case.

The correction itself is minor, but the context reinforces that the proposal faces significant conceptual resistance from Git's leadership, with Junio's original statement (even with the typo) making clear his view that `--hard` should remain unmodified in its behavior. The thread continues to revolve around whether the safety benefits would justify changing this long-standing behavior.
