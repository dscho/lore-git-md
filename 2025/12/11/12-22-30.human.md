This is a discussion about adding safety checks to `git reset --hard` in specific high-risk scenarios. Johannes Sixt joins the thread with a nuanced perspective on the proposal to warn users before `reset --hard` destroys staged-but-uncommitted content in a new repository.

Sixt identifies a key root cause - poorly written tutorials that casually recommend `git reset --hard` without warnings. He extends the original proposal's scope slightly, suggesting the same protection might be needed after an empty initial commit followed by `git add`. His compromise suggestion is to have `reset --hard` refuse to operate when the destination tree would be empty, which might be more palatable than warnings while still preventing catastrophic data loss.

The email shows the discussion evolving from the original narrow pre-first-commit case to considering related scenarios where `reset --hard` could be similarly dangerous.
