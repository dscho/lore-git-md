This is a follow-up in the RFC discussion about making `git reset --hard` safer for uncommitted staged changes. The original proposal suggested requiring `--force` in cases where staged-but-never-committed content would be lost, motivated by preventing accidental destruction of work.

Author Stefanos Koutsouflakis responds to maintainer Junio Hamano's skepticism by refining the scope of concern - specifically highlighting the dangerous case where a user has initialized a repository, staged files, but never committed them. In this scenario, `reset --hard` can completely destroy the entire project state with no recovery path, which the author argues is almost never intentional despite `--hard`'s documented destructive nature.

The exchange continues to center on whether Git's philosophy of explicit destructive operations should be tempered for this particularly catastrophic edge case. While the maintainer maintains that `--hard` should remain unambiguously destructive, the author makes a narrower case about protecting users from irreversible loss of unversioned work. The thread remains at this conceptual impasse with no new technical arguments introduced in this response.
