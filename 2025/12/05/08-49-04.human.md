Feature proposal discussion about multi-branch commit handling in `git-history`. This is part of the ongoing discussion around Patrick Steinhardt's `git-history` command series, which introduces experimental `reword` and `split` subcommands for history editing.

Elijah Newren raises significant concerns about the current implementation's handling of commits that exist in multiple branches. The command currently only modifies the current branch, which Newren argues replicates what he considers a fundamental design flaw in `git rebase` - the inability to properly handle commits shared across branches. He warns this could become a long-term foot-gun since the behavior might not be discovered until after users have come to depend on it.

Newren proposes three alternative approaches: (1) document and warn users to manually check, (2) error out for multi-branch commits unless explicitly overridden, or (3) rewrite all affected branches by default. He strongly advocates for addressing this now rather than later, as the experimental status may not provide enough protection against future compatibility issues.

Steinhardt's position (from the quoted response) is that the current single-branch behavior is intentional and useful for his workflow, where he maintains multiple dependent branches. He expresses concerns about the performance impact of checking all references and leans toward keeping the current behavior, possibly adding a future flag for multi-branch rewrites.

This represents a fundamental design disagreement about the command's philosophy - whether it should prioritize safety (Newren) or performance/current-branch focus (Steinhardt) when handling shared history. The discussion highlights the tension between Git's conservative defaults and the desire for more flexible history editing tools.
