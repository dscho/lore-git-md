This is a continuation of the discussion about `git rev-list --filter` behavior documentation. Patrick Steinhardt, the original implementer of `--filter-provided-objects`, responds to Justin Tobler's question about use cases for the current default behavior where explicitly provided objects bypass filters.

Steinhardt acknowledges the behavior is surprising but explains his conservative approach during implementation - he didn't feel comfortable changing the default to filter provided objects due to potential impact on partial clone users, even though he's unaware of any positive use cases for the current behavior. He emphasizes the need for research before considering any behavior changes, highlighting the challenge of assessing impact on complex systems like partial clones without knowing all usage patterns.

The exchange reveals the current behavior exists primarily as a backward compatibility safeguard rather than by intentional design. While all parties agree the documentation should accurately reflect the status quo, this follow-up surfaces deeper questions about whether the surprising behavior should be preserved long-term, though no immediate changes are proposed. The thread leaves open the possibility of future reconsideration if research can establish the safety of modifying the default behavior.
