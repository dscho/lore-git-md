Maintainer follow-up on `git rev-list --filter` behavior discussion.

Junio Hamano responds to Justin Tobler's question about use cases for the current `--filter` behavior where explicitly provided objects bypass filtering. He acknowledges there may be no good use case for this behavior, suggesting it could be worth changing in the future. However, he provides context for why it might have gone unnoticed - in the primary use case of partial clones, users typically filter commit ranges rather than individual objects, and commit objects are rarely filtered out anyway.

The response reinforces the thread's consensus that while the current behavior is suboptimal, changing it requires caution. Hamano's speculation about why the behavior persisted (commit objects being the typical filtered target) adds nuance to Patrick Steinhardt's earlier explanation about backward compatibility concerns. This maintains the thread's trajectory of documenting the current behavior while leaving the door open for future reconsideration if safety can be established.

Key technical points:
- Reiterates that primary `--filter` use is for commit ranges in partial clones
- Notes commit objects are rarely filtered in practice
- Suggests this might explain why the behavior wasn't noticed earlier

The email doesn't introduce new technical details but provides additional maintainer perspective on the practical realities that led to the current implementation. It maintains the thread's productive tension between documenting reality and wanting better design, with Hamano positioning himself as open to future improvements while recognizing the constraints that led to the current state.
