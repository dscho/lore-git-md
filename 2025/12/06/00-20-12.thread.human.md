This thread focuses on optimizing how Git handles promisor packs (partial clone objects) by skipping expensive hash verification when it's safe to do so. Aaron Plattner's patch series identified that the current implementation unnecessarily decompresses and verifies all objects during promisor set initialization, causing significant slowdown (76 minutes for 176GB packs). His v2 patch modifies `add_promisor_object()` and `parse_object_with_flags()` to skip these checks using `PARSE_OBJECT_SKIP_HASH_CHECK`.

Jeff King (Peff) reviewed the approach, agreeing with the technical direction but suggesting improvements to the patch structure and documentation. The discussion then turned to safety considerations - while skipping checks for blobs is clearly safe, Aaron raised whether trees/commits might need verification since their contents are actually used. Peff has now clarified that the optimization is appropriate for this performance-sensitive path, distinguishing it from integrity-checking contexts like `fsck`. He recommends splitting the changes into two patches: first fixing `OBJ_NONE` handling (a clear bugfix), then applying the hash-skipping optimization with clear documentation about its safety in this specific case.

Benchmarks show the optimization provides dramatic speedups (76 minutes → 2 minutes), and there's consensus on the core approach. The remaining discussion centers on properly documenting the tradeoffs and ensuring the patch structure tells a clear story. Aaron has agreed to restructure the series accordingly, and Peff's latest response provides the rationale needed to proceed confidently with the optimization while addressing the safety considerations.
