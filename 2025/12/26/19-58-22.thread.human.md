This thread addresses a regression in `git subtree` functionality that broke squashed subtree operations starting in Git 2.52.0. The issue stems from commit 83f9dad7d6, which introduced a problematic heuristic for detecting subtree split commits. The heuristic fails when dealing with rebased or cherry-picked squash commits due to incorrect assumptions about commit parentage.

After thorough analysis, Colin Stagner proposed removing the fragile `should_ignore_subtree_split_commit` heuristic entirely rather than trying to fix it. George Dietrich has now weighed in, agreeing with this approach - the optimization was originally meant to help with `split --rejoin` operations but has proven more trouble than it's worth. While this may sacrifice some performance in certain cases, it guarantees correct behavior for squashed commits. The discussion has clarified that GPG signing support (added in 2.51.0) isn't directly related to this particular issue.

With consensus forming around removing the problematic code path, the next steps are clear: implement the removal and add regression tests. The solution trades a minor optimization for robust behavior, which appears to be the right tradeoff given the complexity of maintaining the heuristic versus its limited benefits.
