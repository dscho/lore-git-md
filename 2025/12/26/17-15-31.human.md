This is a technical discussion about performance optimization in `git pack-refs` for repositories with millions of refs, continuing the thread about slow `pack-refs --all` operations.

Brian m. carlson responds to Jeff King's analysis of why `pack-refs` performs object verification and tag peeling, which causes significant overhead. They agree with Jeff that skipping verification for `refs/heads/` would be risky since users sometimes manually create invalid refs. The key new point is a suggestion that the reftable backend might avoid this overhead entirely, since it stores pre-peeled values and wouldn't need to recompute them.

The exchange highlights a fundamental tension between correctness (verifying refs) and performance in large-scale deployments. While no concrete solution is proposed here, the reftable suggestion points toward a potential architectural improvement that could address the root cause rather than just mitigating symptoms. The discussion remains focused on implementation details rather than user-facing changes.
