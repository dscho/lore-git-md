This is a technical follow-up in the ongoing `git-history` command RFC discussion, specifically addressing the multi-branch commit handling debate. Patrick Steinhardt revisits his position on branch updates during history rewriting operations (`reword` and `split` subcommands), now proposing a compromise approach after considering feedback from Junio Hamano and others.

The key development is Steinhardt's proposal to implicitly define the affected commit range as everything between the target commit and HEAD, mirroring `git-replay`'s behavior but without explicit range syntax. This would automatically update all branches pointing into this range while maintaining a simple single-commit UI. He acknowledges this addresses both scalability concerns (avoiding full-branch walks) and user experience goals (not requiring range syntax for simple cases).

The email marks a notable shift from Steinhardt's earlier "current-branch-only" stance toward the majority-preferred automatic multi-branch handling, though he frames it as a pragmatic solution rather than a philosophical alignment. The proposal maintains conceptual distance from Jujutsu's approach by grounding the behavior in Git's existing `replay` machinery rather than adopting JJ's more aggressive branch update semantics.

Technical considerations center on the implicit range detection's performance characteristics and whether the HEAD-to-commit walk provides sufficient safety for protected branches. The solution appears to satisfy Junio Hamano's preference for automatic behavior while potentially addressing Matthias Beyer's explicitness concerns through the constrained, predictable range definition. No new code is shown, but the email signals intent to modify the implementation accordingly if the approach gains consensus.
