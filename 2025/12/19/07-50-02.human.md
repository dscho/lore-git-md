This is a follow-up discussion in the curl compatibility test fixes thread, focusing on the implications of curl 8.18.0's header unfolding behavior changes. Jeff King responds to Daniel Stenberg's review of the patch series, agreeing that the first two patches (cookie path normalization and CRLF fixes) are clearly correct and necessary.

The main discussion centers around the third patch's whitespace handling for unfolded headers. While Daniel plans to restore curl's previous whitespace collapsing behavior, Jeff notes this may not be critical since the more fundamental change is that curl now provides pre-unfolded headers to callbacks. He suggests the test breakage was due to brittle test assumptions rather than real-world impact, since any proper HTTP client should handle unfolded headers regardless of whitespace.

The exchange reveals the careful balancing act between standards compliance and backward compatibility, with both maintainers weighing whether to prioritize strict RFC 7230 adherence or preserve existing behavior. The discussion remains open-ended, with Jeff acknowledging the practical considerations but questioning whether the whitespace handling is truly problematic for real users.
