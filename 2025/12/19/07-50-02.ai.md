This is a follow-up discussion in the curl 8.18.0 test compatibility thread, focusing on header unfolding behavior changes. Jeff King (peff@peff.net) responds to Daniel Stenberg's review of the patch series, particularly regarding curl's handling of whitespace in unfolded HTTP headers.

The exchange reveals that curl 8.18.0 changed its header unfolding implementation to be more standards-compliant (RFC 7230), which affected Git's test suite. While Daniel plans to restore some of the previous whitespace behavior in curl to maintain compatibility, Jeff notes this may not be strictly necessary since: (1) the test case was particularly brittle in its expectations, (2) real-world usage likely wouldn't be affected, and (3) header folding is deprecated anyway.

Key technical points include:
- CURLOPT_HEADERFUNCTION callbacks now receive pre-unfolded headers (changed behavior)
- The test expected collapsed whitespace in unfolded headers
- The test's expectations were implementation-specific rather than standards-based

Jeff suggests the breakage seen in Git's tests may not reflect real-world impact, since any proper HTTP client should handle unfolded headers regardless of whitespace. The discussion highlights the tension between maintaining backward compatibility and moving toward standards compliance, with both parties acknowledging the test's brittleness while working toward a solution.
