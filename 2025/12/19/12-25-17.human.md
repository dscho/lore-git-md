This is a follow-up discussion about commit message conventions in the context of the recent performance regression fix.

Junio Hamano responds to a thread about whether to use `Fixes:` tags in commit messages, clarifying his position on the matter. While acknowledging that the Linux kernel may use `Fixes:` for backporting purposes, he reiterates his long-standing objection to the practice in Git's development process. His primary concern is that labeling something as a "fix" prematurely (before it's been thoroughly tested in real-world use) could lead to incorrect assumptions about the patch's effectiveness.

The discussion stems from earlier messages where contributors were debating whether to tag the performance regression fix (from commit 385e18810f10) with a `Fixes:` trailer. Others had pointed out that since the bug only exists in unreleased versions, such tagging wouldn't be necessary by Git's conventions.

Junio's response serves as both a clarification of project norms and a gentle reminder about the philosophy behind them - that the true effectiveness of a fix can only be determined after extended real-world use, making mechanical processing of "Fixes" tags potentially misleading. The tone is characteristically thoughtful, maintaining the project's careful approach to change management while acknowledging other projects may have different valid approaches.
