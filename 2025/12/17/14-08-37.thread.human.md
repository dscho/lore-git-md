This thread documents a major improvement to Windows symlink support in Git, now finalized in its fifth iteration. The 18-patch series, primarily authored by Bill Zissimopoulos with contributions from Johannes Schindelin (Dscho), Karsten Blees, and Johannes Sixt (Hannes), brings comprehensive POSIX-like symlink operations to Windows through the MinGW compatibility layer. The work handles everything from basic symlink creation and reading to complex edge cases involving directory handling, renaming operations, and index corruption scenarios.

The series has undergone thorough review, with all technical and administrative details resolved. Key improvements include proper `stat()`/`lstat()` implementations, robust `readlink()` functionality, and special handling for directory symlinks. The latest email confirms the robustness of the renaming operation patch (11/18), which replaces MSVCRT's problematic `_wrename()` with direct `MoveFileEx()` calls - a change battle-tested in Git for Windows. Johannes Sixt reviewed the complex logic and confirmed its reliability despite the convoluted implementation.

With maintainer approval and all contributor attributions properly documented, this work represents a significant step forward in Windows-POSIX compatibility for Git. The changes touch core areas like `compat/mingw*` and `read-cache.c`, with careful attention to error handling and edge cases. While future testing may reveal additional platform-specific scenarios, the series is now complete and ready for inclusion in upcoming releases, marking the culmination of years of Windows symlink support development.
