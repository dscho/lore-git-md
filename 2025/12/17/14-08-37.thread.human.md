This thread documents the completion of a major effort to bring comprehensive symbolic link support to Git on Windows, culminating in an 18-patch series (v5) that has now been approved for merging. The work, primarily by Karsten Blees with contributions from Johannes Schindelin (Dscho), builds on years of development in Git for Windows to implement POSIX-like symlink operations in the MinGW compatibility layer. The series covers all aspects of symlink handling - creation, reading, stat'ing, directory operations, renaming, and index tracking - while maintaining backward compatibility through the `core.symlinks` configuration.

The final patch in the series addressed a subtle index corruption edge case from Git for Windows v2.27.0 where symlink target sizes were incorrectly recorded. Earlier patches established non-elevated symlink creation, proper file/directory symlink handling, and robust error reporting. The changes primarily touch `compat/mingw*` files and `read-cache.c`, with careful attention to Windows-specific behaviors while maintaining POSIX compatibility. Junio Hamano has reviewed and approved the series for integration after thorough testing and review.

A minor correction was recently noted by Ben Knoble, who spotted a typo in the commit message of patch 10/18 (a purely editorial fix that doesn't affect the technical content). The series now appears ready for inclusion in upcoming Git releases, marking the completion of a decade-long effort to bring full symlink support to Windows. Future testing may reveal additional edge cases, particularly around cross-process operations, but the core functionality is now in place.
