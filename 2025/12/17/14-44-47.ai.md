This is a technical discussion about symlink handling limits in the ongoing Windows symlink support series. Patrick Steinhardt raises concerns about the unbounded growth in `strbuf_readlink()` after patch 4/5 removed the PATH_MAX limitation. He questions whether there's a reliable way to determine symlink target length upfront, noting that while readlink(3p) doesn't provide this, lstat(3p) might not be universally reliable either. 

Key technical points include:
- The patch removed STRBUF_MAXLINK (2*PATH_MAX) constraint, replacing the while loop with an unbounded for(;;)
- Patrick flags potential undefined behavior when buffer sizes exceed SSIZE_MAX
- He probes whether Windows imposes its own symlink size limits that could reintroduce safe bounds
- The concern stems from implementation-defined behavior in readlink(3p) with oversized buffers

This follows the series' earlier improvements to symlink handling (better fallbacks, config parsing fixes, and performance optimizations) and represents a safety review of the length-unbounded approach. The question implies this might need platform-specific bounds if Windows has natural limits, maintaining the series' theme of robust cross-platform symlink support.
