This is a discussion about potential future subcommands (`drop` and `reorder`) for the proposed `git-history` command, building on the current `reword` and `split` functionality.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to D. Ben Knoble's real-world use cases for the previously implemented (but now removed) `drop` and `reorder` subcommands. Knoble describes two concrete scenarios where these subcommands proved useful in his workflow: (1) moving changes to a new branch without using stashes, and (2) reordering WIP commits while developing. 

Steinhardt acknowledges the value of these subcommands but notes technical complications with implementing them on top of the replay infrastructure, particularly around conflict handling. He suggests a possible path forward would be to initially implement versions that bail on conflicts, then iterate. This indicates these subcommands may appear in a future series after the current `reword`/`split` functionality lands.

The exchange validates that the broader vision for `git-history` addresses real workflow pain points, though the current series focuses on the two most fundamental subcommands. The discussion also surfaces an important technical constraint (conflict-handling limitations of the replay infrastructure) that will need to be addressed for future expansion of the command's capabilities.
