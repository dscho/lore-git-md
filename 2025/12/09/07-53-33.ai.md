This is a response in the ongoing debate about the `git-history` command's handling of commits with multiple branch descendants. The thread centers on a fundamental design decision for the new history editing functionality.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Elijah Newren's strong objections about the current implementation's approach to multi-branch commits. Newren had characterized the current-branch-only behavior as repeating what he views as `git rebase`'s fundamental design mistake, proposing three alternative approaches: erroring out for multi-branch commits, requiring explicit flags, or rewriting all affected branches.

Steinhardt acknowledges the subjective nature of the debate ("a matter of workflows and tastes"), rejecting option (b) (always requiring flags) as making simple cases more complex. He expresses openness to a hybrid approach combining options (a) (erroring for multi-branch commits) and (b), contingent on efficient implementation. The email concludes with a technical question about whether similar logic exists in `git-replay`.

This exchange highlights the core philosophical tension in the thread between Git's conservative defaults (favored by Newren) and more flexible Jujutsu-inspired capabilities (implemented by Steinhardt). The discussion remains unresolved, with implementation efficiency emerging as a new consideration in evaluating the alternatives.
