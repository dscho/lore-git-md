This is a follow-up discussion about patch 7/10 in Johannes Schindelin's series improving Windows symlink test compatibility, specifically addressing t1006-cat-file.sh.

Patrick Steinhardt had questioned whether the test adjustment for MSYS2 symlink normalization indicated a bug in `git cat-file`'s symlink handling that would need future fixing. Johannes clarifies that the issue stems from how MSYS2's `winsymlinks:nativestrict` mode creates symlinks differently than Git's test suite expects - the symlinks physically exist in a different form on disk, making the test's expectations impossible to meet regardless of `cat-file`'s behavior. 

This confirms the patch's approach of skipping the affected assertions on Windows is correct, as the discrepancy lies in the platform's symlink creation rather than Git's symlink following logic. The exchange leaves open whether MSYS2's symlink normalization represents a fundamental platform difference (making the current skip approach permanent) or something that could be aligned with Git's expectations in future Windows improvements. The thread context shows this was already identified as an open question when the patch was approved for inclusion.
