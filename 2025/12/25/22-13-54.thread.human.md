This thread investigates why `git pack-refs --all` runs much slower than JGit on large repositories with millions of refs (5+ minutes vs 20 seconds). The discussion started with Martin Fick's report from a Gerrit server environment with ~3M refs, 90M objects, and NFS storage.

After initial analysis by Jeff King ruled out several potential bottlenecks (including write buffering and verification overhead), new strace data from Martin shows the process spends most of its time in gaps between write operations rather than in the writes themselves. This suggests the slowdown comes from memory operations - possibly inefficient mmap access patterns over NFS or suboptimal in-memory ref lookups. 

The reftable backend would likely perform better in this scenario, but the current focus is understanding and potentially improving the files backend's behavior. The discussion has shifted from I/O patterns to examining how Git interacts with NFS at the memory access level, particularly around mmap operations. Martin and Jeff continue to narrow down the specific causes of the performance gap.
