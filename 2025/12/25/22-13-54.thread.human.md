This thread investigates why `git pack-refs --all` runs slowly (5+ minutes vs JGit's 20s) on large Gerrit repositories with ~3M refs stored on NFS. Martin Fick's flame graph analysis confirmed the bottleneck: 42% of time is spent on page faults, with another ~60% split between SSE-optimized memcmp and object header unpacking, while the actual packed-refs creation only takes 8.7%. The NFS configuration (32KB rsize/wsize) is a hard constraint for Gerrit mirroring.

Patrick Steinhardt, who has consistently advocated for adopting the reftable backend as the long-term solution, was surprised by both the high page fault overhead and that packed-refs creation took as much time as it did. He suggested trying larger NFS buffer sizes (1MB) as a potential short-term mitigation, though he still views reftable as the more promising architectural solution. The thread remains at an impasse - while the performance data clearly shows mmap/NFS interaction as the primary bottleneck, it doesn't provide clear direction on whether to pursue immediate optimizations (like tuning NFS or trying a NO_MMAP build) or focus on transitioning to reftable. Martin's production data validated the technical analysis but hasn't broken the deadlock between these approaches.
