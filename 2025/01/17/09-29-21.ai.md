This is a follow-up discussion on the final patch in a series hardening the reftable backend against corruption issues. The patch adds a guard to prevent modifying update indices after header writing begins in the reftable writer.

Patrick Steinhardt suggests two potential improvements to make the protection even stricter: either detecting when records have already been queued when `set_limits()` is called, or eliminating `set_limits()` entirely by making the indices required parameters in `writer_new()`. He prefers the latter approach as it would enforce correct usage patterns more fundamentally. 

Patrick also recommends changing the error handling from `BUG()` to returning a `REFTABLE_API_ERROR`, noting this is more appropriate for library code (avoiding hard crashes) and reduces dependencies on git-specific headers. This would require updating all callers to handle the new error case.

The discussion focuses on implementation details of the safeguard mechanism rather than questioning its necessity - the thread's earlier consensus was that preventing header/footer mismatches is important. The suggestions aim to make the protection more comprehensive (catching misuse earlier) and more library-appropriate (using return codes rather than assertions).
