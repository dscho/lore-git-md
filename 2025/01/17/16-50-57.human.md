This is a performance discussion about optimizing `git bisect` in large repositories like the Linux kernel.

Junio Hamano builds on Jeff King's earlier proposals for workarounds to the slow bisection performance, while also suggesting a potential algorithmic optimization. The thread has progressed from initial problem reports through practical workarounds to now considering deeper algorithmic changes.

Hamano notes that Jeff's proposed workarounds (using `--first-parent` or approximating with `--topo-order`) trade some accuracy for speed, which may be acceptable in practice. He then suggests a more fundamental optimization: when evaluating merge commits during bisection, we could potentially skip expensive reachability calculations for commits that cannot possibly improve on the current best midpoint candidate.

The key insight is that during the reachability traversal to count commits reachable from a merge M, we could simultaneously determine that ancestors of M cannot be better midpoints than M itself, allowing us to skip their individual calculations. This would require careful tracking during the traversal but could significantly reduce the O(n²) behavior in pathological cases.

This continues the thread's dual-track approach of both providing immediate workarounds for users while exploring longer-term algorithmic improvements. Hamano's suggestion appears technically sound but would require implementation to validate its effectiveness. The discussion remains focused on making bisection practical for large histories while maintaining its core utility.
