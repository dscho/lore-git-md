Performance optimization discussion for `git bisect` in large repositories. Jeff King responds to Askar Safin's concerns about bisection speed in the Linux kernel repository (535k-1M commits between v3.0 and v6.13-rc7) with practical workarounds while the algorithmic improvements are being developed.

King suggests two manual approaches to reduce bisection time: (1) manually selecting approximate midpoints like v4.0/v5.0 to quickly narrow the search space, and (2) using `--first-parent` to follow just the main development line, which completes in seconds but may require a follow-up bisection after finding a merge commit. He demonstrates how a simple linear approximation (`rev-list --topo-order` with midpoint calculation) can find a reasonably centered commit in just 2 seconds.

This email provides temporary solutions while the deeper performance work continues, showing how users can adapt their workflow today. The suggestions acknowledge the tradeoffs between perfect bisection and practical speed, with King noting these approximation techniques could potentially be built into `git bisect` itself. The thread continues to balance immediate usability improvements against the more complex algorithmic changes being discussed.
