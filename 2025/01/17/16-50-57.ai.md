This is a technical discussion about potential algorithmic optimizations for `git bisect` in large repositories, building on Jeff King's earlier performance workarounds.

Junio Hamano responds to Jeff King's suggestion about approximating the midpoint in large commit ranges by proposing a more sophisticated optimization strategy for the core bisection algorithm. The key idea is to avoid unnecessary `count_distance()` computations by leveraging reachability information already gathered during traversal. When evaluating a merge commit M that reaches N commits, we can infer that:
- For N < half_total: ancestors of M cannot be better than M (they reach fewer commits)
- For N > half_total: descendants of M cannot be better than M (they reach more commits)

Junio notes this would require reachability information that might be available through mechanisms like bitmaps, though the exact implementation details remain to be worked out. The discussion continues the thread's theme of balancing precision against performance, now moving from user-facing workarounds to deeper algorithmic possibilities. This suggests the optimization path may split into short-term usability improvements (Jeff's workarounds) and longer-term algorithmic changes (Junio's proposal). The email maintains the thread's focus on making bisection practical for large histories like Linux while preserving correctness.
