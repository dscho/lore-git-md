This is a follow-up discussion on the final patch in a series hardening the reftable backend against corruption issues. The patch adds safeguards to prevent `update_index` modifications after header writing begins in the reftable writer.

Patrick Steinhardt suggests making the protection even stricter by either (1) requiring `set_limits()` to be called before any records are added (detectable via writer state), or (2) eliminating `set_limits()` entirely by making the limits constructor parameters. He prefers the second approach as more foolproof since limits are nearly always needed anyway. 

He also recommends using `REFTABLE_API_ERROR` instead of `BUG()` for better library code hygiene, though acknowledges this would require plumbing error handling through callers. The current patch's approach of checking `w->next` as a sentinel is correct but could be made more robust.

This continues the thread's theme of proactive hardening, with Patrick pushing for even stronger safeguards against misuse of the reftable writer API. The suggestion would represent a more invasive change than the current patch, potentially affecting all reftable writer callers.
