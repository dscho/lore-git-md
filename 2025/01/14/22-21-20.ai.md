This is a performance discussion thread about `git bisect` operations on large repositories, specifically examining why the initial setup phase is so slow when bisecting across hundreds of thousands of commits in the Linux kernel repository.

Ben Knoble responds to Askar Safin's bug report about 21-minute bisect setup times by providing additional timing data points. He notes that a simple `git rev-list --count` across the same commit range (v3.0 to v6.13-rc7) completes in about 15 seconds, suggesting the bisect slowness isn't just about the raw number of commits. He also observes that checking out the first bisect candidate commit takes nearly 30 seconds, hinting that filesystem operations may be part of the bottleneck. 

Key technical details include the 1,070,175 commit range size, timing measurements for comparison operations, and the disappearance of `bisect--helper` in recent Git versions (though the performance issue persists). Knoble suggests trying pathspecs or smaller commit ranges as potential workarounds, though he hasn't fully tested these approaches himself. The email maintains the thread's focus on bisect performance characteristics while adding new timing data points that may help diagnose where exactly the time is being spent during bisect initialization.
