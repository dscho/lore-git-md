[Security] ANSI escape sequence injection vulnerability in git's sideband channel (CVE-2024-32002, CVE-2024-52005/GHSA-7jjc-gg6m-3329). Original series implemented three-state `sideband.allowControlCharacters` configuration (default `color` mode) permitting ANSI colors while blocking other control characters, with core sanitization in `handle_ansi_color_sequence()` and enhanced `strbuf_add_sanitized()`. Test coverage in `t5409-colorize-remote-messages.sh` and documentation in `config/sideband.txt`.

Technical refinements include:
- Andreas Schwab's fix for `iscntrl()` unsigned char casting in `strbuf_add_sanitized()`, with Junio Hamano confirming Git's `sane-ctype.h` handles this correctly via `sane_istest()`
- Phillip Wood's suggestions for whitelist expansion (adding '\e[K' and '\e[G') and UTF-8 DEL character representation improvements
- Junio Hamano's new response to Ondřej Pohořelský acknowledging the patchset has received pushback from Brian Carlson (terminal-emulator-security position) but considers the discussion unresolved, noting the lack of counter-arguments makes the situation appear settled against the patches while personally believing it's premature to conclude

Key debate points:
- Carlson maintains terminal emulators should handle security, citing modern terminal protections and legitimate non-color sequences (progress bars, hyperlinks)
- Hamano acknowledges valid use cases but questions whether screen-clearing sequences are uniquely dangerous versus other malicious content, while observing the lack of counter-arguments may indicate tacit consensus against the patches
- Both highlight binary nature of escape hatches forcing all-or-nothing trust decisions
- Hamano leans toward terminal-level solutions being more appropriate but doesn't reject patches outright, maintaining uncertainty about whether more convincing arguments for the tradeoffs may emerge

Downstream impact:
- Ondřej Pohořelský (Red Hat) inquiry addressed by Hamano with nuanced view - while patches are technically sound, architectural debate creates uncertainty for backporting decisions
- Red Hat faces shipping dilemma due to SLAs, with Hamano's response not providing definitive guidance on whether (1) issue is not critical upstream or (2) fixes might cause regressions
