This is a response in the ongoing reftable decoupling effort, specifically addressing maintenance concerns about potential code duplication between Git's core utilities and the newly independent reftable implementations.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about keeping the reftable's copied utilities in sync with Git's evolving implementations. He argues that most of the duplicated code is simple and unlikely to need updates, while more sophisticated subsystems (like tempfiles and lockfiles) actually share underlying implementations through reftable/system.c shims. The response suggests that real-world usage in libgit2 will provide additional testing coverage, with the expectation that most future bugs will be in reftable's business logic rather than the compatibility layer.

Steinhardt acknowledges that creating a shared implementation would likely recreate the original dependency problem the series aims to solve. He proposes waiting to see if the duplication actually becomes problematic in practice, noting that if issues do surface in the low-level code, he'll revisit potential solutions. The response maintains the pragmatic approach seen throughout the series - making technical progress now while leaving architectural questions open for future consideration if they prove relevant.
