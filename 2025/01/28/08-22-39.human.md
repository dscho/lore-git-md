This is a response in the ongoing reftable refactoring series, addressing maintainability concerns about code duplication between Git's core and the newly independent reftable implementation.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's question about how to keep the reftable's copied utilities in sync with Git's evolving implementations. He acknowledges the theoretical concern, but argues that most duplicated code is simple and unlikely to need updates, while complex subsystems (like tempfiles and lockfiles) already share underlying implementations through reftable/system.c shims.

The response suggests two key points: (1) real-world usage in libgit2 will provide additional testing that should catch most issues, and (2) anticipated problems are more likely to be in reftable's business logic rather than the compatibility layer. While Patrick doesn't have a perfect solution to the synchronization problem, he expresses optimism that it may not be a major practical issue and leaves the door open to revisit if experience proves otherwise.
