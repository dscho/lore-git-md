This is a technical clarification in the ongoing reftable decoupling series, specifically addressing questions about patch 1/19's I/O safety changes. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Justin Tobler's concerns about two aspects of the conversion from Git's `read_in_full()` to direct POSIX `read()` operations.

First, regarding the `off_t` to `size_t` conversion, Patrick explains the existing negative size check ensures safety of the cast, making a separate `xsize_t()`-like helper unnecessary. Second, he clarifies that the simpler EAGAIN/EINTR handling is sufficient because reftable doesn't use non-blocking I/O (O_NONBLOCK), so the more complex `xread()` behavior isn't needed. He plans to document this rationale in the commit message.

The exchange exemplifies the careful technical scrutiny applied to this architectural refactoring, particularly around I/O safety and error handling semantics as reftable moves away from Git's utilities. While addressing the immediate concerns, it doesn't resolve the broader thread discussion about long-term maintenance of parallel implementations.
