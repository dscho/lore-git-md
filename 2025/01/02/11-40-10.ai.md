Chris Torek offers a comprehensive response to Prasad Pandit's question about visibility of changes, explaining Git's fundamental concepts with precision. The email focuses on clarifying several key points about Git's architecture: the immutable nature of commits, how branches are really just movable pointers to commits, and the working tree/index relationship. Torek emphasizes that branches don't automatically share changes - they only share history up to their common ancestor commit.

The explanation includes detailed diagrams showing commit parent relationships and how branch pointers move during commits. It also covers edge cases like untracked files and the dangers of checking out historical commits that might overwrite uncommitted work. The response is technically accurate and pedagogically structured, though quite dense for beginners.
