This is a review response in the ongoing discussion about unsigned integer literals in bit operations. Junio Hamano weighs in on Seija Kijin's patch that adds 'U' suffixes to integer literals to prevent potential undefined behavior from integer promotions.

Hamano points out several issues with the original justification - specifically that "1 <<" can't overflow as written, and that the examples shown (where ce_stage() is at most 3) wouldn't benefit from the change. However, he does acknowledge that using unsigned literals for bit operations is generally good hygiene, particularly for shifts approaching 31 bits where signed overflow could become problematic.

The key takeaway is that while the patch's technical changes may be reasonable, the justification needs work - the current examples don't demonstrate actual overflow risks, and the explanation about UB is unclear. Hamano suggests a better guiding principle would be that bit patterns should default to unsigned unless there's a specific need for signed behavior. This moves the discussion forward by providing clearer framework for evaluating such changes, though the patch will likely need revision to be accepted.
