This is a follow-up to the ongoing discussion about standardizing Git's help output behavior. Jonas Konrad provides additional audit findings about non-builtin commands' help behavior, supplementing Kristoffer Haugsbakk's earlier comprehensive audit of builtins.

Jonas confirms seeing the same 40 builtin commands (out of 142) outputting help to stderr, matching Kristoffer's findings. He then extends the investigation to several non-builtin commands: git-scalar (stderr), git-lfs (no -h support), git-p4 (stdout without -h flag), and notes git-svn and git-cvsserver remain untested. He also flags git-http-backend and git-filter-branch as having special behaviors worth examining.

The email doesn't propose new solutions but strengthens the case for standardization by showing the inconsistency extends beyond builtins. It comes as the thread is converging toward Jeff King's technical proposal for systematic fixes, with Junio Hamano having indicated a preference for stdout standardization. Jonas's findings about ancillary commands suggest the scope of any standardization effort may need to include non-builtins as well.
