This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about standardizing Git's help output behavior. Kristoffer Haugsbakk provides additional data points about non-builtin commands' behavior with `-h` flags, completing the comprehensive audit of Git's help output patterns.

The email examines three special cases: `git-http-backend` (which doesn't support `-h` and fails with a server error), `git-filter-branch` (which shows a warning and 10-second delay before stdout output), and confirms earlier findings about `git-scalar` and `git-lfs`. These edge cases demonstrate that while the core builtins show a clear 28%/72% split between stderr/stdout usage, auxiliary tools have their own idiosyncratic behaviors.

The findings reinforce the maintainer-endorsed direction of standardizing builtin help output to stdout while leaving error cases on stderr. The special behaviors in non-core commands like the 10-second delay in `git-filter-branch` suggest there may be additional cleanup opportunities beyond the current standardization effort, though these would likely be addressed separately from the main initiative.
