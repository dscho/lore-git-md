This RFC thread discusses a bug in `git repack` where promisor packs (used in partial clones) were bypassing `--keep-pack` and `.keep` file checks, potentially causing unnecessary repacking of large packs that should be preserved according to `gc.bigPackThreshold`. 

Tomáš Trnka originally proposed a fix that would modify `repack_promisor_objects()` to properly honor keep rules. Han Young then identified a theoretical flaw in this approach - since promisor objects are collected before packing, simply propagating keep-pack flags might not work as intended. However, Trnka has now responded with real-world testing showing the patch does successfully prevent repacking of large (80GB) promisor packs in their production environment, avoiding significant performance costs on btrfs.

The discussion remains technical, with Trnka defending the practical effectiveness of their solution while acknowledging they may not fully understand the internals. Key open questions include whether to filter keep packs during object collection rather than packing time, and where tests should live (in repack or partial-clone test suites). The thread still awaits input from repack/partial-clone experts like Patrick Steinhardt or Jeff King, and the original formatting issues preventing clean patch application remain unresolved. While the theoretical concerns stand, the practical validation from Trnka suggests the current approach may work despite the objections.
