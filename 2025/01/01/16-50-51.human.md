Bugfix discussion about race conditions in Git's collision detection during object file operations.

This email from Junio Hamano continues a thread about fixing race conditions in Git's object collision checking. The discussion centers around Jeff King's analysis of edge cases where temporary files might disappear during the collision check process, particularly when running operations like `gc --prune=now` concurrently.

The key concern is whether to treat a missing tempfile as an error (current behavior) or silently succeed (proposed change). Jeff argues that silently succeeding could mask real problems, as the code wouldn't be able to verify if the objects were actually identical. Junio agrees with this assessment, noting that while such races might be rare in practice, it's better to maintain the current error behavior to avoid hiding potential issues from users.

The discussion highlights the delicate balance needed in handling concurrent operations in Git's object database, where temporary files and race conditions can lead to subtle but important behavior differences. The consensus appears to be that the safer approach is to maintain the error-reporting behavior rather than risk silent failures.
