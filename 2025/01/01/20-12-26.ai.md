Performance optimization addressing LSan race conditions in test execution, proposing a less invasive solution than previous approaches. This is a follow-up to Jeff King's earlier investigation into false positive leak reports from LeakSanitizer during threaded test runs.

The series presents a new approach that filters out known false positives in LSan output rather than attempting to fix the underlying race condition in the sanitizer's stack unwinding code. The key insight is to stop using `abort_on_error` for leak checking and instead rely on log analysis, similar to the approach taken in commit 370ef7e40d. The solution involves carefully crafted log filtering to distinguish real leaks from the known race pattern while maintaining detection of actual memory issues.

The series consists of 6 patches: the first two match the queued jk/lsan-race-with-barrier series (handling stress test directories and reverting an atomic threads change), while the remaining four implement the new filtering strategy. Jeff provides verification that the approach correctly catches real leaks while ignoring the false positives, demonstrated through a test modification in t/helper/test-date.c.

Changes affect test-lib.sh's LSan configuration and leak detection logic, with modifications to how exit codes and log patterns are processed. The solution aims to be less invasive than previous attempts while maintaining diagnostic quality, though it does involve some complexity in the log filtering logic. This connects to ongoing efforts around test reliability and CI improvements, particularly for threaded operations.
