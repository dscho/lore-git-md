This is a follow-up discussion about the final design decision for the `help.autocorrect` boolean handling, coming after the v4 patch series was thought to be complete.

Junio Hamano acknowledges that Jeff King's suggested approach to make "0" behave consistently with other false boolean values (showing but not running the corrected command) would be more natural than the current v4 implementation where "0" and boolean false have different meanings. He takes responsibility for the current design, calling it his fault, and agrees that Jeff's version makes more sense.

The email indicates the v4 patch series is now in a "stuck state" due to this realization, and suggests the behavior should be updated so that:
- "0", "false", "off", and "no" all mean "show but don't run"
- Only "never" would mean "do nothing" (absolute no-op)

Junio notes he may update the series further based on v4 to implement this change, but makes no firm commitment to do so. This represents a late-stage reconsideration of a key design decision that was previously thought settled, though it doesn't reopen any of the other resolved aspects of the implementation (like the numeric delay handling or the parsing function structure).

The exchange shows the careful attention being paid to boolean handling consistency even after multiple rounds of review, with the project maintainer willing to revisit decisions when better alternatives are pointed out.
