This is a technical discussion about random number generation failures in the reftable stack, specifically when using OpenSSL's CSPRNG backend. The thread has evolved from Patrick Steinhardt's initial patch series (aimed at preventing process death when the entropy pool is exhausted) to a broader examination of OpenSSL's suitability for Git's random number needs.

Key new developments in this email:
- Randall Becker reports the issue occurs on ia64 systems using OpenSSL 3.0.11 with PRNGD, while x86 systems work fine due to hardware RNG support
- Patrick acknowledges this suggests a systemic issue across multiple `git_rand()` callsites, not just reftable
- The discussion shifts toward potentially removing OpenSSL CSPRNG support entirely, as it appears to be:
  * Only used on NonStop platforms
  * Prone to misconfiguration
  * Outperformed by other available backends (arc4random, getrandom, etc.)
- Patrick questions whether Randall's system changes during maintenance might have caused the issue, as problems appeared suddenly between rc1 and rc2 without Git-side changes

Technical context for future summaries:
- OpenSSL's `RAND_bytes()` vs deprecated `RAND_pseudo_bytes()` behavior
- Platform-specific considerations: ia64 vs x86 hardware RNG support
- Alternative backends under discussion: arc4random_buf(), getrandom(), getentropy(), /dev/urandom
- Impact spans beyond reftable to all `git_rand()` callsites
- NonStop platform's unique dependency on OpenSSL backend

The thread appears to be moving toward a potential deprecation of OpenSSL's CSPRNG support in favor of more reliable alternatives, though this would require verification of NonStop platform capabilities. The sudden appearance of issues suggests possible environmental factors on Randall's systems that may need investigation.
