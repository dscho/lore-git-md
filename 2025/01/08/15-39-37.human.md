This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's random number generation strategy, specifically regarding the reftable backend. Junio Hamano is responding to Patrick Steinhardt's confirmation that using insecure random bytes (or even constant values) in the reftable stack wouldn't affect correctness, only potentially increasing contention.

The key points from this exchange:
- Patrick confirms that removing cryptographic randomness from reftable operations (temp filenames and backoff delays) would not create correctness issues, though it might lead to more contention in concurrent operations
- Junio suggests this presents an opportunity to simplify by using system rand() instead of git_rand(), which would both solve the immediate OpenSSL issues on ia64 and reduce reftable's Git-specific dependencies
- The discussion has narrowed from the broader RNG debate to focus on this specific case where cryptographic randomness may be overkill

This email moves the conversation toward a practical compromise - addressing the immediate platform issues while making progress on dependency reduction, without waiting for resolution of the larger RNG strategy questions. The reftable case appears to be a good candidate for simplification since the security properties of the randomness aren't critical to its operation.
