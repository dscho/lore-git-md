This is a follow-up in the ongoing reftable/OpenSSL RNG discussion, where Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's suggestion about simplifying the random number generation in the reftable backend.

Patrick initially agreed that using insecure random numbers (or even constants) would be fine for reftable's needs, but now identifies a potential edge case where Git crashes mid-transaction could cause problems if the same "random" suffix is reused. The reftable library can clean up stale tables, but not proactively enough to prevent issues in this scenario.

He also notes that the OpenSSL RNG failures reported by Randall Becker affect more than just reftable - many tests are failing on ia64 systems when entropy is low. Patrick expresses frustration that this appears to be a platform-specific OpenSSL issue, suggesting the OpenSSL backend may be fundamentally unfit for Git's needs.

The email shows the discussion evolving from a reftable-specific optimization question to a broader concern about Git's RNG strategy, particularly for platforms like ia64. While the reftable case might be solvable with careful handling of transaction failures, the systemic OpenSSL issues remain problematic.
