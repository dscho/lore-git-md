This is a response in the ongoing discussion about converting Git's documentation from `.txt` to `.adoc` extensions. Jean-Noël Avila raises concerns about backward compatibility, particularly for documentation writers and translators who rely on the `.txt` extension in their workflows (like those working on git-scm.com and manpage translations).

The email suggests that experienced documentation writers can already configure their editors (like Emacs) to recognize `.txt` files as AsciiDoc, and proposes either maintaining the status quo or implementing a smoother transition through symlinks between `.txt` and `.adoc` files. This contrasts with the original patch's approach of a clean break to `.adoc` for better default editor support.

The response adds an important perspective about real-world usage of the source files, though it doesn't fundamentally challenge the technical merits of the conversion. This may prompt discussion about transition strategies that balance tooling improvements with existing workflows. The thread had previously shown strong support for the change, so this could be a key counterpoint for maintainers to consider.
