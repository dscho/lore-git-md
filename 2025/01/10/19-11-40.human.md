This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about improving documentation for personal access token (PAT) storage in Git's credential system. Randall Becker chimes in with a practical perspective from the field.

The email continues the thread about warning users that `git-credential-cache` isn't suitable for PATs due to its ephemeral nature. While previous messages focused on documenting built-in alternatives like libsecret and osxkeychain, Becker shares his real-world approach: a custom credential manager specifically designed to handle PATs. 

Becker notes his solution doesn't use OAuth (which was mentioned in the original patch as an alternative), suggesting that OAuth integration can be complex for command-line tools. He floats the idea of integration with authentication apps like Microsoft Authenticator as another potential path forward.

This contribution adds practical implementation experience to the theoretical discussion about documentation improvements. It suggests that while the documentation patch is valuable, there may be more sophisticated solutions worth considering beyond just warning about the cache's limitations. The email doesn't directly critique the proposed documentation changes, but provides additional context about the challenges of PAT management in real-world scenarios.
