Patrick Steinhardt's series to decouple the reftable library from Git's core infrastructure has reached near-final form, with all major architectural and stylistic concerns resolved. The reftable library now stands as an independent component with clean separation from Git's internals, maintaining compatibility across platforms while using proper header inclusion conventions.

Justin Tobler has surfaced one final technical concern in patch 04/20, noting that replacing Git's `COPY_ARRAY()` macro with direct `memcpy` loses the implicit `st_mult()` overflow protection. This affects how object record offsets are handled in `reftable/record.c`. Given the series has already demonstrated careful attention to I/O safety (particularly in patch 01/19's handling of `off_t` to `size_t` conversions), we can expect either a justification for the current approach or a minor adjustment to maintain safety guarantees. With Junio Hamano's prior engagement on similar low-level safety questions, this last point should be resolved quickly before the series is queued.
