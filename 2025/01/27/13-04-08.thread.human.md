Johannes Schindelin (Dscho), the Git-for-Windows maintainer, has raised important concerns about the final piece of Patrick Steinhardt's reftable decoupling work. While the 18-patch series has successfully separated the reftable library from Git's core infrastructure—a significant architectural milestone—Schindelin reports that the Windows-specific header reorganization is causing build failures in Git-for-Windows CI. 

The core issues are practical rather than conceptual: duplicate filenames in different directories (like `compat-util.h` appearing in both mingw/ and msvc/) are making debugging harder, and the current implementation isn't playing nicely with Git-for-Windows' platform patches. Schindelin's feedback suggests the technical approach may need adjustment specifically for Windows integration, though he notes similar naming collisions exist elsewhere in the codebase. 

With all other reviewer feedback addressed and the series otherwise ready for merging, this represents the final hurdle. The ball is now in Patrick's court to either adjust the header organization or work with Schindelin on an alternative that satisfies both mainline Git and Git-for-Windows requirements. Given the significance of this modularization work and the reftable library's importance for projects like libgit2, a resolution is expected soon.
