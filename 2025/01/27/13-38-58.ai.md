This is a design discussion about the final architectural question in the OS version capability series - whether to keep it as a separate capability or merge it into the existing "agent" string.

Christian Couder weighs in against merging with the "agent" capability, arguing that following the web browser User-Agent model would be a mistake. He points to the historical problems with User-Agent strings - their difficult-to-parse format, privacy leaks, and the resulting spoofing that makes analytics unreliable. He notes Git's documentation specifically warns against using the "agent" capability to assume feature availability, suggesting the project's original intent was to use separate capabilities for distinct pieces of information.

The response directly addresses Junio's final open question from the implementation review, providing concrete reasons to maintain the current design of a separate "os-version" capability rather than combining it with "agent". This represents the first substantive counter-argument to Junio's suggestion of merging the capabilities, grounding the position in both historical design principles (Git's capability philosophy) and practical concerns (avoiding the pitfalls seen in web browsers). The discussion now has clear positions on both sides of this final design decision needed before the series can be merged.
