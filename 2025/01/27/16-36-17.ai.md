Bugfix investigation for `git fetch` tag behavior in shallow clones, focusing on transport protocol differences. Bence Ferdinandy provides a technical deep dive into why reordering tag/HEAD prefix additions in `do_fetch()` resolves the regression where tags pointing to shallow boundary commits weren't fetched with `--depth=1`. The analysis reveals that the current code's conditional tag addition (only when `ref_prefixes` is empty) fails because HEAD is always added first, while the working solution adds tags first. Bence traces this behavior to commit e70a3030e7 and notes that while removing the empty check breaks things, reordering works, though it feels like a band-aid solution. The email highlights remaining mysteries about why the original design chose this conditional tag-fetching approach and why reordering works when a direct fix doesn't. Key technical points include the role of `transport_ls_refs_options.ref_prefixes` and the `uses_remote_tracking()` check, with references to two experimental commits demonstrating different approaches. The discussion continues to seek understanding of the underlying protocol-specific behaviors that make this ordering matter.
