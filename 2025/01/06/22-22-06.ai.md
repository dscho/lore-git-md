Type safety improvement in date.c with potential behavior change concern.

This email from Eric Sunshine raises a specific concern about part 2 of the completed type safety patch series. While the overall series systematically converts integer types to avoid MSVC warnings, this particular change in date.c's `approxidate_alpha()` function introduces a potential behavior change that wasn't sufficiently explained in the original commit message.

The key issue involves changing a `strlen()` result from `int` to `size_t` while keeping a `len-1` comparison that could wrap around if `len` is zero. Eric notes this appears dangerous at first glance, though acknowledges it might be safe if either (1) `len` can never be zero in this context, or (2) the condition remains correct despite potential wraparound. He suggests this change deserves either explicit verification in the commit message or isolation as a separate commit for focused review.

This feedback comes late in the review cycle (v2 of a now-complete series) and focuses on just one of the many type conversions made across the codebase. The concern is specific to date.c's handling of string length comparisons in date parsing, separate from the series' broader work on time-related type safety improvements and the established pattern of `size_t` conversions elsewhere.
