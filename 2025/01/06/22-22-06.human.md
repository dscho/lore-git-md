This is a follow-up discussion about a type-safety bugfix in the date.c component of the ongoing MSVC warning fixes series. Eric Sunshine raises a potentially serious concern about one specific change in the patch.

The issue centers around converting a `strlen()` result from `size_t` to `int` in the `approxidate_alpha()` function. While the change aligns with the series' goal of type consistency, Eric points out that subtracting 1 from a zero-length string could cause integer underflow when using unsigned types. This could silently change the behavior of date parsing in edge cases.

Eric suggests two possible resolutions: either prove the length can never be zero (and document this in the commit message), or split this risky change into its own commit for focused review. He acknowledges he hasn't deeply analyzed the code himself, but flags this as something requiring special attention due to its potential behavioral impact.

This is the only outstanding concern in what otherwise appears to be a well-received series addressing MSVC warnings. The discussion highlights how even seemingly straightforward type conversions can have subtle implications that warrant careful review.
