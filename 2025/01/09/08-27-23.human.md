This is a refactoring series targeting Git's combine-diff implementation, which handles showing diffs for merge commits. The series comes from Jeff King (peff) who was inspired to clean up some confusing code after struggling to understand it himself.

The 14-patch series focuses on improving the readability and maintainability of the combine-diff code, particularly around how paths and parent information are handled during diff calculations. The changes are organized into three logical groups: initial allocation cleanups (patches 1-6), removal of a questionable optimization with corresponding simplifications (7-10), and list management improvements (11-14).

Peff acknowledges this could be seen as "churn" since it doesn't fix major bugs (aside from one minor leak fix in patch 1), but argues the improved clarity is worthwhile. He's particularly unsure about patch 14's approach to list tail management, noting it may be a matter of taste. The series touches several core diff-related files (combine-diff.c, tree-diff.c, etc.) with significant line count changes, mostly deletions from simplified code.

The changes appear carefully considered and well-explained, typical of Peff's contributions. While not urgent, this would likely make future work in this area easier, though reviewers will need to weigh the code clarity benefits against the churn cost.
