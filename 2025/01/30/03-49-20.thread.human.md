This thread documents the progression of fixes and improvements to Git's protocol v2 fetch behavior, particularly around tag decoration in shallow fetches. The issue was originally reported by Igor Todorovski, with Jeff King (Peff) leading the technical response.

The initial regression fix, which addressed broken tag decoration in protocol v2 fetches with depth limits, has already been merged and backported to the 2.49 release. This involved modifications to `builtin/fetch.c` and new test coverage in `t/t5503-tagfollow.sh`.

Now in its final stages, the 9-patch architectural improvement series has received comprehensive review from Taylor Blau, who has just given his final Reviewed-by approval. The series refactors ref-prefix handling across multiple components, introducing clearer function naming (`refspecs_to_ref_prefixes`), better fetch/push separation, and optimizations for config-less fetches. Taylor had previously approved key patches 6/9 (adding HEAD optimization) and 7/9 (simplifying ref-prefix list additions), and his latest approval confirms the entire approach aligns with their off-list discussions.

With this approval, the series is effectively ready to proceed, though some final optimizations remain - notably eliminating the `must_list_refs` flag. The changes span `builtin/fetch.c`, `refspec.c`, and test files `t/t5516-fetch-push.sh` and `t/t5702-protocol-v2.sh`, maintaining protocol v2 semantics while improving handling of edge cases like exact-OID fetches and negative refspecs. The thread shows steady progress from initial bug report through architectural refinements to final approval, with Jeff King and Taylor Blau as the primary technical drivers.
