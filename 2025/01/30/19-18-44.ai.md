This is a discussion about documentation practices for Git's GSoC 2025 projects, specifically around the "best-before" maintenance system for microprojects. Junio Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's concerns about project inclusion criteria, raising two key points about the proposed documentation approach.

First, Junio questions whether projects should have single owners, noting this could lead to abandoned work if the owner leaves. Second, he suggests "relevant" might be a better term than "accurate" for project status, since a wishlist item might technically be accurate but no longer pertinent due to other changes.

The email then weighs in on three specific microprojects under debate: sign comparison warnings (which Junio notes could degrade code quality if implemented carelessly), builtin patterns for userdiff (which could work if properly scoped with a curated list), and run_command*() replacements (which Junio implicitly agrees with Patrick is too vague). The discussion continues the thread's tension between including aspirational projects versus strictly bounded work.
