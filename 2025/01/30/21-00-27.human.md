This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's participation in Google Summer of Code 2025, specifically addressing how to handle project documentation. Junio Hamano responds to concerns about project ownership and maintenance in the proposed "best-before" system for tracking microprojects.

Junio suggests simplifying the expiration mechanism - entries would be automatically dropped when their timestamp passes, unless the community actively decides to extend them before that date. This removes the need for designated owners while still allowing projects to be revived later if there's renewed interest. He humorously suggests applying the same principle to long-lived topic branches that never graduate to 'next'.

The proposal represents a shift from the earlier ownership model, addressing concerns about single points of failure when maintainers leave. It maintains the goal of keeping the project list relevant while reducing maintenance burden. The suggestion comes as part of broader discussions about how to document and manage GSoC microprojects, where Junio has been actively shaping the approach alongside the org admins and mentors.
