This is a continuation of the GSoC 2025 microproject documentation discussion, specifically addressing the expiration mechanism for stale entries. Junio Hamano strongly disagrees with Patrick Steinhardt's suggestion that retiring microprojects should require a formal patch submission process.

Junio reiterates his position that expiration should be fully mechanical - entries should automatically drop when their timestamp passes without requiring any patch submission or community review. He argues the burden should be on those wanting to keep entries to justify their retention, not on maintainers to actively remove stale ones. This directly contradicts Patrick's earlier proposal that retirements should go through normal patch review like any other change.

The exchange reveals a fundamental philosophical difference in approach: Patrick wants to maintain community oversight through the standard patch process even for removals, while Junio sees this as unnecessary bureaucracy that defeats the purpose of having automatic expiration. Junio's position would result in silent removals unless someone proactively advocates for keeping an entry, whereas Patrick's would preserve visibility but require more maintenance overhead.

This disagreement represents the last major unresolved issue in finalizing the microproject documentation system, as all other aspects (timestamp comparison, status markers, ownership model) have achieved consensus. The thread is now at an impasse between these two perspectives on how to handle the actual removal of expired entries.
