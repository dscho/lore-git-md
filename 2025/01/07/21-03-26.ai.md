Junio Hamano's follow-up in the reftable entropy handling thread clarifies and refines his architectural objections while acknowledging a misreading of the original patch. This is a key design discussion email in the ongoing debate about cryptographic vs standard library randomness in reftable.

Junio initially thought the patch was introducing git_rand() where rand() previously worked, but now recognizes the code already used git_rand() and was failing. He maintains his position that reftable should minimize dependencies, suggesting either:
1. Reverting to standard rand() where possible
2. Considering constant values if randomness isn't critical for correctness

The email focuses on two specific uses in reftable/stack.c:
1. The retry delay calculation where randomness spreads out retries
2. The filename generation where randomness prevents collisions

Junio questions whether perfect randomness is actually required for these non-cryptographic purposes, implying the CSPRNG changes from part 1 might be overkill for reftable's needs. His "fix your rand(3)" comment suggests pushing back on platforms with broken implementations rather than working around them in Git.

This moves the discussion from technical feasibility (established by Randall's testing) to architectural philosophy - whether reftable should accept Git's CSPRNG infrastructure or maintain stricter isolation. Junio appears willing to accept part 1's general CSPRNG improvements while resisting their use in reftable specifically.
