Junio Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's reftable randomness patch with architectural concerns. While acknowledging the technical validity of the CSPRNG changes from part 1 of the series, he questions whether reftable should be using Git's cryptographic randomness at all given the subsystem's goal of minimizing dependencies.

The key points are:
1. Junio initially misunderstood the patch, thinking it was about platform rand() implementations failing, but now recognizes it's about Git's own git_rand() failing
2. He suggests the first hunk (retry delay calculation) might be better served by simpler randomness or even constant values, since perfect randomness isn't critical there
3. For the second hunk (filename generation), he questions whether cryptographic randomness is truly needed, proposing standard rand() or fixed values if there are no correctness implications
4. Underlying concern is maintaining reftable's independence from Git's core infrastructure where possible

This continues the thread's central tension between solving a real platform-specific issue (OpenSSL PRNGD failures on NonStop) and maintaining clean subsystem boundaries. Junio seems willing to accept the CSPRNG infrastructure but wants to limit its use in reftable specifically.
