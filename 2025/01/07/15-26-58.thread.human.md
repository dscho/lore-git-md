This thread began as a discussion about random number generation in the reftable backend but has expanded into a broader examination of Git's RNG strategy across the entire codebase. The original reftable discussion centered on whether temporary filenames and backoff delays needed cryptographic-quality randomness, with Patrick Steinhardt arguing for simpler approaches. 

Junio Hamano's latest response focuses specifically on the reftable case, noting that the update indices already provide sufficient protection against contention, making the random suffixes non-essential for correctness. He suggests two simpler alternatives: using the system's `rand()` function or even hardcoding a constant value. This would both resolve the immediate OpenSSL CSPRNG issues on ia64 systems (where hardware RNG support is lacking) and reduce Git-specific dependencies in the reftable library.

Meanwhile, the thread has uncovered wider system-level concerns about OpenSSL's CSPRNG reliability, particularly on ia64 and NonStop platforms. Randall Becker has highlighted that these platforms have no fallback RNG mechanisms, making them uniquely dependent on OpenSSL's implementation. With ia64 approaching its 2025 end-of-life, the project faces a tension between maintaining compatibility for legacy systems versus simplifying the codebase by removing problematic dependencies.

The discussion remains polarized between Patrick's push for dependency reduction and Randall's emphasis on platform support, with Junio carefully evaluating the broader implications. While the reftable-specific question appears resolvable through simplification, the systemic RNG issues will likely require more discussion about Git's long-term platform support strategy.
