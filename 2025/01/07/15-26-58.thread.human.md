This thread examines a reftable entropy handling issue that surfaced when OpenSSL's PRNGD server fails under load on NonStop systems. Patrick Steinhardt proposed a two-part solution: general CSPRNG infrastructure improvements (already accepted) and reftable-specific fixes (now under debate). Randall Becker confirmed the problem occurs specifically with OpenSSL's PRNGD on ia64 NonStop systems, validating the technical need for the fix.

Junio Hamano has refined his architectural objections, clarifying that while he initially misread the patch as introducing new git_rand() calls, he maintains that reftable should minimize dependencies. He questions whether perfect randomness is truly needed for reftable's temporary filenames and retry delays, suggesting either reverting to standard rand() or using constant values if randomness isn't critical. His stance reflects a philosophical preference for pushing back on platforms with broken implementations rather than working around them in Git.

The discussion has evolved from technical validation (where Randall's testing confirmed the PRNGD issue) to architectural principles. While the CSPRNG changes are accepted for Git generally, Junio resists their use in reftable specifically, wanting to maintain its dependency isolation. The core question now is whether reftable's needs justify the CSPRNG dependency or if simpler solutions would suffice while preserving the subsystem's design purity. Patrick's comprehensive solution remains technically sound but faces design philosophy objections at the reftable integration point.
