The core issue stems from OpenSSL's CSPRNG failing when entropy is exhausted on ia64 systems, while x86 systems continue working due to hardware RNG support. Randall notes this appeared suddenly after a maintenance window, suggesting an environmental change rather than a Git regression. With ia64 support ending in 2025, he's seeking a temporary solution that works with PRNGD, despite potential randomness quality tradeoffs.

Patrick Steinhardt, who initially focused on reftable-specific fixes, now recognizes this affects all `git_rand()` callsites across Git. He proposes removing OpenSSL CSPRNG support entirely since NonStop is the only platform still using it, but Randall points out ia64 lacks alternative RNG backends like `arc4random_buf` or `getrandom`. This reveals a tension between cryptographic security needs and practical constraints on legacy platforms.

The discussion has evolved from addressing specific test failures to considering architectural changes to Git's RNG system, with platform support timelines becoming a factor.
