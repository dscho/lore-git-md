Junio Hamano raises concerns about the reftable entropy handling patch series, questioning whether the solution is over-engineered for the problem at hand. This is a critical response to part 2 of Patrick Steinhardt's series addressing OpenSSL CSPRNG failures.

Hamano argues that since the reftable code only needs non-cryptographic randomness for retry delays and temporary filenames, using `rand(3)` would be simpler and more appropriate than the more complex `git_rand()` solution. He points out that `rand(3)` can't fail and provides sufficient randomness for these use cases, while the current approach requires special handling of entropy exhaustion cases.

The response suggests pushing back on the NonStop platform's behavior rather than accommodating it, questioning why we should "cater to such an insanity" when standard `rand(3)` would work fine. Hamano also notes this change goes against reftable's trend of reducing dependencies on the rest of Git.

While acknowledging that part 1 of the series makes sense regardless, Hamano's core critique is that part 2's solution may be unnecessarily complex for the problem being solved. The tone is direct but constructive, focusing on whether this is the right technical approach rather than attacking the implementation quality. This appears to be opening a discussion about whether to simplify the solution or maintain the current approach with its platform-specific considerations.
