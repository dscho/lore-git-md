This is a follow-up in the reftable entropy handling thread, where Randall Becker responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about using Git's CSPRNG infrastructure for non-cryptographic purposes in reftable. Randall clarifies that the issue on NonStop systems stems from OpenSSL's PRNGD being overloaded, not from a broken `rand(3)` implementation. He explains that their x86 hardware randomizer works reliably, but the ia64 platform's PRNGD struggles under heavy load, making this a real-world reliability issue rather than a platform quirk to be ignored.

The email pushes back against Junio's suggestion to simply "fix your rand(3)", arguing the problem reveals a systemic issue with PRNGD reliability under load. Randall's response maintains that Patrick Steinhardt's solution (using Git's CSPRNG with insecure fallback) properly addresses the observed failures while acknowledging the architectural concerns about reftable's independence from Git internals.

This exchange highlights the tension between solving immediate platform-specific issues and maintaining clean subsystem boundaries. The technical solution works (as confirmed by Randall's testing), but Junio questions whether it's the right architectural approach for reftable's goals. The discussion now hinges on whether reliability concerns justify the dependency or if reftable should stick to standard library functions despite potential platform quirks.
