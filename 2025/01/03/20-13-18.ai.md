Bug investigation regarding `git log` date filtering anomalies with time-disordered commits. René Scharfe analyzes Sarah Gastner's reported case where commit 501976cc34e appears to have a later commit date than its child commit (March 13 vs March 7), suggesting possible history rewriting with `git rebase --committer-date-is-author-date` that didn't update commit times. The core issue stems from Git's optimization that stops traversal when encountering commits older than the `--after` date, which fails when commit dates are non-chronological. 

Key technical details: the problematic commit chain shows parent-child relationships with timestamps that would require "time travel" (child 4346c5e5e29 dated March 7 appearing after parent 501976cc34e dated March 13). René explores potential solutions - adding slack to searches, special handling of out-of-order commits, or full traversal to root - but notes these would either be expensive to implement or create new edge cases. The current behavior, while imperfect, represents a pragmatic tradeoff where users modifying history must account for date inconsistencies themselves.

This moves the discussion from bug report to design consideration, framing the behavior as a known limitation of the date-filtering optimization rather than a straightforward bug.
