Bug investigation follow-up regarding `git log` date filtering. René Scharfe examines the problematic commit chain that Sarah Gastner reported, revealing an unusual case where commit 501976cc34e appears to have been committed *after* its child commit (March 13 vs March 7) - likely due to a rebase operation that didn't update commit timestamps. 

The core issue stems from Git's optimization that stops traversing history when it hits a commit older than the `--after` date, which fails when commit dates are non-chronological. René explores potential fixes but notes the tradeoffs: adding arbitrary slack to date ranges could be expensive (requiring full history traversal) while special handling of out-of-order commits would add complexity. 

The email frames this as a known limitation rather than a straightforward bug - when users rewrite history without proper timestamp updates, date-based filtering becomes unreliable. No solution is proposed, but the analysis helps clarify why the original issue occurs and invites discussion about whether the current behavior (where history-rewriters bear responsibility for timestamp consistency) is acceptable or needs improvement.
