CI modernization follow-up: performance measurement of containerized jobs.

Jeff King provides empirical data on the performance impact of containerizing Linux CI jobs, addressing his earlier question about potential slowdowns. Comparing Patrick Steinhardt's containerized test runs against the master branch's bare-metal executions, the timing differences appear negligible - most jobs show variations of less than a minute in either direction, with no clear pattern of degradation. The sha256 job shows the most notable increase (37 seconds slower), while some jobs like linux-TEST-vars and linux-leaks actually complete faster in the containerized environment.

This data point confirms that the containerization approach doesn't introduce meaningful performance overhead, removing a potential concern about the patch series' operational impact. The measurement methodology is straightforward - direct comparison of GitHub Actions timing logs between equivalent test runs - and the results support proceeding with the containerization strategy as proposed. No new action items emerge from this analysis, as the performance characteristics meet expectations for production use.
