Here's the human-readable summary of the thread:

The thread began with Christoph Anton Mitterer asking why a shell function alias named "stash" wouldn't work properly - when defined, `git stash` would still show the stash list rather than executing the alias. Junio Hamano explained this is intentional behavior in Git: aliases cannot shadow built-in commands, as this could break scripts and cause other problems. He pointed to the relevant documentation that describes this design choice.

In a follow-up, Elijah Newren strongly reinforced this position, citing a 2009 email from Junio that established this principle. He explained that allowing command shadowing could lead to infinite recursion and make Git's behavior unpredictable. Newren also addressed concerns about the clarity of the alias documentation, defending the existing manpage text as sufficiently detailed.

The thread shows the Git maintainers taking a firm stance against allowing command shadowing through aliases, prioritizing script reliability over individual customization. While the original question was technically resolved with Junio's first response, Newren's follow-up served to firmly close any remaining discussion about potential workarounds. The consistent message is that this behavior is working as designed and won't be changed.
