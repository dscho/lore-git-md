Junio Hamano responds to Phillip Wood's explanation about the performance characteristics of cherry-pick detection during rebase operations. Phillip had noted that while cherry-pick detection occurs within `git log`, it has both fast (commit enumeration) and slow (actual cherry-pick detection) phases, with the latter being particularly problematic when there are many new upstream commits - a known issue that motivated the `--reapply-cherry-picks` optimization in commit 0fcb4f6b62.

Junio questions whether this level of implementation performance detail belongs in the high-level "how it works" section of the documentation, suggesting it might be more appropriate for the `--reapply-cherry-picks` option description where users would actively consider performance tradeoffs. The exchange reflects the ongoing tension between technical accuracy and approachability in the documentation effort, with both contributors aiming to find the right balance between conceptual models and implementation realities.

Key technical points mentioned include the two-phase nature of cherry-pick detection (fast enumeration vs slow comparison), the specific performance optimization introduced in 0fcb4f6b62, and the question of where performance characteristics should be documented in the manual page structure.
