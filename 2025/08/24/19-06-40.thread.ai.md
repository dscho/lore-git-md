Performance optimization and refactoring series for Git's line-log functionality (git log -L), focusing on merge commit processing. Series consists of 4 patches (1 performance optimization + 3 cleanups) now merged into 'seen'. Core change in patch 1 restructures `process_ranges_merge_commit()` to combine two parent-diff passes into one, avoiding unnecessary tree diffs against secondary parents when first parent shows no tracked path changes. Benchmarks show 2.25x speedup in git.git (25.8% merges), 1.32x in linux.git (7.5% merges), and 1.15-1.38x with Bloom filters (confirmed by Derrick Stolee). Historical context from SZEDER Gábor reveals the optimization was originally split into two patches (performance + diffqueues removal) before being squashed, with the original optimization-only diff showing the pass-combining logic.

Patch 2 refactors to eliminate dynamic allocation of diff queues (-12 lines), patch 3 improves `process_ranges_ordinary_commit()` initialization correctness, and patch 4 reorganizes `process_ranges_arbitrary_commit()` control flow for readability by checking merge status first. Derrick Stolee reviewed the entire series positively, confirming performance gains across repository configurations. Junio Hamano engaged on all patches, approving substantive changes and ultimately accepting the final readability improvement as addressing "brokenness" from poor control flow. All changes are internal to line-log.c (-43 LOC across 63 modified lines) with no user-facing behavior changes. The series combines measurable performance gains with structural improvements, now progressing through integration in 'seen'.
