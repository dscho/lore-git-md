Performance optimization discussion for `git describe`'s depth computation. Jeff King responds to René Scharfe's khash-based optimization with an alternative approach using commit-slabs, comparing their performance characteristics.

King notes that commit-slabs were specifically designed for this type of zero-cost lookup scenario, though they allocate space for all commits rather than just active ones. His benchmark shows the khash version is slightly faster (1.03x) than the commit-slab approach, though both significantly outperform the original (1.31x and 1.28x faster respectively).

The email raises several interesting technical questions about when each approach would be preferable, including performance with dense vs sparse commit sets, handling different stride sizes, and the possibility of eliminating the commit->index field entirely. King suggests this could lead to broader improvements in how Git handles object flag space management across subsystems.

This is a thoughtful technical discussion between two experienced contributors about optimizing Git's internals, with both approaches showing merit. The conversation appears likely to influence future design decisions about Git's low-level data structures beyond just this specific `git describe` optimization.
