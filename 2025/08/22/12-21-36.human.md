This is a follow-up discussion in the RFC thread for the proposed `git-history` command series, which aims to provide Jujutsu-inspired history editing capabilities in Git. Patrick Steinhardt responds to feedback from Ben Knoble and Junio Hamano about the initial design.

The exchange focuses on two key constraints in the current implementation: notes handling during history rewrites (where Patrick agrees to address Junio's suggestion about `notes.rewriteref` in a future version) and the intentional limitation to linear history operations for initial simplicity. Ben Knoble draws parallels to `rebase --update-refs` and Jujutsu's behavior, suggesting potential future directions for handling commits reachable from multiple branches.

Patrick maintains a conservative approach for the initial implementation, explicitly choosing to "die in all kinds of situations that require complex handling" to establish a solid foundation before expanding functionality. The discussion doesn't introduce new technical changes to the series but reinforces the careful, incremental design philosophy behind it. This positions the work as a thoughtfully-scoped experiment in bringing Jujutsu-like capabilities to Git's history manipulation toolkit.
