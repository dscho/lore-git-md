This is a response in the ongoing RFC discussion about the new `git-history` command series. Patrick Steinhardt is replying to feedback from Ben Knoble and Junio Hamano about the initial proposal.

The exchange focuses on two key design constraints in the current implementation: notes handling and merge commit support. Steinhardt acknowledges Junio's point about properly handling `notes.rewriteref` (which cherry-pick deliberately avoids) but indicates this will likely wait until v3 of the series. He also responds to Knoble's observations about rebase-like behavior for commits reachable from multiple branches, explaining his current conservative approach of failing in complex cases to keep the initial implementation simple.

The thread continues to explore the tension between the command's current cherry-pick-based implementation and the broader vision for history manipulation. Steinhardt emphasizes an incremental approach, preferring to start with restrictive behavior that can be relaxed later rather than tackling all edge cases upfront. This maintains the series' focus on core functionality while leaving room for future expansion based on the ongoing design discussions about Git's history editing architecture.
