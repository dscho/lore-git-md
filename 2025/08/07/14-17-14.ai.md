This is a technical discussion response in the ongoing thread about introducing a `count_t` typedef for entity counting in Git. Phillip Wood raises several substantive concerns about Patrick Steinhardt's proposal.

The email questions core assumptions of the `count_t` approach, arguing that the main type safety issue is mismatched loop index/limit types rather than the specific integer type used. Phillip notes that hiding the signedness via typedef could make down-counting loops harder to debug, and that negative sentinel values would still require mixed signed/unsigned types anyway.

On the technical implementation, Phillip challenges the choice of `uintptr_t` as the base type, pointing out that most allocations use `ALLOC_GROW` mechanisms which already assume `size_t` sizing. He specifically flags `alloc_nr()`'s growth calculation as a more pressing overflow risk than the theoretical maximum entity count scenario the `uintptr_t` choice addresses.

The response suggests the proposal may be solving a different problem than the most common type safety issues in practice, and that alternative approaches like fixing `alloc_nr()` might provide more immediate benefit. This introduces new considerations about the scope and priorities of the type safety effort beyond the initial `count_t` proposal.
