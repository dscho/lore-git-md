This is a feature proposal in the ongoing discussion about graphical/visual Git history manipulation tools. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's suggestion of a GUI frontend for `rebase -i` by proposing a complementary command-line approach: a new `git history` subcommand that would provide structured operations for commit reordering, dropping, and splitting.

The proposed interface would allow specific history operations through discrete commands like `git history reorder` (moving commits before/after others), `drop` (removing commits), and `split` (dividing commits). This appears to be a middle ground between raw `rebase -i` and a full GUI, offering more user-friendly verbs while staying in the terminal.

Steinhardt positions this as an initial step that could be expanded later, noting he'll share the patch series soon. The proposal seems well-aligned with the thread's exploration of making history manipulation more accessible, though it takes a different direction from the GUI focus of the original request. The approach looks promising for terminal-oriented users who want more structure than raw rebase but don't require mouse-driven interaction.
