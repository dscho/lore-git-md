This is a discussion about the Rust xdiff optimization series, focusing on maintainer guidance and next steps. Elijah Newren responds to Junio Hamano's feedback on the RFC series, clarifying the original motivations and proposing potential paths forward.

The email confirms that the performance improvements (5-19%) come from XXH3 hashing rather than Rust intrinsics, aligning with Junio's observation. Newren explains the backstory: Ezekiel Newren's xdiff work was originally aimed at enabling `git replay` and rebasing merges, with the Rust speedup being a serendipitous discovery during that effort.

Key technical considerations discussed:
- The challenge of finding suitable Rust entry points that allow proper unit testing (needing low-level access rather than FFI calls to C
- Potential next steps including rebasing on am/xdiff-hash-tweak, adding platform support patches, and making xdfile_t FFI-friendly
- The interconnected nature of xdiff components (xdlclassifier struct, hashing, line equality) that may require future modifications to the optimization work

The discussion reveals tensions between:
1) The series' original xdiff cleanup goals vs. its current Rust performance focus
2) The desire to establish Rust framework policies vs. not wanting to block other optimizations
3) The need for testable Rust code vs. the constraints of FFI boundaries

Newren proposes a middle path - keeping the xdiff focus but adjusting the implementation approach to better align with maintainer priorities while still allowing Ezekiel to demonstrate Rust's testing capabilities. The email highlights how architectural decisions in xdiff (like data structure choices) may require revisiting the optimization work later.
