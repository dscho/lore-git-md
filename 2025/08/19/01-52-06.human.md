This is a discussion about the ongoing xdiff Rustification effort, specifically addressing maintainer concerns about performance claims and implementation priorities.

Elijah Newren responds to Junio Hamano's feedback on the Rust-based xdiff optimization series, clarifying the original intent behind the work. The discussion reveals that the performance improvements (5-19%) come primarily from using XXH3 hashing rather than Rust itself, and that the Rust implementation was originally motivated by broader xdiff cleanup needs for `git replay` and rebase functionality.

The email explores potential paths forward, including rebasing the work on top of existing C-based optimizations (am/xdiff-hash-tweak) and suggests focusing first on establishing Rust infrastructure (CI support, FFI patterns) before tackling deeper xdiff restructuring. Newren notes the challenge of finding suitable Rust entry points that allow for meaningful unit testing while avoiding complex C stubbing requirements.

Key points of discussion include:
- Whether to prioritize framework setup over performance claims
- How to sequence the xdiff restructuring work with existing optimizations
- The tension between Rust's unit testing benefits and FFI complexity
- Potential next steps including platform support patches and xdfile_t modernization

The exchange shows the series is evolving from a performance-focused proposal to a more balanced approach that addresses maintainer concerns about architectural foundations while preserving the technical work done so far.
