This thread continues the discussion of Karthik Nayak's reftable fsck validation series, now in its third iteration. The series adds comprehensive integrity checking for the reftable backend, with validation from the stack level down to individual refs. The current focus is on terminology refinement for describing index ordering requirements in patch 4/8.

Junio Hamano suggested using "monotonically increasing" to describe update index ordering, but Karthik points out this could incorrectly imply equality is allowed between adjacent tables' indices. He proposes "ascending order" as a clearer alternative that properly captures the strict inequality requirement (prev_table.max_update_index < cur_table.min_update_index). This builds on earlier feedback from Patrick Steinhardt, who identified that compaction scenarios could legitimately produce non-consecutive indices, leading to relaxed validation requirements.

The series has seen substantial review from Patrick Steinhardt, who has provided detailed technical feedback across multiple patches - from identifying valid compaction scenarios to catching implementation issues around error handling and race conditions. Junio has weighed in on higher-level architectural and documentation concerns. The discussion remains focused and productive, with the author responsive to feedback while maintaining the series' core goal of robust validation that respects the separation between Git's fsck and reftable internals.
