Karthik Nayak has completed his v4 series adding fsck validation to the reftable backend, with the final patch implementing the actual integration with Git's fsck infrastructure. The series strengthens validation while maintaining the reftable backend's design principles, focusing initially on enforcing proper table naming conventions (`${min_update_index}-${max_update_index}-${random}.ref` pattern).

The implementation introduces a callback-based validation framework in the reftable library (patch 6/7) that the final patch connects to Git's fsck system via `reftable_be_fsck()`. Violations are reported as warnings (FSCK_MSG_BAD_REFTABLE_TABLE_NAME) rather than errors. The series incorporates feedback from Patrick Steinhardt and Junio Hamano, particularly around validation scope and race condition avoidance.

Test coverage in t0614-reftable-fsck.sh has been expanded to verify detection of various malformed table name patterns. While this completes the immediate goal of name validation, the callback architecture is designed to support adding more checks in the future. The series maintains the separation between reftable-specific validation and Git's core fsck while ensuring consistent error handling.
