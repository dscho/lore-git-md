Here's a human-readable summary of the thread's current state:

Patrick Steinhardt has proposed a new `git-history` command with three subcommands (`drop`, `reorder`, and `split`) inspired by Jujutsu's functionality. The RFC series consists of thirteen patches that include preparatory work, command scaffolding, subcommand implementations, and supporting refactoring. The implementation builds on Git's sequencer infrastructure and introduces interactive commit splitting with hunk selection via `add-patch`.

Review progress has been delayed due to email delivery issues - patches 8-11 didn't reach reviewer D. Ben Knoble's GMail due to known rate-limiting between GMail and the Linux Kernel Mailing List infrastructure. Ben has acknowledged the issue (having seen recent discussion about it on Mastodon) and mentioned eventual plans to switch email providers, though this isn't an immediate priority. The technical discussion remains on hold until Ben can retrieve the missing patches from the archive.

The proposal currently lacks merge commit support and has several open questions around conflict handling, performance, cache-tree implications, and interactive configuration. The `split` subcommand appears the most developed, with over 300 lines of tests already written. Once the email delivery issues are resolved, we can expect substantive review feedback from Ben Knoble on the later patches in the series.
