The `git-history` command proposal, now in its ninth iteration, has reached a mature state with Elijah Newren's recent fixup patches addressing the final technical gaps—most notably comprehensive detached HEAD handling. The series implements flexible history editing through `reword` and `split` subcommands, building on Git's replay machinery while drawing inspiration from Jujutsu. Key refinements include standardized error codes, removal of redundant fields, and 47 lines of new test coverage for edge cases.

A recent exchange between Newren and Patrick Steinhardt fine-tunes the implementation further. They agreed to simplify the `replay_revisions()` interface by removing a redundant repository parameter, but debated error handling strategies. Steinhardt advocated retaining explicit conflict checks to support future library use cases, while Newren preferred streamlined returns. This reflects the series' transition from RFC to merge-ready, with only minor documentation and workflow considerations remaining open. Junio Hamano will evaluate the replay-based implementation, now complete with detached HEAD support, for potential inclusion.
