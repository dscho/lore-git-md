Patrick Steinhardt's proposed `git-history` command series, which introduces `reword` and `split` subcommands for flexible history editing, has reached a critical juncture in its review. While Junio Hamano believes the series is nearly ready for integration—suggesting limiting initial scope to single-branch cases—Elijah Newren has raised substantive concerns that may require more significant revisions.

Elijah challenges the assumption that single-branch limitation simplifies the implementation, warning that inadequate verification could lead to bugs when users inevitably encounter multi-branch scenarios. He also examines subtle behaviors around index preservation during commit splitting, noting that while the current implementation may correctly sum split commits to the original changes, this behavior needs explicit documentation. Additionally, he questions design choices around commit metadata handling, particularly the preservation of extended headers and the association of original messages with the second commit in a split rather than the first.

The discussion reveals a gap in maintainer expectations: Junio advocates for incremental integration, while Elijah maintains that several architectural decisions—particularly around branch handling and commit metadata—require deeper consideration. Patrick Steinhardt now faces the task of either narrowing the scope as Junio suggests or addressing Elijah's concerns more comprehensively in the next revision. The technical direction remains agreed upon, but the path to integration now involves negotiating these implementation details.
