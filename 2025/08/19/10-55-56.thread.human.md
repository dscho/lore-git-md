Patrick Steinhardt's `git-history` RFC continues to evolve through active technical review, with the v6 series now addressing both the history editing commands (`reword` and `split`) and their supporting infrastructure. The latest discussion focuses on patch 09/11, where Phillip Wood and Elijah Newren are examining the design of `run_add_p_index()` - a new function enabling in-memory index operations for the split functionality. While Phillip acknowledges Elijah's concern about potential future divergence between this and the existing `run_add_p()` function, no fundamental objections have been raised to the overall approach.

Junio Hamano has already approved several key patches, including the `the_repository` removal in commit creation handling. The infrastructure refactoring has largely been completed, with the add-patch/add-interactive separation now finalized according to Phillip Wood's review. Meanwhile, Elijah Newren continues to evaluate the split subcommand's design, particularly whether it should support N-way splits rather than just binary splits, and how commit messages should be handled during splits. The discussion remains productive, with reviewers focusing on implementation details rather than raising new blocking concerns, suggesting the series is nearing readiness for integration.
