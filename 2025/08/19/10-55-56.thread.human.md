This RFC thread introduces Patrick Steinhardt's proposed `git-history` command, which brings Jujutsu-inspired history editing operations to Git through three subcommands: `drop`, `reorder`, and `split`. The thirteen-patch series has seen active discussion, with Junio C Hamano approving the conservative implementation approach while encouraging incremental improvements.

The latest exchange focuses on the `split` subcommand's UX, particularly how commit messages should be handled when dividing changes. Junio advocates showing the original message in both resulting commits to preserve context, while Patrick plans to modify the implementation to at least show the split-out changes in the editor (similar to `git commit`). This follows earlier discussions where Patrick confirmed `drop` will automatically rebase descendants (like Jujutsu's `jj-abandon`) and explained the `reorder` naming choice to avoid confusion with Git's rebase command.

Throughout the thread, Patrick has been responsive to feedback, accepting documentation improvements from Jean-Noël Avila and addressing technical points raised by D. Ben Knoble. The discussion remains focused on practical refinements rather than fundamental redesigns, suggesting the core approach is stabilizing. Several areas are marked for future consideration, including notes rewriting, merge commit support expansion, and potential adoption of revset syntax, but the current priority is shipping a solid initial version.
