Patrick Steinhardt's `git-history` RFC continues to evolve, now at v3 with twenty-six patches implementing history editing subcommands (`drop`, `reorder`, `split`, `reword`) and management operations. The current discussion centers on a confirmed issue where the `split` subcommand fails to show diffs between old and new trees in some environments, with D. Ben Knoble providing a reproducible case that Patrick acknowledges points to wt-status interface problems, though he can't reproduce it locally.

Meanwhile, Knoble has proposed expanding the command's scope with new `squash` and `fixup` subcommands inspired by vim-fugitive's workflow. These would allow creating and automatically applying fixup/squash commits in one step, similar to interactive rebase's autosquash functionality. While this suggestion aligns with the tool's goal of flexible history editing, it's currently a secondary consideration to resolving the immediate diff visibility issues in the `split` operation. The thread now has two parallel tracks: debugging a concrete implementation problem and exploring potential future enhancements.
