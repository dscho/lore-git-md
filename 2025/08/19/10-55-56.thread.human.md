This RFC thread continues to evolve Patrick Steinhardt's proposed `git-history` command, which aims to provide user-friendly history editing operations inspired by Jujutsu (jj). The discussion has progressed through technical refinements and now addresses broader design philosophy.

The latest exchange focuses on command organization, with Patrick responding to Elijah Newren's concerns about proliferating top-level commands. Patrick clarifies his vision for a clear division of labor: `git-replay` would serve as the low-level plumbing for scripts and server-side use, while `git-history` would become the primary user interface for interactive history editing operations like reordering, dropping, and splitting commits. He argues the subcommand model improves discoverability compared to separate commands, though he's open to providing aliases for those who prefer that style.

Patrick establishes clear criteria for what belongs in `git-history` - it should focus exclusively on editing existing commit sequences, explicitly excluding commit creation, import/export operations, and working tree modifications. He's considering whether "histedit" might be a more precise name, though no decision has been made yet. Both developers agree the experimental flag provides flexibility for future adjustments as the feature matures.

The thread shows Patrick thoughtfully balancing technical implementation (now at v3 with eighteen patches) with architectural vision, while remaining responsive to community feedback. Key participants include Phillip Wood (providing detailed implementation suggestions), Junio Hamano (noting integration constraints), and Elijah Newren (raising design philosophy questions). The discussion remains productive as the feature approaches its planned reorganization after the `reorder` implementation.
