Patrick Steinhardt's `git-history` RFC continues to evolve with careful review from the community. The series, now at v4 with seventeen patches, introduces new history editing subcommands (`reword` and `split`) while refactoring underlying infrastructure like the cache-tree and add-patch systems to support in-memory operations.

Karthik Nayak has been providing thorough technical review, with his latest feedback focusing on the `reword` subcommand implementation. He confirms the core functionality is sound - particularly the commit replacement mechanism that preserves history structure and the interactive editing flow - while suggesting several refinements. These include potential optimizations (like reusing `original_commit_tree_oid`), earlier ancestry checks, and documentation clarifications about commit retention. He also notes some style nits around string alignment and usage messages that could be made more consistent with Git conventions.

The series has already addressed prior feedback from several contributors (SZEDER Gábor, Phillip Wood, Oswald Buddenhagen) and resolved merge conflicts with Jeff King's parallel work. While the core architecture appears settled, some open questions remain about hook integration, merge commit handling, and performance with large histories. The careful back-and-forth between Patrick and reviewers like Karthik demonstrates the project's emphasis on both technical robustness and adherence to established conventions as this new command takes shape.
