Patrick Steinhardt's `git-history` proposal continues to mature with Karthik Nayak providing focused technical reviews. The RFC series, now at v4, introduces `reword` and `split` subcommands for non-destructive history editing, building on Git's sequencer while drawing inspiration from tools like Jujutsu.

Karthik's latest review examines patch 10/12, which refactors the add-patch infrastructure to support in-memory index operations - a key requirement for the history editing commands. He found the technical approach sound, noting only a minor style issue (a missing trailing comma in initialization) that should be easy to address. This continues the pattern of thorough but constructive feedback throughout the series, where core functionality has been validated while catching small refinements.

The discussion has progressed to fine details after earlier rounds addressed broader concerns from reviewers like Phillip Wood and SZEDER Gábor. Current open questions focus on documentation wording, header file organization, and naming conventions for new data structures. The series appears to be in good shape technically, with the remaining work being primarily polish and consistency improvements before potential inclusion.
