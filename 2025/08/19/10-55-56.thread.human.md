This RFC thread explores Patrick Steinhardt's proposal for a new `git-history` command, inspired by Jujutsu's interactive history editing capabilities. The current v3 series includes twenty-six patches implementing subcommands like `drop`, `reorder`, `split`, and `reword`, plus management commands. 

Two main discussions are underway. First, D. Ben Knoble identified a reproducible issue where the `split` subcommand fails to show diffs in some environments, involving the `wt_status_collect_changes_trees()` and `wt_status_print()` interfaces. While Patrick confirmed the expected behavior, he couldn't reproduce the issue locally, leaving this as an open implementation challenge.

Second, D. Ben Knoble proposed adding `squash` and `fixup` subcommands (inspired by vim-fugitive's cF/cS keybindings) as future enhancements. Patrick has now responded positively to at least the `squash` proposal, indicating this direction aligns with the command's goals, though without committing to implementation details or timeline. The discussion remains open on how these might integrate with the existing subcommands and editor workflows.

The thread shows active technical investigation of current functionality alongside forward-looking feature discussion, with Patrick maintaining focus on resolving the immediate `split` issues while acknowledging the potential for expanded capabilities.
