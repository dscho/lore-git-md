Dan Allen asked about migrating from `git whatchanged` to `git log` for extracting file modification timestamps, as `whatchanged` may be removed in Git 2.51. The thread established that `git log --raw --pretty=%at` is the maintainer-approved replacement, with Ben Zanin verifying near-identical output between the commands - though `git log` shows 5 additional timestamps in testing.

Jeff King (Peff) has now explained the discrepancy: `git log` includes empty commits (non-merges with identical trees to their parent) due to an internal flag, while `whatchanged` silently skips these. This behavior is hardcoded with no user-facing toggle. While `git log`'s approach is technically more correct by showing all commits, the practical impact depends on whether Dan's timestamp synchronization script needs to process these empty commits. The thread now awaits Dan's evaluation of whether the additional timestamps would affect his workflow before considering any adjustments to the deprecation path.
