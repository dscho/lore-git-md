Here's the updated human-readable summary:

The thread began with Dan Allen seeking an alternative to `git whatchanged --pretty=%at` for extracting UNIX timestamps to set filesystem mtimes, concerned about potential removal of the command in Git 2.51. The community quickly identified `git log --raw --pretty=%at` as the maintainer-approved replacement, though with a minor behavioral difference: `git log` includes empty commits (non-merges with identical trees to their parents) while `whatchanged` omits them. Jeff King pinpointed this as due to an internal `revs.always_show_header` flag, and maintainer Junio Hamano confirmed this as an intentional philosophical distinction - `git log` shows the complete history.

Ben Zanin verified the commands produce nearly identical output in Git 2.51.0's repository, with `git log` showing just five additional timestamps from empty commits. Kristoffer Haugsbakk later confirmed Dan successfully tested the replacement with `--no-merges` and reminded about reply-all mailing list etiquette. The thread appears resolved, with the community consensus being that the minor output differences are acceptable for mtime synchronization purposes. No further technical concerns have been raised about the migration path.
