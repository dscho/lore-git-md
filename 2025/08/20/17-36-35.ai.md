Junio C Hamano's response to the git-history RFC series focuses on two key technical considerations for the proposed history editing functionality.

The first point addresses notes handling, noting that while cherry-pick deliberately doesn't honor notes.rewriteref (since it creates parallel history), the new history editing operations should handle notes differently as they rewrite history in-place. This suggests a need to distinguish between the copy-semantics of cherry-pick and the rewrite-semantics of history editing operations.

The second comment endorses the initial design choice to limit operations to linear history but speculates about future expansion. Junio suggests that multi-branch history rewriting could be useful but would require protection mechanisms for certain branches. This implies the current single-strand limitation is a good starting point but leaves the door open for more complex use cases.

The response also includes a footnote clarifying the conceptual difference between history rewriting (which replaces history) and cherry-picking (which copies commits). This distinction appears relevant to the design philosophy of the new commands and their relationship to existing Git operations.
