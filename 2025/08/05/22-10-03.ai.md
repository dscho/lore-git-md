This is a follow-up in the performance investigation thread about `git cat-file --batch-command`'s `info` subcommand. Rob Browning reports new test results suggesting the previously observed poor performance (32 hashes/sec) may be hardware-specific rather than a Git regression.

The key new finding is that when testing with an internal NVMe drive (instead of the original external USB-connected SSD), performance improves dramatically - starting slow but reaching 37k hashes/sec after warmup. This suggests the earlier pathological behavior (sustained high disk reads without performance improvement) may be related to the external storage setup rather than Git's mmap implementation.

The author notes they will conduct more tests but tentatively concludes the solution may be to replace their hardware rather than modify Git. This shifts the focus of the investigation from potential Git optimizations to hardware/driver considerations, though the root cause of the external storage performance difference remains unexplained.

Technical context remains the same: the issue affects the `info` subcommand of `--batch-command` in large repositories (~125GB, 3M objects). The thread now has two data points showing radically different performance characteristics based on storage configuration, with the better-aligned hardware showing expected cache-warming behavior.
