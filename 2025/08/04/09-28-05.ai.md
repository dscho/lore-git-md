This is a discussion about wildcard matching behavior in the `git refs list` series, specifically addressing concerns around pattern matching semantics inherited from `for-each-ref`. Phillip Wood responds to Junio Hamano's clarification about how refname patterns currently work, pointing out the inherent ambiguity in the matching rules.

The key issue raised is that the current behavior makes it impossible to predict from the pattern alone whether a match will be prefix-based or exact. Phillip notes this creates problems for scripting use cases where callers need deterministic matching behavior. While adding a trailing slash can force prefix matching, there's no equivalent mechanism to enforce exact matching.

This discussion builds on the thread's established context where the team had tentatively agreed to maintain `for-each-ref` compatibility for wildcard handling. Phillip's email surfaces a practical limitation of that approach, though he acknowledges the race condition concern may not be critical in most scenarios. The exchange highlights a tension between backward compatibility and providing more intuitive matching semantics for the new `refs list` command.

Technical specifics mentioned include:
- Current behavior uses WM_PATHNAME mode from wildmatch.c
- Patterns like `refs/heads` do prefix matching while `refs/heads/m` does not
- No way to force exact matching through pattern syntax
- Script workarounds require parsing `%(refname)` output

The discussion remains focused on edge case behavior rather than proposing concrete changes, suggesting this may be documenting a known limitation rather than pushing for immediate resolution.
