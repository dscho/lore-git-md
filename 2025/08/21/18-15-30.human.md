This is a design discussion in the ongoing RFC thread about the proposed `git-history` command, focusing on the relationship between rebase and cherry-pick functionality.

Sergey Organov responds to Ben Knoble's suggestion that `git rebase --onto` already provides the "rebase there" capability that Organov had described as missing. Organov clarifies that `rebase --onto` modifies the source branch rather than preserving it, which differs from the desired behavior of keeping the original history intact while applying changes elsewhere - more akin to cherry-pick's approach but with rebase's features.

The discussion highlights a fundamental tension in Git's command architecture: whether history modification (rebase-style) and commit copying (cherry-pick-style) should remain separate or be unified. Organov argues that rebase's richer feature set (interactive mode, exec hooks, merge handling) makes it the better candidate for user-facing operations, suggesting cherry-pick's range support may have been a historical misstep. This philosophical debate about command boundaries and user experience directly informs the design of the proposed `git-history` command.

The exchange remains cordial but reveals differing perspectives on Git's command architecture - Knoble sees rebase as duplicating cherry-pick functionality, while Organov takes the opposite view. This discussion may influence whether `git-history` eventually subsumes parts of rebase's functionality or maintains clearer separation from it.
