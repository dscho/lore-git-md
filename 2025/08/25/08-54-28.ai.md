This is a technical follow-up in the `git whatchanged` deprecation thread, analyzing behavioral differences between `whatchanged` and its `git log` replacement.

Jeff King identifies the root cause of the 5 additional timestamps Ben Zanin observed when comparing `git whatchanged --pretty=%at` with `git log --raw --no-merges --pretty=%at`. The discrepancy stems from empty commits (non-merges with identical trees to their first parent) - `git log` shows these due to its `revs.always_show_header` flag, while `whatchanged` silently skips them. 

Key technical points:
- The difference is hardcoded behavior (`revs.always_show_header`) with no user-facing option to toggle it
- Pathspec filtering could reduce output but may have unintended side effects
- `git log`'s behavior is arguably more correct by showing all commits
- Practical impact depends on whether Dan Allen's timestamp synchronization script needs to process these empty commits

The email leaves open whether this behavioral difference should be addressed before fully deprecating `whatchanged`, noting it's unclear if byte-for-byte identical output is actually required for the mtime synchronization use case. This provides important technical context for Dan Allen to evaluate whether the additional timestamps would affect his workflow.
