This is the final patch in a refactoring series for Git's line-log functionality, specifically addressing the control flow for merge commit handling. The patch simplifies the condition check in `process_ranges_arbitrary_commit()` by flipping the logic to test for merge commits (multiple parents) first, then falling back to ordinary commit processing. 

The change is small (3 lines modified) and purely stylistic - the behavior remains identical. While the original author SZEDER Gábor found the previous formulation "head-scratchingly backwards", maintainer Junio C Hamano notes the original condition ("is it a root commit? or is it a single-parent commit?") wasn't particularly complex. 

This follows three other patches in the series: a performance optimization for merge commit processing and two other cleanups. The entire series has received positive reviews from both line-log expert Derrick Stolee and maintainer Junio, with this final patch being a minor but logical conclusion to the code quality improvements. The change makes the control flow slightly more direct by putting the merge case first, though the practical impact is negligible.
