This is a technical clarification about the behavior differences between `git whatchanged` and its proposed `git log` replacement for timestamp extraction.

Jeff King analyzes Ben Zanin's finding that `git log --raw --no-merges --pretty='%at'` produces 5 additional timestamps compared to `git whatchanged`. He identifies these as coming from empty commits (non-merge commits with identical trees to their parents) that `git log` shows but `whatchanged` suppresses. The difference stems from `git log` setting the `revs.always_show_header` flag internally.

King notes there's no user-facing option to control this behavior in `git log`, though using pathspecs could filter some cases (with potentially broader side effects). He raises the open question of whether this behavioral difference matters enough to warrant adding a control knob before fully deprecating `whatchanged`, while acknowledging the `git log` behavior may actually be more sensible.

This provides important technical details for Dan Allen's use case of synchronizing filesystem mtimes, as the extra empty-commit timestamps could potentially affect the synchronization behavior. The discussion remains technical rather than making any decisions about `whatchanged`'s deprecation timeline.
