This is a continuation of the Rust infrastructure policy discussion, specifically addressing platform support concerns for NonStop systems. Randall Becker responds to Junio Hamano's suggestion that commercial platform vendors should bear the Rust transition costs, explaining the practical reality for NonStop support.

The email clarifies that despite NonStop being a commercially supported x86_64 POSIX platform, Git maintenance falls to Becker's small team without vendor resources. Becker describes an unsustainable situation where they've single-handedly maintained NonStop compatibility for a decade through personal after-hours work, implying the Rust transition would exacerbate this burden beyond what volunteer effort can reasonably handle.

This strengthens the portability argument by highlighting that excluded platforms aren't just technically incompatible but may lack institutional support structures to adapt. The response counters Junio's vendor responsibility framing by showing Git's platform support often relies on individual contributors rather than vendor teams, making Rust adoption effectively a forced abandonment for some configurations despite their commercial status.
