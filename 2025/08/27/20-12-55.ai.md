This is a policy discussion thread continuation regarding the xdiff Rust infrastructure RFC (v3), specifically addressing maintenance burden concerns around platform support and security backporting.

Taylor Blau responds to Junio Hamano's concern that supporting both C and Rust versions for security fixes would be more costly than anticipated. Blau clarifies that while he acknowledges the additional maintenance burden, he points to the git-security team's track record of supporting old releases as evidence they can handle the challenge. He reiterates his belief that Rust's security advantages may reduce the frequency of needed backports compared to C.

The email reveals two key positions in the ongoing debate:
1. Blau maintains that the security team's historical diligence suggests they can manage dual-language maintenance, though he concedes it won't be trivial
2. He doubles down on his argument that Rust's inherent safety may actually decrease security backport needs over time

This exchange occurs after Randall Becker revealed NonStop's volunteer-based maintenance model, which complicates Junio's earlier stance that vendors should bear responsibility for Rust transitions. The discussion remains at an impasse between those willing to accept reduced portability for Rust's benefits and those concerned about unsustainable maintenance burdens, particularly for platforms like NonStop that lack institutional support structures.
