This is a policy discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing platform support concerns. Randall Becker responds to Taylor Blau's arguments about Rust's impact on Git's portability and security backporting.

Becker, representing the HPE NonStop platform (a commercial x86_64 POSIX system, though not Linux/Windows), expresses concern about feeling abandoned in the Rust transition. He acknowledges Blau's points about security benefits and the project's backporting track record, but emphasizes that his platform remains actively maintained and vendor-supported despite not being a mainstream target.

The exchange highlights the tension between Rust's technical benefits and its impact on niche platforms. Becker's response suggests that while he understands the technical rationale, there's frustration about platforms like NonStop being effectively excluded despite their commercial support status. This keeps the core policy question open: whether Git project policies should accommodate such platforms when technical constraints make Rust support impossible.
