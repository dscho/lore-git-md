Response to Ryan Johnson's gitignore include proposal from brian m. carlson. This is a discussion about a potential feature addition to `.gitignore` functionality.

brian m. carlson responds to Ryan Johnson's proposal for an `include` directive in `.gitignore` files with several technical and practical considerations. They identify three main challenges: backward compatibility (since the proposed syntax could conflict with legitimate filenames), ecosystem impact (many tools parse `.gitignore` and would need updating), and questionable necessity (most projects don't need the extensive templates referenced). The response notes that while the feature might help some users managing large template-based ignore patterns, most projects structure their builds to need only minimal ignore rules. They specifically point to Git's own repository (256 lines) and libgit2 (8 lines) as examples of sufficient ignore files without include functionality. The reply concludes that while not opposed to the idea in principle, they dispute the characterization of this as an "easy" change to implement, given the compatibility and ecosystem impacts. No outright rejection of the concept, but significant implementation hurdles are highlighted.
