Here's the updated human-readable thread summary:

Simon Cheng reported a bug where commit.cleanup=strip fails to remove comments injected by prepare-commit-msg hooks during autosquash rebases, though it works correctly for normal commits. This leaves unwanted hook-generated content in squashed commits. The issue affects Git 2.50.1 and appears to stem from how the rebase machinery processes fixup commits.

Phillip Wood has now analyzed the problem, tracing it to a 2021 change (commit f7d42ceec52) that intentionally preserves fixup commit messages verbatim during rebase. He points out this creates a mismatch with how regular "pick" commands respect commit.cleanup settings, and argues the behavior should be consistent. Phillip notes that since prepare-commit-msg hooks can modify messages anyway, the goal of verbatim preservation isn't fully achievable. He suggests making fixup commits honor commit.cleanup like other operations do.

Phillip indicated he may work on a fix after returning from a week away, unless someone else addresses it first. The proposed change would affect how rebase handles message cleanup when squashing fixups, particularly for hook-modified messages. This would resolve the inconsistency while maintaining the expected stripping of temporary hook content.
