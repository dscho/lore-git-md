Performance optimization follow-up in the khash vs commit-slab discussion. Jeff King provides corrected benchmark results showing the performance gap between the two approaches is smaller than initially reported - around 6% slower for khash in the Linux kernel case, and within noise margins for git.git. 

The email clarifies that earlier surprising results (where khash was 7x slower) were likely due to comparing against the wrong baseline (--date-order instead of --author-date-order). With proper benchmarking, khash shows more expected performance characteristics, though still slightly behind commit-slabs for large repositories.

Jeff notes the 6% difference may still justify keeping specialized data structures like commit-slabs, but raises questions about generalizing object storage. He speculates about the performance impact of moving object flags (currently stored directly in structs) into hash tables, suggesting this might show even larger slowdowns.

The discussion continues to highlight the nuanced tradeoffs between these approaches, with no clear winner emerging. The results reinforce that performance characteristics are workload-dependent, with commit-slabs maintaining an edge in dense traversal scenarios while khash remains competitive.
