Performance optimization discussion continuing the khash vs commit-slab comparison, specifically on topological sorting performance.

Jeff King responds to Junio C Hamano's surprise at the 7x performance difference between khash and commit-slab implementations for author-date storage in topological sorting. While both expected khash to be somewhat slower in this dense-access scenario, the magnitude of the difference is unexpected. Jeff confirms through profiling that the khash operations themselves (obj_timestamp_put/get) only account for a small fraction of the runtime, suggesting the issue isn't with the hash implementation itself. The mystery remains why khash performs so poorly compared to commit-slabs in this case, despite theoretical expectations.

This email highlights the empirical nature of performance optimization work - even experienced developers can be surprised by real-world measurements that contradict their expectations. The discussion remains open-ended, with Jeff expressing uncertainty about whether there might be an implementation error or if this truly represents a fundamental limitation of khash for this use case. The thread continues to explore the practical boundaries of these competing data structures in Git's performance-critical paths.
