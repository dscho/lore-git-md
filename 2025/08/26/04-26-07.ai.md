Performance optimization follow-up in the khash vs commit-slab comparison thread, focusing on topological sorting's author-date storage.

Jeff King provides corrected benchmark results after realizing initial tests may have used the wrong baseline (--date-order vs --author-date-order). The new measurements show:
- On git.git: 2% slowdown for khash (547ms slab vs 559ms hash)
- On linux.git: 6% slowdown for khash (11.02s slab vs 11.68s hash)

These numbers are more in line with expectations than the previously observed 7x difference, though still show commit-slabs maintaining a consistent advantage. The email notes the khash operations (obj_timestamp_put/get) account for only a few percent of runtime in profiling, making the remaining performance gap somewhat mysterious.

Key technical points:
- Confirms commit-slab's advantage holds across repositories (small git.git and large linux.git)
- Suggests the 6% difference may justify keeping specialized data structures
- Raises new question about performance impact of moving object flags (currently in struct) to hash storage
- Maintains uncertainty about root causes of khash's underperformance
- Implies the test case may not be fully representative of other potential uses

The discussion continues to highlight the context-dependent nature of optimization choices, with commit-slabs showing consistent but modest advantages in these dense access patterns. The email leaves open whether these results generalize to other object metadata storage needs.
