This is a follow-up discussion in the now-merged series removing `core.commentChar=auto`. Phillip Wood defends his design choice to provide detailed advice when users encounter the deprecated configuration, responding to Oswald Buddenhagen's critique that the approach is over-engineered.

The core disagreement centers on user experience philosophy - Phillip argues that forced configuration changes warrant helpful guidance to reduce frustration, while Oswald maintains the warnings are unnecessarily complex for an edge case that affects few users. Phillip emphasizes that while experienced users can ignore the advice, it provides a clear path forward for others who might otherwise struggle.

The exchange highlights an ongoing tension in Git's design between providing immediate user-friendly solutions versus encouraging deeper understanding of configuration systems. Phillip's position won out in the final implementation, but this post-merge discussion captures the thoughtful tradeoffs considered during the deprecation process.
