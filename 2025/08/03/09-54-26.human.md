This is a technical discussion in the performance optimization series about converting commit traversal to use priority queues.

René Scharfe responds to Lidong Yan's analysis of why the performance improvements from using priority queues (prio_queue) might not be as significant in practice. Yan had suggested that in many projects, the number of sibling commits (W) may be small enough that the theoretical O(W log W × N) vs O(W² × N) advantage doesn't translate to noticeable speedups.

Scharfe provides a more detailed complexity analysis, explaining that the performance characteristics depend heavily on the commit graph shape. For linear histories (single-parent chains), both approaches have O(N) complexity since they only ever hold one commit at a time. The worst case is a "centipede" merge where all commits are merged together - here the priority queue shows its O(N log N) advantage over the O(N²) behavior of commit_list.

This discussion provides important context about when developers can expect to see benefits from this optimization. While the worst-case improvements are substantial (92% speedup), real-world gains will vary based on repository structure. The exchange highlights the careful thought going into evaluating this change's practical impact.
