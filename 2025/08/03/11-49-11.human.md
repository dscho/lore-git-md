Performance optimization follow-up for `git describe`. This second patch in René Scharfe's series builds on the prio_queue conversion from part 1, further optimizing the queue operations by introducing a `lazy_queue` wrapper that combines peek+replace operations to avoid unnecessary heap rebalancing. The change is implemented through new wrapper functions that maintain the same behavior while being more efficient.

Benchmarks show this provides an additional speedup on top of the first patch's improvements - the new version runs 1.41x faster than the intermediate state with just the prio_queue conversion, and 1.83x faster than Git 2.50.1's original implementation when describing commits between v2.41.0 and v2.47.0 (4751 commits).

The implementation introduces a new `struct lazy_queue` that wraps the prio_queue while tracking pending get operations, with corresponding `lazy_queue_get/put/clear` functions that handle the peek/replace optimization transparently. All existing queue operations in `describe_commit()` and related functions are updated to use the new wrapper while maintaining identical functionality.

This appears to be a clean, well-measured optimization that builds logically on the first patch's work. The changes are focused and the commit message provides clear benchmark evidence of the improvement. No obvious downsides or areas of concern in the implementation.
