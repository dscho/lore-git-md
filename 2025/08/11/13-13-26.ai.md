This is a performance optimization discussion continuing the xdiff string hashing thread. Phillip Wood provides additional benchmarking data and raises practical concerns about Alexander Monakov's proposed hash optimization.

Phillip's hyperfine measurements show only a 2% speedup over the previous patch (1.02x) and 11% over master (1.11x) for `git log`, with his xxhash implementation still 3% faster (1.03x) than Monakov's approach. He used GCC with -O2 -march=native on an i5-8500, noting similar inlining benefits when testing xxhash. Phillip questions whether the REASSOC_FENCE macro's compiler-specific optimization is worth the maintenance cost given modest real-world gains.

Monakov had previously reported larger speedups (7-11% in cycles) and attributes the discrepancy to compiler/CPU variability. He maintains the algorithm should perform well even without the assembly optimization on modern CPUs, though suggests following Glibc's approach of restricting it to x86_64 with GCC/Clang if needed. He rejects using C11's atomic_signal_fence() as unsuitable for this low-level optimization.

The discussion highlights tension between microbenchmark improvements and measurable Git performance gains, with Phillip's data suggesting the optimization may not justify its complexity given existing alternatives like xxhash. The thread remains focused on xdiff/xutils.c optimizations, with open questions about cross-platform behavior and whether to conditionally enable the optimization only on x86_64 with GCC/Clang.
