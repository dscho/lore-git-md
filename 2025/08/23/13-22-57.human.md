This is the second patch in a series addressing signal handling for Git's progress meter, following up on the switch from `setitimer()` to `alarm()`. The patch adds a shutdown state to prevent race conditions during process termination.

The change introduces a three-state system for progress updates (0=no progress, 1=update needed, 2=shutting down) to safely handle timer cleanup. The key modification ensures the SIGALRM handler won't reschedule itself during shutdown by checking the new state flag. The patch also updates progress display logic to use bitwise checks against the state variable.

This appears to be a straightforward and well-contained fix for the race condition mentioned in the first patch. The implementation is minimal (14 lines added, 6 removed) and focused specifically on the shutdown safety issue. Reviewers will likely want to verify the atomicity of the state transitions, but the approach seems sound.

The patch completes the series' goal of modernizing progress meter signaling while maintaining safety. With this change, Git's progress reporting should work reliably across platforms without relying on the obsolescent `setitimer()` interface.
