This is a continuation of the progress meter signal handling discussion, where Junio Hamano is responding to concerns about the signal-free approach proposed earlier. While Hamano had previously expressed reservations about replacing the current signal-based progress meter with frequent gettimeofday() checks, he now seems to be reconsidering his position.

Hamano acknowledges that modern operating systems have optimized gettimeofday() calls, making them less expensive than he initially feared. He notes that since the timer checks would occur during normal operation (not in a busy-wait loop), the overhead might be acceptable after all. This represents a significant shift from his earlier skepticism about the "dirtiness" of frequent time checks compared to the current "set and forget" signal approach.

The email suggests Hamano may be moving toward accepting Johannes Sixt's signal-free implementation, though he doesn't explicitly endorse it yet. This development could help resolve the thread's central debate between signal-based versus timer-based progress updates, particularly given Hamano's role as maintainer and his initial strong reservations. The technical considerations around timer resolution and system impact remain relevant, but the performance concerns appear less severe than initially thought.
