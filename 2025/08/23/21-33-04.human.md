Maintainer reaction to progress meter signal-free proposal

Junio Hamano weighs in on Johannes Sixt's signal-free progress meter implementation, which would replace the current SIGALRM-based approach with periodic wall-clock checks. While acknowledging the technical merits of Sixt's design (which the thread has largely converged around), Hamano expresses some reservations about the performance implications.

The key concern is whether frequently checking the system clock (proposed every 1/8 second) might be more expensive than the current "set and forget" signal approach, despite modern OS optimizations for gettimeofday(). Hamano notes this feels "dirtier" than even the signal-based solutions previously discussed, though he stops short of rejecting the approach outright.

This comes as the thread has already moved past earlier proposals (setitimer() and alarm()) in favor of Sixt's signal-free design. While Hamano's comment doesn't block progress, it surfaces an important performance consideration that may need benchmarking before finalizing the implementation. The tone suggests he's looking for reassurance that the wall-clock checks won't introduce measurable overhead in common Git operations.
