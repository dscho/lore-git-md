This is a follow-up discussion on the recently completed series removing the `core.commentChar=auto` configuration option. Oswald Buddenhagen responds to Phillip Wood's implementation choice to provide detailed advice when users encounter the deprecated setting.

The email questions whether the complexity of the advice-giving code is justified, arguing that:
1) The number of affected users is likely small, making the user-friendly approach an overinvestment
2) The advice focuses on trivial usage patterns rather than helping users understand why the option was set originally
3) The partial advice (when dealing with read-only files) might actually mislead users rather than help them

This appears to be a philosophical discussion about Git's approach to user guidance, with Buddenhagen advocating for simpler, more transparent tool behavior rather than what they see as over-engineered hand-holding. The tone is constructive but skeptical of the chosen implementation's value.

Since this is a post-merge discussion, it's unlikely to change the implementation, but raises interesting points about Git's user experience design principles that may influence future similar changes.
