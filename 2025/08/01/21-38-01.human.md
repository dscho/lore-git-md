This is an experimental update in the ongoing discussion about Git's scalability for AI workflows. Skybuck Flying shares their current test setup using Windows 11, PostgreSQL, Gitea, and Google's Gemini AI to work around Git's concurrency limitations. The approach gives each AI worker its own Git repository to avoid race conditions, with a central coordinator handling integration.

The email describes a custom "Skybuck's Gitflow" system to manage the parallel branches from AI workers, though acknowledges this is still untested. There's notable skepticism about whether AI can handle large merge conflicts, with the author observing that both AI and humans might struggle with complex merges.

Beyond the Git discussion, the email drifts into broader AI experimentation, including observations about different AI models' collaboration capabilities (Gemini vs. Copilot) and a side project modernizing a Delphi-based RAM disk utility. While these tangents show the author's broader AI interests, they don't directly advance the core Git scalability discussion.

The contribution maintains the thread's focus on real-world AI/Git integration challenges but doesn't address the fundamental architectural limitations previously identified. The PostgreSQL-Gitea workaround represents one pragmatic approach to the problem, though it essentially avoids rather than solves Git's core concurrency issues.
