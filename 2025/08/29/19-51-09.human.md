This is a continuation of the SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability storage format discussion, with Eric Wong responding to brian m. carlson's rejection of the SQLite proposal. Wong acknowledges the concerns about Java performance and licensing but pushes back on the decision to abandon SQLite in favor of a custom format.

Wong questions whether SQLite's Java performance is truly problematic, noting the existence of pure Java SQLite implementations (though he admits unfamiliarity with their quality). He also cites the US Library of Congress's endorsement of SQLite as a stable format, though he wryly notes concerns about the LoC's own longevity.

The email primarily focuses on the licensing concerns, arguing that SQLite has surpassed Berkeley DB in stability and adoption, making a license change unlikely. Wong expresses broader concerns about the proliferation of custom formats, viewing it as a threat to digital freedom and security due to the maintenance burden it creates.

This response keeps the SQLite option on the table rhetorically, though the thread's previous consensus had effectively ruled it out. The exchange highlights the tension between using battle-tested external formats versus maintaining control through custom solutions - a recurring architectural debate in open source. Wong's arguments are more philosophical than technical at this point, focusing on long-term ecosystem concerns rather than immediate implementation details.
