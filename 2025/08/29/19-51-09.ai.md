This is a continuation of the SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability storage format debate, with Eric Wong responding to brian m. carlson's rejection of the SQLite proposal. The email focuses on two key objections to SQLite - JGit compatibility and license stability - while maintaining Wong's position that SQLite remains a viable choice despite these concerns.

Wong questions the Java performance issues with SQLite, noting the existence of pure Java implementations (though he acknowledges unfamiliarity with their quality). He cites the US Library of Congress's endorsement of SQLite as a stable format, though with some skepticism about the LoC's own longevity. On licensing, Wong argues that SQLite's widespread adoption makes it more stable than carlson fears, suggesting the community could maintain a fork if needed, and expresses broader concerns about format proliferation.

The email doesn't introduce new technical proposals but reinforces Wong's position in the ongoing debate, particularly around the risk assessment of SQLite's adoption. It highlights the tension between custom solutions (like reftable) versus established standards, with Wong clearly favoring the latter despite the thread's apparent consensus moving toward custom formats. The discussion remains at the architectural/ecosystem level rather than diving into implementation specifics of the remaining proposals.
