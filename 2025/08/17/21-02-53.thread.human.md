This thread began with R. Diez requesting a graphical interface for Git history manipulation, citing frustration with existing tools' limitations for complex operations like commit reordering and squashing. After terminology clarifications from Konstantin Khomoutov and Junio Hamano's suggestion to consider this as a potential GSoC/Outreachy project, Patrick Steinhardt proposed a middle-ground solution: a new `git history` command that would provide structured subcommands for history manipulation, serving both as a direct CLI tool and a potential backend for future GUIs.

The latest contribution from R. Diez adds concrete use cases, describing how messy commit histories emerge during long build processes with iterative fixes. They propose two specific enhancements to Patrick's idea: a `git history squash` subcommand and a "safe mode" that would operate on a duplicate branch to prevent accidental history corruption. While the discussion originated from a GUI request, it has evolved into a productive exploration of CLI-based solutions that could address the underlying workflow pain points while remaining true to Git's command-line roots. The thread now balances technical considerations (new subcommands, safety mechanisms) with real-world scenarios where users struggle with Git's learning curve for history rewriting.
