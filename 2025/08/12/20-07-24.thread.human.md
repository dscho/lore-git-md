Julia Evans has completed her series improving the `git-add` man page, with all five versions now incorporating feedback from Junio Hamano and Chris Torek. The changes focus on making the documentation more accessible to beginners while preserving technical accuracy, particularly around the terminology of "index", "cache", and "staging area". The series simplifies the SYNOPSIS section, adds concrete workflow examples, and clarifies edge cases while maintaining Git's snapshot-based model.

In a follow-up discussion about version control mental models, Julia provides additional context from her experience teaching Git and an informal poll she conducted. She notes that while Git internally uses a snapshot model, many developers - especially those who never used CVS or Subversion - naturally think in terms of diffs in their workflow. The poll showed roughly equal numbers of developers conceptualizing commits as snapshots versus diffs, with many using both models situationally. This supports her documentation approach of balancing technical accuracy (Git's snapshot internals) with practical, example-based explanations that align with how users actually work.

Junio has reviewed and approved the final patches, acknowledging that Git's model may now be the baseline for new developers while maintaining the importance of documenting the snapshot model accurately. The series is now complete and ready for integration into the main Git documentation.
