Julia Evans has proposed a series of improvements to the `git-add` documentation aimed at making it more accessible to beginners while maintaining technical accuracy. The five-patch series restructures the SYNOPSIS, adds concrete examples, clarifies terminology around the index/staging area, and addresses edge cases like the divergence between `--cached` and `--index` flags in `git-apply`.

Junio Hamano, the maintainer, has engaged deeply with the series, providing feedback that reveals a fundamental tension in Git documentation. While patch 2/5 moves toward diff-oriented language that may align better with beginners' mental models, Junio argues this risks obscuring Git's actual snapshot-based behavior. He provides concrete examples where the snapshot model better explains Git's operations, particularly around edit-add-edit sequences and checkout behavior. His feedback suggests alternative phrasing that preserves the accessibility goals while more accurately reflecting Git's internals.

Other contributors have weighed in as well. Chris Torek identified an important edge case in `git-apply` where `--cached` and `--index` behave differently, which the documentation should note. The discussion has also considered broader questions about documentation philosophy - whether to balance conceptual models that match user expectations against those that accurately represent Git's implementation.

The series is now at a point where Junio appears to be seeking wider community input on these philosophical questions before proceeding further. The patches have addressed structural improvements and terminology clarity, but the core challenge of balancing accessibility with technical precision - particularly around the snapshot versus diff mental models - remains an open discussion.
