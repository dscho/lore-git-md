This is a response to Jimmy Thrasibule's synthetic worktree proposal, offering a practical workaround using existing Git features. Antonio Russo shares their experience achieving similar functionality through a combination of gitignore-everything, sparse checkout in non-cone mode, and manual GIT_DIR/GIT_WORK_TREE manipulation. They note this approach requires force-adding files and lacks the proposed layer-based priority system, but serves as a stopgap solution.

The response highlights two key technical constraints of the workaround: the need for `git add -f` due to the ignore-everything approach, and the inability to implement repository layering priorities. Antonio also mentions ostree as a potentially related system, though they don't elaborate on the connection. This suggests the synthetic worktree concept may have parallels in other version control systems worth investigating.
