This thread continues the technical discussion about how to store bidirectional mappings between SHA-1 and SHA-256 object IDs, a key piece of Git's hash algorithm interoperability work. The core debate has narrowed to two architectural approaches: Junio Hamano's proposal to separate static mappings from dynamic metadata, and Derrick Stolee's reftable-inspired custom format. A third option using SQLite, championed by Eric Wong, has been effectively ruled out by brian m. carlson due to concerns about JGit compatibility and long-term license stability.

In the latest email, Wong pushes back on these objections, arguing that SQLite's widespread adoption makes it a stable choice despite carlson's concerns. He questions whether Java performance with SQLite is truly problematic (while admitting he hasn't evaluated specific implementations) and suggests the community could maintain a SQLite fork if needed. Wong also cites the US Library of Congress's endorsement of SQLite as a counterpoint to licensing stability worries. However, the thread participation suggests the consensus is moving toward custom solutions, with Hamano and Stolee's approaches now being the focus of technical evaluation.

The discussion has evolved from pure format design to include broader ecosystem considerations, with the remaining open questions centering on performance benchmarking between the two leading proposals and validating their compatibility with JGit and reftable. While Wong continues to advocate for SQLite, the thread's trajectory suggests this option is unlikely to be revived absent new technical arguments.
