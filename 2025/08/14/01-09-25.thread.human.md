This thread continues the technical discussion about how to store bidirectional mappings between SHA-1 and SHA-256 object IDs, a key piece of the SHA-256 interoperability work. The core challenge is finding a storage format that supports efficient lookups (O(log N) performance) and incremental updates while addressing licensing stability and cross-implementation concerns (particularly with JGit).

The discussion has narrowed to two main approaches: Junio Hamano's proposal for separate static and dynamic mapping files, and Derrick Stolee's reftable-inspired format. Eric Wong had been advocating for SQLite but that approach appears effectively ruled out due to JGit compatibility and licensing concerns, despite Wong's recent defense of its viability.

Patrick Steinhardt has now joined the conversation after a two-week absence, endorsing Stolee's rejection of repurposing pack index formats and suggesting Git's existing chunk format (used in commit-graphs and MIDX) as a potential alternative to designing something entirely new. While not introducing major new arguments, Steinhardt's input reinforces the geometric sequence approach and suggests format reuse where possible.

Key open questions remain about performance benchmarks between the approaches, long-term maintenance characteristics, and how to validate JGit compatibility requirements. The thread has evolved from pure technical evaluation to include broader ecosystem considerations, with Steinhardt's late entry indicating there may still be room to refine the direction before finalizing the approach.
