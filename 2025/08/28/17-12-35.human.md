This is a discussion about option organization in `git range-diff`'s help output, part of a larger performance and safety patch series. 

Elijah Newren weighs in on Junio Hamano's critique of the first patch in the series, which proposed alphabetizing all options. While Newren generally favors lexicographic ordering for unrelated options, he agrees with Junio that grouping related options (like `--left-only`/`--right-only` and `--diff-merges`/--remerge-diff`) by functionality is more important than strict alphabetical order. 

The exchange highlights a tension between mechanical consistency and human usability in command-line interfaces. Both maintainers view the current patch as making the help output less organized rather than more, suggesting the author should reconsider the approach. This discussion doesn't affect the core memory-limiting functionality in patch 2/2, which remains uncontroversial.
