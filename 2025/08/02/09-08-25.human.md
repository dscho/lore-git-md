This is a review comment on a refactoring series that replaces `strbuf_split*()` with `string_list_split*()` for safer string handling. Jeff King (Peff) provides overall positive feedback on the series but raises a few subtle compatibility considerations that warrant attention.

Peff agrees with the series' premise that `strbuf_split*()` is a misdesigned API and supports the conversions to `string_list`. He notes the sub-process.c case where `skip_prefix()` provides an even simpler alternative. While finding nothing objectionable in the patches, he thoughtfully considers potential edge cases that might behave differently between the old and new implementations.

Two specific concerns are raised: 1) the different handling of "max" splits between the APIs (which the series already addresses in one patch), and 2) whether repeated delimiters produce identical output (e.g., whether "foo//bar" splits to include empty elements). He also verifies that the new TRIM behavior (trimming both sides) is appropriate compared to the old single-side trimming, concluding it likely better matches the original intent.

The review reinforces the series' value while demonstrating careful consideration of subtle behavioral differences - exactly the kind of thoughtful review that helps ensure safe refactoring. With these points addressed, the series appears ready to proceed.
