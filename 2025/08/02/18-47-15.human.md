This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing string handling refactoring series, focusing on edge case behavior between the old `strbuf_split*()` and new `string_list_split*()` APIs.

Jeff King and Junio Hamano are working through some subtle behavioral differences in how the two APIs handle whitespace trimming and empty elements. The key points under discussion are:

1. The original `strbuf_split*()` would leave delimiters at the end of split pieces and required manual `rtrim()`, while `string_list_split*()` with `TRIM` handles both left and right trimming automatically. Junio suggests potentially dropping `TRIM` to match the original stricter behavior more closely.

2. There's some ambiguity around whether the original parsers were intentionally strict about whitespace handling or if the current behavior was just an artifact of the old API's limitations. Jeff leans toward keeping the more lenient behavior since the documentation doesn't explicitly require strictness.

The discussion shows the maintainers carefully considering backward compatibility while modernizing the codebase. They appear close to consensus on keeping the more permissive behavior, as neither case seems likely to break real-world usage. This exchange helps document the rationale for the chosen approach while not blocking the overall series.
