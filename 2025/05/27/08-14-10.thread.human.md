The thread started with Piotr Siupa reporting a regression in `git add --update` when used with exclude patterns in empty repositories (Git 2.45.0 through 2.49.0). The command shows duplicate error messages in this case, while working normally once files exist or in pre-2.45.0 versions.

Phillip Wood has now joined the discussion, agreeing the behavior is inconsistent but suggesting the deeper issue might be that Git should consistently error when exclude patterns match nothing - even in non-empty repositories. He points out that while `git add -u does-not-exist` currently errors, `git add -u ':(exclude)*'` silently succeeds when excluding all files, which may not be the right behavior.

The conversation has shifted from fixing a regression to considering a broader behavior change - making pathspec matching consistently error when no files remain after exclusions. However, the empty-repository case remains particularly problematic due to its interaction with Git's implicit '.' pathspec. The discussion is exploring whether to treat this as a bug fix or a behavior improvement.
