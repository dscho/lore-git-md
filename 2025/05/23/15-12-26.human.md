This is a discussion about compiler warning policies in the Git project, specifically regarding the -Wsign-compare flag. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's earlier rejection of a newcomer's patch that attempted to address these warnings in log-tree.c.

Patrick disagrees with Junio's characterization of -Wsign-compare as "mostly garbage," arguing that while the signal-to-noise ratio is high, the warnings have helped catch real security issues like stack overflows in the past. However, he concedes that fixing these warnings is often non-trivial and agrees these shouldn't be listed as microprojects for newcomers.

The exchange reveals an ongoing tension in the project between warning hygiene and developer experience. Patrick leaves the door open for removing the warning if others agree with Junio's position, showing willingness to defer to consensus despite his personal view. The discussion may lead to either policy changes (removing the warning from developer settings) or more careful auditing of type safety issues in the codebase.
