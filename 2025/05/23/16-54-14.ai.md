Junio acknowledges that while `-Wsign-compare` has incidentally helped catch some serious bugs (like stack overflows), its primary function of flagging signed/unsigned comparisons doesn't directly address the most dangerous type-related issues. He provides concrete examples showing how the warning would miss certain dangerous type mismatches (like `a < (int)b` where the cast silences the warning but the underlying problem remains) and how it fails to catch other dangerous operations like `a -= b` that aren't comparisons at all.

- `- Wsign-compare` warnings are technically accurate (not false positives) but often not practically useful
- The warning's focus on signedness rather than size mismatches limits its effectiveness at catching the most dangerous type-related bugs
- Some dangerous operations (like arithmetic between mismatched types) fall outside the warning's scope entirely
- The discussion reveals deeper questions about what kind of type safety checks would actually be valuable
