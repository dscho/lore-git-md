Junio Hamano continues the discussion about compiler warning policies, specifically addressing Patrick Steinhardt's defense of keeping -Wsign-compare. He acknowledges the warning has caught real issues in the past but argues these were "happy accidents" rather than intentional benefits, since -Wsign-compare focuses on signedness mismatches rather than the more dangerous size mismatches that can cause stack overflows.

Junio makes a technical distinction: while -Wsign-compare correctly flags signed/unsigned comparisons (so they're not false positives), they're often "not-false-but-useless" because they don't reliably catch the more serious underlying issues. He provides concrete examples where dangerous code (like integer overflows in `a -= b`) would slip through even with the warning enabled.

Both maintainers agree the original newcomer patch was poorly scoped, and Junio reaffirms this point strongly. The email reinforces Junio's position that the warning's signal-to-noise ratio is too poor to justify keeping it, while acknowledging Patrick's historical perspective. The discussion remains technical and collegial, with both sides making their cases through concrete examples rather than abstract arguments.
