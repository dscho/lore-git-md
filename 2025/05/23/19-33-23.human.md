This is a brief maintainer response in the ongoing Meson test integration thread. Junio Hamano weighs in on the philosophical question raised by Ramsay Jones about whether test runners should fail on unexpected passes (where a test marked as "known breakage" unexpectedly passes). 

Hamano agrees it's a good question and expresses openness to stricter behavior, noting that the current difference between build systems (where make/prove silently accept unexpected passes while Meson surfaces them) is what bothers him. His comment suggests he might be receptive to standardizing on Meson's stricter approach across all test runners, though he doesn't make a definitive ruling.

The exchange represents the final discussion point in this otherwise technically resolved series, which has already addressed platform-specific test output issues and received positive reviews from Karthik Nayak and Phillip Wood. The thread has revealed how Meson's TAP parser uncovered long-standing platform behavior differences that were previously masked by the traditional test infrastructure.
