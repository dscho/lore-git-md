This is a discussion about improving reviewer selection guidance in Git's contribution process, stemming from the MyFirstContribution tutorial update series. K Jayatheerth responds to Junio C Hamano's analysis of current reviewer identification tools, agreeing that the SubmittingPatches documentation could benefit from concrete examples about choosing reviewers.

The conversation explores the limitations of the existing `contrib/contacts/git-contacts` tool, which Junio found inconsistent (due to Perl hash randomization) and overly broad in its inclusion of all trailer roles. Junio suggests a hypothetical "explain mode" that would analyze patch context and historical involvement to provide more nuanced reviewer recommendations.

Jayatheerth proposes an AI-based solution that would parse patches, analyze line history via `git blame`, cross-reference lore.kernel.org activity, and generate human-readable explanations with references. They acknowledge the hallucination risks of LLMs but suggest strict templating could help, while noting the computational costs might make this better as a separate service rather than built into Git.

The exchange demonstrates thoughtful consideration of both immediate documentation improvements and longer-term tooling possibilities, with Jayatheerth showing initiative in proposing technical solutions while remaining open to community feedback. The discussion remains constructive, focusing on practical ways to improve contributor experience without committing to any specific implementation yet.
