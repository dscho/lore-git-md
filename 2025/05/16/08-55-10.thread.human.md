This thread explores performance optimizations for packfile handling, particularly reducing system calls when dealing with missing packs referenced in the multi-pack-index (midx). The discussion began with Patrick Steinhardt's patch that reordered checks to avoid unnecessary `access()` calls for auxiliary files (.keep, .promisor, .cruft) when the main packfile doesn't exist, showing significant syscall reductions in pathological cases.

Jeff King (peff) proposed a more robust solution: adding a third state" (-1) to midx->packs[] to cache failed lookup results, preventing repeated checks for missing packs. Junio Hamano agreed with this approach after initial concerns about historical correctness constraints. The implementation now handles three states in `midx.c` while maintaining the critical invariant that .idx checks must precede .pack checks.

In the latest exchange, Patrick acknowledges Jeff's solution is simpler than expected but raises important concerns about race conditions with auxiliary files. His analysis confirms that while deletion order in `unlink_pack_path()` is correct, the original optimization could have missed auxiliary data if creation ordering wasn't properly maintained. This led him to conclude his initial approach was incorrect for real-world scenarios. Both Patrick and Jeff now agree that while lazy-loading auxiliary files could offer performance benefits, the complexity and potential races may not justify it for most well-maintained repositories.

The thread has clarified key packfile integrity constraints and produced a technically sound solution that balances performance gains with safety. The midx cache modification is moving forward while auxiliary file optimizations may be deferred or approached differently in future work.
