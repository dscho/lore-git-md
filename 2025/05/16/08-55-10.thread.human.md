This thread has converged on a performance optimization for Git's multi-pack-index (MIDX) handling that reduces unnecessary system calls when checking for missing packfiles. The issue was originally identified by Patrick Steinhardt, who proposed a solution that evolved through discussion with Jeff King and Junio Hamano.

The core problem involved stale MIDX entries causing repeated failed lookups of missing packfiles, resulting in multiple redundant syscalls (access() and stat()). While the impact is small in well-maintained repositories, the optimization provides measurable benefits. The solution implements a negative lookup cache in midx.c that stores -1 for missing pack entries, preventing repeated checks.

The v2 patch series from Patrick Steinhardt includes two key parts: first, the documentation of packfile handling invariants, and second, the actual caching implementation. Performance measurements show dramatic improvements - reducing syscalls from 24,083 to just 83 in test cases and delivering a 4x speedup for operations like `git log --patch`. The implementation carefully maintains Git's safety constraints around packfile handling while providing these benefits.

The thread has reached clear consensus, with Junio Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt agreeing on Jeff King's suggested caching approach. The solution avoids riskier alternatives like lazy-loading auxiliary files while still delivering meaningful performance gains. The changes are well-isolated to midx.c and come with clear benchmarks demonstrating their value. This appears ready for merging as a clean optimization that respects Git's core invariants.
