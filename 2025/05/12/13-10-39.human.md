This is a discussion about whether to keep or remove the `git-contacts` script from Git's `contrib/` directory, as part of Patrick Steinhardt's larger cleanup series.

Phillip Wood argues for retaining `git-contacts`, despite it being flagged for removal in the cleanup series. His key points are that the script (1) serves an important onboarding function by helping new contributors identify who to CC on patches, (2) is currently working and documented as the recommended approach, and (3) has advantages over the alternative (`git-related`) given past maintenance issues with that external tool.

The email positions itself against earlier suggestions from Eric Sunshine and Todd Zullinger that `git-contacts` could be removed since `git-related` exists as a more functional alternative. Wood's stance introduces a practical consideration about contributor experience that wasn't previously emphasized in the technical/policy-focused discussion.

This represents a new perspective in the thread that may need reconciliation with the series' established removal framework, particularly around how to weigh contributor workflow impacts against the cleanup criteria. The exchange highlights how even clearly superseded tools might warrant retention if they serve important ecosystem functions.
