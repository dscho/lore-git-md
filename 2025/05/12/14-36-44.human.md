This is the second patch in a series adding SHA-256 support to gitk, focusing on hash length preferences. The discussion centers on whether length preferences should be algorithm-specific (current implementation) or repository-agnostic (Johannes Sixt's suggestion). 

Takashi Iwai defends the algorithm-specific approach, noting that without it, SHA-256 repositories would default to showing only 40 characters (SHA-1 length) rather than the full 64-character hashes. He provides a real-world example from openSUSE's SHA-256 repositories where this causes usability issues. 

The patch itself adds separate configuration storage for SHA-256 length preferences ("autosellensha256") with a default of 64 characters. While functionally complete, the design decision remains under discussion, with Sixt questioning whether users would want consistent length preferences across repositories regardless of hash algorithm. 

This exchange highlights an important UX consideration in the transition to SHA-256 support - whether to optimize for consistency (same behavior across repos) or correctness (appropriate defaults per algorithm). The thread also reveals a testing gap, as Sixt notes he lacks SHA-256 repositories to verify the behavior himself.
