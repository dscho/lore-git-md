This is a bugfix patch tightening FQDN validation in `git send-email` to comply with RFC1035 standards. It's the first of three patches in a series focused on email functionality improvements.

The patch addresses a specific issue where invalid domain names like "MacBook.." were passing validation, causing SMTP failures when sending emails through Outlook servers. The new implementation enforces stricter rules: domains must contain at least one dot, each label must be 1-63 alphanumeric/hyphen characters (no leading/trailing hyphens), and maintains the existing macOS `.local` exclusion. The commit includes clear examples of valid/invalid domains and shows the SMTP exchange before/after the fix.

The technical change replaces a simple dot-check regex with a more comprehensive pattern that validates each label individually. The patch is narrowly scoped to `git-send-email.perl` with a 4-line change (3 additions, 1 deletion). The author demonstrates practical impact through SMTP logs showing the invalid "MacBook.." being rejected and falling back to "localhost.localdomain" after the fix.

This appears well-motivated by real-world email sending failures and carefully implements the RFC spec. As noted in the thread context, this is the fifth iteration of the patch, suggesting previous rounds addressed potential edge cases. The change could theoretically break non-compliant configurations that previously worked, but the fallback to localhost.localdomain should mitigate this.
