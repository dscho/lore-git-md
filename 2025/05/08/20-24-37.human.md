This is a continuation of the ongoing discussion about commit message attribution formats, specifically comparing bracketed annotations versus formal trailers.

Jeff King responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's point that signoff lines already provide attribution information, making bracketed annotations redundant. He notes this only holds true when there is indeed a signoff line present, which isn't guaranteed in all projects or situations. While acknowledging that a "Comment:" trailer might be technically cleaner from a parsing perspective, he pragmatically accepts the existing bracketed format due to its historical use in Git's development culture.

The exchange highlights the tension between technical cleanliness and established practice. Jeff King's position reflects a middle ground - recognizing the theoretical advantages of formal trailers while accepting the reality of entrenched conventions. This maintains the status quo while keeping the door open for future discussion about trailer parsing improvements.
