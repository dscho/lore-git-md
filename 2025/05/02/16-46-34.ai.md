This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing thread about deprecating `git whatchanged`. Junio Hamano clarifies the deprecation strategy in response to Patrick Steinhardt's question about whether to immediately list the command in BreakingChanges.adoc.

Hamano explains that the current patches represent an early "gauge the usage" phase, analogous to how `pack-redundant` was first made inconvenient (requiring `--i-still-use-this`) before full deprecation. The goal is to identify remaining users through their reaction to the new requirement, not to formally announce deprecation yet. He suggests holding the BreakingChanges update for a future step after gathering this feedback.

The exchange confirms the series' intent is to:
1. First measure actual usage through forced opt-in (`--i-still-use-this`)
2. Only later document formal deprecation if usage appears minimal
3. Follow the same phased approach used successfully for `pack-redundant`

This maintains the thread's focus on establishing a standardized deprecation process while clarifying the distinction between early warning phases and formal removal announcements. The discussion remains focused on process rather than technical implementation details.
