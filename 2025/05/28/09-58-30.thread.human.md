The thread explores performance optimizations in Git's promisor-remote subsystem, specifically around the `promisor_remote_get_direct()` function. Han Young initially proposed removing redundant `is_promisor_object()` checks to speed up operations in large repositories, arguing callers already filter out local objects. However, Junio Hamano raised valid concerns that this assumption might not hold universally, as some callers may pass unfound objects without explicitly verifying they are promisor objects. He also noted the patch would change error handling behavior, making the function die on any fetch failure rather than just for promised objects.

In the latest response, Han Young provides technical details explaining why `is_promisor_object()` is slow - it must parse every object in promisor packfiles (which can number in the millions for large repositories) and their references. While acknowledging Junio's safety concerns, Han also shares test results showing that both git-cat-file and git-diff fail in either case, though with different error messages. The discussion references Calvin Wan's prior analysis that caching promisor objects is challenging due to their dynamic nature. The core question remains whether the performance optimization can be achieved while maintaining the safety of promisor object verification, with no clear resolution yet. Key participants Han Young and Junio Hamano are actively discussing the tradeoffs, with Christian Couder and Jonathan Tan CC'd for their expertise in this area.
