Junio Hamano's response to the final patch in the Scalar maintenance configuration series raises an important design question about the semantics of the `--no-maintenance` flag when used with `scalar reconfigure`. While the series has established consistent behavior across `register` and `clone` commands (where `--no-maintenance` means "skip enabling maintenance"), Hamano points out this may not be the most intuitive choice for `reconfigure` since it operates on existing enlistments.

The key technical observation is that the current implementation only allows either enabling maintenance (when the flag is omitted) or leaving it unchanged (with `--no-maintenance`), but provides no way to explicitly disable maintenance during reconfiguration. Hamano suggests a three-state approach (enable/disable/leave-unchanged) would be more flexible, though he leaves open whether this is worth changing at this stage.

This feedback touches on the architectural tension noted in earlier thread summaries - specifically the question of how maintenance control should be abstracted across different Scalar commands. The response doesn't block the series (which is otherwise complete) but highlights a potential inconsistency that could be addressed in future iterations. The discussion remains focused on user experience rather than implementation correctness, as the technical foundation (unified `register_dir()` interface, test coverage) is already solid.
