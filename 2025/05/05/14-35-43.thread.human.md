Akash S from Commvault initiated this thread to explore incremental Git backup strategies that could replace their current practice of daily full `git clone` operations. The discussion has evolved through several technical approaches, with Jeff King (Peff) emerging as a key voice advocating for pack-based state-tracking solutions that offer both space efficiency (56x better than loose objects) and timestamp independence.

The thread has effectively dismissed filesystem-level approaches (ZFS/Btrfs deduplication) as being 14x less space-efficient, though Michal Suchánek continues to refine this alternative. His latest email clarifies that shared clones should use `--bare --mirror` for easier updates and require the `--local` flag during repacking to maintain object sharing. While technically correct, this approach remains untested and doesn't challenge the established superiority of pack-based methods.

The consensus now strongly favors Jeff King's state-tracking proposal using thin packs and ref snapshots, which addresses both the timestamp vulnerability of bundle-based approaches and the space inefficiency of filesystem methods. The remaining open questions center on implementation details rather than fundamental approach, with the thread effectively concluding that pack-based solutions meet Commvault's enterprise requirements for reliability and efficiency.
