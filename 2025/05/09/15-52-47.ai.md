This is a follow-up in the discussion about multi-pane merge visualization, specifically addressing Johannes Sixt's concern about handling merges with more than two parents. D. Ben Knoble provides a practical demonstration that vimdiff can technically handle comparing an arbitrary number of files (demonstrated with 5 files), though notes the interface becomes more difficult to understand with many panes. The email serves as a data point in the ongoing discussion about whether merge tools should be designed specifically for the common 2-parent case or aim for general N-parent support. Knoble's experiment shows that while the technical capability exists in at least one tool (vimdiff), usability degrades with more panes, which may inform the design decision between optimizing for the common case versus handling all possible cases. The email doesn't take a strong position but provides concrete evidence relevant to the tradeoff under discussion.
