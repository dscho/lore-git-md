This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about incremental Git backup strategies, specifically addressing the filesystem-level deduplication approach.

Michal Suchánek responds to Jeff King's quantitative analysis showing that storing objects unpacked (as would be needed for filesystem deduplication) would consume 14x more space than packfiles, even with zlib compression. Michal wryly notes that this would only become space-efficient after 14 incremental backups, jokingly calling it "the best one" since no better alternative has been conclusively established yet.

The exchange highlights the tradeoffs in the filesystem approach - while technically simple to implement using ZFS/Btrfs deduplication, the storage inefficiency makes it clearly inferior to pack-based solutions. This reinforces the thread's earlier conclusions that state-tracking or bundle-based approaches remain more viable options for enterprise backup scenarios where storage efficiency matters. The tone suggests this line of inquiry may be reaching its natural conclusion, with the core technical tradeoffs now well understood.
