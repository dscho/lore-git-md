This is a discussion thread about the final policy decisions for Patrick Steinhardt's `contrib/` cleanup series, specifically focusing on the criteria for removal. Elijah Newren raises concerns about using "not updated in 5 years" as a standalone removal rationale, suggesting it should be coupled with the principle that `contrib/` is meant as a staging area where projects should either graduate or be removed. He notes this policy clarification is planned for a follow-up series.

Newren provides a detailed breakdown of which patches in the series used which removal rationales, showing most removals had multiple justifications (brokenness, better alternatives, or being stubs). He specifically points to patch 4 (where brokenness is suspected) and patch 11 (where usefulness is questioned) as cases where the "not updated" rationale appears weakest. While supportive of the series overall, he cautions that using "not updated" as a primary reason in commit messages might set a problematic precedent that could be misapplied elsewhere in the Git codebase.

The exchange highlights the transition from technical removals to policy formulation, with Newren advocating for clearer documentation of the staging-area principle before relying on time-based removal criteria. This connects to the open questions from the thread summary about formalizing `contrib/README` policies around deprecation timelines and handling of language-specific tools.
