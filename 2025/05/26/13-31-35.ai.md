This is a philosophical discussion about test runner behavior in the ongoing Meson test integration series. Phillip Wood weighs in on Patrick Steinhardt's analysis of TAP specification ambiguity regarding unexpected test passes, strongly advocating for failing on unexpected passes as a way to surface broken assumptions in test expectations.

The email reinforces the emerging maintainer position that test runners should be strict about unexpected passes, contrasting with the current make/prove behavior that silently accepts them. Wood highlights a concrete maintenance risk - that silent acceptance of unexpected passes in CI could lead to unnoticed regressions when tests that should have been promoted from `test_expect_failure` to `test_expect_success` remain incorrectly classified.

This positions the discussion as moving beyond technical implementation (which is fully reviewed and ready for integration) into test infrastructure policy, with Wood's perspective adding weight to Steinhardt's earlier analysis of the TAP specification's flexibility. The exchange suggests the Git project may be moving toward stricter test validation as part of its Meson transition, though the final decision remains with Junio Hamano.
