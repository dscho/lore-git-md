Elijah Newren's series adding efficient mergeability checking to `git merge-tree` is now complete with the addition of the `--quiet` flag. This feature enables hosting platforms to quickly detect merge conflicts without performing full merge computations or writing unnecessary objects to the repository. The implementation builds on earlier plumbing changes to the merge-ort backend that introduced a `mergeability_only` optimization mode.

The final patch implements the user-facing interface, suppressing all output (including conflict messages) and enabling early exit when conflicts are detected. It includes safety checks to prevent incompatible combinations with other options like `--messages` and `--stdin`. Comprehensive tests verify the behavior, confirming empty output is produced, no objects are written unnecessarily, and correct exit codes are returned.

The series went through several iterations, with naming guidance from Junio Hamano (who suggested the initial `--dry-run` approach) and Phillip Wood (who recommended the final `--quiet` naming to align with Git's conventions). Kristoffer Haugsbakk contributed documentation improvements. The solution meets its original goal of providing hosting platforms with an efficient way to check mergeability while maintaining Git's interface consistency and documenting the optimization boundaries. The series is now approved for merging.
