This is a discussion contribution about Change-ID tracking mechanisms, building on Junio C Hamano's proposal for predecessor-successor relationships. Kristoffer Haugsbakk shares their experience with custom email headers (`X-Commit-Hash` and `X-Previous-Commits`) to track patch versions, noting the practical challenges of parsing message IDs for this purpose. They suggest that allowing users to define custom indexes in Git's database could provide flexibility for different Change-ID interpretations while maintaining performance.

The email doesn't propose a specific technical solution but rather explores the tension between standardization and flexibility in tracking changes. Haugsbakk's real-world example of version tracking through email headers demonstrates one pragmatic approach, while their indexing idea hints at a more general mechanism that could accommodate diverse workflows. The thread continues to balance the need for standardization with Git's distributed nature and the variety of contributor needs.
