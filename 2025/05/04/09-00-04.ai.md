This is a discussion response about the proposed 3-pane merge visualization feature. Johannes Sixt raises an important conceptual challenge to Britton Kerin's proposal for extending Meld-style 3-pane views to merge commit review.

The response points out that while 3-pane views make sense during conflict resolution (where we have exactly base/ours/theirs versions), merge commits can have arbitrary numbers of parents, making the 3-pane approach potentially limited. Sixt questions whether 2-parent merges deserve special treatment when the general case could involve more parents, and whether merge tools could reasonably scale to N-pane views.

The email serves as a thoughtful counterpoint to the original proposal, highlighting that while 2-parent merges are common enough to potentially warrant tooling support, the design would need to account for the conceptual inconsistency with higher-order merges. The tone is constructive - presenting the "devil's advocate" view while acknowledging the potential usefulness for common cases. This moves the discussion forward by identifying a key design consideration that would need addressing in any implementation.
