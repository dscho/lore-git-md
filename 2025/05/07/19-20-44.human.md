This is a discussion about commit message attribution conventions, specifically comparing bracketed annotations versus formal trailers for non-code contributions. Junio Hamano responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's suggestion that formal "Comment:" trailers could replace bracketed annotations like "[kh: Added tests]".

Hamano notes the bracketed convention was adopted from the Linux kernel project, where it's widely used. He acknowledges the redundancy with Signed-off-by lines but points out practical advantages: the bracketed form is more concise (especially for contributors with longer initials) and already has established precedent. The response maintains a light tone while defending the status quo, suggesting the project isn't likely to change this convention soon.

The exchange highlights an ongoing tension between attribution clarity and message hygiene, with no immediate resolution. Hamano's position implies the current system works well enough despite its imperfections, particularly given its alignment with kernel practices. The discussion remains relevant for contributors who want to understand Git's commit message norms.
