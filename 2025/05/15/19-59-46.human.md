Junio Hamano responds to Jacob Keller's Linux kernel Change-ID example with probing questions about the underlying reasons for rejection. This is a discussion email in the ongoing Change-ID standardization thread.

Hamano challenges the "eye sore" justification by drawing a parallel to the accepted-but-verbose Signed-off-by trailer. He suggests the real issue may be that kernel maintainers saw no value in the Change-IDs, not just their visual impact. The email explores several possible explanations: whether the IDs were only useful to a closed subsystem team, lacked cross-project standardization, or suffered from inconsistent organizational interpretations.

The response moves the discussion toward understanding the conditions under which metadata becomes acceptable - whether placement (header vs trailer) or shared utility makes the difference. Hamano's questions imply that a successful Change-ID system may need to demonstrate broad value to all stakeholders, not just submitters. This shifts focus from pure aesthetics to the social dynamics of metadata adoption in large projects.
