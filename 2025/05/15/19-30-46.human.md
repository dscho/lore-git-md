This is a naming and integration discussion for the proposed `git-blame-tree` (or alternative) functionality, continuing the ongoing debate about how to best expose this tree-modification-tracking feature.

Jeff King (the original author of the concept) weighs in on Marc Branchaud's "last-touch" naming proposal. He acknowledges the "blame-tree" name was his own historical choice, but reveals the feature was actually replacing a script called "git-last-modified" - bringing the naming full circle. He points out that "tree" may also be a misnomer since the command works with arbitrary pathspecs, not just directory trees.

King explores an alternative approach: integrating the functionality into `git log` via a `--last-modified` flag that would show when each matching path was last changed, with early termination once all paths have been reported. He provides a detailed example of what this might look like, showing how it could handle complex cases like commits that modify multiple Makefiles. While this would produce different output than the current standalone implementation, it would leverage `git log`'s existing flexibility for formatting and diff output.

The email doesn't take a firm position but presents this as another option to consider alongside the three previously discussed approaches (full `git blame` integration, plumbing/public split, or separate command). King's historical context and concrete example add new dimensions to the naming and integration debate, suggesting the functionality might naturally fit into Git's existing path-tracking capabilities in `git log`. The tone is exploratory rather than prescriptive, keeping all options open while providing implementer insight into the feature's origins and possible futures.
