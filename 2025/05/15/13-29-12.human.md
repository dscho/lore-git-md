This is a naming discussion in the ongoing `git-blame-tree` RFC thread. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Marc Branchaud and Junio Hamano's debate about whether the proposed command's functionality truly constitutes "blame" and what it should be called.

Patrick questions Junio's assertion that the operation is fundamentally different from `git blame`, arguing that both commands share the same core concept of tracking when content was last modified, just operating on different object types (trees vs blobs). He sees the tree version as a natural extension of the existing blame concept.

Regarding Marc's suggested alternative name "ascribe-tree", Patrick expresses concern about its accessibility for non-native English speakers, noting he had to look up the word. This practical consideration adds a new dimension to the naming debate, suggesting that whatever term is chosen should be immediately understandable to Git's global user base.

The exchange highlights how naming discussions in open source often balance technical accuracy, conceptual clarity, and practical usability concerns. Patrick's perspective as a non-native speaker introduces an important consideration that wasn't previously emphasized in the thread.
