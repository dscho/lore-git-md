This is a naming discussion in the ongoing `git-blame-tree` RFC thread. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Marc Branchaud's proposal of alternative names ("ascribe-tree", `git ls-tree --ascribe`) for the proposed tree-version of blame functionality.

Patrick questions Junio Hamano's earlier assertion that the operation isn't conceptually similar to blame, arguing that both commands fundamentally track when each part (lines for blobs, paths for trees) was last changed. He sees the tree version as a natural extension of the same concept.

Regarding naming alternatives, Patrick expresses concern that "ascribe" may be too obscure for non-native English speakers (noting he had to look it up himself), implicitly pushing back against Marc's thesaurus-derived suggestions. While not proposing a specific alternative, his comments suggest he may still favor keeping "blame" in the name despite Junio's conceptual objections.

The exchange highlights ongoing tension between:
1. Junio's view that tree attribution is conceptually distinct from line blame
2. Patrick's position that they share core semantics
3. Marc's search for a neutral term that satisfies both technical accuracy and UX concerns

This keeps the naming debate unresolved while adding new considerations about international accessibility of command names. The thread continues to weigh technical integration pathways against naming clarity and conceptual alignment with existing Git commands.
