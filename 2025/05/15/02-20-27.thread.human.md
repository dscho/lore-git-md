This thread discusses Elijah Newren's proposal to make commit messages in interactive rebase todo lists clearly distinguishable as comments by prefixing them with '#'. The change aims to prevent users from mistakenly editing these messages when they should be modifying the rebase instructions. 

The discussion initially saw some pushback from Reto about readability impact, but Phillip Wood strongly supported the approach, noting it aligns with Git's existing uses of '#' in output formats and that editors should adapt to Git's conventions rather than vice versa. Phillip also reviewed the technical implementation, confirming its correctness and suggesting a test improvement.

Junio Hamano, the Git maintainer, has now weighed in with maintainer-level validation. He confirms the change is purely cosmetic - it doesn't affect internal processing like autosquash matching - and dismisses concerns about breaking custom editors as not worth worrying about. His example output shows the transformation from `pick $COMMIT2 two_split` to `pick $COMMIT2 # two_split`, which he finds clearly communicates the intended distinction.

With both Phillip's technical review and Junio's maintainer approval, the patch appears to have consensus. The remaining open questions about editor impact and readability seem resolved in favor of the change, with the maintainers judging the error-prevention benefits to outweigh any minor readability costs. The patch is likely to proceed in its current form unless new substantive objections emerge.
