This is a discussion about Change-ID metadata and email workflows, continuing the thread on how to make such identifiers more widely accepted. Nico Williams responds to Junio Hamano's point about metadata needing clear utility to justify its visual presence in commit logs.

Nico suggests a technical solution: adding `git log` options to filter uninteresting trailers, acknowledging this wouldn't help email viewers. More importantly, he distills the core issue - Change-IDs are rarely useful for most maintainers when reviewing history, even if valuable in specific contexts. This reinforces Hamano's observation that adoption barriers stem from perceived utility rather than just aesthetics.

The exchange highlights the tension between specialized workflows (like Gerrit's) needing persistent identifiers and general Git users who see them as noise. Nico's pragmatic observation about infrequent usefulness suggests any solution must either demonstrate broader applicability or provide better filtering mechanisms. The thread continues probing how to make metadata standards that serve both specialized and general audiences.
