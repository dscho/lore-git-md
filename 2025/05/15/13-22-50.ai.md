This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the proposed `commit.signoff` configuration option, which would persistently enable signoff for commits similar to the existing `format.signoff` for patches.

Chris Down directly challenges Junio C Hamano's characterization of `format.signoff` as a "mistake" by asking for concrete evidence of problems caused by the long-standing (since 2009) patch signoff configuration. The email represents a pushback against the maintainer's philosophical objection by requesting specific data points about negative impacts, rather than accepting the general principle that historical precedent shouldn't justify new features.

The core disagreement remains unresolved - while the technical implementation is ready (adding the config option in `builtin/commit.c` with documentation and tests), the discussion has shifted to whether automatic signoffs in general (whether for commits or patches) represent good practice. Chris's response suggests they believe the burden of proof lies with those opposing the feature to demonstrate harm from the analogous existing functionality.

Key technical references remain the same: `commit.signoff` config option, `builtin/commit.c` changes, `signoff-option.adoc` documentation, and `t7500-commit-template-squash-signoff.sh` test. The thread now hinges on whether the maintainer will provide concrete examples of problems with `format.signoff` or maintain their position based on principle rather than demonstrated issues.
