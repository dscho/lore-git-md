This is a documentation patch in response to review feedback on clarifying Gmail authentication options for `git send-email`. Aditya Garg follows up on Junio Hamano's suggestions about how to present the authentication choices more neutrally.

The patch originally aimed to clarify that Gmail requires app passwords (rather than account passwords) for `git send-email`, and suggested OAuth2.0 as an alternative for users who don't want to enable multi-factor authentication. Junio pointed out that this phrasing could imply a hierarchy of preferred methods, and suggested more neutral wording that presents OAuth2.0 as an equal alternative rather than a fallback option.

The discussion shows the careful attention being paid to documentation phrasing in this email-related work. Junio maintains his role as a meticulous reviewer, catching both technical accuracy issues (the redundant sentence about OAuth2.0) and presentation concerns (avoiding implied preferences between authentication methods). Aditya appears receptive to the feedback and plans to reword the section accordingly.

This exchange is part of the broader effort to improve Git's email documentation that has already seen several rounds of review and refinement. The attention to neutral wording here reflects the project's commitment to clear, unbiased documentation that presents all valid options equally to users.
