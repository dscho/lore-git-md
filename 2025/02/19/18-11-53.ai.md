This is a technical review response in the ongoing thread about gitk's filename encoding issues, specifically addressing the second patch in the series. Junio C Hamano provides detailed feedback on the merge conflict filename handling implementation.

The email focuses on the technical correctness of the patch that attempts to fix Japanese filename garbling during merge conflict resolution. Junio identifies a potential flaw in the byte/character handling approach - while the patch correctly adds encoding conversion, it may be doing the byte slicing (removing the "diff --git" prefix) before the encoding conversion, which could still cause problems with non-ASCII filenames. He suggests converting the entire line first before slicing to ensure proper character handling.

Junio also recommends broadening the patch's scope in its description, noting the fix would apply to all non-ASCII filenames, not just Japanese ones. This reflects the thread's evolution from a specific Japanese encoding issue to a more general non-ASCII filename handling problem in gitk.

The response maintains the thread's technical depth while pushing for a more robust solution. It doesn't reject the patch outright but identifies areas needing improvement, particularly around the sequence of encoding operations.
