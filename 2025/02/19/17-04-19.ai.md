This is a UX refinement discussion for the deferred refs migration optimization patch.

Junio C Hamano responds to Toon Claes's suggestion about renaming the `--skip-reflog` flag to `--no-reflog` for the reference backend migration feature. While Junio initially assumed the meaning of `--skip-reflog` would be obvious (that skipped reflogs would be lost during migration, not preserved), he acknowledges that Toon's interpretation - where users might expect skipped items to remain available - is plausible given Git's typical UX patterns.

Junio strongly endorses the `--no-reflog` naming as more clearly communicating the destructive nature of the operation, even though it creates some asymmetry in the option space (since a `--reflog` counterpart would be meaningless and `--no-refs` would be nonsensical). The discussion highlights how technical implementers' assumptions about behavior may differ from user expectations, and how careful naming can bridge that gap.

This email represents the final UX polish being applied to the already-technically-approved optimization, which remains deferred until the prerequisite `git reflog drop` functionality is implemented. The thread has now reached consensus on both the architectural sequencing and the user-facing details of the eventual implementation.
