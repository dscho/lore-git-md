Performance optimization patch (part of refs iterator reseeking series) implementing seeking capability for merged iterators, building on the earlier iterator lifecycle refactoring work.

This patch from Karthik Nayak reviews and approves Patrick Steinhardt's implementation of seeking for merged iterators (patch 9/14 in the performance optimization series). The key change separates iterator ownership (iter0_owned/iter1_owned) from active iteration (iter0/iter1) to enable proper iterator reuse during seek operations. Nayak confirms the implementation correctly preserves the underlying iterators when exhausted rather than freeing them, which is necessary for subsequent reseeks.

The review notes the straightforward nature of the change, with the main complexity being the lifetime management of the underlying iterators. This patch is part of the broader effort to optimize refname availability checks across all ref backends (reftable, files, packed) by enabling stateful iterator reuse rather than recreating them for each operation. The series has already demonstrated significant performance improvements (1.25x-2.32x speedups in benchmarks) when creating references against large repositories.

Technical details include the split between owned and active iterator pointers in the merge_ref_iterator struct, and the careful handling of iterator advancement failures to maintain the owned reference. This builds on the iterator lifecycle management introduced earlier in the series (patch 9) and enables the final reseeking optimizations that follow in patches 10-16. The review indicates no outstanding concerns with this particular implementation.
