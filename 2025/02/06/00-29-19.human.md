This is a technical clarification in an ongoing discussion about Git's content filtering during history rewriting operations.

Brian M. Carlson responds to Josef Wolf's follow-up about using clean/smudge filters with `git rebase`. He corrects his earlier statement about `merge.renormalize` not existing, noting it does exist but may only work with the older merge-recursive strategy rather than merge-ort. He defers to Elijah Newren (merge-ort's author) for confirmation.

The email addresses a practical issue with the previously suggested `git add --renormalize` approach during rebase - it fails when commits don't touch filtered files. Carlson suggests trying `-X renormalize` with the default merge strategy instead, though notes this may still drop merge commits unless using `--rebase-merges`. 

For complex cases, Carlson reiterates his recommendation to use `git filter-repo` as a more robust solution for history rewriting with filters. The exchange highlights the nuanced behavior of Git's filtering mechanisms during different types of history operations, with Carlson providing authoritative guidance while acknowledging edge cases.
