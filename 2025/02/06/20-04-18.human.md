This is a follow-up in the ongoing thread about Git's clean/smudge filters and history rewriting operations. Josef Wolf reports issues with `git cherry-pick` behavior when clean/smudge filters are involved.

The key observation is that `cherry-pick` appears to bypass the clean filter when applying changes, committing the smudged content directly to the repository. This creates problems that require manual intervention with `git add --renormalize` to correct. Even when starting from a normalized commit, `cherry-pick` attempts to apply the changes without proper cleaning, leading to conflicts.

This surfaces a new edge case in the broader discussion about Git's filtering behavior during history operations. While previous messages had established proper workflows for `rebase` with renormalization, this note highlights that `cherry-pick` has different (and arguably incorrect) behavior with respect to content filtering. The issue appears to be a fundamental one about when and how filters are applied during cherry-pick operations, rather than just a workflow misunderstanding.

The message doesn't propose a fix but rather documents observed behavior that seems inconsistent with Git's filtering model, where content should always pass through clean filters before being committed. This adds to the thread's growing collection of edge cases and conceptual gaps in Git's handling of filtered content during history operations.
