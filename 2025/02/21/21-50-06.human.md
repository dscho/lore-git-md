Technical response in ongoing repository size management discussion.

Elijah Newren weighs in on the thread about repository pruning, offering a nuanced perspective on history rewriting. While acknowledging Konstantin Ryabitsev's graft/replacement commit approach as technically feasible, Newren expresses strong reservations about promoting history destruction, particularly for users who may not fully grasp Git's integrity model.

The response provides concrete technical details about implementing the graft approach: creating replacement refs to make desired commits appear parentless, then rewriting history to make the graft permanent. Newren notes this is a documented but discouraged use case in git-filter-repo, where it serves as a "last resort" example for users who insist on this path despite the warnings.

The email reinforces Brian Carlson's earlier points about Git's design philosophy while adding practical implementation details. Newren's position is particularly notable for its balance - he provides the technical solution while clearly stating his preference against its use, especially for users who don't understand the implications. This continues the thread's educational tone while moving the technical discussion forward with specific implementation guidance.
