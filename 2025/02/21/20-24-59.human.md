Performance optimization discussion for `git bisect` midpoint selection.

Christian Couder responds to Junio Hamano's explanation of the current midpoint selection algorithm in the ongoing bisect performance thread. The discussion focuses on two key aspects: the relationship to first-parent bisection (already implemented since 2020) and potential tuning of the midpoint selection threshold.

Christian clarifies his earlier suggestion about "first parents first" bisection - he's proposing a hybrid approach that would start with first-parent-only bisection before switching to the full algorithm, rather than replacing it entirely. He argues this could combine the speed of first-parent traversal with the completeness of full bisection.

The core of the email debates the threshold values used in the midpoint selection algorithm. Christian suggests the current 0.1% threshold (using division by 1024) could be made more aggressive (3% via division by 64) throughout most of the process, not just for large commit sets. He notes that integer division would naturally make the threshold effectively exact for the final steps anyway.

This is a technical refinement discussion between two experienced contributors about optimizing an already-working solution (Junio's midpoint traversal patch that achieved 30x speedup). The exchange shows careful consideration of edge cases and tradeoffs between precision and performance, with Christian proposing to make the optimization more consistently aggressive while maintaining correctness.
