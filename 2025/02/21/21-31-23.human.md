Junio Hamano weighs in on Phillip Wood's patch to change `git add -p`'s hunk splitting behavior. This is a follow-up discussion to the first patch in a two-part series that makes split hunks default to "undecided" rather than inheriting their parent's selection state.

Junio acknowledges the current behavior dates back to his original implementation and wasn't deeply considered - it simply didn't account for the workflow where users might want to split an already-selected hunk. While he sees merit in the change, he raises a potential usability regression for users who intentionally split selected hunks to quickly deselect just a few lines from an otherwise good hunk. 

The discussion highlights two competing workflows: Phillip's fix better supports users who select a hunk by mistake and want to split it to select only parts, while Junio notes it could inconvenience users who deliberately select first to capture most changes, then split to fine-tune. This appears to be the first time this tradeoff has been explicitly considered in the thread.

The comment doesn't reject the patch but invites further discussion about whether the change might negatively impact some established workflows. As the original author of this code path, Junio's perspective carries particular weight in evaluating the change's broader implications.
