Performance optimization for `git bisect` initialization in large repositories.

Junio C Hamano shares experimental improvements to address the known performance issue where `git bisect` initialization takes prohibitively long (20+ minutes) in massive repositories like the Linux kernel. The patch demonstrates three potential optimizations, with the first already showing dramatic results - reducing runtime from 20 minutes to 30 seconds in test cases.

The key change reorders how merge commits are evaluated during bisection initialization, starting from the middle of the commit list rather than the beginning. This takes advantage of the existing "good enough" heuristic that allows early termination when a sufficiently central commit is found. The patch includes two additional disabled optimizations that could be explored later: loosening the halfway-point threshold for large repos, and pruning clearly suboptimal candidates early.

The experimental results show the new approach finds a commit just 19 revisions from the theoretical halfway point (versus 17,871 with current code) while being 40x faster. Junio suggests the first optimization is likely sufficient, with the others being less impactful or more complex to implement correctly. The patch includes trace2 instrumentation to help analyze performance characteristics.

This appears to be a promising direction for solving a long-standing pain point, though the maintainer is soliciting feedback before finalizing the approach. The changes specifically target the initialization bottleneck reported in the thread, not the subsequent bisection steps.
