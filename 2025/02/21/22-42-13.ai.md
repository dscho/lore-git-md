This is a technical follow-up in the ongoing discussion about repository size management strategies, specifically comparing shallow clones and partial clones. Brian M. Carlson responds to Emily Shaffer's recommendation of partial clones by adding important nuance to the performance characteristics of both approaches.

Carlson clarifies that while partial clones may be better for GitHub users as Shaffer suggested, shallow clones have different tradeoffs that make them situationally preferable. He explains that shallow clones themselves are not server-intensive - the expensive operation is fetching into them. This makes shallow clones viable for CI systems that clone once and don't fetch updates, though they become problematic at scale when many jobs fetch into shallow clones.

The key distinction Carlson highlights is offline capability: partial clones require constant network access to fetch missing objects, while shallow clones work offline after the clone. He provides concrete examples where this matters - airplanes, poor hotel WiFi, and remote locations - showing how the optimal solution depends on the user's connectivity needs and workflow patterns.

This email doesn't introduce new technical concepts but rather refines the decision framework for choosing between the two approaches. Carlson maintains the thread's educational tone while emphasizing that both options have valid use cases, with the best choice depending on specific operational constraints around server load, scale, and connectivity requirements. The response reinforces that there's no universal solution, only context-appropriate tradeoffs.
