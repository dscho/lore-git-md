This is a technical follow-up in the thread about repository size management, focusing on the tradeoffs between shallow and partial clones. Brian M. Carlson responds to Emily Shaffer's recommendation of partial clones by adding nuance to the discussion.

Carlson clarifies that while partial clones may be generally preferable, shallow clones have specific advantages in certain offline workflows. He explains that shallow clones themselves aren't server-intensive - the real resource cost comes when fetching into them. This makes shallow clones viable for individual users who occasionally need offline access, though still problematic for large-scale CI systems.

The email highlights two key practical considerations: partial clones require constant internet access, while shallow clones enable offline work (useful for travel or poor connectivity situations). Carlson positions this not as disagreement with Shaffer's recommendation, but as important context for choosing between the options based on specific needs.

This maintains the thread's educational tone while adding real-world usage perspectives. The exchange solidifies that both approaches have valid use cases, with the best choice depending on whether internet dependency or server load is the greater constraint for a given user.
