This is a technical discussion about `git bisect` performance optimization strategies, specifically comparing different approaches to commit selection during bisection.

Christian Couder responds to Junio Hamano's clarification about first-parent bisection, noting that while first-parent bisection exists (since 2020), he's suggesting a hybrid approach that would first use first-parent bisection to narrow the search space before switching to regular bisection. He argues this could combine the speed benefits of first-parent bisection's O(N) approach with the thoroughness of full bisection.

The discussion then shifts to the threshold for determining when a commit is "good enough" to count as a midpoint. Christian suggests the current 0.1% threshold (implemented via division by 1024) could be made more aggressive (3% via division by 64) throughout the entire bisection process, not just for large candidate sets. He notes that integer division would naturally make the threshold effectively exact for small enough candidate sets (around 33 commits or fewer).

Christian proposes making the threshold configurable via a user preference flag that would choose between speed-optimized (64) and precision-optimized (1024) modes. The exchange highlights ongoing considerations about balancing bisection speed against accuracy, with Christian advocating for more aggressive optimization throughout the entire process rather than just for large commit sets.
