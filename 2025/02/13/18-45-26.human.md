This is a follow-up discussion about performance optimizations in the legacy merge-recursive code, specifically regarding the `get_unmerged()` function. Elijah Newren, the merge machinery maintainer, is responding to Junio Hamano's questions about potential O(n) optimizations.

Newren clarifies that while an O(n) approach is theoretically possible (by leveraging the index's sorted nature to avoid lookups and sorting), it's not worth pursuing for several reasons: merge-recursive is rarely used, has bigger performance bottlenecks elsewhere, and is slated for eventual removal in favor of merge-ort. He notes this optimization wouldn't benefit the ort backend.

The email reinforces Newren's earlier recommendation to abandon this optimization attempt, marking a clear endpoint to this thread. The discussion serves as a case study in when not to optimize legacy code, especially when replacement infrastructure (merge-ort) already exists. The only remaining action appears to be merging the previously approved rename-processing optimization from the first patch.
