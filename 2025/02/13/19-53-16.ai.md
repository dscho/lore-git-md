This is a technical discussion about iterator infrastructure limitations in the reftable backend's performance optimization efforts. Jeff King responds to Patrick Steinhardt's analysis of the challenges in implementing efficient refname collision checking during mass ref operations.

The exchange focuses on the fundamental iterator limitations that prevent optimal performance. Steinhardt explains that the current iterator design doesn't support reseeking - iterators are immediately freed upon completion, making it impossible to reuse them for checking multiple refnames. King acknowledges the complexity, noting that even the files backend would struggle with efficient seeking, though it could at least restart binary searches.

Key technical points include:
1. The need for iterator infrastructure to support stateful reseeking while maintaining position
2. Differences in how files and reftable backends would handle seeking operations
3. The observation that sorted refname input could enable forward-only seeking optimizations

This discussion represents a deeper architectural work needed to enable the batching optimizations that could address the reftable performance issues. While not introducing new patches, it clarifies the prerequisite work Steinhardt is undertaking to revamp iterator lifetimes, which will be foundational for subsequent performance improvements. The tone is collaborative, with both developers recognizing the non-trivial nature of the underlying technical challenges.
