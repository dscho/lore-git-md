Performance optimization discussion for merge-recursive, with one patch approved and another discouraged.

Elijah Newren responds to Meet Soni's updated patch series, providing clear direction on the two proposed optimizations for merge-recursive.c. The first patch, which optimizes string list processing in `process_renames()` from O(n²) to O(n log n), is approved with no further changes needed. This follows earlier review feedback that has now been addressed.

The second patch, which attempts similar optimizations for `get_unmerged()`, faces stronger pushback. Newren notes technical problems with the implementation and questions the value of investing further effort in merge-recursive optimizations at all. He explicitly recommends dropping this second patch rather than expanding the optimization effort to other parts of the file.

The email reveals Newren's strategic perspective on merge-recursive - while willing to review patches from new contributors as a mentoring opportunity, he considers the file essentially legacy code not worth significant optimization work, given its planned replacement by merge-ort. This positions the discussion in the broader context of Git's architecture evolution, where optimizations in deprecated components may not be the best use of contributor or reviewer time.

Key technical points:
- Approved: `process_renames()` optimization (string_list_append()+sort() pattern)
- Rejected: `get_unmerged()` optimization in current form
- File context: merge-recursive.c (legacy merge strategy)
- Strategic context: merge-ort as the future direction

The response effectively closes the RFC exploration of broader optimizations while accepting the one clearly valuable change. Future discussion would need to either present compelling performance data to justify the second optimization or shift focus to merge-ort instead.
