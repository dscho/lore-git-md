Performance optimization discussion for reftable backend's conflict checking during mass ref updates.

Jeff King responds to Patrick Steinhardt's analysis of the reftable performance regression, digging deeper into why conflict checking remains slow even with small reftable files. He identifies that the current implementation makes a full pass over all refs for each new ref being added, which explains the linear scaling. The key insight is that this could be optimized by processing all new refs in a single pass against the merged refs list, avoiding redundant work and potentially deduplicating common prefix checks.

King also examines Steinhardt's proposed `repo_get_oid()` optimization, noting that the performance hit comes not from general revision parsing but from a specific warning check for ambiguous refnames. He suggests both a quick workaround (disabling the warning via config) and a cleaner long-term solution involving a flag to bypass the warning.

This email advances the discussion by proposing concrete optimization strategies while maintaining technical rigor. The suggestions appear well-considered, building on Steinhardt's findings while offering additional insights from someone deeply familiar with Git's ref handling internals. The exchange demonstrates productive collaboration on a complex performance issue.
