This is a response from Git-for-Windows maintainer Johannes Schindelin regarding the Windows compatibility aspects of Patrick Steinhardt's reftable decoupling series, specifically addressing the header reorganization in patch 15/18.

Schindelin raises two concrete concerns about the current approach to splitting POSIX functionality from Windows-specific headers. First, he notes the changes have caused significant build failures in Git-for-Windows CI (referencing a "dark red" CI status), indicating the transition hasn't been smoothly integrated with their platform-specific patches. Second, he objects to the architectural choice of having duplicate filenames in different directories (like `compat-util.h` in both mingw/ and msvc/), which complicates debugging and code navigation.

The email serves as a warning that while the technical approach may work for mainline Git, it creates practical problems for the Git-for-Windows ecosystem that will need resolution before merging. Schindelin's feedback suggests the current header organization may need reconsideration to avoid filename collisions and ensure smoother Windows integration, though he acknowledges similar naming issues exist elsewhere in the codebase. This represents the final outstanding integration concern for the otherwise merge-ready reftable decoupling series.
