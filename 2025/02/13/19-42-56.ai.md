Performance optimization discussion focusing on ambiguous refname warning overhead in reftable backend.

Jeff King responds to Patrick Steinhardt's analysis of the `refs_verify_refname_available()` bottleneck, proposing solutions for the ambiguous refname warning overhead that accounts for significant performance impact during mass ref operations. The discussion centers on two approaches:

1. A quick fix temporarily disabling the global `warn_on_object_refname_ambiguity` flag in `update-ref.c`, similar to existing patterns in other commands. This would provide immediate relief but requires careful restoration of the flag state.

2. A more architectural solution introducing a `GET_OID_SKIP_OBJREF` flag to suppress warnings per-call, demonstrated through a comprehensive patch modifying:
   - `object-name.c` to honor the new flag
   - Multiple commands (`cat-file`, `pack-objects`, `revision.c`, `submodule.c`) to use it
   - Removal of the global warning flag from environment handling

Key technical observations:
- The per-call flag approach creates complications in revision.c where stdin vs command-line inputs would both be affected
- Some commands like `rev-list` have mixed use cases where warning suppression might not be desirable
- The warning's utility is questioned given it mainly catches edge cases like `git branch $oid`

The discussion suggests the warning may not justify its performance cost, with King leaning toward either keeping the simpler global flag approach or removing the warning entirely. This ties into the broader reftable performance work where eliminating unnecessary checks could significantly improve mass ref operations.
