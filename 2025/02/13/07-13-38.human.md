Performance investigation into reftable backend slowdown during mass ref operations. Patrick Steinhardt has identified the root cause of the severe performance regression (78x slower than files backend in his tests) when deleting and recreating many refs. The issue stems from how reftable handles tombstone records (deleted refs) - they remain in small tables that don't trigger auto-compaction, forcing expensive conflict checks during subsequent ref creation. 

Steinhardt found two key insights: (1) manually packing refs after deletion makes reftable actually outperform files backend (1.73x faster), and (2) optimizing `git-update-ref` to skip unnecessary `repo_get_oid()` calls when exact OIDs are provided yields significant speedup (from 16.3s to 5.9s for reftable, with smaller gains for files backend). 

While this doesn't fully close the performance gap (reftable remains 32x slower than files backend even with the optimization), it substantially improves the regression. Steinhardt plans to send patches for the `repo_get_oid()` optimization soon while noting the fundamental tombstone behavior may require design-level consideration. The findings validate the original report while providing actionable improvements and deeper understanding of reftable's scaling characteristics.
