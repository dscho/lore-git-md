This thread investigates a performance regression in Git's reftable backend when creating large numbers of references, where operations that take 5.5 seconds with the traditional files backend balloon to nearly a minute with reftable. The core issue stems from tombstone records in reftable preventing auto-compaction while requiring expensive conflict checks, with the `refs_verify_refname_available()` function emerging as the primary bottleneck.

Patrick Steinhardt has already made progress by optimizing `repo_get_oid()`, narrowing the performance gap from 30x to 32x. Jeff King's analysis shows that about 80% of the remaining overhead comes from reference verification, particularly from warnings about ambiguous refnames. The discussion has shifted from initial focus on batching optimizations to recognizing deeper iterator infrastructure limitations that need addressing first.

In the latest exchange, Jeff King proposes two approaches to tackle the ambiguous refname warning overhead: a quick fix temporarily disabling the global warning flag, or a more comprehensive solution introducing a per-call flag to suppress warnings. While the per-call approach is architecturally cleaner, it introduces complications in some commands like `rev-list` where warning suppression might not always be desirable. King questions whether the warning's utility justifies its performance cost, suggesting it may be better to either keep the simpler global approach or remove the warning entirely.

The thread shows the project's careful balancing act between performance optimization and maintaining useful functionality, with Steinhardt and King collaboratively working through both immediate fixes and longer-term architectural solutions. The discussion remains grounded in real-world benchmarks (linux.git with 50,000 refs) while acknowledging that some solutions may require foundational changes to Git's iterator infrastructure.
