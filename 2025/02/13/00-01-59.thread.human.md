This thread investigates a significant performance regression in Git's reftable backend when creating large numbers of references, originally showing a 30x slowdown compared to the traditional files backend. The discussion has identified three key optimization areas: iterator infrastructure limitations, verification bottlenecks, and overhead from ambiguous refname warnings.

The core issue stems from how reftable handles tombstone records during mass ref operations, which prevents auto-compaction while requiring expensive conflict checks. Patrick Steinhardt has already reduced the gap from 30x to 32x through `repo_get_oid()` optimizations. Jeff King's analysis revealed that about 80% of remaining overhead comes from `refs_verify_refname_available()`, with ambiguous refname warnings being a major contributor. The technical discussion has deepened understanding of iterator limitations, showing the need for reseeking capability before batching optimizations can proceed.

In the latest development, Junio C Hamano has weighed in, agreeing with Jeff King that the ambiguous refname warnings (introduced in commit 798c35f) may not justify their performance cost during mass operations. Both maintainers suggest these checks would be better performed at ref creation time rather than during reads. While this confirms alignment on moving away from the current warning implementation, the exact approach (global flag, per-call flag, or complete removal) remains undecided. The discussion continues to use the linux.git 50,000-ref benchmark as the key test case, with attention now shifting to balancing correctness and performance in the verification path while Steinhardt works on the prerequisite iterator improvements.
