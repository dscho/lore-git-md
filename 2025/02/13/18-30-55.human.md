Performance optimization discussion for merge-recursive, with one patch ready and another facing objections. This is a follow-up in the thread about optimizing string list processing in merge-recursive.c. Elijah Newren responds to Meet Soni's revised patch series, giving clear direction on how to proceed.

The first patch, which optimizes `process_renames()` from O(n²) to O(n log n) complexity, has been reviewed and approved with all feedback addressed. It's now considered merge-ready. The second patch targeting `get_unmerged()` remains problematic despite revisions, with Newren suggesting it out entirely rather than pursuing further fixes.

Newren makes the strategic position clear: while reviewing patches from new contributors has value, actively optimizing merge-recursive may not be worth the effort given its planned deprecation in favor of merge-ort. He explicitly advises against expanding the optimization work to other parts of merge-recursive. The email effectively closes the door on further algorithmic improvements to this legacy code path while preserving the one clear win from the series.

This moves the thread toward resolution, with the first patch likely to proceed and the second being abandoned unless strong new evidence emerges about its value. The maintainer's guidance here helps focus contributor effort on more forward-looking areas of the codebase.
