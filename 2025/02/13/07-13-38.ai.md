Performance investigation into reftable backend's mass ref creation slowdown, with initial findings and optimization opportunity.

Patrick Steinhardt follows up on the reported 30x performance regression in the reftable backend during mass ref creation scenarios. He confirms the issue is reproducible and identifies the root cause: when deleting all refs before recreating them, the reftable backend maintains tombstone records that prevent auto-compaction while still requiring expensive conflict checks. This creates a worst-case scenario where the reftable backend performs 78x slower than files (17.5s vs 223ms in his linux.git test).

The email reveals two key insights: First, manually packing refs after deletion makes reftable actually outperform files (129ms vs 223ms). Second, Steinhardt found an optimization in `git-update-ref` where skipping `repo_get_oid()` for exact object IDs provides significant speedups - reducing reftable time from 16.3s to 5.9s in his tests (though still slower than files' 181ms). The optimization also slightly benefits the files backend.

Steinhardt notes this appears to be a fundamental limitation of reftable's tombstone design rather than a straightforward bug, but plans to send patches for the `repo_get_oid()` optimization soon while continuing to investigate other potential improvements. The benchmarks show the optimization cuts the performance gap from 30x to about 32x, indicating there may be more low-hanging fruit in the ref update path.
