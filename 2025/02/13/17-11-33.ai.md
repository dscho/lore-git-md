Performance optimization follow-up for merge-recursive's string list processing, specifically addressing the `get_unmerged()` function. This is the second patch in a series where the first (optimizing `process_renames()`) was recently approved by Elijah Newren.

The patch proposes replacing O(n²) `string_list_insert()` with O(n) `string_list_append()` followed by sort and deduplication in `get_unmerged()`, similar to the approach taken in the first patch. Key technical changes include:
- Removal of pre-insertion duplicate checks via `string_list_lookup()`
- Addition of post-construction `string_list_sort()` and `string_list_remove_duplicates()`
- Maintenance of stage data allocation pattern through `xcalloc`
- 10-line modification in merge-recursive.c

Newren raises several substantive concerns about this RFC patch:
1. Questions the performance impact claims, noting that:
   - The number of conflicts (n) is typically small
   - Other O(n²) operations in merge-recursive may dominate
   - The current implementation may effectively be O(n log n) already due to index structure
2. Requests evidence for the "significant" performance improvement claim
3. Expresses uncertainty about whether correctness is fully maintained
4. Suggests an O(n) approach might be possible but likely not worth the complexity

The email continues the thread's technical discussion about optimizing merge-recursive's string list handling, with this patch now under scrutiny following the successful review of the first optimization. Newren's feedback indicates this change may need stronger justification and testing before proceeding, contrasting with his earlier approval of the similar `process_renames()` optimization.
