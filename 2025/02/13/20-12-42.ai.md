This is a performance optimization discussion in the ongoing reftable backend investigation, specifically addressing the handling of ambiguous refname warnings. Junio C Hamano is responding to Jeff King's analysis of warning overhead in the ref verification process.

The email shows agreement with King's position that ambiguous refname warnings (introduced in commit 798c35f) may not provide enough value to justify their performance cost during mass ref operations. Hamano concurs with King's suggestion that these checks would be better performed at ref creation time rather than during reads, as this would eliminate the repeated scanning overhead while still catching the problematic cases.

This brief interaction confirms alignment between maintainers on moving away from the current warning implementation, though the exact replacement approach (global flag vs per-call flag vs complete removal) remains open. The discussion continues to focus on balancing correctness with performance in the reftable backend's verification path, with particular attention to the linux.git 50,000-ref benchmark case that originally revealed the 30x slowdown.
