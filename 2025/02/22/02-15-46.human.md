Performance optimization for `git bisect` with merge commits.

Junio Hamano acknowledges Christian Couder's correction about the math behind the bisection threshold calculations in their ongoing discussion about optimizing `git bisect` performance with large repositories. The key points clarified are that the threshold for using the looser midpoint selection criteria is actually 10,000 commits (not 1,000 as previously mentioned), and that division by 64 allows for a 1.6% deviation from the exact midpoint (not 3%).

Hamano emphasizes that the specific threshold number (10,000) is somewhat arbitrary - it could have been 5,000 or 100,000 - and that the more important consideration is ensuring the optimization doesn't compromise correctness when the remaining commit set becomes small. The discussion shows the careful balance being struck between performance gains and maintaining accurate bisection results.

This email continues the technical refinement of the bisect optimization patch that was previously shown to provide dramatic speed improvements (reducing time from 20 minutes to 30 seconds in test cases). The conversation demonstrates the maintainer and contributors working through precise mathematical details to ensure the optimization is both effective and correct.
