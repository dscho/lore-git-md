This is a technical discussion about Git's clean/smudge filter behavior during history rewriting operations, specifically focusing on cherry-pick's limitations. Chris Torek responds to Josef Wolf's frustration about Git operating on smudged files in certain scenarios.

Torek explains that Git's speed optimizations are at the root of the issue - the system avoids unnecessary filter operations as a performance optimization, which causes problems when filters change between commits. He confirms that while merge operations (both recursive and ort) have internal renormalization capabilities, cherry-pick lacks this functionality, though checkout does support it.

The email highlights a key architectural tension between Git's performance optimizations and consistent filter application. Torek suggests this might warrant exposing renormalization to cherry-pick operations in builtin/revert.c, noting the current inconsistency where checkout handles renormalization but cherry-pick does not.
