This is a maintainer perspective on Git's filter behavior during history operations, specifically addressing cherry-pick's handling of clean/smudge filters.

Junio C. Hamano responds to Josef Wolf's frustration about Git's inconsistent application of clean/smudge filters during operations like cherry-pick. The maintainer suggests the current behavior stems from historical factors rather than technical necessity - many Git tools were developed before clean/smudge filters became common, and the developers who could update them haven't personally needed this functionality.

The response implies that while the current behavior may seem inconsistent, it's not due to any fundamental limitation but rather a lack of developer motivation to implement filter support in older commands. Hamano hints that demonstrating real-world need could spur development, effectively inviting the community to make the case for improving filter handling in cherry-pick and similar operations.

This email doesn't propose any technical solutions but provides important context about why certain commands behave differently with filters, framing it as an opportunity for community contribution rather than an inherent system limitation.
