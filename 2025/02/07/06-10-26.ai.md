This is a technical follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's clean/smudge filters and history rewriting operations, specifically addressing cherry-pick behavior. Chris Torek responds to Josef Wolf's findings about cherry-pick's problematic interaction with filters by suggesting an alternative approach.

The email challenges the fundamental premise of using `git cherry-pick` for this workflow, arguing that since the goal is to create a linear sequence of cleaned commits, cherry-pick's merge-based approach is inappropriate. Instead, they propose a simpler method: directly restoring files from the original commit followed by manual add/commit operations, which would bypass cherry-pick's filter-related issues entirely.

This suggestion represents a significant workflow alternative to the previously discussed solutions involving rebase with renormalization or filter-repo. It avoids both the cherry-pick filter bypass problem and the complex rebase/renormalize workflows, though it may require more manual intervention for each commit. The proposal doesn't address all the edge cases raised in the thread (like commits that don't modify filtered files), but offers a conceptually simpler path forward for the linear history case.
