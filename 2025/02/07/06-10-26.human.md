This is a technical follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's clean/smudge filters and history rewriting operations. Chris Torek responds to Josef Wolf's discovery that `git cherry-pick` behaves unexpectedly with content filters, bypassing the clean filter when committing changes.

Torek suggests the root issue may be cherry-pick's default merge behavior, which isn't actually needed for the stated goal of creating a linear sequence of cleaned commits. Instead of using cherry-pick, he proposes a simpler workflow: directly restoring files from the original commit, then manually adding and committing them to ensure proper filter application.

The email is brief but makes a key architectural point - that cherry-pick's merge-based approach may be fundamentally mismatched for this filtering use case. This builds on the thread's earlier findings about the limitations of Git's filtering system during history operations. While not providing a complete solution, Torek's suggestion offers an alternative path that avoids the problematic cherry-pick behavior entirely.
