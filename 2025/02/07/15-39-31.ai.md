This is a maintainer perspective on Git's filter behavior limitations, particularly regarding cherry-pick operations. Junio C. Hamano responds to Josef Wolf's frustration about Git's inconsistent handling of clean/smudge filters during history rewriting operations.

The email provides historical context for why many Git commands (like cherry-pick) don't properly handle filters - they were implemented before filters became widely used, and current maintainers haven't prioritized updating them since they don't personally need this functionality. Hamano suggests that motivating the right developers could lead to improvements, implying this is more a matter of prioritization than technical impossibility.

Key technical points:
- Confirms cherry-pick's current filter behavior is a historical artifact rather than intentional design
- Implies the functionality could be added if there was sufficient developer motivation
- Positions this as a community prioritization issue rather than a fundamental limitation

This email doesn't introduce new technical solutions but provides important maintainer perspective on why these filter inconsistencies exist and what would be needed to address them. It suggests the path forward requires either demonstrating strong user need or finding developers personally motivated to work on filter support.
