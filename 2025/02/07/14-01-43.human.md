This is a technical clarification in the ongoing discussion about Git's renormalization behavior during history rewriting operations. Elijah Newren responds to Chris Torek's point about cherry-pick lacking renormalization support by explaining the deeper architectural constraints.

Newren confirms that while `-Xrenormalize` can be used with cherry-pick, it wouldn't help in this case because the merge machinery only applies renormalization during 3-way content merges. Since the user's rebase-to-root operation involves merging against an empty history (where no files are modified on one side), no automatic renormalization occurs regardless of strategy. He emphasizes this isn't a bug but a necessary optimization, as universal renormalization would be prohibitively slow.

The email reinforces Phillip Wood's earlier suggested workflow (`git rebase --root -x 'git add --renormalize . && ...'`) as the correct solution for linear history cases, while providing important context about why Git behaves this way. This adds architectural depth to the thread's understanding of when and why renormalization applies during different operations.
