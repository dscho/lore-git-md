This thread began with Karthik Nayak proposing a performance optimization for reference backend migrations - a `--skip-reflog` flag to avoid costly reflog migration when reflogs aren't needed. The technical implementation was well-received, with Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler providing thorough reviews and approval. The patch modifies the refs migration infrastructure to support this optimization while maintaining test coverage.

However, Junio Hamano has now raised a fundamental architectural question: should reflog management be handled separately from backend migration? While acknowledging the technical soundness of the implementation, he suggests a more general `git reflog drop` command might be the better approach.

Karthik has responded with a nuanced defense of the migration-specific optimization. He argues that for large repositories (particularly GitLab's production systems with millions of refs), skipping reflog migration provides significant performance benefits when reflogs are known to be unwanted. While agreeing that a standalone reflog management tool would be useful, he maintains that the migration-specific optimization serves a distinct use case - particularly for server-side repositories where reflogs may have been temporarily enabled for debugging but aren't needed long-term. The discussion now centers on whether this performance optimization belongs in the migration command or should be handled through separate tooling, with Karthik making the case that both approaches have merit.
