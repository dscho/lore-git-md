Here's an updated human-readable summary of the thread:

The discussion began with Uwe Kleine-König reporting severe performance issues in `git bisect` initialization for large repositories like the Linux kernel (572k+ revisions), where the initial step could take 18+ minutes on SSDs or an hour on HDDs. This connected to prior 2023 discussions about bisect performance in large repositories, with Junio C Hamano noting this was known behavior rather than a new regression.

Junio has now proposed a solution with an experimental patch that dramatically improves performance. The key optimization changes how merge commits are processed during bisect initialization - instead of working through them in commit-list order, it starts from the middle of the merge list and works outward. This simple change alone reduces runtime from ~20 minutes to ~30 seconds in test cases while still finding good bisection points (19 commits from theoretical halfway vs the original 17,871). 

The patch also explores but doesn't yet implement two additional ideas: loosening the halfway threshold criteria during early steps, and early discard of non-viable candidates. These show promise but require more careful implementation. The current version focuses on the proven midpoint traversal approach, adding instrumentation to help analyze performance.

This represents significant progress on a long-standing performance pain point. The solution appears both effective and relatively straightforward to maintain, though there may be room for further refinement with the additional optimization approaches Junio outlined. The thread now awaits testing feedback from users with large repositories and potentially further discussion of the disabled optimization ideas.
