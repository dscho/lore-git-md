This is a follow-up discussion about optimizing Coverity static analysis workflow in the Git project.

Jeff King responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about workload distribution for Coverity issue triaging. He acknowledges that while Coverity reports require human judgment (with many being false positives), the current approach of scanning the 'jch' branch may not significantly increase his workload compared to scanning 'next', since he'd be reviewing these issues eventually. The key factor is the quality of patches when they reach 'jch' rather than the quantity.

King identifies two main challenges: the closed nature of Coverity's interface makes collaboration difficult (no shared accounts or linkable reports), and there's no good way to mark issues as already-reviewed to prevent duplicate work. He floats the idea of a bot that could summarize Coverity results for mailing list discussion, but notes this would be technically challenging given Coverity's email limitations.

The email concludes with a pragmatic stance - they'll continue with the current 'jch' scanning approach while monitoring its effectiveness, keeping open the possibility of future workflow improvements if the benefits prove substantial enough to warrant the effort. This maintains the status quo while acknowledging the ongoing tension between early bug detection and maintainer workload.
