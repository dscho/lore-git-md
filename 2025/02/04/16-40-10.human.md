Junio Hamano follows up on the ongoing discussion about quoting behavior in `git rev-list`'s missing object reporting feature. This is a key design-level response in the thread that questions fundamental assumptions about the quoting approach.

Hamano clarifies his position that robust quoting of non-ASCII characters may be unnecessary, noting that:
1. Some quoting is inevitable (for spaces, quotes, and newlines) to maintain line-oriented output

3. The output format differs from `--objects` (which shows raw paths) but shouldn't necessarily quote everything

He suggests the current proposal to add `QUOTE_PATH_IGNORE_CONFIG` may be solving a non-issue, since consumers will need unquoting logic regardless. This continues the thread's shift from implementation details to core design philosophy, with the maintainer signaling that simpler, minimal quoting aligned with Git's future direction (where `core.quotePath` may default to 'no') would be preferable.

The email doesn't introduce new code but provides important guidance that will likely require the feature author to reconsider their quoting strategy. The discussion remains at a critical juncture about how strictly machine-readable output needs to handle path quoting.
