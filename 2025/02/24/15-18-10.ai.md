Performance optimization follow-up in the refs subsystem. This email from shejialuo provides review feedback on the latter half of Patrick Steinhardt's v2 series (patches 9-16/16) that optimizes refname availability checks across Git's ref backends. The series had already been merged when this performance analysis was posted.

The key technical focus here is on the iterator reseeking optimizations implemented in patches 10-16, which build on earlier work to deduplicate prefix checks (patch 9) and batch refname availability validations. shejialuo confirms the series achieves significant performance gains, particularly for the reftable backend where iterator reseeking reduces overhead substantially (1.25x speedup in typical cases, up to 7.56x in edge cases with tombstones). The files backend shows more modest improvements (1.01-1.27x) due to inherent filesystem I/O constraints.

The email notes that while the extreme benchmark case (deleting then recreating all refs) shows reftable still trailing files backend performance, more realistic scenarios with preexisting refs demonstrate reftable outperforming files by 15x after optimizations. shejialuo indicates having reviewed the iterator implementation details in patches 9-16 but leaves other parts of the series for others to examine, suggesting the technical discussion is winding down with consensus on the implementation approach.
