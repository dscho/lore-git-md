This is a maintainer response in the ongoing discussion about type safety and warning elimination in Git's patch application code.

Junio Hamano responds to Zejun Zhao's question about whether to continue with their patch series addressing `-Wsign-comparison` warnings in `apply.c`. The maintainer makes several key points:

1. He distinguishes between two types of changes: those fixing actual allocation-size truncation bugs (which he considers valuable) versus blanket type-widening changes to silence warnings (which he questions).

2. Specifically regarding line numbers, Hamano expresses skepticism about converting them from `int` to `size_t`, suggesting this may not be the right approach.

3. He references an external article criticizing `-Wsign-compare` as problematic, implying the project shouldn't blindly follow its warnings.

4. While not outright rejecting the series, Hamano encourages focusing on fixes for genuine allocation-size issues rather than wholesale type changes to eliminate warnings.

The response leaves the door open for Zhao to either:
- Identify and resubmit just the patches addressing real allocation-size problems
- Potentially pivot to a different microproject if they prefer

This represents a significant maintainer steer away from the current approach of comprehensive type conversion, while still valuing the general goal of improving type safety where it matters most. The ball is now back in Zhao's court to decide how to proceed.
