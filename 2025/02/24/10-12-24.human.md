This is part of an ongoing discussion about converting Git's oid-related unit tests to the Clar framework, specifically focusing on the oid-array test conversion in patch 2/5. Phillip Wood responds to Seyi Kuforiji's defense of using simple assertions (`cl_assert()`) rather than more verbose failure messages (`cl_failf()`).

Phillip makes a practical case for richer error messages, arguing that when tests fail on CI platforms that developers don't have direct access to (like different OS environments), detailed failure output is crucial for debugging. He challenges Seyi's position that assertion failures are rare enough to justify simpler tests, noting this underestimates the real-world debugging challenges faced by maintainers.

The exchange reveals a philosophical divide in test design priorities - Seyi values simplicity and maintainability in the common case, while Phillip prioritizes debuggability in failure scenarios. Phillip also asks for more details about Seyi's mention of potential upstream Clar improvements for assertion messages, noting that `cl_failf()` already exists in Git's codebase.

This continues the thread's central debate about assertion style that will need resolution before the patch series can proceed. The technical content remains sound (aside from a minor redundant hash initialization that Seyi has agreed to fix), but the disagreement over test philosophy represents a key decision point for the series.
