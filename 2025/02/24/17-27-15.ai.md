Ben Knoble chimes in on the ongoing bisect performance discussion, supporting Christian Couder's suggestion for a hybrid bisection approach that would combine first-parent bisection with regular bisection. The email clarifies that while first-parent bisection has existed since 2020, there isn't currently a mode that automatically switches from first-parent to full bisection.

Knoble provides concrete use cases where this hybrid approach would be valuable - particularly in large repositories with slow build/test cycles. The proposed workflow would first use fast first-parent bisection to narrow down to a problematic merge commit, then switch to regular bisection for fine-grained analysis of the merge's parents. This matches manual workflows some developers already use when bisecting complex histories.

The email reinforces the practical benefits seen in the thread's earlier performance improvements (30x speedup from Junio's midpoint traversal) while suggesting this additional optimization path. It also serves as community validation that the hybrid approach would solve real-world bisection pain points, particularly in large codebases like the Linux kernel where the original performance issues were reported.
