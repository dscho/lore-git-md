This thread explores how to improve Git's branch status messages to better reflect the probabilistic nature of "up to date" status in distributed workflows. The discussion has evolved through several phases, from initial problem identification to technical feasibility analysis.

The core issue stems from users misunderstanding that "up to date" only reflects local tracking branches, not the actual upstream state. Manuel Quiñones proposed tracking fetch timestamps to provide freshness indicators, but Patrick Steinhardt raised serious scalability concerns about maintaining per-reference timestamps in large repositories. Junio Hamano has now countered that the performance impact would be minimal compared to fetch operations themselves, especially with modern protocols and the reftable backend, keeping the technical solution viable.

Three solution tracks are being considered in parallel: technical improvements (like timestamp tracking), clearer message wording (with multiple proposals from Bram van Oosterhout and others), and better user education about Git's distributed nature (championed by D. Ben Knoble). Recent contributions from Bram provide concrete evidence of real user confusion, while Junio and Patrick's exchange clarifies the technical constraints.

The thread now presents a balanced view of all dimensions - while timestamp tracking remains technically possible, the community must weigh its implementation complexity against the benefits. There's growing consensus that immediate UX improvements through better messaging and education may be the most practical path forward, even as technical solutions remain possible for future consideration.
