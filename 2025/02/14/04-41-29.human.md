Performance optimization for merge-recursive's rename processing, now approved for merging. This is the first of two patches in a series, with the second one having been dropped after discussion.

The patch changes how `process_renames()` builds sorted string lists in merge-recursive.c, switching from an O(n²) insertion approach to building unsorted lists followed by an O(n log n) sort. The change is straightforward - replacing `string_list_insert()` with `string_list_append()` and adding explicit `string_list_sort()` calls.

Elijah Newren has reviewed and approved this optimization, confirming it as a valid improvement to legacy code. The second patch in the original series (optimizing `get_unmerged()`) was abandoned after Newren's assessment that it wouldn't provide meaningful real-world benefits, with the author accepting this feedback.

This patch stands alone as a clean performance win for merge-recursive, though the thread discussion makes clear that merge-ort remains the strategic focus for future development. The change is small (15 lines modified) but provides measurable algorithmic improvement in a performance-sensitive area.
