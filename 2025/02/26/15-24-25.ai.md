This is a feature patch series introducing a new "reflog-expire" maintenance task, part of the ongoing effort to make Git's maintenance more flexible and potentially replace `git gc`. The series consists of 6 patches that refactor reflog expiration internals to make them usable by the new maintenance task.

The motivation comes from a gap where reflog entries wouldn't be expired when the "gc" maintenance task is disabled. The series first does significant refactoring of the reflog expiration code (patches 1-4) to make the expiration logic more modular and remove global state. Patch 5 splits out the reflog expiration functionality from `builtin/gc.c`, and patch 6 finally introduces the new maintenance task.

The changes touch several key areas: reflog expiration internals (moving from global to local state), maintenance task infrastructure, and related documentation. The author suggests this could be a step toward eventually making the "incremental" maintenance strategy the default, with the new task enabled by default in that strategy.

The series appears well-structured, with each patch building logically on the previous ones. The refactoring work in the first 4 patches is particularly thorough, eliminating global state in favor of making the reflog expiration logic more self-contained and reusable. The final maintenance task integration follows Git's established patterns for maintenance subcommands.
