This is a proposal to refactor Git's garbage collection heuristics by shifting responsibility from `git gc --auto` to `git maintenance`. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's suggestion about systematically addressing GC tasks by proposing an alternative architectural approach.

The key suggestion is to leverage `git maintenance`'s existing task-based architecture rather than expanding `git gc --auto`'s heuristics. Steinhardt notes that `git maintenance` already has a more modular design with per-task callbacks that could determine when specific cleanup operations (like reflog expiration) are needed. He proposes two concrete steps: adding a dedicated reflog expiration task to `git maintenance`, and having it replace `git gc`'s current all-in-one approach.

The email acknowledges the technical challenges Junio raised about querying stale data across subsystems (reflog, rerere, worktree), particularly the expense of scanning reflogs to determine what needs pruning. Steinhardt suggests a potential optimization - checking just the HEAD reflog as a heuristic - while recognizing this wouldn't be as thorough as a full scan.

This represents a significant architectural pivot from the thread's earlier focus on fixing `git gc --auto`'s heuristics. The proposal would affect multiple components: `git maintenance` would gain new capabilities while `git gc` might be deprecated for auto-GC use cases. The change would enable more configurable and incremental maintenance operations (like geometric repacking) that are difficult under the current `git gc` model.
