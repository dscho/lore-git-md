Junio Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's scalability concerns about tracking fetch timestamps for remote references. This is part of the ongoing discussion about improving Git's branch status messages to better reflect the probabilistic nature of remote state.

Junio argues that the scalability issue Patrick raised is largely orthogonal to the timestamp proposal. He points out that fetching thousands of references is already expensive, and modern Git protocols already optimize this by only advertising relevant references. For users who do need to track many references, Junio suggests the overhead of recording timestamps would be minor compared to the fetch operation itself, and notes that the reftable backend could help mitigate performance concerns.

The response maintains Junio's neutral stance from earlier in the thread - he's not advocating for the timestamp approach, but is pointing out that the scalability concerns may not be as severe as Patrick suggested. This keeps the door open for potential implementations while acknowledging the technical complexities involved. The exchange highlights how Git's distributed nature and performance characteristics create inherent tensions when trying to provide more accurate status information to users.
