This is a technical follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's renormalization behavior with clean/smudge filters. Josef Wolf reports back on their attempts to implement various suggested workflows for rewriting history with XML file normalization, building on Elijah Newren's earlier architectural explanations.

Josef confirms that combining `-X renormalize` with `git add --renormalize` in a rebase does work, but only when preceded by an initial manual renormalization step. They highlight several pain points: the approach fails when commits don't modify filtered files, generates excessive conflicts (particularly with their XML files that get randomly reordered by a proprietary application), and behaves unexpectedly with ignored hash files that are regenerated by their smudge filter.

The email reveals deeper technical challenges beyond just line endings, showing how the current renormalization machinery struggles with more complex filtering scenarios. Josef questions why virtual check-out/check-in operations seem to produce conflicts when the theoretical model suggests they shouldn't, pointing to a potential disconnect between documented behavior and actual implementation. The message serves as valuable real-world validation of the limitations discussed earlier in the thread, while pushing for clearer explanations of the underlying mechanics.
