This thread addresses the operational challenge of managing thousands of small Git repositories (typically ~5 commits each) stored on NFS servers, where inode exhaustion occurs before disk space becomes a constraint. The repositories cannot be deleted (they contain valuable data) and must remain accessible as remotes via their .git directories.

The discussion has progressed from initial problem identification to concrete technical proposals. Randall S. Becker first suggested aggressive garbage collection and sparse-checkout as potential solutions. Brian M. Carlson then provided detailed configuration recommendations including pack file optimizations (setting `fetch.unpackLimit=1`, adjusting `gc.autoPackLimit`, and using `git repack -d --cruft`), reftable migration considerations, and filesystem-level tuning for ext4/btrfs.

Kousik Sanagavarapu's latest response confirms adoption of some suggestions while raising new considerations. They've implemented `transfer.unpackLimit=1` (the more precise version of Brian's initial suggestion) and specific GC settings (`receive.autogc=true`, `gc.auto=1`, `gc.autopacklimit=1`). However, they express concern about performance impacts given their high volume of push operations and note they cannot change from their current NFS/ext4 setup despite awareness of alternatives like btrfs.

Key open questions remain about the effectiveness of these configurations in practice, the migration path to reftable, and how to balance Git-side optimizations with filesystem constraints. The thread has evolved from theoretical proposals to real-world implementation testing, though performance impacts and immutable infrastructure constraints may require further adjustment of the approach.
