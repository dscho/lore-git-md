This is a response in an ongoing thread about managing inode exhaustion on NFS servers hosting thousands of small Git repositories. The original poster described their operational challenges with repositories that must be retained indefinitely despite being very small (~5 commits each), where traditional solutions like repacking and archiving have proven insufficient.

Randall Becker suggests two concrete approaches: running `git gc --aggressive` to compress pack files (noting some users have thousands of unpacked objects), and considering `sparse-checkout` to reduce working directory footprint if applicable. The response also mentions checking `/tmp` and `lost+found` directories as potential sources of inode consumption.

The suggestions appear practical but may not fully address the core issue of Git's one-file-per-object storage model overwhelming NFS inode limits at scale. The thread remains open for more comprehensive solutions to this specialized high-volume, small-repo use case.
