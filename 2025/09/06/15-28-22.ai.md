This is a response in an ongoing thread about managing inode exhaustion on NFS servers hosting many small Git repositories. The thread began as a help request describing operational challenges with thousands of tiny repos (~5 commits each) where inode limits are hit before disk space becomes an issue, and where repositories cannot be deleted due to data retention requirements.

The response from rsbecker@nexbridge.com suggests two concrete approaches: running `git gc --aggressive` to compress pack files (noting this has helped similar cases with thousands of pack files), and considering `sparse-checkout` to reduce working directory content if feasible. The email also mentions checking /tmp and lost+found directories as potential sources of inode consumption.

The suggestions focus on Git-specific optimizations rather than filesystem-level changes, building on the thread's earlier discussion of repacking and archiving strategies. The `--aggressive` flag recommendation implies this may be a stronger optimization than what the original poster had previously tried with regular repacking. The sparse-checkout suggestion introduces a new dimension to the discussion - potentially reducing working tree content rather than just optimizing object storage.

Key technical details for future reference: the proposed solutions involve Git's garbage collection mechanism (`git gc --aggressive`) and working tree management (`sparse-checkout`), both targeting the inode consumption problem from different angles. The response maintains the thread's constraints of mandatory data retention and NFS storage of .git directories as remotes.
