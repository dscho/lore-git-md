This is a reply in the ongoing RFC thread about making Rust mandatory in Git, focusing on distribution packaging concerns. Brian m. carlson responds to Michael Orlitzky's objections about Rust ecosystem stability and maintenance burden for source-based distributions like Gentoo.

Key technical points in this response:
1. Acknowledges bootstrap challenges with self-hosting languages (comparing to Haskell's GHC) but suggests mrustc as a potential solution, noting it could be extended to more platforms
2. Reiterates Rust's safety benefits (thread/memory safety eliminating segfaults and null dereferences) and external pressures from security-conscious organizations
3. Outlines conservative dependency policy targeting Debian stable versions, emphasizing use of already-packaged tools like bindgen/cbindgen rather than frequent crate updates
4. Asserts major distros already package needed crates, downplaying packaging instability concerns

The reply maintains the original RFC's security-focused justification while attempting to address distribution concerns through constrained dependency management. It notably does not engage with the specific Gentoo/riscv/musl build challenges mentioned by Orlitzky beyond the general mrustc suggestion. The core tension remains between Rust's safety advantages and its ecosystem's impact on distribution workflows, with carlson positioning Git's needs as compatible with existing distro packaging practices.
