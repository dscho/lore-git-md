This is a policy discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series, specifically about the timeline for making Rust mandatory in Git 3.0 and how policy decisions are incorporated into the technical implementation.

Junio Hamano expresses concerns about the aggressive timeline for Rust becoming mandatory, particularly for platforms that currently lack Rust support. He argues that expecting these platforms to be ready within 18 months is "hopelessly optimistic" and takes issue with presenting this as an ultimatum. This continues his earlier stance that the transition should be more gradual to accommodate platform diversity.

Brian m. carlson defends the timeline as comparable to the SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability work, suggesting LLVM ports for unsupported platforms could be feasible with Rust upstream support. The exchange reveals tension between technical progress goals and practical platform inclusivity concerns.

A secondary point clarifies how Brian's policy document became part of Ezekiel Smith's patch series - it was shared via a branch collaboration where Ezekiel modified and incorporated it. Hamano seeks transparency about how extensively the original authors endorse these modifications, as this isn't visible to outside observers.

Key technical context mentioned includes:
- The varint subsystem (varint.rs) as the first mandatory component
- The 18-month timeline to Git 3.0 where Rust becomes required
- Ongoing work on platform support (particularly m68k alignment issues)
- The distinction between single-hash operation (Rust optional) and hash conversion (Rust required)

This discussion surfaces important governance questions about how policy decisions are made and documented in the project, particularly when they affect platform support requirements. The exchange suggests the timeline may still be fluid, with Hamano advocating for more flexibility while acknowledging technical constraints around hash interoperability.
