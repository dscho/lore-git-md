This is a response in the ongoing Rust-in-Git discussion, focusing on platform compatibility concerns. Michael Orlitzky pushes back against brian m. carlson's comparison of Rust's bootstrap challenges to Haskell's, using Gentoo's experience with Haskell (specifically GHC and pandoc) as a concrete example.

Orlitzky details how Gentoo supports 23 architecture/libc combinations, but Haskell tooling only works on 5 of them (and only with outdated compiler versions). He shares firsthand experience bootstrapping GHC on riscv/musl, emphasizing the extreme difficulty of such efforts. While agreeing with Rust's safety benefits in principle, he argues the practical costs (in terms of platform support) currently outweigh them, warning that a Rust-required Git could leave many Gentoo users without access to Git at all.

The email reinforces the thread's central tension between Rust's technical advantages and its ecosystem's limitations, with Orlitzky providing concrete data points about Gentoo's multi-architecture reality. His closing line - "all of the cures are worse than the disease" - succinctly captures the pragmatic resistance to mandatory Rust adoption from distribution maintainers dealing with diverse platforms.
