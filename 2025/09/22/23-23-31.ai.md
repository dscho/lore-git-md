This is a reply in the ongoing Rust-in-Git discussion, specifically addressing platform compatibility concerns. Michael Orlitzky responds to brian m. carlson's comparison of Rust's bootstrap challenges to Haskell's GHC, using Gentoo's experience with pandoc as a concrete example.

The email highlights Gentoo's extensive architecture support (23 arch/libc combinations) versus pandoc's limited availability (5 glibc-only architectures, requiring GHC 9.2 from 2021). Orlitzky, who has personal experience bootstrapping GHC on riscv/musl, argues that Rust adoption would similarly limit Git's availability across Gentoo-supported platforms. While agreeing with Rust's technical benefits (static typing, memory safety), he maintains the transition costs currently outweigh the advantages for distribution packaging.

Key technical points include Gentoo's architecture matrix (alpha through sparc), musl support limitations, and the specific example of riscv/musl bootstrapping effort. The response underscores the tension between language safety features and practical distribution constraints, particularly for less common architectures. This continues the thread's central debate about whether Rust's benefits justify its ecosystem impact on niche platforms and source-based distributions.
