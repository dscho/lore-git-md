This is a policy discussion about the timeline for making Rust mandatory in Git, specifically around the hash interoperability work.

Brian m. carlson responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about the aggressive timeline for Rust adoption in Git. They clarify that the plan was for Git 3.0 (replacing 2.55) to make Rust mandatory after about a year (4 release cycles), not the 18 months Junio recalled. Brian notes this timeline is similar to the hash interoperability work's schedule.

They realistically assess this workload given personal commitments, suggesting the timeline might slip to 2.57/2.58 if more help isn't forthcoming.

Brian also explains how their policy document about Rust requirements ended up in Ezekiel Smith's patch series - it was shared from Brian's personal `rust` branch. The discussion highlights tensions between technical progress and platform support timelines, with Brian maintaining the current schedule is feasible but acknowledging potential delays if the workload proves too great.
