This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust integration thread, where Junio Hamano responds to Brian m. carlson's defense of the proposed Rust transition timeline. 

Hamano expresses concern that the timeline (Rust becoming mandatory in Git 3.0 within ~18 months) is too aggressive for platforms currently lacking Rust support, particularly niche architectures like m68k. He distinguishes between the project's internal workload (which they control) and external platform readiness (which they don't), calling the expectation that these platforms will adopt Rust in time "hopelessly optimistic." 

The email also clarifies how Brian m. carlson's policy document about SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability requirements became part of Ezekiel Smith's technical patch series - it was shared via a private branch that Smith then modified. Hamano notes this process lacked visibility into how extensively the policy was changed during incorporation.

This exchange highlights the project's tension between technical progress (the Rust transition and hash interoperability work) and platform inclusivity concerns. While the technical direction seems settled (Rust will be mandatory in 3.0 for varint support and hash conversion features), the timeline remains contentious, with Hamano advocating for more gradual adoption to accommodate slower-moving platforms.
