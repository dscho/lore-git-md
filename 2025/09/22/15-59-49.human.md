This is a response in the ongoing discussion about making Rust mandatory in Git, focusing on package management concerns. Michael Orlitzky counters the technical platform compatibility debate by highlighting a different issue: the impact on Linux distributions' package management systems.

While acknowledging Rust's technical portability challenges (noting his own experience building on riscv/musl), Orlitzky argues the deeper problem is Rust's ecosystem instability forcing distributions to violate their own packaging policies. He describes how current Rust packaging practices require bundling outdated crates, undermining the core value proposition of Linux distributions - centralized, efficient dependency management.

The email positions Git's importance ("the most important program I use") against what the author sees as an even more fundamental system concern: maintaining functional package management. This shifts the discussion from technical feasibility to ecosystem stability concerns, suggesting mandatory Rust adoption would regress dependency handling to pre-modern practices. The tone is pragmatic but firm, coming from a maintainer perspective rather than opposing Rust on technical grounds.
