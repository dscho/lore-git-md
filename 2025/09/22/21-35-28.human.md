This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about making Rust mandatory in Git, focusing on distribution packaging concerns. Brian Carlson responds to Gentoo maintainer Michael Orlitzky's worries about Rust's ecosystem stability and maintenance burden.

Carlson acknowledges the bootstrap challenges with self-hosting languages like Rust (comparing it to Haskell's GHC) but suggests mrustc could help, though it doesn't yet work everywhere. He strongly defends the Rust transition, citing memory safety benefits and pressure from governments/enterprises as compelling reasons, noting Rust would prevent many common C vulnerabilities he's encountered in Git.

Regarding distribution concerns, Carlson pushes back on the idea that Rust will force package managers into dependency hell. He outlines conservative dependency policies (targeting Debian stable versions) and expects to use already-packaged tools like bindgen rather than frequently updated crates. While recognizing the validity of distribution maintainers' concerns, he maintains the security benefits outweigh these costs for Git's future viability.

The exchange highlights the tension between Rust's safety advantages and real-world distribution maintenance realities, with Carlson taking a firm stance that the transition is necessary despite the acknowledged challenges.
