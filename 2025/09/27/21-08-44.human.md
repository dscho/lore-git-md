This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about worktree visibility in `git status` output. Jason Cho responds to Michal Suchánek's observation about inconsistent handling of `.git` directories between the main worktree and sibling worktrees.

Jason acknowledges the technical inconsistency Michal pointed out (where the main worktree's `.git` is hidden while sibling worktree `.git` files appear untracked), but questions the practical impact. He suggests this might be intentional since `.git` is inherently Git-related, and extends that logic to ask whether worktrees should similarly be hidden as they are also Git-derived structures.

The email takes a skeptical tone about the need for change, asking for concrete use cases where the current behavior causes problems - specifically questioning whether having "hundreds of work trees within the same repo directory" is a realistic scenario that justifies modifying the status behavior. This suggests Jason sees the issue as more theoretical than practical at this stage.

The discussion continues to explore the boundaries of what should be considered "Git internals" versus user content in the working directory, with no resolution yet on whether the inconsistency Michal identified warrants design changes.
