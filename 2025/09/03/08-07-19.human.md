This is a review comment on patch 3/5 of the reftable fsck validation series, focusing on stack count verification. Patrick Steinhardt raises concerns about the strictness of checking that the number of files in the reftable directory exactly matches what's listed in `tables.list`.

He points out two practical scenarios where this check could produce false positives: during concurrent updates when another process is modifying the stack, and on Windows where files may temporarily remain after deletion due to being held open. The reftable library already handles these cases gracefully with retry logic.

Instead of counting files, Patrick suggests a more robust approach would be to verify there are no files with unexpected names in the directory. This would catch actual corruption while being more tolerant of normal operational edge cases. The comment maintains the series' technical focus while identifying a potential improvement to make the validation more production-ready.

This feedback follows Patrick's earlier detailed review of patch 2/5 and continues his pattern of suggesting concrete implementation refinements. It doesn't challenge the overall approach but offers a more nuanced solution to a specific validation case. The suggestion aligns with the series' goal of adding thorough but practical fsck checks for reftable integrity.
