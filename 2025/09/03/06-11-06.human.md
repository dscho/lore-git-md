A user continues to advocate for preserving `git whatchanged` in the ongoing discussion about its deprecation. This is a follow-up to Junio C Hamano's firm rejection of renaming `--raw` as an alternative.

The email's author, jorg@ml1.net, maintains their position that `--raw` is a poor name for the functionality, but acknowledges the futility of changing it after 20 years of use. Rather than accepting the transition path to `git log --raw`, they now explicitly state they "vote for keeping git whatchanged" as-is.

This represents a hardening of the original position - where before the user was proposing `--whatchanged` as an alias for `--raw`, they now reject the deprecation entirely. The tone suggests frustration with the naming issue but also recognition that the technical argument has been settled by the "20 years too late" response.

The exchange highlights the challenges of deprecating long-standing commands, particularly when the replacement's naming doesn't clearly communicate its purpose to users. While the technical outcome appears decided, this shows user resistance may persist even after core maintainers have made a final decision.
