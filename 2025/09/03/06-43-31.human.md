This is a late-arriving technical response in the ongoing SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability storage format discussion. Patrick Steinhardt weighs in on Derrick Stolee's earlier proposal for a reftable-like format to store bidirectional hash mappings.

The email expresses general agreement with Stolee's approach of using a geometric sequence-based format (like reftable) rather than repurposing pack indexes or requiring multi-pack indexes. Steinhardt suggests considering Git's existing chunk format (used in commit-graph and MIDX files) as a possible alternative to creating an entirely new format from scratch, though they acknowledge the discussion may have already moved forward without them.

The response is brief and somewhat tentative, recognizing they're joining an advanced discussion. While not introducing new technical arguments, it reinforces the consensus direction toward a custom format rather than SQLite or pack index adaptations, while gently proposing to leverage existing Git infrastructure if possible. The tone suggests this is more of a supportive "me too" than a substantive new proposal at this stage.
