Policy discussion on Rust transition timelines and platform support commitments.

Phillip Wood proposes concrete language for announcing Git's Rust transition, focusing on three key elements: a 6-month optional period before making Rust mandatory, default Rust enablement to surface platform issues early, and a 3-year security update commitment for the last Rust-optional version. The proposal aims to give distributors time to adapt while limiting long-term maintenance burdens.

Wood's suggested announcement text serves as a starting point for formalizing the transition plan, addressing concerns raised earlier in the thread about adequate notice and platform support. The structured timeline balances immediate adoption (with Rust enabled by default) against practical constraints for platforms without Rust toolchains. The three-year security update window appears designed to satisfy conservative stakeholders while preventing indefinite support obligations.

This email advances the policy discussion by moving from abstract concerns to concrete proposal language, though it doesn't yet resolve the debate between Wood/Steinhardt's distributor-focused timeline concerns and Newren's position that existing optional availability in v2.49.0 constitutes sufficient notice. The security update commitment aspect directly responds to earlier concerns from Sam James and others about non-Rust platform viability.
