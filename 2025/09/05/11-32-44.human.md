This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, focusing on transition planning and distribution concerns. Sam James (Gentoo) strongly supports Phillip Wood's arguments about the need for clearer communication and transition planning for Rust adoption in Git.

The email reinforces Wood's position that past discussions about Rust were inconclusive and didn't clearly signal mandatory adoption to the broader ecosystem. James adds specific distribution perspective points: optional bindings are treated differently than core requirements, Rust's lack of system-wide library support makes bindings currently impractical, and the current contrib/ location and Meson build system integration gaps further reduce visibility.

Key concerns raised:
- The difference between mailing list discussions and formal release note announcements
- The practical implications for distributions between optional bindings and mandatory Rust requirements
- The need for clear security update commitments for non-Rust platforms
- The current implementation's limitations (location in contrib/, missing Meson support) that make it invisible to many packagers

The email shows growing alignment among distribution maintainers (Gentoo and Debian perspectives represented) that formal transition planning with clear ecosystem communication is needed before any mandatory Rust adoption. This continues the thread's shift from technical implementation debates to operational and policy concerns from the packaging/distribution perspective.
