This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure debate, specifically addressing the transition period and notice requirements for making Rust mandatory in Git builds.

Phillip Wood weighs in on the "advance notice" discussion between Elijah Newren and Patrick Steinhardt, arguing that while Rust adoption has been discussed for years, the mailing list conversations haven't clearly signaled an intent to make Rust mandatory rather than just optional. He distinguishes between the enthusiastic technical discussions and what would constitute proper notice to the broader ecosystem, particularly for platform maintainers who may not closely follow mailing list debates.

Wood questions Newren's characterization of an "existing transition period," noting that having optional Rust bindings (libgit-rs) is fundamentally different from requiring Rust to build core Git. He expresses support for Rust adoption but emphasizes the project should provide concrete notice in release notes once plans solidify, including clear commitments about security updates for platforms without Rust support.

The email advances the policy discussion by bringing a maintainer's perspective on practical transition concerns, particularly around platform support timelines and the difference between technical enthusiasm and formal project policy signaling. It doesn't introduce new technical elements but reinforces Steinhardt's position about the need for clearer ecosystem communication.
