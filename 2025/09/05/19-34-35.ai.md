This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, focusing on platform support and LTS considerations. Brian m. carlson responds to Eli Schwartz's concerns about Rust/LLVM platform compatibility by proposing alternative approaches and questioning assumptions about gcc-rs viability.

The email makes several key points about platform support strategies: First, it suggests that distributions can maintain out-of-tree LLVM/Rust patches as a fallback, noting this is common practice despite being suboptimal. It highlights LLVM's broader utility beyond just Rust compilation (clangd, Doxygen, Mesa, PostgreSQL) as motivation for platform maintainers. Regarding gcc-rs, while supportive of the project, carlson expresses skepticism that it would solve Rust upstream's resistance to platform-specific defines, noting libstd/libcore support is equally critical.

On LTS policy, carlson endorses a 2-year time limit rather than gcc-rs readiness as the determining factor, citing uncertainty about gcc-rs's future. They also propose investigating mrustc as a potentially simpler bootstrap solution. The email concludes by noting alternative Git implementations (Game of Trees, libgit2, Dulwich) could fill the gap for platforms unable to adopt Rust, though suggests this may be more problematic for those platforms than for Git itself.

This contributes to the thread's core policy debates by:
1) Providing concrete technical alternatives to gcc-rs dependency
2) Questioning assumptions about Rust upstream's flexibility
3) Endorsing time-bound LTS with specific duration (2 years)
4) Introducing mrustc as a new variable in the portability equation
5) Cataloguing fallback options for non-Rust platforms

The response maintains the thread's technical depth while shifting focus from ideal solutions to pragmatic fallbacks, reflecting carlson's experience with SHA-256 transition challenges. It notably avoids taking sides in the Git 3.0 timing debate while advancing the LTS discussion with specific duration proposals and alternative implementation paths.
