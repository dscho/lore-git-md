This is a design discussion about the ongoing refactoring of commit graph handling as part of the pluggable ODB effort. Derrick Stolee weighs in on the architectural debate between Junio Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt about whether commit graphs should be repository-wide (Hamano argues) or source-specific (Steinhardt's series implements).

Stolee makes several key observations: commit graphs act as a structured cache that's conceptually repository-level despite being stored in object stores, currently follow a "first one wins" loading pattern, and are used in contexts where the source ODB isn't predictable. He supports most of Steinhardt's technical cleanups in patches 1-5 but agrees with Hamano that the graph state should remain at the object_database level rather than moving to odb_source.

The email advances the thread by providing concrete technical rationale for the repository-wide view while acknowledging the value in parts of the series. It leaves the core design question unresolved but helps frame the tradeoffs more clearly. The discussion remains at an architectural level with no immediate resolution in sight.
