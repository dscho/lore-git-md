This is a policy discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series, specifically addressing the long-term support (LTS) strategy for platforms that cannot support Rust compilation.

Phillip Wood responds to Matthias Aßhauer's concerns about the open-ended commitment to maintain a pre-Rust LTS version until alternative Rust compilers like gcc-rs become available. Phillip argues that maintaining security updates indefinitely for non-Rust platforms is unrealistic, as most security work comes from companies with little commercial interest in these niche systems. He proposes a fixed 2-3 year window instead, after which affected platforms would need to either port Rust or take over their own security maintenance.

The exchange highlights a key policy decision point in the Rust transition - how to balance backward compatibility with practical maintenance realities. Phillip's position reflects a pragmatic approach that acknowledges both the ecosystem's security needs and the limited resources available for legacy platform support. This discussion forms part of the broader timeline debate in the series, complementing Phillip's earlier proposal for a 6-month transition period.
