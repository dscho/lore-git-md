This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing the LTS (Long-Term Support) strategy for non-Rust platforms during the transition period.

Eli Schwartz responds to Patrick Steinhardt's proposal for a time-bound LTS period (8 release cycles, roughly 2 years) for the last non-Rust Git version. Eli expresses general agreement with this approach but emphasizes two key points: 1) The LTS branch should be officially hosted by the Git project itself rather than being relegated to external repositories, and 2) While dependent organizations should take responsibility for maintaining the branch after the initial support period, the project must commit to hosting it.

The email reinforces Phillip Wood's earlier arguments for a time-bound LTS policy rather than one tied to gcc-rs readiness, but adds the important institutional consideration of where the LTS branch will live. This addresses potential concerns about discoverability and legitimacy of externally-hosted maintenance forks. The response is brief but strategically important as it comes from a distribution maintainer (Gentoo) and supports the time-bound approach while ensuring the project's continued stewardship of official branches.

Key technical/policy points referenced:
- 8 release cycle (~2 year) LTS window proposal
- Maintenance handoff mechanism to dependent organizations
- Institutional hosting requirements for LTS branches
- Implicit rejection of gcc-rs-dependent LTS duration

This moves the LTS discussion forward by providing clear criteria for evaluating Patrick's counterproposal while maintaining alignment with Phillip Wood's platform support arguments. The emphasis on official project hosting may help address concerns about fragmentation while still allowing for reduced core team maintenance burden after the initial period.
