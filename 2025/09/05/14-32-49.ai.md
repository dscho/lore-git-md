This is a policy discussion email in the Rust infrastructure thread, focusing on platform support concerns and LTS duration debates. Eli Schwartz responds to Matthias Aßhauer's questions about platform compatibility and the gcc-rs dependency in the proposed transition plan.

Eli provides technical context about platform support challenges, explaining that while Rust/LLVM theoretically could support all platforms, political and technical hurdles exist in practice. He contrasts this with GCC-rs's broader platform support, noting its importance for platforms like those using non-standard architectures. The email strongly advocates for the time-bound LTS alternative, arguing that gcc-rs is unlikely to be abandoned given its backing by major projects like the Linux kernel and substantial funding.

Key technical points include:
- Platform support limitations in Rust/LLVM versus GCC-rs
- Security implications of forcing platforms to use unsupported versions
- Analysis of alternative VCS options for unsupported platforms
- Confidence in gcc-rs's future based on current development momentum

The email reinforces Phillip Wood's earlier arguments for a time-bound LTS policy rather than tying it to gcc-rs readiness, while providing new technical justification. It also highlights the security risks of prolonged LTS periods without updates. This continues the thread's central policy discussion about transition timelines and support guarantees, with Eli taking a strong position favoring predictable time-based commitments over feature-dependent ones.
