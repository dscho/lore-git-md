This is a policy discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing the LTS (Long-Term Support) strategy for non-Rust platforms during the transition to mandatory Rust compilation.

Eli Schwartz weighs in on Patrick Steinhardt's proposal for a time-bound (2 year) LTS period for the last non-Rust Git version, with maintenance potentially handed off to dependents after that period. Eli agrees with the general approach but emphasizes that the LTS branch should remain under the official Git project umbrella rather than being forked elsewhere, ensuring continued visibility and proper maintenance.

The email reinforces Phillip Wood's earlier arguments for a fixed-duration LTS window rather than tying support to the uncertain timeline of gcc-rs maturity. This continues the thread's evolution from technical implementation questions to policy decisions, with the LTS strategy being one of three key unresolved issues (alongside transition timeline and build defaults).

The tone is constructive, with Eli supporting Patrick's flexible approach while adding an important governance consideration about branch hosting. This moves the discussion forward while still awaiting maintainer input from Junio Hamano.
