This is a policy discussion email in the Rust infrastructure RFC series, specifically addressing concerns about the transition timeline and LTS strategy for making Rust mandatory in Git builds.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Matthias Aßhauer's questions about the Rust transition plan documentation (originally introduced in patch 5/7 of the series). The discussion clarifies two key aspects: platform support distinctions and the phased rollout timeline.

On platform support, Steinhardt distinguishes between platforms where Rust could theoretically work but isn't yet available (like Cygwin) versus those with no Rust support at all (like NonStop). He acknowledges the documentation needs clearer wording here.

For the transition timeline, Steinhardt proposes revised wording for the four phases, addressing Aßhauer's concerns about ambiguous "mandatory but opt-out" language in Phase 2 and unclear defaults in Phase 3. The new version makes the progression clearer: from initial detection (2.52), to breaking-changes default (2.53), to universal default (2.54), to mandatory (3.0).

The most substantive discussion concerns the LTS policy for the pre-Rust-mandatory version. Steinhardt suggests two alternatives to the original gccrs-dependent approach: either an 8-release-cycle (~2 year) time-based commitment, or handing maintenance to dependents after that period. He notes this awaits Junio Hamano's input.

The email shows productive discussion refining the policy aspects of the Rust transition, with Steinhardt responsive to documentation clarity issues and open to adjusting the LTS strategy. The technical implementation remains unchanged from the original series, but the policy details are being carefully considered based on community feedback.
