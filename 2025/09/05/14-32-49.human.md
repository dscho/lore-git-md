Feature proposal discussion about Rust transition timeline and platform support. This is responding to concerns about the long-term support (LTS) policy for non-Rust platforms in Patrick Steinhardt's RFC series introducing Rust to Git.

Eli Schwartz weighs in on two key points from Matthias Aßhauer's earlier questions about the BreakingChanges document. First, they explain why some platforms struggle with Rust adoption - it's not just about technical feasibility but also political hurdles in getting platforms recognized by the Rust project. They contrast this with GCC-rs (an alternative Rust backend), which has broader platform support and more welcoming maintainers.

Second, regarding the LTS duration tied to GCC-rs readiness, Eli argues strongly against setting a time limit, noting that abandoning non-Rust platforms would leave them without any viable DVCS options. They provide a detailed rationale for why GCC-rs is unlikely to be abandoned, citing significant investment, corporate backing, and Linux kernel needs. The response suggests the Git project should maintain the GCC-rs-dependent LTS approach rather than imposing an arbitrary time limit.

This email advances the policy discussion by providing concrete arguments from a distribution/maintainer perspective about why platform support matters and why the GCC-rs path is more viable than some might assume. It doesn't introduce new technical changes but strengthens the case for Patrick's original LTS proposal against Phillip Wood's time-bound alternative.
