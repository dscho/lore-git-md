Policy discussion follow-up in the Rust infrastructure series, focusing on LTS support duration for non-Rust platforms. Phillip Wood responds to Matthias Aßhauer's concerns about the open-ended commitment to support pre-Rust Git versions until gcc-rs becomes viable. Wood argues the current language is unrealistic, proposing instead a fixed 2-3 year LTS window for platforms without Rust support.

Key technical points raised:
- Current proposal ties LTS duration to gcc-rs readiness (seen as problematic)
- Alternative suggested: time-bound LTS period (2-3 years)
- Practical considerations around security update maintenance for niche platforms
- Corporate contributor incentives for maintaining non-Rust support

The email advances the policy discussion by introducing concrete parameters for the LTS period, moving away from the gcc-rs-dependent proposal. Wood's argument centers on maintainability realities, noting that corporate contributors are unlikely to indefinitely support platforms without Rust. This follows his earlier timeline proposal (6-month transition vs Git 3.0 target) and continues the pattern of advocating for more bounded, predictable policy decisions.

The exchange highlights an important subsystem consideration: how to handle platforms that cannot or will not adopt Rust toolchains. Wood's position suggests treating this as a temporary migration challenge rather than a permanent compatibility requirement, with clear expectations set for platform maintainers. This policy question remains one of the last major unresolved items in the otherwise technically complete Rust infrastructure series.
