Phillip Wood responds to Gabriel Scherer's proposal for a `--detach-other-worktrees` option in the ongoing discussion about branch conflicts across worktrees. This is part of the series improving worktree behavior when branches are checked out in multiple locations.

Phillip expresses reservations about the proposed `--detach-other-worktrees` approach, noting that Git generally avoids having commands in one worktree affect another. He reiterates the core concern about having the same branch checked out in multiple worktrees, suggesting that simply advising `--detach` might be more acceptable than either the `--ignore-other-worktrees` or the new detach proposal.

The email suggests an alternative approach where more detailed discussion could be moved to documentation, with hints simply pointing users there rather than trying to concisely explain the tradeoffs in an error message. This maintains the thread's focus on finding the right balance between safety and usability in multi-worktree operations, while avoiding solutions that might violate Git's principle of worktree isolation.

Key technical points:
- Rejects `--detach-other-worktrees` due to cross-worktree effects
- Prefers simpler `--detach` advice over complex solutions
- Suggests moving detailed discussion to documentation
- Maintains concern about multiple branch checkouts being fundamentally problematic

The response continues the thread's central tension between safety and flexibility, while pushing back against solutions that would modify other worktrees' states. It suggests documentation as a potential path forward for explaining the risks while keeping the interface simple.
