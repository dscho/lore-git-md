This RFC thread continues to explore the implications of making Rust mandatory in Git, with the discussion now focusing on distribution packaging concerns. The thread originated with technical objections around platform compatibility (NonStop, PowerPC variants) and has evolved to include broader systems administration considerations.

brian m. carlson has been advocating for Rust's adoption, emphasizing its safety benefits in preventing memory-related bugs, while distribution maintainers like Sam James (Gentoo) and Michael Orlitzky have raised concerns about build system impacts and packaging stability. The latest exchange shows some progress: carlson's commitment to target Debian-stable crate versions addresses Gentoo's primary concern about dependency churn, though James notes this will require Gentoo to adapt its packaging philosophy. The mrustc bootstrap compiler has been proposed as a partial solution for platforms lacking Rust support.

However, significant open questions remain unresolved. Platform support gaps persist for NonStop and PowerPC architectures, and there's been no clear path forward for niche builds like riscv/musl. The discussion has revealed a fundamental tension between Rust's safety advantages and the practical challenges it introduces for distribution maintainers and platform porters. While the thread shows movement toward consensus on dependency management policies, the broader questions about Git's platform support matrix and long-term maintenance costs remain active concerns.
