The discussion around making Rust mandatory in Git continues, focusing on the practical challenges for distribution maintainers. The thread began as an RFC proposing Rust adoption, with brian m. carlson advocating for its safety benefits while addressing concerns from various distributions and platforms. Gentoo maintainers Michael Orlitzky and Sam James have been key participants, highlighting how Rust's requirements would impact their packaging workflows and platform support.

In the latest exchange, Orlitzky responds to carlson's technical arguments by drawing parallels to Gentoo's experience with Haskell's GHC compiler, using the pandoc package as a concrete example. He points out that while Rust offers clear technical advantages like memory safety, its adoption would significantly reduce Git's availability across Gentoo's supported architectures - currently 23 combinations, compared to pandoc's limited support on just 5 glibc platforms. Orlitzky, who has firsthand experience bootstrapping GHC on riscv/musl, warns that Rust would impose similar constraints.

The core tension remains unresolved between Rust's safety benefits and its ecosystem impact on niche platforms and source-based distributions. While there's growing acceptance of Rust's inevitability, particularly regarding dependency management approaches, significant concerns persist about platform support gaps (NonStop, PowerPC variants, riscv/musl) and the practical challenges for distribution maintainers. The discussion has evolved from purely technical compatibility to include broader systems administration and policy considerations, with Git's centrality in Linux distributions amplifying the stakes of any change.
