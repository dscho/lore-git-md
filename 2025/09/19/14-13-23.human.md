This is a discussion about branch safety in multi-worktree operations, continuing the thread on Gabriel Scherer's patch series. Phillip Wood responds to Gabriel's proposal for a new `--detach-other-worktrees` option that would automatically detach branches in other worktrees during checkout.

Phillip expresses reservations about having commands in one worktree affect another's state, which goes against Git's general design principles. He suggests the team remains uncomfortable with the core concept of having the same branch checked out in multiple worktrees. Instead of Gabriel's proposed solution, Phillip thinks suggesting `--detach` in the advice message would be less controversial, while any mention of `--ignore-other-worktrees` would need strong warnings.

The exchange highlights the ongoing tension between workflow flexibility and safety guarantees in Git's multi-worktree model. Phillip's preference leans toward clearer documentation of the risks rather than introducing new commands that cross worktree boundaries. This maintains the thread's central debate about how to balance user convenience with protection against potential data loss scenarios.
