Feature proposal discussion with strong philosophical pushback on semantic conflict handling during rebase.

Elijah Newren engages with Antonio Mennillo's `git-rebase-clean` proposal, raising fundamental concerns about the tool's approach to handling semantic conflicts during rebase operations. While acknowledging the technical innovation in v2.0.0's content-agnostic diff/apply mechanism, Newren questions whether the core premise of resolving conflicts in a squashed state then reconstructing commits can work reliably across various conflict types.

The email identifies several technical edge cases that challenge the tool's methodology: overlapping line conflicts, non-content conflicts (file modes, renames), intermediate-state conflicts that disappear in final merge, and the classic "change-and-revert" scenario. Newren suggests the approach may effectively randomize commit content assignment when reconstructing history after squashed resolution.

Philosophically, Newren strongly disagrees with the premise that atomic commits are unachievable, countering each of Antonio's justifications (junior developers, emergency fixes, legacy codebases). He advocates for teaching proper commit practices rather than accommodating fragmented history, drawing parallels to his well-known opposition to GitHub's squash-merge workflow. The response frames this as a fundamental disagreement about Git's role in enforcing versus accommodating development practices.

Key technical references include Git's documentation on merge/rebase edge cases and specific conflict types the tool would need to handle. Newren maintains skepticism about whether semantic inconsistency in reconstructed history is an acceptable tradeoff, even while acknowledging the tool's potential to reduce conflict resolution burden. The email leaves open whether these concerns represent fundamental limitations or implementation challenges that could be addressed in future versions.
