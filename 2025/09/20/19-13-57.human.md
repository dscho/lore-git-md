This is a technical discussion about the `git-rebase-clean` tool's approach to handling semantic conflicts during rebase. Elijah Newren raises several pointed questions about the tool's ability to handle complex commit histories and edge cases.

Newren expresses skepticism about the tool's core premise of resolving conflicts once in a squashed state and then reconstructing the original commit structure. He identifies several problematic scenarios: overlapping conflicts in different commits, non-content conflicts (file modes, renames), intermediate-state conflicts that disappear in the final merge, and cases where the squashed resolution contradicts the logical commit sequence. He argues these cases either make the approach unworkable or result in history that preserves granularity but loses logical meaning.

The email also challenges the tool's philosophical justification. Newren strongly disagrees with characterizing atomic commits as unachievable, arguing instead that they are especially valuable in legacy codebases and that junior developers should be trained to create proper commits rather than accommodated. He draws parallels to his long-standing criticism of GitHub's squash-merge workflow.

While acknowledging the bias in his position, Newren frames his critique as identifying real technical limitations the tool would need to address. The tone remains constructive despite the substantive disagreement, inviting the author to explain how their approach might overcome these challenges. The discussion continues to highlight the tension between Git's design philosophy and practical development constraints.
