This is a discussion about the rollout strategy for Rust integration in Git, specifically addressing concerns about platform support and testing. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Phillip Wood's suggestion to make Rust support enabled by default from the start rather than opt-in.

Steinhardt acknowledges Windows support is currently untested and proposes a compromise: keep Rust disabled by default initially but enable it in the next release (rather than waiting for the breaking_changes flag). He notes that while Windows support patches exist (from Ezekiel Newren's series), he wants to focus first on agreeing to the overall roadmap before implementing platform-specific support.

Wood expresses concern about getting sufficient testing with an opt-in approach but accepts the compromise if Windows support truly can't be ready in time. He questions why Windows support is progressing slowly given existing patches and notes uncertainty about the next release timing. Steinhardt clarifies that implementing Windows support isn't technically difficult but wants to prioritize establishing consensus on the roadmap first, viewing this as a multi-year effort where rushing the opt-out transition isn't necessary.

The exchange shows Steinhardt maintaining a measured approach to the rollout while being responsive to testing concerns, with both parties finding middle ground on the timeline while acknowledging remaining uncertainties about platform support and release scheduling.
