This thread discusses an unexpected behavior in `git add` where including any invalid pathspec (like "bogus_file") alongside valid ones causes the entire command to fail without staging the valid files. Jon Forrest initially reported this as potentially surprising, and K Jayatheerth confirmed it's intentional by design, pointing to the PATHSPEC_PREFER_FULL flag in builtin/add.c that enforces this strict checking.

Jeff King provided important historical context, tracing this behavior back to Git's early days (2006) and even its predecessor cogito. While cogito originally behaved as Jon expected (ignoring invalid pathspecs), this was considered a bug at the time. Jeff highlighted several reasons the current behavior persists: technical challenges with exit codes for partial success/failure, Git's conservative philosophy of avoiding ambiguous operations, and the risk of changing long-established behavior. 

The discussion remains open, but the consensus so far suggests this behavior is unlikely to change despite differing user expectations. The core tension is between Git's historical design philosophy (favoring explicit failure over partial success) and some users' expectations for more permissive handling of mixed valid/invalid pathspecs. No concrete proposals for change have been made yet.
