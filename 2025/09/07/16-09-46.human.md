Policy discussion on Rust transition's impact on security updates and LTS commitments.

Randall Becker raises critical concerns about how the proposed Rust transition could impact security updates and long-term support (LTS) for Git, particularly for enterprise users and distributors. The key points:

1. **Security Update Concerns**: Becker highlights a real-world scenario where a CVE in Git 2.51.0 would leave organizations unable to upgrade if they can't build newer Rust-dependent versions, creating compliance risks for security-conscious environments.

2. **LTS Policy Ambiguity**: He notes conflicting LTS duration proposals (6 months to 5 years) and the lack of clear Git team commitments for backporting security fixes to older versions, which could force distributors to maintain their own forks.

3. **Enterprise Impact**: The email emphasizes how platform-specific certification requirements (especially for NonStop systems) and third-party audit constraints could lead to reduced Git adoption if the codebase diverges due to Rust integration.

4. **Distributor Burden**: Becker stresses that without clear support commitments from the Git project, distributors may face increased costs and management challenges in justifying continued participation.

This represents a significant escalation of Patrick Steinhardt's earlier concerns, moving from theoretical transition issues to concrete security and compliance risks that could affect enterprise adoption. The response challenges core assumptions about version support policies and highlights potential unintended consequences of the Rust transition that weren't fully addressed in previous discussions.
