This is a follow-up in the ongoing thread about managing inode exhaustion on NFS servers hosting many small Git repositories. Kousik Sanagavarapu responds to Brian M. Carlson's performance analysis of their proposed GC settings.

The exchange focuses on two key configuration parameters:
1. `transfer.unpackLimit=1` - Confirmed as safe and performant, with Brian noting major forges use this successfully due to packed object optimizations like bitmaps.
2. GC auto-repack settings - Kousik had initially set `gc.auto=1` with `autopacklimit=1`, which Brian warns would trigger expensive repacks after every push. He shares a real-world benchmark from their workplace where repacking every ~40 pushes strikes a better balance between inode conservation and performance.

Kousik acknowledges the feedback and indicates they will experiment with higher repack intervals. The discussion continues to refine the operational tradeoffs between inode reduction (needing frequent GC) and system performance (needing less frequent repacks), with Brian providing concrete implementation experience to guide the tuning. The thread remains focused on practical configuration adjustments within the constraints of an ext4/NFS environment handling high push volumes to small repositories.
