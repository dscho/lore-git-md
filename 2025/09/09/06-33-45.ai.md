Feature and contributor process discussion in the Rust integration thread.

Elijah Newren raises concerns about both the technical rollout strategy and the contributor experience in this response to Patrick Steinhardt's Rust integration plan. On the technical side, Newren argues that making Rust optional in early releases (Git 2.52-2.53) fails to adequately prepare distributors for the eventual mandatory requirement, suggesting instead that Rust should be enabled by default immediately with an opt-out option to maximize awareness. He sees the current phased approach as potentially leaving distributors unprepared when the switch becomes mandatory.

More significantly, Newren critiques the project dynamics around this work, pointing to a pattern where new contributors' Rust-related patches have been displaced by competing series from established maintainers without sufficient feedback or collaboration. He cites two specific instances where contributor work was effectively sidelined, including one case where platform compatibility work done with git-for-windows help was discarded. Newren frames this as creating a potentially discouraging environment for new participants, even if unintentionally.

The email highlights tensions between technical rollout strategy (how best to transition to mandatory Rust) and project process (how to integrate new contributors working in this space). Newren's critique suggests the current approach may be optimizing for technical caution at the expense of contributor experience and distributor awareness. The response maintains the thread's focus on Rust integration while surfacing important process considerations that could impact both the technical implementation and community dynamics around this major change.
