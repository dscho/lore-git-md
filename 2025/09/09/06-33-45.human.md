This is a discussion about the Rust integration timeline and contributor dynamics in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series. Elijah Newren responds to Patrick Steinhardt's proposed phased rollout plan (auto-detection in 2.52, default-enabled with breaking changes in 2.53, mandatory in 2.54) with two main critiques.

First, Newren argues the gradual rollout doesn't serve the stated goal of giving distributors early warning about mandatory Rust, suggesting instead making Rust default-enabled immediately in 2.52 with an opt-out option. He sees the current plan as functionally equivalent to existing optional Rust bindings (libgit-rs/libgit-sys) that haven't raised awareness.

Second, Newren expresses concern about project dynamics, noting a new contributor's Rust work was effectively sidelined twice - first by competing patches in their initial area, then by this series replacing their platform compatibility work. While acknowledging this likely wasn't intentional, he warns the pattern could discourage contributors.

The exchange reveals tension between technical rollout strategy and community management. Newren's critique suggests the current approach may both delay necessary ecosystem preparation and inadvertently signal unwelcoming project norms, despite the technical merits of a cautious rollout. The thread continues wrestling with how to balance technical preparation, ecosystem communication, and contributor experience in this major transition.
