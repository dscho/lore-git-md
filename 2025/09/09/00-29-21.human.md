This is a performance tuning discussion in the ongoing thread about managing inode exhaustion on NFS servers hosting many small Git repositories. Brian M. Carlson responds to Kousik Sanagavarapu's configuration changes for garbage collection.

The exchange focuses on the tradeoffs of aggressive GC settings. Kousik has implemented `receive.autogc=true` with `gc.auto=1` and `gc.autopacklimit=1`, which triggers a repack after every push. Brian notes this may be too frequent, sharing that their workplace repacks only after about 40 pushes to balance performance with storage efficiency.

Brian confirms that `transfer.unpackLimit=1` (previously discussed in the thread) is widely used setting that shouldn't cause performance issues, as packed objects can leverage optimizations like bitmaps. The key insight is that while frequent repacking helps with inode conservation, it comes at a CPU cost that may not be justified for high-push-volume environments.

This moves the discussion forward by providing real-world benchmarks (40 pushes per repack) and validating one of the earlier proposed configurations. The thread continues to refine the balance between storage efficiency and operational performance under production constraints.
