This is a technical clarification in the Rust adoption thread, specifically addressing NonStop platform constraints. Randall Becker responds to Ezekiel Newren's suggestion about cross-compiled Git binaries by explaining why Windows-based compilation isn't viable for NonStop production environments.

The email provides two key reasons why cross-compilation from Windows is insufficient: security/audit requirements (NonStop provides a trusted, malware-free build environment that Windows cannot match) and production control limitations (Windows builds only cover development aspects, missing critical production artifact management capabilities). Becker notes some customers outright prohibit NonStop builds from external platforms.

This reinforces the thread's conclusion that Rust cannot be made mandatory due to NonStop's technical constraints, as even cross-compilation workarounds fail to meet the platform's operational requirements. The response effectively closes the last potential avenue for Rust support on NonStop by highlighting real-world deployment constraints beyond just compiler availability.
