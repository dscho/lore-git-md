This is a concluding email in the Rust-on-NonStop discussion, reinforcing the systemic barriers that prevent Rust from becoming mandatory on NonStop platforms. Randall Becker (the NonStop maintainer) responds to Ezekiel Newren's summary of the "chicken-and-egg" problem, clarifying that while there is internal momentum for Rust support, the technical and organizational timelines simply don't align with Git's Rustification plans.

Key points: Becker confirms that NonStop does actively use and support Git (contrary to some assumptions), but the Rust toolchain situation remains blocked by vendor timelines and technical constraints. He emphasizes this isn't a policy issue but a hard reality of platform development cycles. The email closes with Becker's personal commitment to keep Git viable on NonStop despite these constraints.

This effectively ends the technical debate, as all possible solutions (native builds, cross-compilation, Windows/Linux-based workflows) have now been ruled out. The thread leaves Git maintainers with a clear but difficult choice: either make Rust optional or accept that NonStop support will break when Rust becomes mandatory. Becker's tone suggests this isn't theoretical - he's already fielding pressure from users who will be impacted.
