This is a technical response in the ongoing discussion about Rust adoption challenges on NonStop platforms. Randall Becker replies to Ezekiel Newren's inquiry about cross-compiler possibilities for NonStop systems.

The email clarifies that while NonStop has C99/C11/C17 compilers, the only Windows cross-compiler available is C99-based and requires proprietary licensing from HPE that Becker cannot provide. He also notes there are no non-commercial cross-compilers available for NonStop, and that standard configure processes don't work for Windows-to-NonStop compilation.

This response effectively closes off another potential path for Rust adoption on NonStop systems, as it indicates there are no viable cross-compiler solutions that could bridge the gap between NonStop's constrained C++17 environment and Rust's requirements. The technical constraints appear increasingly insurmountable, which may force the Git project to reconsider either mandatory Rust adoption timelines or make Rust support optional for platforms like NonStop.

The exchange demonstrates the real-world challenges of platform compatibility in open source projects, particularly when dealing with proprietary systems with limited toolchain availability. Becker's response maintains the thread's technical focus while providing definitive information that will likely shape the policy discussion to follow.
