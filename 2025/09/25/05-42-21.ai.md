This is a licensing policy discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing concerns around GitOxide compatibility and derivative works.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King's clarification about the proposed GPLv2+exception licensing approach, agreeing that any solution should be project-agnostic rather than GitOxide-specific. He suggests LGPL or GPL with linking exception as viable alternatives, though acknowledges he lacks expertise to evaluate Phillip Wood's operational concerns about license tracking and laundering risks. The email notably proposes involving the Software Freedom Conservancy (SFC) for legal guidance, marking the first concrete suggestion to seek external legal counsel in this thread.

The discussion remains at the policy level, with no technical implementation changes proposed. Key unresolved questions include how to prevent license laundering through intermediary projects (GitOxide or otherwise) and whether a general permissive license would adequately address both enterprise compliance needs and copyleft integrity concerns. Steinhardt's SFC suggestion may provide a path forward for resolving the legal impasse that has been blocking progress on Rust infrastructure adoption.
