Adrian Ratiu and Emily Shaffer's 12-part series refactoring Git's hook subsystem has reached completion with the conversion of receive-pack's receive hooks to the new `hook.h` API. This final patch standardizes the last major hook type, bringing all Git hooks under a unified callback-based infrastructure while maintaining backward compatibility.

The series introduces structured stdin handling, output capture, and parallel execution capabilities through a carefully designed API. Key improvements include batched processing of ref updates (500 lines per callback) to maintain performance with large inputs, preserved sideband output capability, and proper handling of push certificates and options. The implementation replaces direct `child_process` management and manual SIGPIPE toggling with safer, more maintainable abstractions.

The work has undergone extensive review, with sign-offs from Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason and co-author Emily Shaffer on critical patches. Maintainer feedback from Junio Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt has been incorporated throughout the iterations, particularly around API design and memory safety. All CI tests have passed through multiple versions, confirming the stability of these changes.

This series represents a significant modernization of Git's hook subsystem, providing a foundation for future enhancements while ensuring existing hook implementations continue to work as expected. The completion of receive-pack hook conversion marks the successful standardization of all major Git hooks onto the new infrastructure.
