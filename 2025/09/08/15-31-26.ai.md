This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing enterprise impact concerns around version support and CVE backports. Elijah Newren responds to Randall Becker's concerns about NonStop platform certification and enterprise adoption risks.

Newren proposes a model where Junio could officially designate maintainers for a Rust-optional LTS branch, similar to how Linux handles stable releases. This would allow enterprise platforms (particularly NonStop) to receive security updates without requiring every distribution to maintain their own fork. The key technical point is maintaining first-party status for backported fixes to satisfy auditor requirements.

The email highlights two distinct enterprise concerns: NonStop platform's certification needs (where divergent codebases historically hurt adoption) versus general distribution practices (where version lag is already common). Newren's suggestion attempts to address the former while noting the latter may not require policy changes.

This continues the thread's bifurcation between NonStop's special-case requirements and broader distribution practices. The proposal introduces a new technical governance angle (official LTS maintainer designation) while maintaining the existing technical implementation path for the Rust transition.
