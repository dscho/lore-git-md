This is a policy discussion update in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, focusing on enterprise compliance requirements for LTS branches. Randall Becker responds to Elijah Newren's suggestion of designated LTS maintainers by detailing the regulatory constraints faced by enterprise users in finance and healthcare sectors.

Becker emphasizes that enterprise auditors require security fixes to come from official, recognized sources - not just designated maintainers. He explains that compliance frameworks like PCI, SWIFT, and HIPAA mandate fixes be applied through channels they can validate, which currently means the main Git repository. The distinction between Linux's model and Git's needs is highlighted, with Becker noting that regulatory requirements are outside contributors' control but critically impact adoption.

The email reinforces earlier points about enterprise adoption being tied to official backport channels, with Becker warning that without this, organizations are already exploring Git alternatives. This adds concrete regulatory weight to the case for repository-hosted LTS branches, while also revealing the inflexibility of compliance frameworks that govern enterprise software deployment.

Key technical constraints mentioned:
- Auditors reject third-party backports regardless of maintainer status
- Compliance requirements are non-negotiable for regulated industries
- Current enterprise adoption was enabled by unified codebase approach
- Open questions this addresses:
  - Official LTS branch requirements (#53)
  - Enterprise audit validation (#54)
  - Platform-specific vs general policies (#9/14)

The response shows enterprise needs may require stricter LTS policies than Linux's model, with compliance frameworks effectively mandating repository-hosted backports. Becker's participation remains contingent on establishing auditor-approved channels, making this a make-or-break issue for certain enterprise users.
