Feature implementation discussion in the Rust infrastructure series, focusing on the varint subsystem conversion and transition timeline debate.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's critique of calling the varint conversion a "test balloon", clarifying that while the terminology may not perfectly fit, the intent is to establish Rust infrastructure through a small, self-contained subsystem. He argues this approach allows early iteration on tooling and gives downstream packagers time to test the infrastructure before mandatory adoption. The varint conversion serves as a practical starting point while waiting for more substantial optional features to emerge.

The discussion then shifts to transition timelines, with Patrick acknowledging Junio's concern that 6 months is too short for mandatory Rust adoption. He outlines his proposed timeline of roughly one year leading to Git 3.0, with gradual steps (auto-detection, then default-on) before mandatory status. Patrick suggests committing to 1.5 years of security fixes for the C-only path, though he notes the exact duration remains open for discussion. This continues the broader thread debate between Patrick's phased approach and others' proposals for either immediate default-on (Elijah Newren) or longer transition periods.

Key technical points include:
- The varint conversion's role as infrastructure validation rather than true "test balloon"
- macOS meson.build limitations still in place
- Makefile dependency tracking and error handling improvements still needed
- Windows support explicitly deferred to future work
- Transition documentation being refined to clarify LTS hosting commitments

The email demonstrates ongoing refinement of both technical implementation details and policy positions, with Patrick maintaining his phased transition strategy while showing flexibility on exact timelines and security support duration. The varint subsystem remains positioned as a practical starting point despite Junio's preference for optional feature development.
