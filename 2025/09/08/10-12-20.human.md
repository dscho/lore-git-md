This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust transition thread, specifically addressing enterprise impact concerns raised by Randall Becker. Phillip Wood responds to Becker's worries about CVE backports and long-term support for non-Rust Git versions on platforms like NonStop that may not get Rust support.

The email clarifies that the Git project is still discussing support timelines (with proposals ranging from 6 months to 3 years) but notes nobody has volunteered to provide 5 years of support as Becker suggested. Wood questions whether Becker's company contributes developer time to Git maintenance while expecting free long-term security updates from the community.

Key points raised:
- The practical reality that platforms without Rust support will likely need to find alternatives
- A suggestion to host community-maintained LTS branches in the main repository as a possible compromise
- Acknowledgement that Rust adoption will be disruptive for some users, but the community must weigh these costs against Rust's benefits

The tone is sympathetic but firm, recognizing the real business impacts while maintaining that the project cannot indefinitely support all legacy platforms. This continues the thread's central tension between modernization and backward compatibility, with Wood representing the project's pragmatic perspective.
