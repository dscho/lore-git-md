This is a technical discussion about the ongoing refactoring of commit graph handling as part of the pluggable ODB effort. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Derrick Stolee's concerns about moving commit graph tracking from object_database to odb_source.

Steinhardt argues that commit graphs are fundamentally tied to specific storage formats, not the repository as a whole. He presents scenarios where alternative storage backends (like databases) might handle graph information more efficiently without needing separate commit-graph files. The key point is that this refactoring aims to let each storage backend decide how to provide graph information, rather than assuming a one-size-fits-all commit-graph file approach.

The discussion highlights a fundamental architectural tension: whether commit graphs are repository-wide infrastructure (as Stolee and Junio Hamano suggest) or storage-format-specific optimizations (as Steinhardt maintains). Steinhardt clarifies that his series isn't about loading multiple graphs, but about enabling different storage backends to provide graph information in their own ways.

This email advances the thread by providing concrete examples of why storage-specific graph handling might be preferable, though it doesn't yet resolve the core disagreement about the conceptual role of commit graphs in Git's architecture. The debate remains open, with both sides making reasonable technical arguments from different perspectives.
