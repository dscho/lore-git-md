This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing security backport policies and transition timelines for the xdiff Rust port. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Elijah Newren's position on handling security fixes during the C-to-Rust transition.

The email focuses on two key concerns: 1) the practical implications of maintaining security fixes for both C and Rust versions during transition, and 2) the notice period for distributors who may be stuck on older versions. Steinhardt clarifies that his concern isn't about requiring the latest version, but rather about distributors who may be completely blocked from building any Rust-enabled version for years. He proposes a compromise of providing LTS window for the last C version before transferring maintenance to the community.

New technical details emerge about the current Rust test infrastructure (libgit-rs/libgit-sys in contrib/) and its limited real-world usage, though Newren notes some historical adoption by Google and recent external contributions. The discussion converges on the need for a more effective "test balloon" approach than the current optional components, with Steinhardt outlining a gradual tightening of Rust defaults before Git 3.0.

The email highlights unresolved tensions around:
- Security backport policies for mixed C/Rust codebases
- Concrete LTS maintenance timelines (Steinhardt's X-year proposal)
- Notice mechanisms for distributors (beyond current contrib/ components)
- Gradual enforcement timeline leading to Git 3.0

This continues the thread's shift from technical implementation to policy formulation, with Steinhardt attempting to balance project evolution against enterprise/distributor realities while Newren maintains a more optimistic view of organic transition.
