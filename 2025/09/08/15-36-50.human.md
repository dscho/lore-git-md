This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, focusing on enterprise compliance requirements for LTS (Long-Term Support) branches. Randall Becker responds to Elijah Newren's suggestion about designated LTS maintainers by explaining the strict audit requirements in regulated industries like finance and healthcare.

Becker emphasizes that enterprise auditors require security fixes to come from official, recognized sources - not just any designated maintainers. He notes that compliance frameworks like PCI, SWIFT, and HIPAA mandate fixes be applied through channels they approve, which currently means the main project repository. This creates a hard requirement for the Git project to either host an official LTS branch or risk losing enterprise adoption.

The email reveals concrete business impacts: companies in Becker's community are already evaluating Git alternatives due to uncertainty around future CVE backport policies. He stresses that without official project support for backports, enterprise adoption could decline, reversing the gains made when NonStop achieved codebase parity. The response highlights the gap between typical open source maintenance models and the rigid validation requirements of regulated industries.

This exchange moves the discussion from theoretical policy to real-world constraints, showing how enterprise compliance needs may require structural changes to Git's maintenance approach. Becker's perspective as a platform maintainer dealing with these requirements daily adds weight to his warnings about adoption risks.
