This is a policy discussion about the Rust transition timeline in Git, specifically addressing when Rust should become mandatory. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Elijah Newren's push for immediate default-on Rust in Git 2.52, defending his more gradual approach.

The key points:
1. Steinhardt maintains his phased transition plan (auto-detection in 2.52, default-on with breaking changes in 2.53, mandatory in 2.54/3.0) as a way to build infrastructure incrementally and avoid exposing early issues to all users.
2. He argues this gives downstream more time to adapt than an immediate switch, while still providing visibility through the BreakingChanges document.
3. The discussion touches on Git 3.0 timing - Steinhardt suggests it could happen within a year (as 2.55 becoming 3.0) but acknowledges SHA-256 interop work as a key dependency.
4. While open to decoupling from 3.0, Steinhardt notes Junio Hamano's concern that 6 months is too short for mandatory Rust transition, making ~1 year more realistic.

The exchange highlights the tension between Newren's "rip the bandaid" approach and Steinhardt's gradual rollout, with the 3.0 release timing and SHA-256 work adding complexity to the timeline discussion. The technical merits of each position remain unresolved, though Steinhardt's response shows willingness to adjust timing while maintaining the phased approach's core benefits.
