This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing enterprise impact concerns raised by Randall Becker. Phillip Wood responds to Becker's points about CVE backport policies and long-term support (LTS) for non-Rust Git versions during the proposed Rust transition.

The email focuses on clarifying the project's position regarding security support for older versions when Rust becomes mandatory. Wood questions whether open-source projects typically offer 5-year LTS without commercial support contracts, noting that no one has volunteered to provide such extended maintenance. He acknowledges the disruptive impact on platforms like NonStop that lack Rust support but suggests the community must weigh Rust's benefits against these costs.

Key technical/policy points raised:
- The unresolved question of where to host any community-maintained LTS branch for non-Rust versions
- Implicit tension between enterprise certification requirements and third-party patches
- Reference to the earlier 6-month vs 3-year LTS debate, now with Becker advocating for 5 years
- The business reality that some organizations may need to switch from Git if Rust adoption creates unsupported divergence

Wood's response indicates the project is still determining what support promises it can realistically make, while recognizing the serious consequences for enterprise users who cannot adopt Rust. The email advances the thread by probing the practicalities of Becker's proposed 5-year LTS model and suggesting potential repository structures for community maintenance, but does not resolve the fundamental conflict between Rust adoption goals and enterprise platform constraints.
