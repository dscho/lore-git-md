This is a discussion about safety considerations for the `--ignore-other-worktrees` feature in Git's worktree functionality. Junio Hamano responds to Gabriel Scherer's defense of allowing branch updates across worktrees, raising serious concerns about data integrity.

The exchange focuses on real-world scenarios where users might want to bypass worktree safety checks. Scherer presents a use case where someone wants to update a branch in one worktree despite it being checked out in another inactive worktree. Hamano counters that detached HEAD operations would be safer in such cases, preventing potential loss of work from the other worktree.

Hamano emphasizes that the risks go beyond simple recoverable surprises - rebasing or force-pushing could make previous states unreachable, effectively losing work. He strongly advocates for detached HEAD usage when examining others' branches rather than moving branch refs that other worktrees depend on.

The discussion reveals a fundamental tension between convenience and safety in Git's worktree model. While Scherer argues for more flexibility in branch management across worktrees, Hamano maintains that the current conservative approach prevents subtle but serious data integrity issues that users might not immediately recognize. This debate directly informs ongoing decisions about how strongly to warn users (or prevent them entirely) from operations that could desynchronize worktrees.
