This is a technical discussion about shallow clone limitations in GitLab CI workflows, specifically around the `--shallow-exclude` feature.

The email continues the conversation about limitations of `--shallow-exclude` in GitLab's "Merged Result Pipelines" scenario. Philipp Hahn confirms Ben Knoble's understanding that `git rev-list master..topic` would normally identify the desired commits, but explains why this fails in GitLab's temporary merge topology. The temporary merge commit "X" causes `master..temp` to only include "X" itself rather than the full topic branch commits "A-B-C".

Philipp also identifies another blocker - using `--shallow-exclude=E` makes "A" become a root commit containing all files up to that point, when what's needed is for "E" to be the crafted commit while preserving "A-B-C" exactly. This appears to be a fundamental limitation of how shallow exclusion currently works with commit hashes.

The discussion explores Ben's suggestion of using blobless clones (`--filter=blob:none`) as an alternative approach. Philipp indicates this might be viable based on positive experiences with `--filter=tree:0` in other cases, though notes they haven't tested it yet for this specific scenario. The blobless clone approach would fetch more commits than strictly needed but avoid the shallow exclusion limitations while still being more efficient than a full clone.

Key technical points:
- The temporary merge topology in GitLab CI breaks branch-based exclusion
- Commit hash exclusion creates unwanted crafted root commits
- Blobless clones emerge as a potential workaround
- Performance tradeoffs between shallow and blobless clones remain to be evaluated

This email doesn't introduce new solutions but clarifies the technical constraints and shows openness to alternative approaches. The thread remains focused on finding practical solutions for CI workflows while acknowledging protocol limitations.
