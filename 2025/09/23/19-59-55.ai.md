Localization discussion follow-up regarding git-format-patch headers. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Eric Sunshine's historical context about the intentional translation of "Range-diff:" and "Interdiff:" headers in patch emails. The exchange clarifies that while the original 2018 implementation deliberately made these technical headers translatable, there's now consideration of whether they should be treated differently from user-facing messages.

Haugsbakk acknowledges Sunshine's point about potential non-English projects benefiting from localized headers, but maintains his concern that users might unintentionally send localized output to English-dominated mailing lists. He emphasizes that his inquiry was purely exploratory, with no intention to submit patches to change the current behavior given the lack of widespread complaints.

The thread now shows two perspectives: one valuing consistency in localization (including technical outputs) and another prioritizing English as the lingua franca for technical collaboration. Haugsbakk's closing remarks indicate he's satisfied with the explanation and doesn't plan further action, leaving the status quo intact unless others voice concerns. The discussion references Junio Hamano's past rejection of diffstat translation as a parallel case where technical outputs were kept English-only.
