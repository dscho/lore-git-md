This is a governance discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series, specifically about the BreakingChanges.adoc documentation that announces Rust becoming mandatory in Git 3.0.

Patrick Steinhardt proposes adding language to the BreakingChanges document that would make Rust's mandatory adoption contingent on evaluating downstream ecosystem impact and the maintenance burden of keeping it optional. He suggests this would parallel how other breaking changes (like reftable adoption) have been handled, giving distributors more visibility while maintaining flexibility.

Junio Hamano responds that while he assumed such flexibility was implicit, he has no objection to making it explicit in the document. He notes this approach should apply consistently to all breaking changes listed, not just Rust. The tone suggests this is a minor clarification rather than a substantive policy change, with both parties largely aligned on the principle of evaluating real-world impact before finalizing breaking changes.

The exchange shows the careful balancing act in this transition - wanting to signal clear direction while retaining the ability to adapt based on ecosystem feedback. This appears to be a refinement of the governance approach rather than reopening any major technical questions from the Rust infrastructure series.
