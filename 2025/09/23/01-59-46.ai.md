This is a governance clarification in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series, specifically addressing how policy decisions are incorporated into the technical work. Elijah Newren responds to Junio Hamano's inquiry about the provenance of Brian m. carlson's hash interoperability policy document that appeared in Ezekiel Smith's patch series.

Newren explains that the policy document originated in his own work (available in his `rust` branch) and was shared with Ezekiel Smith, who then modified and incorporated it. This confirms the document's lineage but leaves open questions about the review process for such modifications. Hamano's follow-up expresses concern about understanding the degree of changes made by Smith and whether the original authors endorse the modified version.

The exchange highlights an ongoing governance gap in the Rust transition - while technical components like the varint subsystem and build system are progressing, the process for documenting and vetting policy decisions (especially those with platform support implications) remains unclear. This is particularly relevant as the BreakingChanges documentation is being finalized, with Rust's planned mandatory status in Git 3.0 approaching. The thread shows maintainers seeking more visibility into how such cross-cutting decisions are made and incorporated into the codebase.
