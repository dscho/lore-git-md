This is a governance discussion about the Rust transition's flexibility provisions in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series.

Junio C Hamano responds to Phillip Wood's approval of the newly added flexibility clause in the BreakingChanges.adoc document (patch 7/9). While acknowledging the clause's intent to avoid blindly pursuing Rust adoption, Hamano questions the practical mechanics of deferring the Rust mandate to a minor release if the 3.0 transition proves too disruptive.

Key technical concerns raised:
- Questions whether a truly "breaking change" can be deferred to a minor release without fundamentally altering its nature
- Suggests the only viable deferral path would be significantly reducing the change's scope
- Notes the alternative - waiting for distros to adopt Rust support - risks indefinite delay and would make the change non-breaking for compliant distros

Hamano interprets the clause's intent differently than Wood, reading it primarily as reserving the right to abandon optional Rust support due to maintenance burden rather than as a mechanism for graceful deferral. This continues the thread's governance tension around transition flexibility, though now focused on the specific mechanics of versioning policy rather than LTS branch management.

The exchange highlights unresolved questions about how versioning semantics interact with major infrastructure changes, with Hamano probing the proposed policy's practical implications while Wood had endorsed its risk-mitigation intent. This occurs against the backdrop of the series' otherwise stable technical progress, with all patches now acked and CI validation complete.
