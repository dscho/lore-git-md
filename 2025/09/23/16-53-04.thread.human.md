This thread explores whether git-format-patch's technical email headers ("Range-diff:" and "Interdiff:") should remain translatable, given that they appear in patch submissions where English is the de facto standard. Kristoffer Haugsbakk initially raised the question, prompting historical context from Eric Sunshine about the 2018 decision to localize these strings. While the theoretical concern remains—that technical communication might suffer if localized—real-world experience suggests otherwise.

Ben Knoble confirmed that his French-localized headers (e.g., "Diff-intervalle contre v1") have appeared in past patch emails without causing confusion. Jeff King (Peff) observed that the meaning stays clear from context, and the current behavior adds a touch of character to the mailing list. The discussion has shifted from questioning the design to acknowledging that, in practice, localized headers haven't posed problems for reviewers.  

With no concrete issues reported and no strong push for change, the thread appears to be settling on keeping the status quo. The exchange serves as a useful reference for future discussions about translation boundaries in technical workflows, but for now, no action is expected. The Git community's pragmatic approach—valuing real-world function over theoretical purity—seems to have carried the day.
