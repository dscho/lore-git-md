This is a discussion about localization (l10n) of technical email headers in Git's patch submission workflow, specifically the "Range-diff:" and "Interdiff:" headers produced by `git format-patch`.

Eric Sunshine responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's question about why these headers are translated, providing historical context from their original 2018 implementation. Sunshine recalls the decision was intentional, with arguments on both sides: the headers are human-readable (supporting translation) but also part of a technical workflow (where English is often standard). He notes there was no reviewer pushback at the time, and mentions this is only the second time the decision has been questioned.

The response includes two notable data points that could inform future discussion: (1) Sunshine's recollection that some projects may prefer non-English technical headers, and (2) a reference to Junio Hamano previously rejecting translation of diffstat output as precedent for keeping certain technical outputs in English. The email leaves the door open for changing the current behavior, stating Sunshine wouldn't oppose a patch to make these headers non-translatable.

Key technical details for future reference: the affected strings are in `git-format-patch` output, specifically the `_("Interdiff against v%d...")` and similar range-diff format strings. The discussion connects to broader questions about what technical outputs should be localized versus kept in English for interoperability.
