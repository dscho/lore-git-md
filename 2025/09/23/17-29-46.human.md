This is a governance discussion about the Rust transition's flexibility provisions in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series.

Junio C Hamano responds to Phillip Wood's approval of the flexibility clause in the BreakingChanges document, which allows deferring Rust's mandatory status if downstream impact proves too severe. Hamano questions the practical mechanics of such a deferral - pointing out that minor releases (by definition) shouldn't contain breaking changes, and that true deferral would require watering down the change until it's no longer breaking.

He suggests the clause's real purpose may be to preserve the option to enforce mandatory Rust if maintaining optional support proves too burdensome, rather than actually accommodating resistant distributions. The exchange reveals Hamano's characteristically precise thinking about versioning semantics and his skepticism toward open-ended deferral promises.

This continues the thread's central tension between Steinhardt's structured transition plan and Hamano's preference for decentralized, pragmatic solutions. While technical implementation progresses smoothly, these governance nuances around versioning policies and downstream impact remain unresolved.
