This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series, specifically addressing the timeline for making Rust mandatory in Git 3.0. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about the aggressive timeline being "hopelessly optimistic" for some platforms to adopt Rust support.

The key proposal is to make the Rust requirement conditional rather than absolute, similar to how the reftable default was handled. Steinhardt suggests adding language to the BreakingChanges document that would evaluate downstream impact before finalizing the Rust mandate, considering both ecosystem readiness and maintenance burden of keeping Rust optional.

The email highlights two important tensions in the transition:
1. The balance between Git's internal needs (wanting to drop optional Rust support complexity) versus external constraints (platforms needing more time for Rust adoption)
2. The precedent of making breaking changes contingent on ecosystem readiness, as demonstrated by the reftable transition

This continues the thread's central governance question about how to document and modify policy decisions with platform support implications. The conditional approach represents a potential middle ground between the original aggressive schedule and Hamano's concerns about platform readiness.
