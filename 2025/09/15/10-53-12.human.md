This is a documentation patch in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series, specifically addressing the BreakingChanges documentation to announce Rust becoming mandatory in Git 3.0. The email contains Patrick Steinhardt's response to Junio C Hamano's feedback on the phrasing and practical aspects of the transition plan.

The discussion focuses on two main points of clarification in the documentation. First, Steinhardt explains the rationale behind listing two categories of Rust adoption (subsystem interoperability and new features) even though only the first currently exists - this is to explicitly encourage future Rust feature development. Second, they discuss terminology around calling these changes "test balloons," acknowledging the term may not be perfect but lacking a better alternative.

The more substantive discussion revolves around the proposed LTS maintenance model. Junio raised concerns about the practicality of the "hand over but still review" approach, particularly around security embargoes. Steinhardt clarifies that the key goal is keeping the LTS releases in the canonical repository, which necessitates ongoing project involvement in reviewing backports, though the actual maintenance work could be handled by distributors. They suggest trusted LTS maintainers would need access to git-security@ for embargoed fixes.

This email shows the series is moving from technical implementation details (now largely settled) to the policy and documentation aspects of the Rust transition. The discussion remains productive, with Steinhardt responsive to feedback while clearly explaining the reasoning behind the current proposals. The LTS model appears to be the most open question remaining in the series.
