This is a discussion about extending the `--ignore-other-worktrees` option to `git rebase`, part of Gabriel Scherer's series improving worktree branch safety checks.

Phillip Wood raises concerns about whether rebase integration is actually desirable, arguing it's fundamentally more dangerous than the checkout case. While checkout operations might just read from a branch, rebase actively updates it - which could cause confusion when the branch is checked out elsewhere. He suggests an alternative approach of recommending detached HEAD rebases (`git rebase <upstream> <branch>^0`) for experimental changes, though acknowledges this might be confusing for users.

The technical implementation in Gabriel's patch adds the flag to `struct rebase_options`, updates documentation, and includes test coverage showing both the default failure case and successful override with `--ignore-other-worktrees`. The changes are cleanly implemented, but Phillip's response suggests the maintainers may want to reconsider whether this functionality should exist at all for rebase, given the different risk profile compared to checkout operations.

This exchange highlights a key design decision point in the series - while the checkout integration appears settled, the rebase case now faces deeper questions about whether bypassing worktree safety checks is appropriate given rebase's branch-modifying nature. The thread seems poised to either refine the warning messaging (as Junio suggested for checkout) or potentially drop rebase support from this feature.
