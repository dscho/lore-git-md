This is a discussion about the safety implications of extending `--ignore-other-worktrees` to `git rebase`, part of Gabriel Scherer's series improving worktree branch safety checks.

Junio Hamano responds to Phillip Wood's concerns about allowing rebase operations on branches checked out in other worktrees. While the original patch proposed adding the flag to rebase, both maintainers express reservations about the workflow safety implications. Junio notes that unlike checkout operations (which may be read-only), rebase actively modifies branches, creating potential confusion when multiple worktrees are involved.

The discussion highlights two key scenarios where this could cause problems: (1) when a user has uncommitted changes in the original worktree that would be lost or complicate the rebase, and (2) when the detached HEAD workflow might actually be safer for experimental changes. Junio suggests that directing users to rebase in the original worktree (where the branch is checked out) may be less risky approach than supporting cross-worktree rebases.

This email represents a significant maintainer pushback on the rebase portion of the series, suggesting the feature may need reconsideration or stronger warnings if implemented. The thread has shifted from implementation details to fundamental questions about whether rebase should support branch independence at all, given its more dangerous nature compared to checkout operations.
