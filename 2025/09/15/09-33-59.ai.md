This is a discussion about command consolidation in the ongoing `git-history` feature development. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Elijah Newren's concerns about command proliferation by clarifying his vision for how `git-history` and `git-replay` should coexist.

Patrick proposes a clear roles: `git-replay` as the plumbing-level tool for scripts/server-side use, while `git-history` becomes the user-facing home for history editing operations. He defends the subcommand approach (rather than separate commands like `git-reorder`) as better for discoverability, while acknowledging aliases could provide the separate-command experience if desired.

The email establishes clear boundaries for what belongs in `git-history` - operations that edit existing commit sequences, ruling out commit creation (`commit`), import/export operations, and working tree-affecting commands like `reset`. Patrick considers but doesn't commit to renaming to `git-histedit` as potentially more precise. The exchange shows Patrick being receptive to design feedback while maintaining a coherent vision for the command's scope, with both developers agreeing the experimental flag allows for future adjustments.
