Feature proposal for improved rebase handling of semantically dependent commits, now with history-preserving approach.

Antonio Mennillo responds to feedback on his `git-rebase-clean` tool, which originally addressed semantic conflict cascades during rebase by squashing feature branches first. The new v2.0.0 version now preserves commit history by: (1) temporarily squashing to resolve conflicts with full context, then (2) automatically re-expanding the original commits using saved diffs. This aims to combine the benefits of atomic conflict resolution with granular history preservation.

The author acknowledges Brian's point about atomic commits being ideal, but notes real-world constraints when working with junior developers. He also clarifies that `git rerere` doesn't solve semantic mismatches (like interface/implementation drift). The updated approach has passed 23 integration tests covering edge cases like binary files and empty commits.

This represents a significant evolution from the initial proposal that drew criticism for losing history. The author now positions it as a potential experimental rebase strategy (`--squash-restore`), while remaining open to feedback about overlap with existing mechanisms. The implementation appears more polished, though still untested in production. The core tension remains between Git's philosophy of atomic commits and practical workflow challenges with dependent changes.
