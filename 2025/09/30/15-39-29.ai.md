Junio C Hamano provides background on the evolution of Git's cherry-pick commit messages, explaining why they originally included only bare SHA-1s and why they became opt-in rather than default. The response clarifies that the current format stems from early Linux kernel workflows where both original and cherry-picked commits were public in the same project, making SHA-1s sufficient identifiers. Hamano recounts how the feature was originally always-on but was made opt-in after users complained about "useless" references when cherry-picking across private branches.

This email adds important historical context to the ongoing discussion about cherry-pick tracking, confirming that the current trailer was designed for a specific public workflow rather than ideological reasons. The explanation helps ground the technical debate in actual user needs and historical constraints, particularly around when SHA-1-only references are (and aren't) sufficient. While not proposing new solutions, this provides valuable perspective for evaluating the various tracking approaches under discussion (hooks, git-notes, and existing mechanisms).
