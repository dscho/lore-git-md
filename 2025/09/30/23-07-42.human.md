This is a release planning discussion about the timing of Git 3.0. Brian m. carlson raises the conversation after discussions at the Contributor Summit, noting that most planned functionality for 3.0 is already implemented.

The main open questions revolve around SHA-256 interoperability work, which still requires significant effort (200-400 patches, with only 100 completed so far). There's also concern about third-party adoption, as some forges and projects don't yet have full SHA-256 support, though major forges are reportedly working on it.

The email presents two approaches: either stick to the original timeline (about a year/4 releases away) to pressure ecosystem adoption, or wait until SHA-256 work is complete. Brian personally favors not making the interoperability work a blocker, citing lack of contributor commitments and personal bandwidth constraints.

The discussion appears to be seeking broader community input on whether to maintain the aggressive timeline or adjust expectations based on technical readiness. The email serves as a thoughtful starting point for what will likely be an important decision about Git's next major version.
