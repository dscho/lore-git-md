The thread continues discussions around Git 3.0 release planning, particularly focusing on SHA-256 interoperability challenges that emerged from the Contributor Summit. brian m. carlson initiated this with proposals for migration tooling (including a `git hash convert` subcommand) and process discussions about coordinating the ecosystem transition. The technical work is progressing in the `sha256-interop` branch, with about half of the needed patches completed.

A key development in recent messages is the realization that language bindings like pygit2 may face significant challenges in adopting SHA-256 support, even when their underlying libraries (like libgit2) implement it. Michal Suchánek pointed out that these bindings don't automatically inherit SHA-256 capabilities and may require major API redesigns - potentially necessitating entirely new major versions (libgit3/pygit3). This adds another layer of complexity to the transition planning, as it affects a wider swath of the ecosystem than previously considered.

The discussion has evolved to include both technical implementation details (like the migration tooling design and protocol constraints) and process considerations (like stakeholder outreach to major forges and now language binding maintainers). While SHA-1 will remain supported for existing repositories, the community is working through the implications of making SHA-256 the default, with Patrick Steinhardt, brian m. carlson, and Michal Suchánek being the primary voices shaping both the technical and coordination aspects of this significant change.
