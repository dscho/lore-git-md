This thread is discussing the planning for Git 3.0, with SHA-256 interoperability emerging as a potential blocker. The discussion was initiated by brian m. carlson following the Contributor Summit, where it became clear that while about 100 patches for SHA-256 support have been completed, another 200-400 may be needed. The core tension is between maintaining the original ~1 year timeline (which would require 75-100 patches per release cycle) versus delaying until SHA-256 work is complete, given that major forges and dependent projects still have incomplete support.

Luca Milanesio has now joined the conversation, bringing concerns from the Gerrit/JGit perspective. Having just dealt with Gerrit 3.0 compatibility issues at a separate summit, he's wary of rushing Git 3.0 with potential breaking changes that could impact other implementations. Milanesio is seeking more information about the Contributor Summit discussions and probing for concrete commitments from other projects, specifically asking brian m. carlson about available resources from their employer. His participation highlights the ecosystem coordination challenges, as different parts of the ecosystem (C-Git, JGit, forges) may not be equally prepared for SHA-256 changes. While willing to participate, Milanesio is looking for clearer signals about broader readiness before proceeding, maintaining the thread's central tension between schedule pressure and feature completeness.
