The thread continues discussions around planning for Git 3.0, particularly focusing on the challenges of SHA-256 interoperability. The core issue remains whether to proceed with the original release timeline or delay until the SHA-256 work is complete (currently about halfway done). brian m. carlson has proposed a `git hash convert` subcommand to handle SHA-1 to SHA-256 conversion, while Patrick Steinhardt is leading efforts to gather broader ecosystem input.

Recent contributions have expanded the scope of considerations, with Michal Suchánek adding Gitea to the list of forges to consult and raising concerns about language bindings and pure implementations like dulwich. Randall Becker highlighted platform-specific constraints, noting that while their NonStop front-end is SHA-256 ready, Rust toolchain availability creates a hard blocker. The latest addition from SZEDER Gábor suggests including Codeberg.org in the list of forges to consult, showing the community's attention to ensuring no significant Git-using platform is overlooked in the transition planning.

Technical work continues on the `sha256-interop` branch, with recognition that both libgit2 bindings and pure implementations may require significant adaptation. The discussion confirms SHA-1 will remain supported for existing repositories even after SHA-256 becomes default. Open questions remain about release timing given platform-specific blockers, process design for ecosystem coordination, and technical implementation details of migration tooling.
