This is a documentation patch in Julia Evans' ongoing series to clarify Git's push behavior and upstream branch documentation. It's part 4 of 5 in v4 of the series, focusing specifically on making the "what to push" section of `git-push` more accessible to users.

The patch addresses several common points of confusion identified from user feedback, including unclear syntax explanations, hard-to-parse configuration references, and misplaced default behavior descriptions. The key changes reorganize the push behavior explanation into a clearer numbered precedence list and provide more accurate (though still simplified) description of `push.default=simple` behavior. The patch also links to the new "UPSTREAM BRANCHES" section introduced earlier in the series.

Notably, the patch acknowledges but deliberately avoids diving into the full complexity of `push.default=simple`'s behavior, which has diverged from its original design and now has special cases depending on remote configuration. Instead, it focuses on making the documentation more scannable and actionable for typical users while directing those who need deeper understanding to other sections.

This continues the series' trend of balancing technical accuracy with pedagogical clarity, building on earlier feedback from Junio Hamano and others. The changes appear well-considered, addressing specific pain points while maintaining the documentation's correctness. The patch should make `git-push` behavior more approachable without sacrificing the nuances that advanced users need to understand.
