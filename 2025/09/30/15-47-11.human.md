This is a continuation of the discussion about git-worktree behavior and documentation, with Junio Hamano responding to Michal Suchánek's questions about best practices for worktree placement.

Junio acknowledges that the current documentation does not explicitly state that worktrees should not be created inside the main worktree, explaining this was likely an oversight because the feature's designers didn't anticipate users would want to do this. He contrasts Git's approach with other version control systems that may allow side-by-side checkouts, but emphasizes that in Git's case, placing worktrees next to (rather than inside) the primary worktree is the more natural and established pattern.

The exchange highlights a documentation gap that could be addressed, with Junio inviting someone to contribute clarification. The discussion remains philosophical about user expectations versus tool design, with Junio maintaining that Git's current behavior reflects its underlying architecture rather than being an arbitrary restriction.
