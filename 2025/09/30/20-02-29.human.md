This is a discussion about Git's patch workflow optimization, specifically addressing concerns around the proposed "ack" requirement for patches. Taylor Blau responds to Patrick Steinhardt's skepticism about whether adding an ack step would actually speed up the process.

Taylor clarifies that the ack system is designed to be optional and non-blocking - maintainers (including the primary maintainer) could still merge patches without acks if they choose to. The key idea is that acks from trusted reviewers would allow the maintainer to spend less time reviewing patches that already have consensus, not add a mandatory hurdle. 

He acknowledges Patrick's point about uneven reviewer coverage across subsystems, noting that his earlier "subsystem maintainers" suggestion was more of a thought experiment than a concrete proposal. The exchange highlights the delicate balance between improving visibility of review consensus and avoiding process overhead, with both participants trying to find a solution that reduces accidental delays without adding bureaucracy.
