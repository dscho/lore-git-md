This thread discusses a contradiction in Git's behavior around modifying pseudorefs like `MERGE_HEAD` using `git update-ref`. The issue stems from a recent change (8e4f5c2dc26e) that prevents writing to pseudorefs, despite Git's own documentation (since b6d2558c9ea3) specifically recommending `update-ref` for aborting merges by removing `MERGE_HEAD`. Johannes Sixt initially reported how this creates problems, particularly in worktree scenarios where directly editing `.git/MERGE_HEAD` is cumbersome.

Patrick Steinhardt has now proposed a solution that would restore the ability to modify pseudorefs through `update-ref` while maintaining architectural boundaries. His approach suggests special-casing pseudorefs in the command without treating them as normal refs, keeping their updates outside regular ref transactions. The proposal focuses first on `MERGE_HEAD` - the immediate pain point - while leaving open whether other pseudorefs should be included. This would resolve the current contradiction where Git's advice and behavior don't match, though implementation details around transaction handling and scope remain to be worked out. The discussion now turns to whether this middle path is the right approach.
