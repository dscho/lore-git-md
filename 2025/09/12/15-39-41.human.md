This is a discussion contribution about Git's `user.email` validation, adding new perspectives to the ongoing thread. Thomas Guyot argues against strict client-side validation of email addresses, proposing that server-side hooks are a better enforcement mechanism. He notes that Git already provides warnings about unset/invalid emails during commits, but enforcing strict rules would be problematic for several reasons.

The email makes three key points: (1) server-side validation (like LDAP checks) is more appropriate than client-side enforcement, (2) Git would need to overhaul commit message formatting to truly enforce email validation, and (3) preserving historical commits from other SCMs with non-standard email formats is valuable. This builds on previous discussion about GitHub's SSO token storage in `user.email` and the technical challenges of email validation.

The contribution maintains the thread's technical depth while introducing practical considerations about commit history preservation and the limitations of client-side validation. It doesn't propose specific solutions but reinforces arguments for maintaining flexibility in email format handling. The tone is constructive, focusing on real-world constraints rather than theoretical purity.
