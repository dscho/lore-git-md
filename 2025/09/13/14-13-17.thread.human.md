This thread discusses Gabriel Scherer's series improving how Git handles branch conflicts across multiple worktrees, particularly focusing on the `--ignore-other-worktrees` option. The series has gone through three versions, with the most recent discussion centering on safety concerns should lead to dropping rebase support entirely.

The core changes introduce better advice messages when operations fail due to branch conflicts and extend `--ignore-other-worktrees` to more commands. While the checkout/switch improvements have largely been agreed upon, significant concerns have been raised by Phillip Wood and Junio Hamano about allowing this option for rebase operations, since rebase actively modifies branches and could lead to data loss or confusion when used across worktrees.

In the latest exchange, Gabriel responds to Junio's critique of the warning messages, arguing they follow existing Git patterns while acknowledging room for improvement. He suggests adding documentation to better explain the implications and defends some legitimate use cases for ignoring worktree conflicts. The discussion has now shifted from implementation details to broader questions about when (if ever) it's appropriate to bypass these safety checks, with particular focus on whether rebase should support this feature at all given its more dangerous nature compared to read-only operations.
