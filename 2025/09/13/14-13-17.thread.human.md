This thread centers on Gabriel Scherer's series improving Git's handling of branch conflicts across worktrees, particularly through the `--ignore-other-worktrees` flag. The patches have evolved through three versions, with recent discussion focusing on two key areas:

First, the safety implications of allowing `--ignore-other-worktrees` during rebase operations. Junio Hamano has raised serious objections, arguing that rebase actively modifies branches in ways that could lead to unrecoverable states if multiple worktrees access them concurrently. He suggests detached HEAD operations as a safer alternative for experimental changes. Gabriel has defended some use cases but appears likely to concede on rebase support given Junio's technical arguments about potential data loss scenarios.

Second, the wording of advice messages when branch conflicts occur. Junio wants stronger warnings that clearly communicate risks like worktree desynchronization, distinguishing these from more benign cases like HEAD detachment hints. While Gabriel prefers consistency with existing message patterns, Junio insists the higher stakes warrant more explicit risk disclosure. Recent exchanges have refined the phrasing to better explain consequences while maintaining usability.

The discussion has reached a point where the rebase integration may be dropped from the series, while the core checkout/switch improvements proceed with enhanced safety messaging. Junio's latest response emphasizes that documentation must clearly explain the risks if the feature remains, maintaining Git's philosophy of protecting users from footguns even at the cost of some convenience.
