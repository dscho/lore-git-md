This thread centers on Gabriel Scherer's series improving Git's handling of branches checked out in multiple worktrees, particularly through the `--ignore-other-worktrees` flag. The initial patches added this functionality to checkout/switch commands and were progressing well, with feedback focused on refining advice messages and testing edge cases. However, the discussion took a significant turn when the series extended the flag to `git rebase`.

Maintainers Phillip Wood and Junio Hamano have raised serious concerns about allowing rebase operations across worktrees. Unlike checkout operations (which may be read-only), rebase actively rewrites branch history, creating risks when the same branch is modified from multiple worktrees. Junio notes this could lead to lost work or confusing states when users have uncommitted changes in the original worktree. Both maintainers suggest that detached HEAD workflow or operating in the original worktree may be safer alternatives for rebasing.

The checkout-related portions of the series appear to have consensus, with Junio providing detailed suggestions for improving the advice messages to better explain the risks of ignoring worktree checks. However, the rebase integration now requires reevaluation - the maintainers are questioning whether the feature should exist at all for rebase, given its inherently dangerous nature when used across worktrees. Gabriel will need to either make a strong case for keeping the rebase support (with appropriate safeguards) or consider limiting the scope of the safer checkout/switch improvements.
