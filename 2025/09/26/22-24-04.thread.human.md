This thread continues the discussion about optimizing Git's patch workflow, particularly around how to better signal when patches are ready to move from the `seen` to `next` integration branches. Junio Hamano initiated the conversation to address bottlenecks, with Taylor Blau originally proposing mandatory acks as a solution. The discussion has since evolved to focus on improving visibility of review status without adding unnecessary process overhead.

Taylor Blau's latest response clarifies that any ack requirement would maintain flexibility - Junio as maintainer could still bypass it when needed, using others' acks primarily as a way to reduce his review burden. He acknowledges Patrick Steinhardt's concerns about potential slowdowns and uneven subsystem coverage, walking back his earlier "subsystem maintainers" idea as more speculative than concrete. The core tension remains between standardizing review signals to improve visibility versus maintaining the project's lightweight, flexible approach. Both sides seem to agree the goal is reducing accidental delays through better information flow rather than adding rigid process steps. Key open questions include how to define qualified reviewers and structure review requirements without overburdening contributors.
