Junio Hamano initiated a discussion about optimizing Git's patch workflow, identifying bottlenecks in the current lifecycle - particularly delays moving patches from 'seen' to 'next' branches. The thread examines how maintainer bandwidth appears to be the primary constraint, with some patches remaining in 'seen' despite being technically ready. The original proposal floated the idea of introducing multiple maintainers with voting rights.

Taylor Blau has now engaged with the discussion, offering a nuanced perspective. While cautious about shortening the 7-day 'next' branch waiting period (due to limited data about who tests these builds), Taylor suggests a more concrete change: requiring at least one positive "ack" from another contributor before merging, similar to GitHub's code review policies. He's even provided a patch to formalize this in Documentation/SubmittingPatches. The response thoughtfully analyzes the challenges of both a "maintainer quorum" model and subsystem maintainers, shifting the focus from simply expanding maintainer capacity toward improving review participation across the community. This moves the discussion from observation to concrete policy proposals while maintaining the original data-driven approach.
