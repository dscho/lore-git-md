This thread explores ways to optimize Git's patch workflow, particularly addressing delays between when patches land in the 'seen' branch and when they progress to 'next'. Junio Hamano initiated the discussion to examine bottlenecks, with Taylor Blau originally proposing a system of mandatory acks that has since been refined through community feedback.

The core tension centers on balancing review requirements against contributor experience. Junio has firmly maintained that inclusion in 'seen' only indicates the maintainer has noticed a patch—not reviewed or approved it—directly addressing Taylor's earlier suggestion about maintainer merges counting as implicit acks. Patrick Steinhardt contributed empirical data from GitLab showing how CI testing against 'next' and 'master' can catch issues, while Kristoffer Haugsbakk highlighted documentation-specific needs for faster turnaround.

In the latest exchange, Junio responds to Patrick's concerns about process overhead, clarifying that the proposed ack system aims to make review states more visible to prevent well-reviewed patches from being overlooked. While acknowledging this could reduce latency for patches waiting for maintainer attention, Junio expresses uncertainty about whether the reviewer burden might outweigh these benefits. The discussion continues to weigh the tradeoffs between process standardization for visibility and maintaining flexibility that avoids bureaucracy, with Junio consistently emphasizing that any system must clearly separate patch awareness from evaluation.

Key open questions remain about defining qualified reviewers, structuring requirements without discouraging newcomers, and practical testing approaches. The thread has drawn participation from core contributors with distinct perspectives: Patrick warning about process slowdown risks, Taylor seeking balanced solutions, Junio maintaining clear distinctions in patch states, and Kristoffer advocating for documentation-specific considerations.
