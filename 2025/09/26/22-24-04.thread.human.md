This thread is a discussion initiated by Git maintainer Junio Hamano about optimizing the patch review workflow, particularly focusing on reducing delays when moving patches from the 'seen' to 'next' integration branches. Junio's analysis identifies that patches often wait unnecessarily in 'seen' even after receiving sufficient review feedback, suggesting maintainer bandwidth may be the bottleneck rather than review quality.

The email examines four recent merged topics as case studies, showing how both simple and complex patch series experienced delays in this phase. While acknowledging that early review cycles (initial feedback rounds) are hard to accelerate due to developer time constraints, Junio notes that the 7-day 'next' graduation period serves an important quality control purpose. The most concrete suggestion is introducing multiple maintainers who could vote to advance patches, though the email primarily aims to start a discussion about process improvements rather than push a specific solution.

Junio is explicitly soliciting community input on how to optimize this workflow phase while maintaining quality standards. The discussion focuses on measurable timing data and human collaboration patterns rather than technical implementation details, indicating this is primarily about improving the project's development process efficiency.
