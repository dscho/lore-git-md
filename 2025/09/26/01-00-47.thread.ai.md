This thread discusses the deprecated `git whatchanged` command and migration paths to modern alternatives, initiated by Kristoffer Haugsbakk. The original discussion established that `git whatchanged` is a legacy wrapper around `git log` with specific defaults, and proposed concrete replacement commands: `git log --no-merges --raw` for direct output parity, with `--stat` or `--name-only` as more readable alternatives. Junio C Hamano has now weighed in with a practical refinement, suggesting the `--no-merges` option may not be worth including in the standard replacement recipe due to verbosity concerns, implying `git log --raw` might suffice for most users.

The thread shows consensus that `git whatchanged` should be phased out in favor of more flexible `git log` invocations, with ongoing discussion focused on command-line ergonomics rather than implementation details or objections to deprecation. Current alternatives under consideration are: `--raw` (with or without `--no-merges`), `--stat`, and `--name-only`. Junio's position introduces a subtle but potentially impactful simplification to the recommended migration path by questioning the necessity of one flag in the standard substitution pattern. No technical objections to the core deprecation premise have been raised, and the discussion remains focused on optimizing the user experience when transitioning away from the legacy command.
