This is a governance discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure series, specifically addressing the versioning policy for making Rust mandatory in Git 3.0.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about the flexibility clause in the BreakingChanges document, which allows deferring the Rust mandate if downstream impact proves too severe. Hamano questioned whether such a deferral could realistically happen in a minor release without fundamentally changing the nature of the breaking change.

Steinhardt acknowledges the uncertainty, noting the project is counting on emerging tools like gccrs to mitigate adoption barriers but recognizes there may still be "victims" in the transition. He agrees to reword the document to say "this change" rather than "this breaking change" to better accuracy. The exchange highlights the project's cautious approach - while committed to the Rust experiment, they're keeping options open given the lack of real-world experience with the mixed C/Rust codebase.

This discussion occurs against the backdrop of near-complete technical readiness (the v6 series has addressed all build system concerns), with remaining governance questions about versioning semantics and long-term maintenance models being the primary outstanding issues before final approval.
