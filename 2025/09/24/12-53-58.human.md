This is a governance discussion about the Rust transition's long-term support (LTS) strategy, specifically addressing how to handle "Rustless Git" maintenance after Rust becomes mandatory in Git 3.0.

Patrick Steinhardt proposes softening the LTS handover language in `BreakingChanges.adoc` to defer detailed decisions about community-maintained Rustless builds. The current patch series had specified that LTS tags would remain in the canonical repository even after maintainership transfer, but Junio Hamano expressed concerns about rubber-stamping potentially untrusted community work. 

Steinhardt acknowledges the complexity, noting unanswered questions about timing, maintainer selection, and actual demand for Rustless builds. His revised wording removes the controversial repository-tagging commitment while preserving the core LTS promise (4 cycles of bug fixes, 6 for security). The change reflects an emerging consensus that precise LTS mechanics should be determined later based on real-world needs.

The discussion reveals deeper tensions between centralized governance (Hamano's preference for clear project boundaries) and distributed maintenance models (Steinhardt's openness to hybrid approaches). While technical aspects of the Rust transition appear settled, this LTS policy question remains a key governance hurdle before the series can proceed.
