Junio C Hamano responds to Gabriel Scherer's defense of the current hint wording for branch conflicts, drawing a key distinction between two scenarios:

1. The existing `--detach` hint (which Junio considers appropriate) helps users correct a simple command-line syntax issue when trying to detach HEAD, where there's no inherent risk in the operation itself.

2. The proposed branch conflict hint (which Junio wants improved) warns about a fundamentally risky operation - concurrent branch access - where users may not even be aware of the potential dangers. He emphasizes this requires stronger warnings about consequences like desynchronized worktrees.

Junio clarifies his position on hint messages:
- They should clearly communicate risks upfront
- They don't need exhaustive documentation but should provide key pointers
- The branch conflict case warrants more serious warnings than the benign detach case

The exchange reveals a philosophical difference in approach:
- Gabriel favors discoverability through hints that mirror existing patterns
- Junio prioritizes safety through explicit risk communication, especially for operations that could lead to data loss

This continues the thread's central tension between convenience and safety, with Junio maintaining that branch conflicts require more stringent warnings than other operations.
