The thread discusses the ongoing deprecation of `git whatchanged` and its replacement with `git log --raw`. The original poster raised concerns that `--raw` isn't an intuitive replacement name for users familiar with `whatchanged`, suggesting adding a `--whatchanged` option to `git log` to ease the transition.

Junio Hamano, the Git maintainer, has now responded to clarify the current thinking. He confirms that `--raw` is indeed the direct functional equivalent to `whatchanged`, producing compatible output, though he acknowledges it may not be the most discoverable option. He notes there are actually several alternatives depending on what level of detail users want - `--name-only` for just filenames, `-p` for patches, or his personal preference `--compact-summary` for a more concise view.

While not taking a firm position on whether to keep `whatchanged` or add a new option, Junio's response validates the core UX concern while explaining the technical rationale behind the current recommendation. The discussion remains open about how best to handle the transition, particularly around making the replacement functionality more discoverable for users.
