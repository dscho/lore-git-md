This is a policy discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing the timeline for Rust adoption in Git. Brian Carlson responds to Patrick Steinhardt's proposal to delay mandatory Rust until Git 3.0 with an LTS release for non-Rust platforms.

Carlson makes several key points: First, he notes that gccrs (a potential Rust compiler alternative) targets an outdated Rust version (1.49) and may not be production-ready soon enough. He emphasizes that platform maintainers should contribute to making Rust work on their systems rather than holding back adoption. 

Most significantly, Carlson argues against delaying Rust adoption until Git 3.0, citing his current work on a Rust-based loose object mapping that demonstrates Rust's advantages - simpler code, better performance (using BTreeMap avoids sorting), and stronger safety guarantees. He's concerned that postponing Rust would delay this work and potentially lose Ezekiel Newren's contributions. 

The email shows growing tension between those wanting cautious adoption (Steinhardt, Gentoo) and those like Carlson pushing for faster integration to unlock Rust's benefits. Carlson's practical experience with Rust development lends weight to his argument that immediate adoption would accelerate important work like the object mapping system. The exchange highlights how technical merits of Rust are increasingly accepted, while the transition timeline remains contested.
