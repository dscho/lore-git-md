This is a cover letter for an RFC patch series introducing a new `git-history` command for flexible commit history editing, now at version 3 with 18 patches. The series builds on Git's sequencer infrastructure while taking inspiration from Jujutsu (jj)'s history editing capabilities. The current subcommands include `drop`, `reorder`, `split`, and `reword` for editing operations, plus management commands `abort`/`continue`/`quit`.

The implementation touches several areas: sequencer infrastructure (adding history editing mode, hook support), cache-tree (in-memory index writing), wt-status (tree diff display), and add-patch (refactoring for in-memory use). The series is marked experimental and includes extensive test coverage, including verification of hook execution.

Key technical details include:
- `split` subcommand uses wt-status`'s `wt_status_collect_changes_trees()` and `wt_status_print()` for interactive splitting
- New `oidmap`-based commit rewriting infrastructure tracks original-to-rewritten commit mappings
- Hooks (prepare-commit-msg, post-commit, post-rewrite) are properly executed for all operations
- `commit.verbose` config respected for change summaries

The cover letter notes this may be split into smaller series after this RFC round, stopping after the `reorder` command implementation. The author also mentions potential future subcommands like `squash` and an equivalent to Jujutsu's `absorb`, though these aren't implemented yet. The implementation appears thorough, with careful attention to Git's existing conventions and infrastructure.
