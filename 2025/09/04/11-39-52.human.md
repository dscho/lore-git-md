This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust adoption debate, specifically addressing transition timelines and platform compatibility concerns. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Brian Carlson's arguments for immediate Rust adoption by advocating for a more gradual approach.

Steinhardt acknowledges Carlson's point about prior Rust discussions but emphasizes that concrete transition planning is still needed. He argues distributions require time to adapt, citing feedback from maintainers about the work involved. The email proposes treating Rust as optional for transitional components (like the previously discussed `varint.c` test balloon) while allowing new features to use Rust immediately since they don't break existing functionality.

Key points of contention include:
- Whether Rust adoption was sufficiently telegraphed (Carlson cites 2024 consensus vs Steinhardt's call for concrete transition planning)
- The feasibility of platform support via LLVM ports (Carlson sees this as straightforward, Steinhardt is skeptical)
- The maintenance burden of optional components (Carlson rejects dual implementations, Steinhardt frames it as temporary scaffolding)

The exchange reveals differing perspectives on project governance - Carlson emphasizes contributor productivity and security benefits, while Steinhardt focuses on distribution impact and incremental adoption. Both reference historical precedents (C99 transition for Steinhardt, Python's cryptography package for Carlson) to support their positions. The thread remains at an impasse between immediate adoption and gradual transition, though both sides seem to agree Rust's eventual inclusion is inevitable.
