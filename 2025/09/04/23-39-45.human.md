This is a response from Ezekiel Newren to Patrick Steinhardt's RFC patch series introducing Rust infrastructure to Git, specifically commenting on the varint subsystem rewrite.

Newren offers an alternative perspective on the build system approach, having previously worked on Rust integration himself. He notes his surprise that Meson can handle Rust tests, as he chose Cargo for his own approach due to several practical considerations: Makefile compatibility, better IDE support in RustRover, and the belief that Rust will eventually replace C in Git. He also explains his rationale for making Rust a hard dependency in his own work (ease of development and discussion clarity) while remaining open to making it optional.

The response highlights an ongoing tension in the Rust integration discussion between different technical approaches (Meson vs Cargo) and philosophical positions (mandatory vs optional Rust). Newren's perspective as someone who has worked on similar Rust integration adds weight to the discussion, though he doesn't directly critique Steinhardt's implementation. The email suggests there may be multiple viable paths forward for Rust integration, with different tradeoffs around build system compatibility and development workflow.
