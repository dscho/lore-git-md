Patrick Steinhardt's 6-patch series refactoring commit graph handling for the pluggable ODB effort remains at a design crossroads. The series mechanically transitioned commit graph tracking from object_database to odb_source through incremental changes across commit-graph.[ch], odb.[ch], packfile.c, revision.c, blame.c, and bloom.c (-164/+167 lines total). The first five patches established new access patterns (removing redundant checks, returning graph pointers directly, updating merge functions), while the sixth completed the architectural shift by moving graph state to odb_source.

Junio Hamano has raised fundamental objections to the series' core premise, arguing commit graphs logically span the entire object store rather than individual sources. Derrick Stolee now reinforces this architectural concern, characterizing commit graphs as a "structured cache" that operates at repository level despite physical storage location. Stolee notes the current "first one wins" loading behavior and observes that commit parsing and Bloom filter operations work repository-wide. While supporting patches 1-5's mechanical cleanups, he suggests keeping graph state at object_database level while using odb_source for I/O operations.

- Hamano's earlier point about optimizing NULL checks in `get_bloom_filter_settings()` (now acknowledged by Steinhardt)
- The tension between commit graphs' conceptual role and physical storage needs
- Whether to maintain repository-wide graph coherence or allow source-specific graphs
