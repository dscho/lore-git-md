Junio Hamano raises conceptual concerns about Patrick Steinhardt's commit graph refactoring series. While the technical implementation appears sound, Hamano questions the fundamental premise of moving commit graph tracking from object_database to odb_source.

Hamano argues that commit graphs logically span the entire object store rather than being tied to individual sources, since they represent commit parent relationships that transcend storage boundaries. He provides a concrete example where a repository might split history between different storage tiers, yet still want a unified commit graph covering all commits regardless of their physical storage location.

This is a significant architectural critique of the series' direction, suggesting the change might actually make the codebase less flexible for future multi-store scenarios. The objection comes from a high-level design perspective rather than implementation details, indicating this may require deeper discussion about the role of commit graphs in Git's object model.

The email doesn't reject the patches outright but signals they may need stronger justification or reconsideration of their core premise before proceeding. This appears to be the first substantial pushback in what had been a technically smooth review process.
