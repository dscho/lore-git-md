The thread continues to explore the architectural implications of where commit graphs should live in Git's evolving pluggable object database (ODB) system. Patrick Steinhardt's patch series moves commit-graph storage to `struct odb_source`, treating graphs as backend-specific optimizations. This has raised concerns from multiple reviewers about behavior changes and architectural direction.

Taylor Blau previously identified two concrete issues with the current implementation: inconsistent caching behavior and unintentional changes to alternate ODB handling. More fundamentally, he and Derrick Stolee have argued that commit graphs serve as fundamental repository metadata, not just storage optimizations, particularly for features like generation numbers and Bloom filters.

Junio Hamano has now weighed in strongly on this side of the debate, emphasizing that core Git operations depend on consistent access to graph data regardless of storage backend. He argues that treating graphs as repository-level concepts would simplify the architecture by avoiding backend-specific optimization paths. This directly challenges Steinhardt's vision where different backends could implement their own optimization structures.

The discussion reveals a deep philosophical divide about the nature of commit graphs in Git's architecture. While the mechanical changes in the patch series continue, the unresolved design questions about graph ownership and cross-source behavior remain open. Steinhardt plans to produce a design document after two more patch series to formalize his source-specific approach, but will need to address the concerns raised by Junio, Taylor, and others about maintaining consistent graph semantics across the repository.
