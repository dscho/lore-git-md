This thread continues the discussion around Patrick Steinhardt's series to refactor commit graph handling as part of the larger pluggable object database (ODB) effort. The core debate remains unresolved between two architectural approaches: Steinhardt's proposal to treat commit graphs as implementation details of individual storage backends versus Junio Hamano and Derrick Stolee's view that they should remain repository-wide concept.

The latest email brings Taylor Blau into the conversation, though on a narrower technical point. He reviews a specific patch that removes a redundant NULL check for the commit graph in the blame code, confirming this cleanup is correct since the subsequent function call handles the same case. While this change is uncontroversial and likely to be accepted, it doesn't address the larger design questions still open in the thread.

The fundamental disagreement about whether commit graphs should be managed at the repository level or as part of individual storage backends remains the key unresolved issue. Steinhardt has indicated he plans to produce a design document to further articulate his position after completing two more patch series. For now, the discussion continues to balance abstraction purity against practical implementation concerns, with this latest email representing a small but positive step forward on one piece of the technical implementation.
