This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, where Sam James (Gentoo) responds to Patrick Steinhardt and Brian Carlson about Rust adoption timelines and platform support concerns.

Sam reinforces the need for a gradual transition period where Rust remains optional, arguing this gives distributions time to adapt their packaging without sudden pressure. He pushes back against Carlson's characterization of LLVM porting as "very easy" and notes most distributions likely aren't tracking these Git-specific Rust plans yet. While acknowledging Carlson's points about Rust's growing ecosystem presence (like librsvg2 and Python cryptography), Sam maintains that Git should follow historical patterns of cautious transitions, similar to how the project handled C99 adoption.

The email highlights the tension between Carlson's position (Rust adoption is overdue and platforms should adapt) and distributor concerns about abrupt mandatory requirements. Sam suggests even a single release cycle with optional Rust components would help, though he personally prefers a longer transition. This continues the thread's broader debate about balancing technical benefits against real-world deployment constraints, with Gentoo's perspective emphasizing the practical needs of downstream maintainers.
