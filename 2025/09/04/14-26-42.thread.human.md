This RFC patch series introduces Rust infrastructure to Git core, now in its fifth iteration with 9 patches. The technical work is progressing well, with Patrick Steinhardt leading the effort on the varint subsystem proof-of-concept, CI modernization, and a phased transition plan (optional in Git 2.52, default in 2.53, mandatory by 3.0). However, governance questions have emerged around how policy decisions are being made and documented.

A key concern arose when Brian m. carlson's hash interoperability policy document appeared in Ezekiel Smith's patch series without clear visibility into the review process. Elijah Newren has now clarified that the document originated in his own work and was shared with Smith, who modified it before incorporation. Junio Hamano remains concerned about understanding the extent of these modifications and whether the original authors endorse the changes. This highlights a broader governance gap in the Rust transition - while technical components are advancing, the process for documenting and vetting policy decisions (especially those affecting platform support) needs more clarity.

The series currently maintains Junio's ack, but open questions remain about the build system architecture, Windows platform specifics, and timeline for niche architecture support (particularly m68k's 16-bit alignment issues). The discussion is now focusing on ensuring proper process as the BreakingChanges documentation is finalized, with Rust's planned mandatory status in Git 3.0 approaching.
