Performance and workflow discussion regarding Git patch integration process.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to two key points in the ongoing thread about accelerating Git's patch flow. First, he questions Taylor Blau's proposed requirement for patches to receive at least one positive "ack" from another contributor before merging, suggesting this might actually slow down the process by adding another mandatory step rather than speeding it up. This directly engages with Junio Hamano's earlier concerns about defining qualified reviewers and the potential for low-quality acks.

Second, regarding maintainer expansion models, Steinhardt agrees with Hamano that Git isn't large enough for subsystem maintainers, and raises additional concerns about the quorum model potentially skewing patch acceptance rates toward subsystems with more active contributors (particularly those maintained by hosting providers like GitLab and GitHub). He notes this could disadvantage less active subsystems and drive-by contributions that tend to touch various parts of Git without concentrated maintainer attention.

The email advances the thread by introducing new considerations about unintended consequences of proposed solutions - both the ack requirement potentially slowing things down and the maintainer model potentially creating imbalance between subsystems. It maintains the thread's technical focus while adding practical concerns about how changes might affect different contributor groups differently. The discussion remains at the conceptual level without proposing concrete alternatives, instead surfacing potential pitfalls in the current proposals.
