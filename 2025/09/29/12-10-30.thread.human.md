This thread explores how Git could better track cherry-picked commits across repositories, with three competing approaches on the table: Rasmus Villemoes proposes a new hook mechanism, Oswald Buddenhagen suggests using git-notes, and brian m. carlson advocates leveraging existing prepare-commit-msg hooks. 

Junio Hamano provided crucial historical context, explaining that Git's current SHA-1 trailer format originated from Linux kernel workflows where both original and cherry-picked commits were public in the same project. In his latest response, Hamano defends Git's merge-centric design as structurally superior for maintaining clear ancestry, pushing back against the characterization of cherry-picks as "second-class." He expresses skepticism about creating artificial commit relationships, preferring solutions that reinforce Git's existing ancestry model rather than adding parallel systems.

The discussion reveals a fundamental tension between Git's original same-project assumptions and modern distributed workflows. While technical solutions exist (hooks, notes), the debate now centers on whether to work within Git's current model or extend it with new relationship semantics. Buddenhagen's notes approach appears more aligned with Hamano's preference for non-invasive changes, while Villemoes' hook proposal would require deeper integration. All parties acknowledge the limitations of SHA-1 references in distributed contexts, but differ on how to address them.
