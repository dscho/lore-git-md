This is a follow-up in the thread about GPG signature stability in Git's test suite. Junio C Hamano is responding to Eric W. Biederman's discovery that adding an exclamation mark to GPG's `--faked-system-time` parameter properly freezes timestamps, resolving intermittent test failures in t1016-compatObjectFormat.sh.

The issue stemmed from GPG's SHA-1 signatures varying slightly under heavy system load due to microsecond timestamp differences, even when using `--faked-system-time`. Biederman found the undocumented need for an exclamation mark suffix to truly freeze time. This explains why SHA-1 signatures were unstable while SHA-256 signatures remained consistent - the latter appears less sensitive to tiny timestamp variations.

Hamano acknowledges the fix and notes he'll remove his earlier proposed workaround (disabling flaky tests) from consideration. The exchange highlights how subtle GPG behavior differences between hash algorithms can affect test reliability, and how thorough investigation can uncover non-obvious solutions to intermittent failures. The fix is small (7 lines changed) but effectively addresses the root cause.
