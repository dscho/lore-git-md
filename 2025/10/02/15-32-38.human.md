This is a discussion about Git 3.0 release planning and SHA-256 interoperability challenges. Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's proposal to gather roadmaps from key implementations to inform the release timing.

Junio raises important practical considerations about Patrick's roadmap-based approach. He questions what happens if stakeholders don't plan to support SHA-256 at all, or if their timelines don't align with Git's. Junio notes that while Git will maintain SHA-1 compatibility, the broader ecosystem (issue trackers, CI services) creates lock-in that makes it hard for users to switch providers even if they want SHA-256 support.

The email highlights the tension between technical readiness and real-world adoption constraints. Junio seems skeptical that simply collecting roadmaps will solve the coordination problem, pointing out that many ecosystem components beyond Git itself affect users' ability to migrate. His footnote suggests hosting providers have little incentive to make migration easier.

This continues the thread's evolution from technical constraints to release management strategy, now focusing on the practical limitations of Patrick's proposal. The discussion remains productive but shows the complexity of coordinating such a fundamental change across the Git ecosystem.
