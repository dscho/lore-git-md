This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about deprecating `git whatchanged`. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Jiří Bašek's report of still using the command by providing specific migration instructions to `git log`.

The email offers a concrete translation of `git whatchanged` invocations to equivalent `git log` commands, suggesting `git log --no-merges --raw` as the direct replacement. It further advises considering `--stat` or `--name-only` instead of `--raw` for human-readable output when not processing the results programmatically.

This response reinforces the deprecation stance while attempting to address the practical concerns of current users. The technical details clarify the relationship between the two commands (same machinery, different defaults) and provide actionable alternatives. The tone remains helpful rather than confrontational, focusing on easing the transition rather than debating the deprecation decision itself.

Key technical points for future reference:
- Replacement syntax: `git whatchanged <opts>` → `git log <opts> --no-merges --raw`
- Suggested alternatives to `--raw`: `--stat` or `--name-only` for readability
- Underlying implementation shared between the commands
- No new arguments against deprecation introduced, just migration guidance

The email doesn't challenge Jiří Bašek's usage report but provides tools to adapt to the planned removal, suggesting the deprecation path remains on course unless stronger objections emerge.
