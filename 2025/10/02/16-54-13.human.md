Git 3.0 release planning discussion continues with timeline strategy proposals. Ben Knoble responds to Patrick Steinhardt's roadmap-gathering approach by suggesting a middle path between indefinite waiting and arbitrary deadlines: establish a proposed timeline now based on existing estimates, then adjust it based on feedback from the ecosystem. This would maintain momentum while still allowing for new information.

The email notes this approach could motivate providers to firm up their own timelines, addressing Steinhardt's concern about lack of pressure. Knoble also raises an important practical question about representation - while major players like GitHub/GitLab participate actively, it's unclear whether smaller forge operators (GitTea, SourceHut) are engaged in these discussions, despite being part of the broader ecosystem affected by SHA-256 transition plans.

This contribution moves the discussion forward by offering a concrete procedural suggestion while highlighting a potential blind spot in the current planning process. The tone remains constructive, focusing on how to gather the necessary input rather than critiquing existing positions.
