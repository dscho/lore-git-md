Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's proposal for gathering SHA-256 adoption roadmaps from ecosystem stakeholders. The email raises three key scenarios about how to handle different roadmap responses from providers and implementations, questioning the practical implications of each case.

First, Junio notes that providers content with SHA-1-only support would face no immediate impact since Git will maintain SHA-1 compatibility. Second, he highlights the captive nature of many users due to non-Git tooling (issue trackers, CI systems) that makes migration difficult even if a provider's SHA-256 timeline is too slow. Third, he asks whether aligned roadmaps require further impact assessment if both parties meet their commitments.

The response suggests skepticism about the effectiveness of roadmap-based planning, pointing out real-world constraints that may limit user choice regardless of Git's protocol capabilities. A footnote expands on this, noting the lack of incentive for hosting providers to enable true portability of non-Git components like issue trackers.

This continues the thread's evolution from technical constraints to release strategy, now focusing on the practical limitations of ecosystem coordination. Junio's questions probe the roadmap approach's assumptions about user mobility and provider incentives, suggesting the need to consider these factors when evaluating SHA-256 adoption timelines.
