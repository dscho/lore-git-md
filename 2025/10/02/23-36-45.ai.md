This is a follow-up discussion in the atomic ref updates for `git replay` series, focusing on design philosophy and future extensibility. Siddharth Asthana responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's suggestion about adopting a `git-for-each-ref`-style `--format` approach for the command's output.

The core discussion revolves around whether `git replay` should maintain its current direct `update-ref --stdin` compatibility or shift toward a more flexible formatting system. Kristoffer argues for a composable design that could better support future needs like `post-rewrite` hooks, while Siddharth acknowledges the merit but notes the current implementation prioritizes atomic update simplicity for server-side workflows.

Key technical considerations mentioned:
- Current output format is tightly coupled with `git update-ref --stdin` requirements
- Potential future `--format` could use `%(old)` and `%(new)` atoms for commit mapping
- Hook integration (particularly `post-rewrite`) is recognized as important for client-side use cases
- The discussion doesn't affect the already-approved atomic update functionality but explores directions for future evolution

The exchange shows the series maintainers considering how to balance immediate server-side atomic update needs with potential future client-side feature requirements. Siddharth indicates the `--format` approach may be revisited when expanding toward interactive rebase functionality, while keeping the current simpler design for this atomic-focused iteration.
