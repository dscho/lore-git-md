Junio C Hamano responds to the cherry-pick tracking discussion with a technical defense of Git's current approach, clarifying that the preference for merges over cherry-picks stems from concrete workflow limitations rather than ideology. He explains the structural advantages of the trunk-first development pattern where fixes are developed on the oldest applicable branch and merged both forward to mainline and backward to maintenance branches, creating clear ancestry relationships.

The email pushes back against Buddenhagen's characterization of cherry-picks as "second-class," arguing the current trailer-based approach intentionally avoids creating what he views as artificial relationships between commits. Hamano expresses skepticism about proposals to add non-ancestral commit relationships, describing them as "vague" and "ill-defined," while leaving room for tooling that could help users implement the preferred merge-based workflow more easily.

Key technical points include the emphasis on structural history relationships over ad-hoc commit linking, the distinction between cherry-pick tracking in same-project versus distributed contexts, and the preference for solutions that reinforce Git's existing ancestry model rather than creating parallel relationship systems. The response maintains the thread's focus on whether cherry-pick tracking should be improved within Git's current model (Buddenhagen's notes approach) or require new relationship semantics (Villemoes' hook proposal).
