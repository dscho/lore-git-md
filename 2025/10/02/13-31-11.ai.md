This is a discussion about Git 3.0 release planning and SHA-256 interoperability challenges. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Taylor Blau and Luca Milanesio's concerns about ecosystem readiness for SHA-256 support, particularly focusing on non-C-Git implementations like JGit and hosting providers.

Patrick argues that waiting indefinitely for universal SHA-256 support is not viable, as it removes any pressure for adoption. Instead, he proposes gathering concrete roadmaps from key Git implementations and hosting providers to establish an informed release timeline. Without such roadmaps, the project faces either perpetual waiting or arbitrary deadlines that may prove unrealistic. He suggests actively soliciting these roadmaps from known projects to enable proper planning.

The email highlights the tension between technical readiness and ecosystem coordination, with Patrick advocating for a more structured approach to assessing and incorporating external dependencies into the release timeline. This continues the thread's progression from technical implementation details toward release management strategy, now focusing on concrete planning mechanisms rather than just technical constraints.
