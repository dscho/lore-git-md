Subject: Rant about excessive merge commits in corporate workflows
Thread type: Discussion about Git usage practices
Authors: Mark Bauermeister (original), Nico Williams (response)

The thread examines contrasting perspectives on merge commits versus rebasing in large-scale development. Mark Bauermeister's initial post described a workplace conflict where he was fired from a government job partly over disagreements about merge commit usage, with a colleague insisting on creating merge commits at every opportunity (including repeatedly merging master/release branches into feature branches). His emotional rant framed this as "soul crushing damage" and advocated strongly for rebasing.

Nico Williams counters with historical context from Sun Microsystems' development practices, arguing rebase-heavy workflows are necessary for large projects. Key technical points include:
- Sun Microsystems used rebase-like workflows since 1992 (pre-Git, not called "rebase" then)
- Merge commits were explicitly forbidden ("merge turds") in Solaris development
- The Solaris organization successfully managed thousands of developers across multiple large projects using rebasing
- Resulting linear history was easier to understand
- Claims this approach scales better than merge-heavy workflows for very large projects like Solaris, Windows, and Linux

The discussion remains philosophical about workflow preferences rather than proposing technical changes to Git. Mark's original position emphasizes personal experience and emotional frustration, while Nico's response provides concrete historical examples from enterprise-scale development. No consensus has emerged - the posts represent opposing viewpoints in the ongoing merge-vs-rebase debate, with Nico's response adding enterprise-scale evidence to counter Mark's individual anecdote.
