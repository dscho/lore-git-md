Subject: Rant about excessive merge commits in corporate workflows
Thread type: Discussion about Git usage practices

The thread examines contrasting perspectives on merge commits versus rebasing in large-scale development. Mark Bauermeister's initial post described a workplace conflict where he was fired from a disagreement about merge commit usage, advocating strongly for rebasing. Nico Williams countered with historical context from Sun Microsystems, showing how rebase-like workflows successfully scaled to thousands of developers.

Junio C Hamano (Git maintainer) now weighs in with a nuanced position, identifying problems with both extremes - excessive merge commits ("pointless back-merges") and excessive rebasing ("pointlessly rebase their topic every time"). He notes rebasing can hide testing history while merge commits can clutter the history. His technical suggestion is for occasional "trial merges" to test trunk compatibility without preserving these in final history.

Key technical points from all participants:
- Mark's experience shows emotional impact of workflow disagreements
- Nico's Sun Microsystems case demonstrates rebasing at enterprise scale (Solaris development)
- Junio's maintainer perspective advocates middle path: test via merges but clean history
- Rebasing risks losing test context while merges may create noise
- No tooling changes proposed - discussion remains philosophical

The thread now represents three distinct perspectives: personal experience (Mark), historical enterprise practice (Nico), and maintainer guidance (Junio). Junio's intervention shifts the discussion from opposing extremes toward practical workflow advice while maintaining Git's neutral stance on the underlying debate.
