This is a discussion post from Junio C Hamano (Git maintainer) weighing in on the merge-vs-rebase debate. The email responds to Mark Bauermeister's suggestion of an "anti-merge commit" campaign by taking a more nuanced position than either the original rant or Nico Williams' pro-rebase historical perspective.

Junio acknowledges both extremes as problematic - excessive merge commits ("pointless back-merges") and excessive rebasing ("pointlessly rebase their topic every time"). He notes rebasing can be particularly insidious as it hides the testing history. The key technical suggestion is that developers should make occasional "trial merges" to test compatibility with trunk, but not preserve these in the final history.

The response maintains Git's neutral stance on workflow preferences while offering practical advice that avoids both extremes. It suggests a middle path where developers periodically test integration (via merges) but clean up their history before submission. This positions Git's maintainer as a voice of moderation in the heated debate, though without proposing any concrete tooling changes to enforce or encourage this approach.
