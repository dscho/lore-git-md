Junio C Hamano, Git's maintainer, has weighed in on the ongoing discussion about merge commits versus rebasing that began with Mark Bauermeister's emotional account of workplace conflicts over Git workflows. While Mark had advocated for rebasing and Nico Williams provided historical examples of rebase-heavy workflows at Sun Microsystems, Junio takes a more balanced view.

He identifies problems with both extremes - excessive merge commits that clutter history with "pointless back-merges" and excessive rebasing that obscures the actual testing history. His practical suggestion is for developers to periodically perform "trial merges" to test integration with the main branch, but to clean these up before final submission rather than preserving them in the history. This middle path maintains Git's traditional neutrality on workflow preferences while offering concrete advice that avoids the pitfalls of both approaches.

The thread now presents three distinct perspectives: Mark's personal frustration with merge-heavy workflows, Nico's enterprise-scale evidence favoring rebasing, and Junio's balanced technical guidance from the maintainer's viewpoint. While no consensus has emerged, Junio's intervention provides a measured framework for considering the tradeoffs without taking sides in the philosophical debate.
