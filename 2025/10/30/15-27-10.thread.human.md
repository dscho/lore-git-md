This thread began with Mark Bauermeister's emotional account of workplace conflicts over Git merge commit usage, where he was fired from a government job partly due to disagreements with a colleague who insisted on creating merge commits at every opportunity. His rant framed this as "soul crushing" misuse of Git workflow.

Nico Williams has now responded with a counterpoint from Sun Microsystems' historical practices, arguing that rebase-heavy workflows are not only valid but necessary for large-scale projects like Solaris, Windows, and Linux. He describes how Sun successfully managed thousands of developers using linear histories (calling merge commits "merge turds") since 1992, long before Git existed. The response provides concrete examples to challenge the original poster's workplace anecdote, though both messages remain firmly in the realm of workflow philosophy rather than proposing technical changes to Git itself.

The discussion now presents two strongly-held perspectives: one side viewing excessive merge commits as history pollution, the other seeing them as essential for traceability, with each backed by different organizational experiences. No consensus has emerged yet, and the thread remains focused on debating the merits of these approaches rather than proposing concrete actions.
