This is a documentation patch in the final polishing stage of Julia Evans' series to improve the `git reset` man page. The discussion focuses on terminology choices between "working directory" and "working tree" in describing reset behavior.

D. Ben Knoble responds to Julia Evans' explanation for preferring "working directory" (more familiar to users) over "working tree" (Git's preferred term). He provides technical rationale for the distinction: a working tree encompasses all tracked files in the repository, while the working directory refers specifically to the current directory. He notes this becomes important when operating from subdirectories, and mentions the connection to `git worktree`.

The exchange shows careful attention to both technical precision and user experience in documentation wording. This is part of a broader collaborative refinement of the `git reset` documentation that has already addressed several other aspects of the command's behavior. The discussion appears productive, with participants offering rationale for their preferences rather than debating positions.
