This is a continuation of the thread discussing attribute-based file exclusion for `git grep`. Thomas Braun responds to Brian m. carlson's concerns about backward compatibility when searching binary files, while acknowledging the valid use cases presented.

The email reinforces the tension between user convenience (wanting to ignore binaries by default) and script compatibility (needing predictable behavior across environments). Thomas notes that even with an opt-in config, environment differences could still cause issues. He questions whether the proposed attribute-based solution would actually resolve the compatibility concerns better than a simple config option.

Key technical points referenced include the handling of quasi-binary files like PostScript and PDFs with searchable content, and XMP metadata in images. Thomas observes that while the 2012 "filetype" proposal discussed by Junio and Peff is interesting, implementing such an attribute system now may be more challenging than it would have been over a decade ago.

The discussion remains focused on finding a solution that balances flexibility for users who want to exclude certain files from grep results against the need to maintain consistent behavior for scripts and workflows. No new technical proposal is made in this email - it primarily serves to acknowledge the opposing viewpoints while questioning whether the attribute-based direction fully addresses the compatibility concerns raised earlier in the thread.
