This thread discusses a patch series to make `git add` respect the `ignore=all` submodule configuration consistently with other commands like `status` and `diff`. The series modifies core behavior to treat these submodules like ignored files - they won't appear in diffs or be added without `--force`. The implementation touches several built-in commands and adds comprehensive test coverage.

Phillip Wood, an experienced contributor (though not a submodule expert), has raised important concerns about the design. He points out that commit 5556808690e intentionally made `git add` ignore submodule settings, unlike `diff`, and questions why the series changes `add` but not `reset`. Phillip suggests the change could be implemented more simply by removing an existing flag rather than adding new `--force` logic. He also questions whether `--force` is the right mechanism, as it conflates different concepts of ignoring changes versus ignoring untracked paths.

The review doesn't reject the approach outright but identifies several areas needing clarification: justification for the selective behavior change, the choice of `--force` versus a dedicated option, and simpler implementation alternatives. The author will need to address these points in a revision, particularly explaining why changing `add` but not `reset` makes sense and whether the `--force` approach is truly the cleanest solution. The technical discussion remains open while the design questions are worked through.
