This is a technical discussion about SHA-256 interoperability challenges, specifically addressing concerns about language bindings for libgit2. Michal Suchánek responds to Patrick Steinhardt's earlier email about excluding bindings like pygit2 from the stakeholder outreach list, arguing that bindings don't automatically inherit SHA-256 support from their underlying libraries.

The email highlights that libgit2's experimental SHA-256 support hasn't propagated to pygit2, and that adding such support may require significant API redesign work. Michal warns that the changes could potentially break existing APIs, possibly necessitating entirely new major versions (libgit3/pygit3) rather than incremental updates. This suggests the SHA-256 transition may have broader ecosystem impacts than previously considered, particularly for language bindings that may need to reimplement or significantly adapt their interfaces.

The discussion continues the thread's theme of carefully considering all affected components in the SHA-256 transition, now expanding beyond core implementations to include the binding layer. This represents a refinement of the stakeholder analysis rather than introducing new technical work items, but does surface additional complexity in the migration path that may need to be addressed in planning.
