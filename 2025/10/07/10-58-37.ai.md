This is a refactoring and bugfix series targeting tag peeling behavior in Git's refs subsystem, with a performance optimization as the original motivation. The series starts with structural improvements to the refs iterator interface, then addresses inconsistencies in tag peeling, and concludes with the performance tweak that kicked off the investigation.

The first 7 patches refactor the refs iterator infrastructure to eliminate the `peel_iterated_object()` hack, introducing a cleaner `reference_get_peeled_oid()` interface. Patches 8-9 remove now-obsolete peeling infrastructure. Patches 10-12 fix issues where tag objects might store mismatched type information for their tagged objects, adding verification in `peel_object()` and fixing specific cases in the reftable backend and ref-filter code. The final patch (13) implements the original goal: optimizing `git-for-each-ref` to avoid parsing objects unnecessarily.

The author notes some uncertainty about patches 10-12, describing them as "whack-a-mole" fixes rather than a complete solution, and invites alternative approaches. The series touches a wide range of files (65 modified) but focuses on refs handling, with particular attention to the reftable backend. The changes are mostly mechanical refactors with some behavioral fixes, suggesting careful review of the tag verification logic would be valuable. The series builds on recent master and notes a merge conflict with the incremental MIDX work.
