This is a process discussion email in the ongoing Git 3.0 release planning thread, focusing on SHA-256 interoperability and ecosystem coordination. Patrick Steinhardt responds to concerns raised by Michal Suchánek and Junio Hamano about how to handle stakeholders who may not adopt SHA-256 or who have incompatible timelines.

The email proposes concrete next steps for gathering ecosystem input, suggesting direct outreach to maintainers of key Git implementations (libgit2, JGit, Gitoxide, go-git) and major forges (GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Forgejo, SourceHut). Patrick acknowledges the list may be incomplete and invites others to contribute additional stakeholders. The discussion recognizes that some projects may choose to remain SHA-1-only, while others may need timeline adjustments, with Patrick suggesting flexibility in Git's own schedule if stakeholders provide advance notice of delays.

Key technical context includes the understanding that SHA-1 support will remain available for existing repositories even after SHA-256 becomes the default, allowing for gradual migration. The email continues the thread's parallel tracks of technical implementation (currently focused on brian m. carlson's migration tools) and release process coordination, with this message firmly in the latter category. No new technical work is proposed here - the focus is on information gathering and timeline management strategies.
