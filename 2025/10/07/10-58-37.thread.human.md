This thread documents a performance optimization and refactoring series for Git's refs subsystem, building on Patrick Steinhardt's earlier work. The series began as an effort to speed up `git-for-each-ref` but evolved to address deeper architectural issues around tag peeling behavior. The current v3 series of 14 patches refactors interfaces, cleans up obsolete code, fixes tag verification bugs, and concludes with lazy object parsing optimizations that deliver a 13% speedup for format operations.

Key participants include Patrick Steinhardt as the primary author, with Karthik Nayak now joining the review process. Karthik has reviewed the series since v1 and finds the latest changes acceptable, indicating growing consensus around the technical approach. The series introduces important changes like the `PEEL_OBJECT_VERIFY_OBJECT_TYPE` flag for ref storage operations and prevents caching of invalid peeled OIDs, while maintaining robustness through new test coverage for both files and reftable backends.

While the performance goals have been achieved, Patrick's earlier expressed uncertainty about the tag verification strategy remains an open question. The series appears ready for merging from a technical standpoint, but the architectural discussion around invalid tag handling may continue post-merge. The work connects to several in-flight topics including Taylor Blau's MIDX improvements and Jonathan Tan's ref transport work, completing an important piece of the refs subsystem modernization.
