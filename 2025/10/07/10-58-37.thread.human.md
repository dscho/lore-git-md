Patrick Steinhardt has proposed a pragmatic path forward in the ongoing discussion about refs subsystem naming conventions, following the recent merge of his 14-patch performance optimization series. The thread, which began as technical review of the merged changes, has evolved into a deeper architectural conversation about the roles of `struct reference` and `struct ref` in Git's refs subsystem.

Steinhardt suggests maintaining the current naming in the recently merged work to avoid disrupting in-flight changes, while committing to address naming concerns through follow-up patches. His proposal would rename the more problematic `struct ref` to something like `struct remote_ref` in a future series, while gradually expanding the use of `struct reference` into new refs APIs like `refs_read_ref()`. This approach acknowledges Jeff King's concerns about `struct reference` potentially becoming too generic while recognizing the technical complications around `peer_ref` links that make immediate renaming difficult.

The discussion shows notable movement toward consensus, with King having shifted from opposition to conditional acceptance of the `struct reference` name, provided its scope remains well-defined. Both principal contributors now agree that `struct ref` is poorly named but recognize the complexity involved in changing it. Steinhardt's email frames this as the beginning of a methodical naming refinement process rather than a final resolution, proposing concrete next steps while allowing the merged performance improvements to stabilize.

Key open questions remain about whether the community will accept this phased approach, what specific new APIs might adopt `struct reference`, and how to prevent scope creep in the structure's definition. The thread continues to involve core contributors including Junio Hamano and Toon Claes, with the maintainer having previously endorsed the importance of these naming considerations despite the series already being merged.
