The thread discusses a proposal to remove Git's potentially confusing `git diff X..Y` and `X...Y` syntax, which the double-dot form shows a simple diff while appearing to suggest a range, and the triple-dot form shows a merge-base diff. Martin von Zweigbergk originally proposed removing the double-dot syntax for Git 3.0, with Justin Tobler expanding the scope to include the triple-dot notation. While participants agree the current behavior is confusing, Junio Hamano notes the triple-dot syntax is used beyond `git diff` (particularly in `git checkout` for fork points) and would require coordinated changes. brian m. carlson strongly opposes the change due to compatibility risks.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk has now suggested an alternative approach: gradual deprecation rather than immediate removal in Git 3.0. He points to Git's typical practice of deprecating features before removing them, citing examples like `core.commentString=auto` and the reftable transition. This introduces a potential middle path between Martin's original proposal and brian m. carlson's compatibility concerns. Martin had previously acknowledged his patch was meant to start discussion and expressed willingness to add a `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` guard in a v2 version. The thread now faces the question of whether to proceed with immediate removal, follow Git's typical deprecation pattern, or find another compromise that addresses the syntax confusion while maintaining compatibility.
