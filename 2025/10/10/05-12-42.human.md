This is a continuation of the post-merge discussion about refs subsystem naming conventions, specifically focusing on the role and naming of `struct reference` versus `struct ref`.

Jeff King engages with Patrick Steinhardt's vision of establishing `struct reference` as a central type for reference operations, similar to how `struct object` works in Git's object database. While initially skeptical about the generic name, Jeff acknowledges that if this becomes the standard representation (as Patrick suggests for future APIs like `refs_read_ref()`), the name could be appropriate. He expresses concern about potential scope creep adding fetch/push-specific fields but seems open to the idea.

The discussion also touches on the problematic naming of `struct ref`, which contains both local and remote ref data due to `peer_ref` links. Both agree that renaming it (perhaps to `struct remote_ref`) would be a substantial but worthwhile effort, though not something to tackle immediately. Jeff concludes that while "struct ref" might be a better name for the new central type, the existing usage makes this impractical now.

This email shows the discussion moving toward consensus on Patrick's architectural vision while acknowledging the maintainers' naming concerns. The tone is constructive, with both participants weighing tradeoffs between generic naming and precise scope.
