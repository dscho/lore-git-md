This is a follow-up discussion in the Windows platform compatibility refactoring thread about include file organization. Junio Hamano weighs in on the earlier exchange between Johannes Schindelin and Patrick Steinhardt about automated include sorting versus manual cleanup.

Hamano makes a nuanced distinction between code formatting (which must be human-readable) and include directives (which are primarily scanned rather than read). He argues that while both could theoretically benefit from automation, the priorities differ - include sorting serves more as a scanning aid rather than a readability concern. This contrasts with Schindelin's position that treated both formatting and include sorting similarly in terms of preferring manual work.

The discussion remains philosophical rather than technical, with no changes proposed to the already-approved patches. Hamano's intervention provides a maintainer's perspective on the underlying principles, though it doesn't alter the practical outcome that the mingw.c cleanup will proceed as originally proposed. The thread continues to reveal differing views on style enforcement approaches within the project.
