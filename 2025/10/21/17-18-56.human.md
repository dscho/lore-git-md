This is a technical discussion about type choices in the xdiff refactoring series, specifically addressing whether `ssize_t` or `ptrdiff_t` is more appropriate for pointer offsets in the xdiff data structures.

Junio C Hamano responds to Phillip Wood's concern about using `ssize_t` for the `dstart` and `dend` fields in xdiff's `xdfile_t` structure. Phillip had argued that `ptrdiff_t` would be more correct since these fields represent pointer offsets, and `ssize_t` isn't guaranteed to match `size_t` width while being POSIX-specific rather than standard C. 

Junio acknowledges this as a valid point, noting that while `ssize_t` is commonly used to leverage its negative range for error signaling (when values are known to stay within half of `size_t`'s range), the approach has caused problems before. The reference to a past incident suggests this isn't just theoretical - there's prior evidence that mixing `ssize_t` and `size_t` can lead to subtle bugs.

The exchange highlights an ongoing tension in the series between type correctness and practical considerations, with maintainers now weighing whether to adjust the type choices based on these cross-platform and standard-compliance concerns. This follows earlier discussions about similar type-safety tradeoffs regarding `uint8_t*` vs `char*` and the proliferation of casts in the codebase.
