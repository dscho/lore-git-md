This is a feature patch in Patrick Steinhardt's ongoing `git-history` series, specifically implementing the `reword` subcommand. The patch allows rewriting commit messages while preserving all other commit metadata, similar to interactive rebase's reword action but as a standalone command.

Junio Hamano's review expresses enthusiasm for the functionality while raising several points. He questions why the implementation uses merge machinery for what should be a straightforward operation, suggesting a simpler approach of walking the commit chain and recreating commits with new messages. He also notes the subcommand syntax could be improved (putting `<commit>` after options) and observes that the command-less form in SYNOPSIS seems premature.

The review maintains the positive tone established in earlier feedback on this series, with Junio calling the functionality "something obvious and trivial" that he's long wanted. His technical critique focuses on implementation choices rather than fundamental design, suggesting the series remains on track for inclusion pending resolution of these points. The exchange continues the pattern of constructive review seen throughout this well-received series.
