This is a discussion email in the ongoing thread about Message-ID tracking in Git commits, proposing an alternative approach using Git's notes mechanism.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to James Bottomley's proposal for storing Message-IDs in commit headers by suggesting an alternative approach using Git's notes feature. The key idea is to have contributors share Message-ID mappings via a special notes`ref (like refs/notes/amlog) that tools could fetch and merge across repositories. This would avoid modifying commit objects while still providing the Message-ID tracking functionality needed for email workflows.

The email explores both the potential benefits and challenges of this approach. On the positive side, it would be less invasive than modifying headers and could work across multiple contributors' repositories. However, it acknowledges usability concerns for individual developers who would need to configure special refspecs to access the notes, and discusses potential issues with notes pollution and namespace management.

Haugsbakk references several kernel mailing list discussions about similar tracking mechanisms (like Link trailers) and their limitations, showing awareness of the broader context. The proposal appears thoughtful but raises open questions about adoption complexity and whether it would satisfy all the kernel community's needs, particularly around email archive linking.

This continues the thread's exploration of how to balance the kernel's specific workflow requirements with Git's general design principles, offering a new technical direction that may address some of Junio Hamano's earlier concerns about header storage.
