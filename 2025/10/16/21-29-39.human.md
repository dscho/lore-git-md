This is a legal clarification follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's Signed-off-by tag and its legal implications. Collin Funk responds to D. Ben Knoble's comments about copyright assignment processes in GNU projects versus Git's lighter-weight DCO approach.

The email focuses on addressing Knoble's concern about GNU's copyright assignment seeming to require contributors to "give up" their own rights. Funk explains that GNU's process actually includes a "license grantback" clause that preserves contributors' rights to modify and share their code elsewhere, even under different licenses. This provides context about how GNU's more formal process differs from Git's DCO while still protecting contributor rights.

The discussion remains non-technical and doesn't propose any changes to Git's processes. It serves to clarify GNU's position for comparison purposes, while the thread has already established Git's Signed-off-by as specifically representing DCO certification per Junio Hamano's earlier authoritative response.
