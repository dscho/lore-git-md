This is a discussion about the Message-ID tracking feature proposal, specifically addressing concerns about header storage versus trailers.

Junio Hamano responds to James Bottomley's explanation about why format-patch must drop Message-ID headers (to comply with RFC uniqueness requirements). Hamano argues against storing Message-IDs in commit headers, expressing concern that this approach would break the ability to trace a forwarded patch back to its original mailing list discussion. He specifically objects to the idea that `-m` would overwrite existing Message-ID trailers, as this would destroy valuable provenance information.

The exchange highlights a key tension in the design: while header storage approach works better for kernel workflows (where trailers are often rejected), it creates problems for maintaining message provenance across forwarding chains. Hamano's position suggests he would prefer a trailer-based approach despite the kernel workflow limitations, as it better preserves the ability to track patches back to their original discussion.

This continues the thread's central debate about whether the header storage mechanism can be made robust enough to handle Git's history rewriting operations while still providing value to email-based workflows. The discussion remains technical and constructive, with both sides clearly articulating their concerns about the tradeoffs involved.
