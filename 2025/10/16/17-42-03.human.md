This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about potentially removing the `git diff X..Y` and `X...Y` syntax in Git 3.0. Kristoffer Haugsbakk suggests an alternative approach to the breaking change: deprecating the notations first rather than immediately removing them, noting that most other deprecated features in Git have had a longer deprecation period before removal.

The email references Martin von Zweigbergk's acknowledgment that his initial patch was meant to start discussion rather than be immediately applied, and that he's willing to add the suggested `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` macro guard for a v2. Kristoffer points out that the current BreakingChanges document shows one case (`core.commentString=auto`) that was deprecated and removed in the same cycle, but most other deprecations had a longer lead time.

This suggests a potential middle ground in the debate between those wanting to remove the confusing syntax and those concerned about compatibility. The email doesn't take a strong position but raises the question of whether deprecation-first would be more consistent with Git's historical approach to breaking changes. The thread continues to explore how to balance clarity with backward compatibility in Git's command syntax.
