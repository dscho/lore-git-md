This is a follow-up in the legal discussion about Git's Signed-off-by tag, continuing the conversation about its legal implications and contrasting it with GNU's copyright assignment approach.

The email acknowledges Junio Hamano's earlier clarification that Git's Signed-off-by specifically represents DCO certification, while noting Funk's concern that contributors may not fully consider copyright ownership when signing off. Knoble finds the GNU copyright assignment process "relatively heavyweight" and ironically restrictive compared to Git's lighter approach.

The discussion remains non-technical, focusing on project governance models rather than proposing any changes to Git. Knoble appears satisfied with Git's current position (as clarified by Hamano) but continues exploring the broader philosophical differences between DCO and copyright assignment models. The email serves primarily to document this contrast for future reference, particularly regarding Knoble's planned response to the original StackExchange question that prompted this thread.
