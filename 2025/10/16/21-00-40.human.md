Feature proposal for Message-ID tracking in commits, responding to implementation concerns.

James Bottomley replies to Junio Hamano's objections about storing Message-IDs in commit headers. The author maintains that header storage is necessary for reliable email workflow tracking, since trailers (the alternative) are often omitted in kernel development. He acknowledges the rebase/cherry-pick maintenance burden but suggests Git should naturally preserve extra headers during these operations.

On the format-patch|am workflow concern, Bottomley explains the design intentionally drops Message-IDs during re-export to comply with email RFCs requiring unique message identifiers. This means the "poor man's rebase" will still function but lose the tracking information, which he presents as an acceptable tradeoff for proper email standards compliance.

The exchange reveals tension between the kernel community's need for reliable email tracking (where trailers are politically untenable) and Git maintainers' concerns about commit header proliferation. Bottomley appears willing to implement whatever header preservation might be needed but hasn't yet proposed specific solutions to Hamano's technical objections. The thread remains unresolved on whether header storage can be made sufficiently maintainable or if an alternative approach must be found.
