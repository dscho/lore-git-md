Kristoffer Haugsbakk's notes proposal has gained traction as a potentially cleaner solution that avoids modifying commit objects.

A new security critique from Brian Carlson has added important considerations. He raises serious concerns about automatically embedding Message-IDs in commits, warning of privacy risks, potential information exfiltration, and the inclusion of malicious content from unvalidated Message-IDs. These security concerns particularly challenge the header-based approaches while not directly addressing the notes alternative.

Key open questions remain about whether notes can satisfy kernel workflow requirements given historical objections, how to implement distributed notes merging, and how to document the tradeoffs between approaches. The security dimension introduced by Carlson adds weight to arguments against automatic header inclusion, potentially strengthening the case for the notes-based solution or requiring additional safeguards in any implementation.
