The thread discusses a proposal by James Bottomley to add Message-ID tracking to Git commits, specifically targeting email-based workflows like the Linux kernel's. The initial three-patch series (v1) implements this by storing Message-IDs in commit headers, keeping them visible in raw output but hidden by default. This approach differs from Change-ID systems and aims to reliably link commits to mailing list discussions.

Junio Hamano raised significant concerns about storing Message-IDs in commit headers, noting potential issues with rebase/cherry-pick operations and compatibility with the format-patch|am workflow. In response, James Bottomley explained the rationale for headers - they ensure Message-IDs are always present in email workflows, unlike trailers which can be inconsistent (particularly in kernel development where Linus Torvalds often rejects them). 

A key technical clarification emerged: format-patch won't preserve Message-IDs when re-exporting commits as email (to comply with RFC requirements for unique message-ids), meaning the "poor man's rebase" workflow will still work but will drop these headers. The discussion now centers on whether header storage remains the right approach given these constraints, or if trailers might be preferable despite their inconsistency in some workflows. The tension lies between the kernel community's need for reliable Message-ID tracking and Git's commit header maintenance concerns, with James indicating openness to implementation adjustments.
