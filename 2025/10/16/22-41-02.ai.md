This is a security-focused critique of the Message-ID commit header proposal in the ongoing thread about tracking email Message-IDs in Git commits. Brian Carlson raises several security and abuse concerns about automatically embedding Message-IDs as commit headers.

The email argues that Message-IDs should not be automatically included in commits due to multiple risks: potential privacy violations through change tracking, possible information exfiltration via Message-ID contents, and the inclusion of malicious or offensive content from malformed Message-IDs. Carlson notes that unlike commit messages and author lines which are visible during patch application, Message-IDs are often hidden in email clients, making silent inclusion particularly risky.

Key technical objections include the lack of validation for Message-ID contents (which could contain profanity, malware test strings, or other problematic content) and the potential for abuse through intentionally problematic timestamps or hostnames in Message-IDs. The critique draws parallels to existing timestamp abuse patterns in Git repositories.

This response directly challenges the security assumptions of the original proposal's automatic header insertion approach, suggesting it creates new attack surfaces without adequate safeguards. The concerns align with but expand upon Junio Hamano's earlier objections to header storage, while not directly addressing the notes-based alternative currently under discussion. The security perspective adds new dimensions to the ongoing evaluation of technical approaches for Message-ID tracking.
