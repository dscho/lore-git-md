Legal discussion about Signed-off-by tags and contributor agreements. Collin Funk responds to D. Ben Knoble's inquiry about the legal standing of Git's Signed-off-by mechanism, offering a non-lawyer's perspective on its practical role in the contribution process.

Collin suggests that while Signed-off-by tags may not have been tested in court, they serve as an acknowledgement that contributors have the rights to submit their work under an open source license. However, he expresses concern that many contributors may add the tag mechanically without properly considering copyright ownership, particularly regarding employer rights over work products.

The email contrasts Git's lightweight Signed-off-by approach with the more rigorous copyright assignment process used by GNU projects, noting that while assignments force deeper consideration of ownership issues, they also create higher barriers to contribution. Collin observes that even some GNU projects now accept DCO-style signoffs as an alternative to full assignments.

This response adds practical considerations to the legal discussion, highlighting the tradeoffs between legal certainty and contributor accessibility. The email provides useful context about how other projects handle similar issues, though it explicitly disclaims formal legal expertise. The thread remains open for further input from knowledgeable contributors.
