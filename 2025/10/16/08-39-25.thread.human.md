This thread has focused on optimizing the performance of `git last-modified`, a command that identifies the most recent commit modifying each specified path. The original implementation was found to be inefficient for rarely-modified paths due to excessive history walking. Toon Claes led a substantial rework of the algorithm, introducing several key improvements: a generation-sorted priority queue, path activity tracking via EWAH bitmaps, and Bloom filter optimizations to skip unnecessary diffs.

The discussion has progressed through multiple rounds of technical review, with significant contributions from Taylor Blau and Jeff King (Peff). They helped validate the approach and identify edge cases, including a correctness issue where the old implementation produced different results between filtered and unfiltered runs due to timestamp vs topology discrepancies. The new generation-sorted queue fixes this while delivering substantial speedups - from 2.5x faster on git.git up to 111.7x faster for extreme cases on linux.git.

The latest email from Toon Claes confirms the team has settled on the current hashmap-based approach for path storage after evaluating alternatives. While string_list and per-commit hashmaps were considered, benchmarking showed the v3 implementation's dual storage (hashmap for lookups plus an all_paths array) provides the best balance of performance and maintainability. With this final design question resolved and Taylor Blau's Acked-by on v3, the patch series appears ready for merging, having addressed all technical concerns raised during review.
