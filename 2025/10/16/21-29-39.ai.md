Legal clarification follow-up in the Signed-off-by discussion, providing additional context about GNU's copyright assignment process.

Collin Funk responds to D. Ben Knoble's observation about GNU's copyright assignment being potentially heavy-handed by explaining the FSF's license grantback provision. This allows contributors to retain certain rights to their code even after assignment. The response reinforces the earlier-established distinction between Git's DCO-based Signed-off-by approach and GNU's more formal copyright process, while addressing Knoble's specific concern about rights retention.

Key technical details include the FSF's grantback clause that preserves contributors' ability to modify and share their code under different licenses, though this remains tangential to Git's DCO-focused Signed-off-by mechanism. The email serves as an informational supplement rather than advancing the core discussion about Git's legal standing, as Junio Hamano's earlier clarification had already settled Git's position.

The exchange highlights the ongoing contrast between Git's lightweight contribution model and GNU's more rigorous approach, with Funk providing concrete documentation (an FSF bulletin article) to explain GNU's perspective. This adds nuance to the thread's exploration of different project governance models but doesn't change the resolution of Git's specific Signed-off-by interpretation.
