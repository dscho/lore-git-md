This is a follow-up in the ongoing investigation of flaky GPG signature tests. Eric W. Biederman reports being able to reproduce the intermittent test failures locally when running with `--stress`, confirming this isn't just a CI environment issue. The core problem appears to be inconsistent SHA-1 signatures being generated for identical inputs, while SHA-256 signatures remain stable.

Biederman's analysis shows that in failing cases, the SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories become out of sync specifically due to differing GPG signatures, with the SHA-1 signatures varying between runs. This suggests the test infrastructure is correctly detecting a real issue with GPG's signature generation rather than just having setup problems.

The email includes raw GPG signatures showing the divergence - two different signatures generated for what should be identical tag objects in the SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories. This provides concrete evidence that the flakiness stems from GPG itself producing inconsistent outputs for the same inputs under certain conditions.

The investigation continues, but this email moves the discussion forward by:
1. Confirming local reproducibility
2. Narrowing the problem to SHA-1 signature instability specifically
3. Providing concrete examples of signature divergence
4. Suggesting this may reveal a genuine GPG behavior issue rather than just test infrastructure problems
