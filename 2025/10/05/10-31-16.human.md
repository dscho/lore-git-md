This is a discussion about the deprecation of `git whatchanged`, part of an ongoing thread about how to handle this legacy command. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Jeffery Carr's suggestions about alternatives to complete removal.

The email clarifies that `git whatchanged` is effectively already an alias of `git log` with different defaults, having been superseded by `git log` over 12 years ago. Haugsbakk provides specific instructions for replacing `git whatchanged` with equivalent `git log` invocations, suggesting `--no-merges --raw` as the direct replacement, while noting that `--stat` or `--name-only` might be more readable alternatives for human consumption.

Haugsbakk confirms that future versions will include a more helpful deprecation message suggesting alternatives, addressing Carr's earlier feedback. The response reinforces that the removal is justified by the long-standing availability of equivalent functionality in `git log`, while acknowledging the need for clear migration guidance. The tone suggests this is a straightforward technical transition rather than a contentious removal.
