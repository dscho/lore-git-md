This is a discussion about the proposal to enable `rerere.enabled` by default, focusing on usability concerns for novice users. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) expresses reservations about flipping the default while the feature still has sharp edges that could confuse beginners. 

Hannes highlights a key pain point: when novices accidentally record incorrect merge resolutions, they face a convoluted workflow to correct them (requiring aborting and restarting the merge). While acknowledging rerere's value, he argues the current cache management is too opaque for those still learning merge conflict resolution. He suggests an alternative approach where rerere could update its cache with corrected resolutions after they're reused, which might simplify the workflow.

 

The email reinforces concerns raised earlier in the thread about rerere's user experience, particularly Kristoffer Haugsbakk's findings about the non-intuitive `git rerere forget` process. Hannes' perspective as someone cautious about beginner experience adds weight to the argument that UI improvements should accompany any default-flip. His technical proposal about cache updates offers a potential middle ground between immediate enablement and waiting for a major version bump. The discussion continues to balance rerere's proven utility against its remaining rough edges.
