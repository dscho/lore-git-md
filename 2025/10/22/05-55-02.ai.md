This is a discussion about the usability concerns around enabling `rerere.enabled` by default, focusing particularly on the difficulty of correcting wrong resolutions in the rerere cache. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's earlier description of the cumbersome workflow for removing incorrect resolutions from the rerere cache.

Hannes expresses concern that enabling rerere by default could create problems for novice users who might struggle to understand or correct incorrect cached resolutions. He highlights the sharp edge where wrong resolutions persist and become difficult to remove, potentially confusing users who are still learning merge conflict resolution.

The email suggests an alternative approach where the rerere cache could be updated after a resolution has been reused, allowing modified resolutions to enter the cache. This would provide a more intuitive way for users to correct mistakes without the current multi-step workflow involving `git rerere forget` and merge restarts.

This continues the thread's exploration of whether UI/UX improvements should precede flipping the default, with Hannes leaning toward keeping it opt-in until the cache management workflow is more user-friendly. The technical suggestion about updating the cache post-reuse represents a potential middle ground that could address some of the usability concerns while still moving toward default enablement.
