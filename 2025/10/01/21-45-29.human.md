This is a technical discussion about hash algorithm compatibility in Git's submodule handling, part of the ongoing extended tree format proposal. Brian m. carlson responds to Jeff King's findings about accidental cross-algorithm submodule compatibility in Git's current implementation.

The key point is whether to add explicit validation in `add_to_index()` to reject submodules using a different hash algorithm than the main repository. Jeff King had proposed such a check, noting that current cross-algorithm support appears to work by accident through hash truncation/zero-padding. Brian agrees this validation should be added as an interim measure, preferring to reject incompatible cases rather than risk data corruption, even though it breaks some existing accidental functionality.

Brian notes they haven't encountered this issue in their interop testing yet, as their work has focused on test repositories rather than real-world submodule usage. The discussion reflects the careful balancing act between maintaining compatibility and ensuring data integrity during the transition to supporting multiple hash algorithms. This patch would represent a conservative step toward stricter validation while the broader extended tree format design continues to evolve.
