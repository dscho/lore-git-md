This is a response in the ongoing policy discussion about AI-generated contributions in Git, specifically addressing Christian Couder's v2 proposal to add an "Use of Artificial Intelligence" section to SubmittingPatches.

Chuck Wolber raises several substantive concerns about the current proposal's wording and approach. The key critique is that the current draft risks creating unfair double standards and subjective enforcement by rejecting content that merely "looks AI generated" - which could inadvertently penalize human contributors, especially non-native English speakers. Wolber argues this moves away from Git's traditional focus on technical merit alone.

The email identifies three main problem areas: (1) the legal section's vagueness ("It's not yet clear") and redundancy with existing DCO text, (2) the subjective nature of "looks AI generated" criteria that could lead to false accusations, and (3) the apparent contradiction between a strict rejection policy and the encouragement of responsible AI use. Wolber suggests alternative approaches: requiring explicit AI content declarations (referencing declare-ai.org) and focusing documentation on self-reliance principles rather than generation prohibitions.

Notably, Wolber agrees with the positive guidance about using AI for step-by-step assistance, debugging, and style checking, seeing this as timeless advice. The response maintains the thread's core tension between prohibiting problematic AI use while accommodating legitimate assistance tools, but pushes for a solution that avoids subjective judgments and potential bias against non-native speakers.

This intervention adds new considerations to the open questions about policy phrasing, particularly around enforcement fairness and non-native speaker impacts. It also introduces the novel idea of mandatory AI content declarations as an alternative to subjective detection. The response indicates willingness to propose specific wording changes if the conceptual concerns are addressed.
