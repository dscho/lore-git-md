This is a thoughtful critique of the proposed AI policy patch, raising important concerns about implementation and fairness. Chuck Wolber responds to Christian Couder's v2 proposal to add AI usage guidelines to SubmittingPatches, arguing that the intent to discourage blind AI use isn't effectively realized in the current wording.

Wolber identifies several key issues: the draft's vague "looks AI generated" standard could lead to false accusations and inconsistent treatment, particularly disadvantaging non-native English speakers. He questions the legal section's usefulness given its uncertainty, suggesting instead consolidating this with existing DCO guidance. The critique also highlights a tension between the strict rejection criteria and the encouragement of responsible AI use later in the proposal.

The response advocates for an alternative approach centered on mandatory AI content declarations (referencing declare-ai.org) and maintaining focus on technical merit regardless of origin. Wolber offers to help refine the proposal, positioning this as a starting point for further discussion rather than outright rejection. The email thoughtfully balances support for the policy's goals with concerns about its current formulation's potential unintended consequences.
