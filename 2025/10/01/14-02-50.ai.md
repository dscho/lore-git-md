This is a documentation patch proposing to add AI contribution guidelines to Git's SubmittingPatches file, part of the ongoing discussion about AI-generated content policy. It's the second version of Christian Couder's proposal to address legal and quality concerns around AI-generated contributions.

The patch adds a new "Use of Artificial Intelligence" section that outlines two main concerns: unclear copyright/license status of AI output and the risk of low-quality submissions. It proposes rejecting content that appears AI-generated, overly formal, or incomprehensible while still permitting AI as a debugging/guidance tool. The text draws inspiration from existing Git project mentoring guidelines and includes specific examples of acceptable AI assistance (step-by-step guidance, style checking) versus prohibited uses (direct copy-paste of generated content).

Key technical details include the new section placement after the trailer formatting guidelines, the specific rejection criteria ("AI slop", "overly formal", etc.), and the distinction between prohibited generation and permitted assistance. The patch references related discussions in Git's general application guidelines and previous mailing list threads. Legal considerations focus on DCO compliance uncertainty with AI-generated content.

This version appears to address the thread's movement toward establishing clear boundaries between prohibited generation and permitted assistance, though it doesn't yet incorporate QEMU's exact wording or resolve all edge cases around commit message tools. The proposal maintains the thread's consensus against AI-generated code/prose while attempting to carve out space for non-generative assistance, though the language about rejecting "anything that looks AI generated" may need refinement to avoid over-broad interpretation.
