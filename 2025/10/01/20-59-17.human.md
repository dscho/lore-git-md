This is a policy proposal update in the ongoing discussion about AI-generated contributions in Git, now at version 2 with input from legal counsel.

Junio Hamano responds to Christian Couder's v2 patch that would forbid AI-generated content in Git contributions. While supporting the overall goal, Hamano suggests softening the language around detection - rather than rejecting patches that "look AI-generated" (which could be subjective and unfair), he proposes focusing on whether the submitter can explain their work when questioned. This addresses concerns raised earlier in the thread about potential bias against non-native English speakers.

The proposal maintains its core position that AI-generated code and documentation pose legal and quality risks, while still permitting AI as an assistive tool for debugging and style checking. Hamano's feedback indicates maintainer alignment with the policy's intent but seeks more objective enforcement criteria. The response keeps the conversation moving toward a balanced solution that protects project integrity without creating unfair barriers.

This exchange shows the proposal maturing through community feedback, with the next steps likely involving wording refinements to the detection" section while preserving the clear stance against wholesale AI generation. The legal input from the Software Freedom Conservancy adds weight to the policy's foundation.
