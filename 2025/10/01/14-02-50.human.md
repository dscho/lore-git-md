This is a documentation patch proposing to add AI contribution guidelines to Git's SubmittingPatches file, following up on an ongoing thread about establishing policy around AI-generated content.

Christian Couder submits version 2 of the patch, which aims to discourage AI-generated submissions while still allowing beneficial AI assistance. The patch adds a new section that cites two main concerns: unclear copyright status of AI output and the risk of low-quality submissions. It establishes that Git will reject content that appears AI-generated, overly formal, or incomprehensible, while encouraging more responsible uses like debugging aid and style checking.

The proposal builds on existing mentoring program AI guidelines and references the Developer Certificate of Origin requirements. The wording appears more concrete than the initial discussion, specifying rejection criteria like "AI slop" and content the submitter can't explain. However, as noted in the thread context, questions remain about precisely defining the boundary between prohibited generation and permitted assistance tools, particularly for commit messages.

The patch moves the discussion forward with specific policy language, though the thread indicates further refinement may be needed around edge cases and non-native English speaker impacts before finalizing. The submission is well-formed with proper sign-offs and references to prior related discussions.
