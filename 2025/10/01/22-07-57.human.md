This is a continuation of the ongoing discussion about git-worktree behavior and user expectations. Junio Hamano responds to Jakub T. Jankiewicz's comparison between worktrees and submodules, clarifying their fundamental differences in design philosophy.

Junio emphasizes that worktrees are intentionally designed to exist outside the primary working area, allowing parallel work on different branches without interference. He pushes back against the comparison to submodules, noting they serve entirely different purposes - submodules are components of a superproject, while worktrees are independent instantiations of the same repository.

The exchange highlights the persistent mental model mismatch some users experience when first working with git-worktree. Junio maintains the original design position that worktrees should be peers rather than nested under the main worktree, though the thread has shown this isn't always how users intuitively approach them. The discussion continues to balance design intent against real-world usage patterns.
