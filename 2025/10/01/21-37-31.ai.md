Policy proposal refinement for AI-generated content in Git contributions, with Brian M. Carlson providing concrete suggestions to strengthen the prohibition language. This follows Christian Couder's v2 patch and maintains the thread's focus on establishing clear boundaries around AI use while addressing legal concerns with the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO).

Carlson advocates for more direct language in the policy text, proposing explicit statements like "we cannot accept this content in our project" rather than leaving interpretation to contributors. They highlight the risk of ambiguity, especially for non-native English speakers, and suggest either a complete prohibition ("Contributors may not use AI to write contributions to Git") or a narrow exception for cases where copyright provenance is fully documented (e.g., an LLM trained exclusively on the contributor's own code).

The email identifies specific weaknesses in the current draft's phrasing, particularly the "carefully and responsibly" guidance which Carlson compares to vague speed limit language. They propose modeling the policy's clarity after Git's code of conduct, with explicit do/don't boundaries. The distinction between prohibited wholesale generation and permitted assistive uses (debugging, style checking) remains supported, with Carlson noting these secondary uses are "less objectionable."

Key technical suggestions include: (1) replacing subjective quality judgments ("looks AI generated") with clear policy statements, (2) considering copyright documentation requirements if allowing any LLM-generated content, and (3) ensuring the text prevents arguments over what constitutes "responsible" use.
