This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing thread about prohibiting AI-generated contributions in Git, responding to concerns about legal risks and patch quality.

Brian M. Carlson provides a detailed rebuttal to Chuck Wolber's objections about the proposed AI policy. The core argument centers on legal liability - Carlson explains why simply declaring AI use (as Chuck suggested) is insufficient, since contributors and distributors could still face copyright claims in jurisdictions where AI output is deemed illegal. They emphasize that Git's global nature and limited resources make this risk unacceptable, unlike corporate projects that can afford legal defenses.

The email clarifies that technical quality alone doesn't resolve the fundamental issues with AI contributions: lack of understanding by submitters and inherent legal uncertainties. Carlson supports an outright ban, arguing that even exemplary AI-generated patches can't satisfy Git's requirements for explainability and legal safety. They dismiss the idea of productive AI use in contributions, comparing LLMs to "fancy Markov chains" that already demonstrate harmful behaviors elsewhere.

This response strengthens the case for prohibition by focusing on concrete legal risks rather than just quality concerns. It addresses the international dimension of Git's contributor base and the impracticality of retroactively fixing copyright violations. The tone is firm but measured, providing substantive legal reasoning while acknowledging the need for clearer policy wording. The discussion appears to be converging toward a strong prohibition with narrow exceptions, though implementation details remain to be finalized.
