Feature proposal discussion regarding extended tree format for conflict markers. Elijah Newren weighs in on brian m. carlson's RFC for storing conflict information in Git trees, arguing against the current approach of marking conflicts at the tree level.

Newren clarifies that prior discussions about "toplevel" conflicts referred to commit objects, not trees. He raises two key concerns: performance impact and interoperability. Storing conflicts in trees would force `git log` to walk additional objects just to detect conflicts, imposing overhead on all users regardless of whether they use the feature. He also notes this diverges from how jj and GitButler handle conflicts, suggesting we follow their lead for better ecosystem alignment.

The email suggests deferring conflicted tree support from the initial proposal, focusing instead on the core mixed-hash submodule use case. This reflects ongoing tension between feature richness and performance/maintainability tradeoffs in the extended tree format discussions.
