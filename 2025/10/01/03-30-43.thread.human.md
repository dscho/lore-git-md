This thread explores how Git maintainers review patches, with a focus on the tools and workflows they use. It began with Henrique Soares asking about current practices, prompting Kristoffer Haugsbakk to share a 2016 email from Jeff King describing his historical review methods. The discussion has now expanded with Konstantin Ryabitsev introducing a modern tool recommendation - the `b4` utility, which streamlines fetching and applying patch series from mailing list messages.

The thread currently presents both historical context and contemporary solutions. While Peff's 2016 workflow provides baseline understanding, Ryabitsev's `b4` recommendation offers a concrete, modern approach to handling patches. The tool's `shazam` command can apply series either directly to HEAD or into FETCH_HEAD, demonstrating practical improvements since 2016. The example uses a real patch series URL to make the workflow tangible for readers.

Key questions include how widely such tools are adopted among maintainers, whether additional workflow documentation would help new contributors, and what other modern practices have emerged since Peff's 2016 description. The thread remains open for further contributions about review methodologies and tooling evolution.
