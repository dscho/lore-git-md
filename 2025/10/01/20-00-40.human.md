Junio Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt's concerns about potential process slowdowns from proposed review requirements. This is part of the ongoing discussion about optimizing Git's patch workflow.

Hamano clarifies that the goal of explicit reviewer acknowledgments is to prevent well-reviewed patches from languishing in the 'seen' branch due to maintainer oversight. He agrees this could reduce time spent in limbo for ready patches, but acknowledges the open question of whether the additional reviewer burden might offset these gains.

The exchange highlights the delicate balance between process improvements (ensuring reviewed patches get noticed) and potential new bottlenecks (reviewer workload). Hamano maintains his position that any system must clearly separate patch visibility from evaluation, while remaining pragmatic about implementation tradeoffs.

This response keeps the conversation focused on practical outcomes rather than theoretical ideals, reflecting the project's iterative approach to process improvements. The core tension remains whether additional structure would help or hinder the patch flow.
