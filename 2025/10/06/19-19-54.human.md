This is a discussion note from the Git Contributor's Summit about standardizing Change-ID headers in commits, part of the ongoing history rewriting tools conversation.

The notes capture a working session led by Philip Metzger exploring how to persistently associate Change-IDs with commits across operations like rebase. While there's rough consensus on storing Change-IDs in commit headers (matching GitHub/Gerrit approaches), semantic questions remain about when to preserve versus regenerate IDs - particularly during cherry-picks where tools currently disagree. Taylor Blau and others highlight the need for consistent semantics when collaborating in shared repositories, though some argue the standard could treat Change-IDs as opaque identifiers without strict versioning.

The discussion reveals practical progress (header storage seems settled) alongside philosophical divides about how Change-IDs should represent commit lineage. Caleb Donovick notes real-world divergence between GitButler and Gerrit's handling, while Elijah Newren cautions that complex cases like commit splitting may defy simple rules. The group appears to favor letting implementations experiment with the header format before standardizing semantics, with Elijah suggesting revisiting after a year of practical experience. This positions Change-IDs as an evolving solution for tracking logical changes, connected to broader `git replay` and history manipulation work.
