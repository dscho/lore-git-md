This is a policy documentation patch in the ongoing discussion about AI-generated contributions to Git. Junio Hamano proposes adopting QEMU's firm stance against AI-generated content, with minimal adjustments for Git's context. The policy would decline any contributions suspected to include AI-generated material due to unresolved legal risks around copyright and licensing compliance with the Developer's Certificate of Origin (DCO).

The proposed text takes a strong position, explicitly naming tools like Copilot, ChatGPT, and Claude as prohibited for patch generation, while allowing non-contribution uses like API research. It leaves room for future reconsideration if legal clarity emerges, but requires case-by-case exceptions to demonstrate clear licensing status.

This moves the discussion forward by providing concrete policy language that aligns with Brian M. Carlson's legal concerns about international distribution risks and DCO compliance. The proposal notably doesn't include the hybrid approaches or explanation requirements previously discussed, instead opting for a straightforward prohibition modeled after QEMU's approach. The policy's firm stance may help address Carlson's concerns about legal exposure while providing clear guidance to contributors.
