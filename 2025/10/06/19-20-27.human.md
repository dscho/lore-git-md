This is a continuation of the Git 3.0 planning discussion from the Contributor's Summit, focusing on the SHA-256 transition timeline and ecosystem readiness.

The email captures a lively debate about whether to set a hard deadline for Git 3.0 that includes SHA-256 support. Taylor Blau notes their team has been using end of 2026 as an internal benchmark, while others express concerns about ecosystem readiness. Brian m. carlson emphasizes the security rationale (SHA-1 collisions now cost ~$10k to produce) but acknowledges interop work may not be complete by the target date.

Key tensions emerge around forge adoption - Elijah Newren and Peff question whether releasing SHA-256 support without major forges being ready would create user frustration. Brian counters that smaller forges won't prioritize the work until Git itself supports it. The discussion ends with agreement to continue the conversation on-list, with Brian volunteering to kick it off.

The email highlights the classic open-source coordination challenge: balancing technical readiness with ecosystem adoption. While security concerns push for an aggressive timeline, practical considerations about forge support suggest a more cautious approach may be needed. The debate remains unresolved but appears headed for a mailing list discussion where broader community input can be gathered.
