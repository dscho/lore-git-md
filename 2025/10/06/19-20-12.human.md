This is a discussion summary from the Git Contributor's Summit about implementing resumable fetch/push operations, continuing the thread's exploration of major technical initiatives for Git's future.

The notes capture a wide-ranging discussion about making large transfers more resilient, particularly for environments with poor connectivity. Key points include: both client and server-side solutions are needed (unlike existing fetch-only resumable options like bundle-uris), with push operations being particularly challenging. Several approaches are floated - server-side caching with opaque tokens, deterministic single-threaded pack generation, and protocol extensions for handling partial transfers. Real-world pain points are noted, like GitHub support advising users to split large pushes into smaller branches.

Technical considerations dominate: attack surface implications, the tradeoffs of chunk-based transfers versus byte-offset resumption, and whether to modify the pack format itself. Patrick's (Peff) suggestion of opaque server cache tags emerges as a promising direction, allowing servers to implement resumption heuristics without protocol changes. The discussion connects to other ongoing work like packfile optimizations and large-file handling, suggesting this will be a multi-year effort rather than a quick fix.

The notes represent raw brainstorming rather than concrete proposals, but reveal strong consensus that resumable operations are a high-value feature needing protocol-level solutions. The complexity suggests this will likely progress incrementally, with fetch improvements building on existing bundle-uri work while push solutions require more fundamental rethinking.
