This is the final discussion in a series introducing a `--required` flag for reference optimization commands. The thread has been highly active with multiple review cycles, and this email captures Junio Hamano's thoughts on the flag naming.

Junio expresses reservations about the `--required` name, noting it could be misinterpreted as making the command fail if optimization isn't possible. He considers `--dry-run` but rejects it as implying more thorough checking than intended. Instead, he suggests a `--check-*` prefix with a modifier (like `--check-for-auto`) to better convey the lightweight check behavior.

The discussion highlights the careful consideration going into CLI design, where names must precisely communicate behavior to users. This comes after the technical implementation (spanning reference backends and reftable compaction) was thoroughly reviewed and refined. The series appears functionally complete, with this naming question being one of the final polish items before merging.

For context, this flag enables GitLab's Gitaly service to efficiently check if reference optimization is needed without performing the potentially expensive operation - a use case that emerged from real-world scaling needs. The implementation carefully maintains separation between backends while providing consistent behavior.
