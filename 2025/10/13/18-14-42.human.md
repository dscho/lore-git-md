Junio Hamano responds to Christian Couder's concerns about the proposed AI contribution policy in this ongoing policy discussion. The email clarifies several key points in the debate:

He pushes back on Christian's argument that strict wording might discourage legitimate contributions, suggesting the practical difference between strict and lenient policies may be minimal since contributors must self-police either way.

The discussion touches on practical enforcement challenges, with Junio acknowledging that AI-assisted fixes (like static analysis suggestions) may be indistinguishable from human work, especially for simple changes. He expresses no strong preference between the QEMU-derived policy and Christian's alternative, but leans toward keeping his current version since it aligns with existing mentoring program guidelines and has legal review from the Software Freedom Conservancy.

This exchange highlights the tension between legal risk mitigation and practical enforcement that has characterized the thread. Junio's position suggests the policy is likely to move forward in some form, with the exact wording being less critical than establishing clear boundaries around AI-generated content.
