This is a legal clarification in the ongoing AI policy discussion thread, specifically addressing copyright concerns around AI-generated content.

Michal Suchánek responds to Christian Couder's argument that AI output trained on open-source code would be GPL-compatible, pointing out that even permissive licenses like MIT still require attribution - a requirement current LLMs typically fail to meet. This directly challenges Couder's suggestion that training on open-source material could eliminate legal concerns, highlighting that attribution failures create legal uncertainty regardless of the training data's license.

The email reinforces the legal risks Brian Carlson and Junio Hamano have raised, though from a different angle - not just about training data provenance but about output compliance with basic open source requirements. It suggests the policy discussion needs to account for both the input (training data) and output (attribution) sides of the legal equation.

This intervention strengthens the case for caution around AI contributions by identifying an additional legal vulnerability that persists even in Couder's proposed "open-source-trained AI" scenario. The argument about MIT license requirements provides concrete technical grounding for the legal concerns, though it doesn't directly address Couder's broader point about existing review mechanisms being sufficient to catch problematic cases.
