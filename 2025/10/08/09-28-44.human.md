Christian Couder responds to Junio Hamano's proposed AI policy text with concerns about being overly restrictive and impractical. This is part of the ongoing policy discussion about how to handle AI-generated contributions in Git development.

Couder argues the current wording is too firm and could prevent legitimate uses of AI tools while failing to stop bad actors. He questions specific aspects like banning static analysis tools that might suggest fixes, listing specific prohibited tools by name, and creating an exception process that may be unworkable. 

The response continues Couder's position from earlier in the thread that existing review mechanisms can handle AI detection without special prohibitions. He suggests starting with a less restrictive policy and only tightening it if needed, rather than beginning with firm prohibitions that may be overbroad.

Key concerns raised include:
- The policy's firm wording may prevent acceptable uses like translation tools
- Listing specific AI tools could be misleading about what's actually prohibited
- The exception process may be impractical to implement
- The project shouldn't take responsibility for contributors' DCO compliance

This keeps the debate active around how to balance legal risks with practical policy implementation, with Couder advocating for a lighter-touch approach than Junio's QEMU-inspired proposal. The discussion now has multiple perspectives to reconcile in any v3 of the policy text.
