This is a process discussion email in the ongoing Git 3.0 release planning thread, focusing on timeline flexibility for SHA-256 adoption.

Taylor Blau responds to Patrick Steinhardt's proposal for gathering ecosystem input on SHA-256 readiness, agreeing with the need for coordination but advocating for pragmatic flexibility in deadlines. The key concern is how to handle implementations that either lack clear roadmaps or miss their projected timelines. Taylor suggests setting a reasonable baseline deadline but allowing some accommodation for minor delays (like one release cycle), while still preventing indefinite postponement of the SHA-256 transition.

The email reflects the broader thread's tension between maintaining momentum for the SHA-256 migration and ensuring ecosystem compatibility. Taylor's position lands between strict deadlines and complete flexibility, acknowledging that some implementations (like JGit or platform-specific cases mentioned earlier in the thread) may face legitimate delays. The discussion remains unresolved but moves toward defining what constitutes acceptable slippage" versus unacceptable delay.
