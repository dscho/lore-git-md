This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing AI contribution policy thread, with Christian Couder responding to Junio Hamano's proposed prohibition text. The email focuses on objections to the strictness and practicality of the proposed AI policy.

Couder argues against the firm prohibition approach, suggesting it may be overbroad and counterproductive. He raises concerns that the strict wording could prevent acceptable uses (like commit message translation) while failing to actually stop bad actors. The response also questions the practicality of distinguishing between prohibited and allowed AI uses, particularly around static analysis tools that may suggest fixes.

Key technical objections include: the listed tool examples being potentially misleading (as they may have legitimate uses), the impracticality of exception processes, and disagreement with the project assuming legal responsibility for contributor compliance. Couder suggests starting with a less strict policy and tightening only if needed, rather than beginning with firm prohibitions.

The email continues the thread's core tension between legal risk mitigation and practical policy application. It challenges Junio's QEMU-style prohibition approach while not directly addressing the new MIT license attribution concerns raised by Suchánek. The response maintains Couder's position that existing review mechanisms can handle AI detection without special procedures, though it doesn't propose alternative policy text.
