This is a discussion about documentation terminology in the ongoing `gitdatamodel.adoc` patch series. Junio Hamano weighs in on two specific points regarding the explanation of Git's data model.

First, he responds to Patrick Steinhardt's suggestion about how to explain Git's space efficiency. While agreeing with the technical accuracy, Junio suggests the proposed explanation about object compression might not be the most helpful first response to users who think commits can't be snapshots due to space concerns. He notes that the key insight is that most commits only change a few files, so the bulk of the tree is shared between commits.

Second, they discuss terminology for `refs/remotes/<remote>/HEAD`. While Patrick suggests calling it a "remote reference," Junio notes that historically it didn't track remote state but now does, and he's comfortable considering it a remote-tracking branch like others in `refs/remotes/*/`.

The exchange shows careful consideration of both pedagogical effectiveness and technical precision in documenting Git's internals, with Junio providing historical context about how remote HEAD references have evolved. This continues the thread's theme of balancing accessibility with accuracy in the new documentation.
