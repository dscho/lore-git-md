This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing thread about AI-generated contributions in Git. Christian Couder responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about enforcement mechanics for the proposed AI policy.

Couder argues that the process for evaluating potentially AI-generated patches should follow Git's existing patch review norms rather than requiring special procedures. He contends that if someone raises a credible concern that a patch appears AI-generated (backed by good arguments), the community and maintainers should evaluate those arguments alongside other patch merits, with Junio as the final arbiter in disagreements.

The email highlights a key tension: Junio wants to avoid subjective "looks AI-generated" judgments by focusing on whether submitters can explain their work, while Couder points out that bad actors could game such explanations by copying AI responses. He suggests the policy should allow maintainers to reject patches based on overall suspicious characteristics, similar to how the code of conduct allows action based on perceived harm rather than rigid checklists.

This exchange crystallizes a core debate in the policy formulation - whether to rely on subjective assessments of patch quality (with inherent false positive risks) versus attempting to define objective criteria for AI detection (with potential loopholes). Couder's position leans toward trusting maintainer judgment while acknowledging the inherent subjectivity.
