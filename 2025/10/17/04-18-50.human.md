This is a legal discussion thread about contributor agreements and copyright assignment in open source projects, specifically comparing Git's Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) approach with the Free Software Foundation's (FSF) copyright assignment process.

Theodore Ts'o responds to Collin Funk's earlier explanation of FSF's grantback provision by highlighting another significant concern with FSF's copyright assignment: the indemnification clause. He strongly advises against signing agreements containing such clauses without legal counsel, noting they could put personal assets at risk. Ts'o contrasts this with Git's DCO approach, which was vetted by Linux Foundation lawyers and avoids such onerous terms.

The email provides historical context about FSF's evolving policies, noting they've become more flexible over time - from requiring full copyright assignments to now sometimes accepting DCO-style contributions. This reflects broader industry pushback against FSF's traditional approach.

The discussion remains informational rather than proposing any changes to Git's processes. Ts'o reinforces the legal validity of Git's DCO model while providing additional perspective on why some developers avoid FSF's contribution requirements. The thread serves as useful background about different open source legal frameworks, though it doesn't suggest any action items for Git.
