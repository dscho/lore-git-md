This is a legal discussion thread continuation about contributor agreements and copyright assignment, specifically comparing Git's DCO approach with the FSF's policies.

Theodore Ts'o responds to Collin Funk's explanation of the FSF's grantback provision by highlighting another major concern with FSF copyright assignments: the indemnification clause. He strongly advises against signing agreements containing such clauses without legal counsel, noting they could expose contributors to significant personal financial risk. Ts'o contrasts this with the Linux Kernel's DCO (which Git also uses), emphasizing it was vetted by legal professionals from multiple organizations.

The email provides historical context about the FSF's evolving policies, from strict copyright assignment requirements to their current more flexible stance accepting DCO-style contributions in some cases. Ts'o references his own avoidance of FSF projects due to their earlier policies and suggests market pressure has driven the FSF's policy changes.

Key technical/legal details include:
- The specific indemnification language in FSF agreements ("indemnify and hold harmless")
- Reference to the Linux Foundation's legal vetting of the DCO
- FSF's current acceptance of DCO contributions in limited cases (with citation to their licensing blog)

This continues the thread's educational comparison between models while introducing new concerns about contributor liability. The discussion remains informational rather than proposing any Git changes, though it reinforces the DCO's legal standing through Ts'o's professional experience.
