This is a discussion follow-up in the geometric repacking maintenance strategy thread, addressing performance considerations for unreachable object checks.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Justin Tobler's question about the performance impact of checking for unreachable objects during all-into-one repacks (which occur when a geometric repack would merge all packs). He acknowledges this adds overhead but argues the relative impact is small compared to the already-expensive full repack operation. The key points are:

1. The reachability check is necessary to eventually prune unreachable objects, and doing it during full repacks is a reasonable tradeoff.

2. A more open question is whether full repacks (and thus reachability checks) should happen more frequently than just when packs would be completely merged. The current approach means unreachable objects may persist for long periods until enough new objects trigger a full repack.

3. Patrick suggests the initial implementation is acceptable but flags this as a potential area for future iteration, possibly incorporating time-based heuristics to trigger reachability checks more regularly.

The exchange highlights an important design consideration in the new maintenance strategy - balancing the performance benefits of geometric repacking with the need to eventually clean up unreachable objects. The discussion remains technical and constructive, with Patrick acknowledging valid concerns while defending the current approach as a reasonable starting point.
