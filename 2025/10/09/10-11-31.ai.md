This is a follow-up discussion in the refs modernization thread, focusing on naming conventions for reference-related data structures. Toon Claes weighs in on the debate between Patrick Steinhardt and Jeff King about whether `struct reference` is appropriately named.

The email supports Patrick's position that `struct reference` accurately represents what a reference is (containing refname, target, oid, flags, and peeled_oid), but suggests improving the documentation comment to clarify its purpose. Toon argues for keeping the generic name rather than making assumptions about future use cases, noting that naming can be revisited if conflicts arise.

The discussion also touches on the problematic naming of `struct ref`, acknowledging Jeff King's concerns about its complexity due to `peer_ref` links connecting both local and remote refs. Toon remains neutral on whether `struct ref` should be renamed to `struct remote_ref`, recognizing the practical challenges this would entail.

Key technical points:
- The debate centers on whether `struct reference` should be tied specifically to iteration context (Jeff's preference) or serve as a general reference representation (Patrick/Toon view)
- `peer_ref` links complicate any renaming of `struct ref` since they connect both local and remote references
- Documentation improvements suggested to clarify `struct reference`'s role in iteration callbacks
