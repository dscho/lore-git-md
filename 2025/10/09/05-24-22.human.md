Security proposal discussion on preventing arbitrary code execution in untrusted repositories.

Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on the ongoing security discussion, bringing a more incremental approach to the problem. He identifies two separate concerns: (1) defining what "unsafe" behavior looks like (disabling certain configs and hooks), and (2) determining how to mark repositories as safe/unsafe (where the token proposal fits). 

Peff suggests focusing first on implementing the unsafe behavior mechanics before settling on the safety marking system. He proposes starting with opt-in mechanisms like environment variables (`GIT_ASSUME_UNSAFE`) or command-line flags (`--assume-unsafe`) rather than immediately changing defaults. This would allow real-world testing before committing to a particular safety marking approach like the token system.

The email provides useful historical context, linking to a previous discussion, and positions itself as a moderating voice between the more radical token proposal and the config-based alternatives. Peff's suggestion to decouple the problems and gather experience before making permanent changes represents a pragmatic maintainer perspective, though it doesn't directly engage with the specific technical merits of either the token or config-based approaches currently under debate.
