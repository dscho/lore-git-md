Ongoing discussion about refs subsystem naming conventions post-merge of Patrick Steinhardt's refactoring series. This email continues the debate about whether `struct reference` (introduced in the series) is too generic, and whether the legacy `struct ref` should be renamed to better reflect its remote-specific purpose.

Steinhardt argues that `struct reference` is appropriately named as it represents the core concept of a Git reference, similar to how `struct object` serves as the fundamental type in Git's object system. He suggests this could enable future refs subsystem improvements, like making peeling available outside iterator contexts. 

Jeff King's concern about `struct reference` being too generic is acknowledged, but Steinhardt counters that having a central reference type owned by the refs subsystem could be beneficial. The discussion also touches on renaming `struct ref` to `struct remote_ref`, though both note complications from its `peer_ref` links that connect local and remote refs.

This is a technical design discussion between experienced contributors about long-term naming patterns in Git's core. The conversation remains constructive, with both sides acknowledging valid points while maintaining their positions. No immediate action is proposed - this appears to be establishing context for potential future refactoring work.
