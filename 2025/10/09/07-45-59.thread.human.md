This thread centers on a technical discussion about how to best organize include statements in Git's Windows compatibility code (`compat/mingw.c`). Johannes Schindelin (Git for Windows maintainer) originally proposed converting relative includes to project-relative paths and alphabetizing them, sparking a debate about include styles.

The discussion has revealed three main perspectives: Schindelin advocates for project-relative paths (`#include "compat/foo.h"`) for consistency, while Johannes Sixt (Hannes) argues relative paths (`#include "../foo.h"`) better show file relationships. Junio Hamano (Git maintainer) has now weighed in with a more nuanced view, distinguishing between project-local headers (preferring full paths) and system-replacement headers (preferring angle brackets).

While there's agreement on alphabetizing the includes, the path style debate remains unresolved. Hamano pragmatically suggests accepting the current patch despite the philosophical differences, noting two missed cases (`win32.h` and `win32/lazyload.h`) that could be addressed later. Patrick Steinhardt had earlier suggested extending this cleanup to the `refs/` directory, but that discussion is on hold pending resolution of this more fundamental question.

The thread shows the project's careful approach to even seemingly small changes, with maintainers and Windows experts debating both technical merits and long-term conventions. While no firm decision has been made, Hamano's intervention suggests the series will likely proceed in its current form, with potential follow-up work to refine the approach.
