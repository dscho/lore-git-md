This is a discussion follow-up in the ongoing thread about documenting and potentially deprecating the `--committer-date-is-author-date` option in Git's `am` and `rebase` commands.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Junio C Hamano's defense of using the term "lie" (rather than "override") in the documentation for the timestamp-manipulating option. The author reflects on their initial misunderstanding of the option's purpose, acknowledging they previously viewed it as a "vanity" feature but now recognizes the technical consequences of misusing committer dates. The discussion highlights the conceptual difference between author dates (which contributors naturally understand as reflecting actual work timelines) versus committer dates (whose purpose is less intuitive to many users).

The exchange reinforces the documentation direction to use strong "lie" terminology when describing committer date manipulation, as this accurately conveys the technical risks (timestamp ordering violations) while discouraging casual misuse. Kristoffer notes this framing helps users sharpen their understanding of when such manipulation is appropriate versus when it creates problems. The thread continues to focus on refining language that properly conveys the technical gravity of timestamp manipulation while maintaining clarity for end users.
