This is a post-merge discussion about naming conventions in Git's refs subsystem, specifically focusing on the distinction between `struct reference` (new) and `struct ref` (legacy). The thread follows Patrick Steinhardt's recently merged 14-patch series that refactored tag peeling behavior and ref iteration interfaces.

Jeff King reiterates his concern that `struct reference` is too generic a name for its specific purpose of passing ref data to iterator callbacks, worrying developers might misuse it in other contexts. While acknowledging Patrick's counterpoint that `struct ref` is the misnamed entity (as it contains remote-specific data), Jeff remains skeptical about renaming it to `struct remote_ref`, noting the structure handles both local and remote refs via `peer_ref` links.

The discussion highlights two key structural questions: whether the new iteration-focused `struct reference` needs a more context-specific name, and whether the legacy `struct ref` should be renamed to better reflect its remote-related purpose. Jeff emphasizes the practical consideration that while `struct ref` may be poorly named, changing it would be disruptive due to its widespread use.

Technical context from the merged series remains relevant: `struct reference` contains refname, target/symbolic destination, OID, flags, and peeled OID, while `struct ref` holds remote-specific fetch/push tracking data.
