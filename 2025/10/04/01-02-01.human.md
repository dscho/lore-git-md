This is a discussion about C vs Rust language features, sparked by the ongoing Rust integration work in Git. Eric Wong responds to Ezekiel Newren's comparison of C and Rust capabilities, offering counterpoints about why certain Rust features may not be as advantageous as they appear.

The email focuses on several key areas where Wong believes C can hold its own against Rust, or where Rust's features come with hidden costs. He questions the practical benefits of Rust's borrow checker (citing compile time impacts), async/await (memory usage concerns), and package management (centralization issues). Wong also notes that many safety features Newren attributes to Rust can be approximated in modern C through compiler warnings, attributes, and coding discipline.

Wong expresses skepticism about wholesale adoption of Rust, preferring incremental improvements to C and maintaining a clearer separation between high-level and low-level languages. The tone is technical but opinionated, reflecting long-standing debates in systems programming circles. While not directly opposing the current Rust integration work, the email serves as a reminder of the tradeoffs involved in language choices.

This discussion appears tangential to the main Rust integration series (which has already received acks for merging) but captures important philosophical differences that may resurface in future language-related decisions for Git.
