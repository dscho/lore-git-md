Oswald Buddenhagen continues the discussion about cherry-pick tracking with a rebuttal to Junio Hamano's defense of Git's current ancestry-based model. This is part of the ongoing debate about whether to improve cherry-pick metadata through hooks (Villemoes), git-notes (Buddenhagen), or existing mechanisms (carlson).

Buddenhagen argues that Hamano's ideal trunk-first workflow, while technically sound, is impractical in real-world development scenarios. He lists several concrete problems: difficulty determining oldest target branches, excessive testing requirements in CI-heavy environments, messy merge conflicts, and complex history visualization. The email suggests these practical constraints make cherry-picks a necessary evil that should be better supported rather than discouraged.

The discussion takes an interesting turn as Buddenhagen expands the scope beyond cherry-picks, proposing a more general commit provenance system that could also track rebases and amends. This would address stale cherry-pick links while providing broader change tracking capabilities, referencing an earlier thread about standardizing change-ids. The proposal remains conceptual but suggests git-notes as the implementation vehicle, building on Hamano's earlier positive reaction to that approach.

The exchange highlights the tension between Git's ancestry-focused design philosophy and real-world workflow needs, with Buddenhagen making a pragmatic case for better tooling around non-ancestral relationships while acknowledging the complexity such a system would introduce.
