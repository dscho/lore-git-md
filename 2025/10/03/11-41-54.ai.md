This is a continuation of the cherry-pick tracking discussion, with Oswald Buddenhagen responding to Junio Hamano's technical defense of Git's current cherry-pick implementation and trunk-first development philosophy. The email pushes back against Hamano's ideal workflow assumptions while proposing an expanded vision for commit provenance tracking.

Buddenhagen argues that Hamano's preferred trunk-first merge workflow makes unrealistic assumptions about developer discipline and faces practical barriers in large projects with slow CI systems. He outlines four specific challenges: difficulty determining oldest target branches, excessive testing requirements, merge conflicts requiring messy fixups, and complex history visualization. The response suggests these realities make cherry-picks with proper tracking a more practical solution than enforcing merge-based workflows.

The email then expands Buddenhagen's earlier git-notes proposal into a broader system for tracking various commit relationships (cherry-picks, rebases, amends) as "weak provenance info" that could also address change-id standardization needs. This would require handling stale links through garbage collection but could provide more flexible history tracking than ancestry-based approaches. The proposal remains conceptual, with acknowledgement that "lots of details" need working out.

Key technical elements include the ongoing debate between ancestry purity (Hamano) versus practical relationship tracking (Buddenhagen), with this email focusing on real-world workflow constraints that complicate ideal merge-based approaches. The git-notes implementation path remains central, now framed as part of a larger commit provenance system that would need to handle rewrite operations and stale reference cleanup.
