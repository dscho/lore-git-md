Junio Hamano responds to Christian Couder's defense of using stylistic patterns to detect AI-generated contributions in the ongoing policy discussion about AI-assisted patches. This is a key exchange in the thread's central debate about enforcement mechanics.

Hamano reiterates his preference for an explanation-based standard over pattern recognition, arguing that requiring contributors to explain their code is both a more reliable test of authorship and avoids subjective debates about whether something "looks AI-generated." He raises a concrete scenario where maintainers might disagree about whether a patch appears AI-generated, noting this could lead to unproductive arguments. 

The core of Hamano's position is that the policy's primary goal - ensuring contributors have rights to their submissions - is better served by focusing on whether they can demonstrate understanding of their work rather than debating its stylistic characteristics. This maintains the thread's focus on practical enforcement while challenging Couder's proposed hybrid approach that combines explanation requirements with pattern recognition.

The exchange highlights the unresolved tension between objective enforcement criteria (which pattern recognition might provide) and the project's need for clear, legally-defensible standards (which explanation requirements better address). Hamano's response suggests he remains unconvinced that stylistic analysis adds meaningful value beyond what explanation requirements already provide.
