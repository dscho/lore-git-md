This is a policy documentation patch in the ongoing discussion about AI-generated contributions in Git. Christian Couder responds to Chuck Wolber's concerns about the v2 proposal to restrict AI-generated content in project contributions.

The response clarifies several points from the earlier discussion: Christian maintains that the policy doesn't introduce new risks of false accusations against human contributors, explains why requiring explicit declarations of AI use (as suggested by declare-ai.org) would be impractical, and defends the need for some subjective judgment calls about what constitutes problematic AI-generated content. He acknowledges the proposal could be misinterpreted as a complete ban on AI assistance and is open to wording improvements to avoid this.

Key takeaways from the response:
1) The policy aims to discourage wholesale AI-generated submissions while still allowing responsible AI assistance
2) Christian sees pattern recognition (like detecting AI stylistic markers) as a necessary enforcement tool alongside contributor explanations
3) The proposal is evolving - Christian has already agreed to soften language from "anything that looks AI generated" to "significantly AI generated" in v3
4) The response provides concrete examples of why comprehensive AI-use declarations would be burdensome and difficult to define

The exchange shows the policy is nearing final form, with the main outstanding questions being about precise wording rather than fundamental approach. Christian appears willing to incorporate constructive feedback into v3 while maintaining the core protections against problematic AI use.
