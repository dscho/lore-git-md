This is a policy discussion email in the ongoing thread about AI-generated contributions to Git. Randall Becker proposes a legal declaration approach as an alternative to the current debate between explanation requirements versus stylistic pattern recognition for detecting AI-generated code.

The email suggests requiring contributors to include explicit legal statements in their cover letters or commit messages asserting: (1) their authority to submit the patch, (2) originality of the work, and (3) either non-AI origin or verification of AI-generated content's legality. Becker frames this as a Conservancy-maintained agreement that would shift responsibility to contributors while providing legal protection.

The proposal enters a thread where maintainers are currently debating enforcement mechanisms - Junio Hamano favors relying solely on explanation requirements while Christian Couder advocates combining explanations with stylistic analysis. Becker's approach represents a third path focused on legal declarations rather than detection methods.

This suggestion appears well-intentioned but may face practical challenges. The thread has previously rejected similar verification-heavy approaches (like declare-ai.org) as unworkable. The proposal also doesn't address how maintainers would verify the truthfulness of such declarations or handle cases where AI-generated content slips through despite the statement. It does however refocus the discussion on the core legal concerns that motivated the policy.
