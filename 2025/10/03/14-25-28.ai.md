Documentation policy patch in the ongoing AI contribution policy series, responding to legal and phrasing concerns from Brian M. Carlson. This email from Christian Couder defends the v2 proposal's nuanced approach against calls for more absolute prohibitions, while acknowledging the need for clearer language.

The core tension is between legal certainty (Carlson's push for unambiguous prohibitions) and practical flexibility (Couder's insistence that some AI-assisted contributions should be permitted). Couder argues the current "significantly AI generated" threshold better accommodates legitimate uses like translation assistance, style checking, and small-scale refactoring where human oversight is clear. He rejects both Carlson's suggested blanket ban and the declare-ai.org model as impractical extremes.

Key technical clarification: The policy intentionally avoids requiring contributors to document LLM training data provenance, as Couder considers this unverifiable. He also resists defining precise quantitative limits ("too big") in favor of qualitative human judgment. The email reveals an unstated assumption that human involvement in AI-assisted work creates sufficient copyright basis, with stylistic anomalies serving as review-time proxies for assessing this.

Open questions persist around operationalizing the "significant" threshold and whether to incorporate Carlson's suggested positive examples of acceptable AI use. The response indicates willingness to refine phrasing but maintains the fundamental dual enforcement approach (explanation + pattern recognition) against Carlson's preference for absolute prohibitions. Formatting corrections from v1 remain pending, with v3 expected to address these while preserving the policy's graduated response to AI assistance levels.
