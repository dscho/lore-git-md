Policy documentation update in the ongoing AI contribution policy discussion.

This is Christian Couder's response to Junio Hamano's feedback on the v2 proposal to ban AI-generated contributions in Git. The discussion centers on how to phrase the prohibition - while Hamano suggested focusing on whether contributors can explain their work, Couder argues for explicitly calling out AI-generated content as grounds for rejection. He provides a concrete example where stylistic patterns (like overuse of "--" and "absolutely") could indicate AI generation, even if not definitive proof.

The exchange shows the policy taking clearer shape: it will likely include both the ability-to-explain standard (per Hamano's preference) and explicit AI-generation language (per Couder's argument). Couder plans to refine the wording in v3 to say "significantly AI generated" rather than just "AI generated", showing responsiveness to feedback while maintaining the core position. The thread continues to balance legal concerns (DCO compliance) with practical enforcement considerations.
