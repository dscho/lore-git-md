This is a licensing policy discussion in the ongoing Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing the GitOxide-compatible licensing proposal for Git's Rust code.

Jeff King clarifies the distinction between Johannes Schindelin's GitOxide-specific licensing exception approach and Phillip Wood's interpretation of a more general permissive license. King notes that Schindelin's proposal (as quoted by Wood) is narrowly targeted at GitOxide, similar to libgit2's git.git-authors file precedent, rather than being a broad linking exception.

The email highlights two key legal challenges: (1) the wording of any relicensing grant (whether project-specific or general), and (2) the practical difficulties in obtaining relicensing approval for existing code that has been touched by many contributors over Git's history. King suggests these are separate concerns that need to be addressed regardless of whether the exception is GitOxide-specific or more general.

This continues the thread's deepening examination of licensing strategy options, with King maintaining focus on implementation challenges rather than advocating for any particular approach. The discussion remains grounded in practical considerations around contributor agreements and historical code ownership, while the broader policy debate between targeted exceptions versus general permissive licensing continues unresolved.
