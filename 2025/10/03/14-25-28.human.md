Documentation policy discussion on AI-generated contributions, with Christian Couder responding to Brian M. Carlson's feedback on the v2 proposal. This is part of the ongoing effort to establish Git's stance on AI contributions, building on earlier discussions with Junio Hamano and addressing Chuck Wolber's critiques.

Couder pushes back against Carlson's suggestion for an outright ban on AI-generated content, arguing that some AI-assisted contributions (like translations, refactoring, or line wrapping) should be acceptable when human involvement is sufficient. He maintains the proposal's dual enforcement approach - requiring both explanation capability from contributors and stylistic pattern recognition from reviewers - while acknowledging the fuzzy boundary between acceptable and problematic AI use.

Key points in this response include: (1) rejecting the idea that Git should track LLM training data copyrights as impractical, (2) clarifying that the policy aims to prohibit only "significantly AI generated" content rather than all AI use, and (3) defending the intentionally vague "careful and responsible" language as a prompt for thoughtful consideration rather than a hard rule. The email reveals ongoing tension between wanting clear policy boundaries and the practical realities of AI-assisted contributions.

This continues to refine the policy's language while leaving open questions about exactly how to define the threshold for unacceptable AI generation. The discussion appears to be converging toward a prohibition-with-exceptions model grounded in DCO compliance, with enforcement relying on both contributor explanations and reviewer judgment of code characteristics.
