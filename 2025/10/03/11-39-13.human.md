This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's push optimization behavior when servers already have most objects. Sainan provides a concrete test case showing that even with bitmaps enabled, a branch that diverged 1000 commits ago (with only ~30 new small commits) still pushes 475,000 objects when it should theoretically only transfer about 100.

The email challenges Jeff King's earlier suggestion that `git repack -abd` might solve the object reuse issue, demonstrating that in real-world scenarios with significant divergence, bitmap-based optimization still falls short. This suggests the problem may be more complex than initially thought, particularly for long-lived feature branches in active repositories.

The case highlights a practical limitation of the current push optimization strategy, where even with bitmaps, the amount of transferred data can be orders of magnitude larger than necessary. This continues the thread's exploration of how Git might better recognize and leverage existing server-side objects during push operations.
