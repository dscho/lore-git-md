This is a licensing policy discussion in the ongoing Git Rust infrastructure thread, specifically addressing the GitOxide compatibility question. Phillip Wood responds to Jeff King's analysis of two distinct legal challenges in relicensing Git's Rust code.

Wood acknowledges King's separation of concerns between (1) crafting a GitOxide-specific exception wording and (2) obtaining contributor approval for relicensing existing code. He raises new concerns about the practicality of project-specific exceptions, referencing libgit2's git.git-authors precedent while questioning its scalability.

The email highlights growing consensus around needing formal legal guidance, with Wood explicitly suggesting consultation with the Software Freedom Conservancy. He inquires whether this was discussed at the recent Contributor Summit, indicating this may become an agenda item for future project governance discussions.

Key unresolved questions from this exchange:
- Viability of project-specific exceptions versus general permissive licensing
- Administrative burden of tracking and maintaining multiple license grants
- Need for SFC's legal analysis to inform next steps
- Whether Contributor Summit discussions yielded relevant insights
