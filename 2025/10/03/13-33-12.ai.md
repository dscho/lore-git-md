This is a documentation patch in the ongoing thread about establishing Git's policy on AI-generated contributions. Christian Couder responds to Chuck Wolber's concerns about the v2 proposal, defending the core approach while showing openness to wording refinements.

The email focuses on three key rebuttals to Wolber's critique: (1) maintains that false accusation risks are not materially increased by the policy, (2) rejects the declare-ai.org approach as impractical given the varied and often invisible ways AI might assist contributions, and (3) clarifies that the policy targets "significantly AI generated" content rather than all AI use. Couder provides concrete examples of the operational challenges that would arise from requiring declarations of all AI assistance, particularly around edge cases like IDE integrations and multi-AI workflows.

Notable technical details include the proposed softening of language from "anything that looks AI generated" to "significantly AI generated" (previously discussed with Junio Hamano), and the distinction between outright rejection versus iterative improvement of AI-tinged patches. The response also reveals unstated policy assumptions - that human involvement in AI-assisted work creates sufficient copyright basis under DCO, and that stylistic markers serve as proxies for assessing this involvement.

The thread advances with Couder maintaining the dual enforcement approach (explanation + pattern recognition) while signaling willingness to: (1) further refine prohibition phrasing, (2) reduce perceived redundancy with DCO section, and (3) clarify that the policy doesn't ban all AI use. Wolber's core concern about judging patches on technical merit alone remains unaddressed, setting up a likely continuation of this philosophical debate in future replies. The response notably does not engage with Wolber's suggestion to model the policy after existing quality standards rather than creating AI-specific rules.
