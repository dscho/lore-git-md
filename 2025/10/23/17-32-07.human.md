Git project lead Junio Hamano makes an executive decision to move forward with the AI contribution policy, opting for the SFC-lawyer-reviewed version that closely follows QEMU's model. He acknowledges the practical enforcement challenges between the two proposed versions are minimal, especially for simple fixes where contributors may not even realize AI assistance was used. The decision prioritizes having any policy over perfecting the wording, with the understanding that adjustments can still be made later.

Junio addresses Elijah Newren's earlier concern about grandfathering existing AI-assisted contributions (like d12166d3c8bb) by noting that problematic contributions - whether AI-generated or not - would need to be reverted regardless of origin. This maintains consistency with how Git handles other guideline violations retroactively. The message signals the policy will soon land in the 'next' branch, marking a key step in formalizing Git's stance on AI-generated content while leaving room for future refinements.
