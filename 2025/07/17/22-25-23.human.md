This is a policy discussion in the ongoing Rust integration thread. Taylor Blau weighs in on several key points from the RFC series that proposes making Rust a hard dependency while optimizing xdiff performance.

Taylor supports the Rust dependency overall, arguing there's never a perfect time for such a change and that memory safety will attract new contributors. He expresses concern about maintaining parallel C/Rust build paths, fearing it would create maintenance headaches and inconsistent feature support across builds. On platform support, he agrees with brian m. carlson that platform maintainers should handle Rust toolchain availability rather than blocking progress.

The most substantive part of Taylor's response focuses on Rust version policy. While admitting limited expertise in Rust's release cadence, he endorses brian m. carlson's proposal to align with Debian stable's version plus one year (resulting in ~3 year support windows). This positions him as supportive of the cautious, distro-friendly approach while deferring to brian's technical judgment on the specifics. The tone is constructive and consensus-seeking, typical of Taylor's mailing list style.
