This RFC thread continues to explore the complex policy and technical challenges around introducing Rust code into Git's xdiff component. The discussion has now split into two main tracks: enterprise compliance requirements (led by Becker and Newren) and licensing concerns (with Wood, King, and Schindelen as key voices). 

The latest contribution from Yee Cheng Chin adds a new dimension to the licensing debate by highlighting how the current lack of explicit license headers in the Rust xdiff implementation could inadvertently break compatibility with downstream projects that rely on xdiff's LGPL status, particularly libgit2 and Vim/Neovim. This surfaces a concrete technical consequence of the broader licensing uncertainty that Phillip Wood had previously raised regarding Rust code adoption.

While the technical implementation (ivec interop layer, module structure) remains stable, the policy discussions continue to deepen. Wood has now formally suggested consulting the Software Freedom Conservancy for legal analysis, particularly regarding the viability of GitOxide-style exceptions versus broader relicensing. The upcoming Contributor Summit may provide a venue to advance these governance discussions, though no concrete outcomes have emerged yet. Meanwhile, enterprise concerns around compliance validation, LTS branches, and NonStop platform support remain unresolved, keeping the technical implementation effectively on hold pending these policy decisions.
