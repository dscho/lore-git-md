This thread continues the long-running debate about introducing Rust into Git's codebase, now focused on transition timing and platform compatibility. The RFC series for Rust infrastructure in xdiff (currently at v3) remains blocked on policy grounds despite technical implementation being largely complete. Patrick Steinhardt has proposed a tactical shift to test Rust/C interoperability through a minimal component (`varint.c`) while maintaining Rust as optional until Git 3.0.

Key technical points settled include the design of `ivec` for C/Rust interop (after extensive discussion about why Rust's native `Vec` won't work), build system changes under Junio's review, and style decisions like header inclusion conventions. The community has largely coalesced around Steinhardt's gradual adoption plan, now with formal support from Gentoo (via Sam James), though Brian Carlson continues advocating for faster adoption based on demonstrated productivity gains in his own Rust work.

The debate now centers on platform support tradeoffs. Randall Becker has highlighted NonStop's commercial status and revealed its maintenance relies on individual effort rather than vendor resources, while Ramsay Jones reports concrete impact on Cygwin where Git builds have already stopped due to Rust unavailability. Carlson counters that platform maintainers should adapt to Rust rather than holding back adoption, pointing to LLVM's cross-compilation advantages and arguing that gccrs's outdated Rust 1.49 target wouldn't solve platform issues anyway.

Junio Hamano has raised new concerns about the practical scope of optional Rust components and the unresolved status of existing `libgit-*` directories in contrib/. Steinhardt's latest response emphasizes distributor preparation needs and draws parallels to Git's historical C99 adoption, suggesting small test components could validate the approach without forcing immediate full adoption. The thread shows growing consensus around a Git 3.0 timeline for mandatory Rust, but fundamental questions remain about platform exclusion and long-term maintenance sustainability.
