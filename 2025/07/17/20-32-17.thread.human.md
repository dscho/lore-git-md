This RFC thread continues to refine the Rust infrastructure proposal for Git, with the policy debate now focusing on transition mechanics and distributor impact. The technical implementation for xdiff (including Rust modules and the `ivec` interop layer) remains stable, while build system adaptations for optional Rust components are under review.

Patrick Steinhardt and Elijah Newren have narrowed their differences, with Newren now supporting an optional-feature transition path while challenging some of Steinhardt's framing. The key sticking points revolve around whether Git's three-release transition period (established in v2.49.0 release notes) has already satisfied the notice requirement, or whether additional time is needed for distributors to prepare. Steinhardt emphasizes the practical challenges this creates for backporting fixes when parallel C/Rust implementations exist, while Newren argues the technical groundwork is already sufficient.

Junio Hamano remains cautiously engaged, suggesting `patch-delta.c` as a more meaningful test case than the current `varint.c` proposal. Brian Carlson's SHA-1/SHA-256 compatibility efforts may be affected by these transition rules, though Steinhardt leaves open the possibility of treating it as a "new feature" exception. The discussion has moved beyond whether to include Rust to how best to manage the transition, with distributor concerns (particularly around LTS support and backporting) now taking center stage alongside the ongoing technical implementation work.
