This RFC thread continues to explore the Rust infrastructure proposal for Git, now focusing on licensing policy as it relates to ecosystem coordination. Johannes Schindelin has proposed adopting GPLv2 with an exception clause specifically allowing Gitoxide to reuse Git's Rust code, citing lessons from libgit2's historical licensing challenges that hindered code sharing. 

The discussion has bifurcated into two major policy tracks: enterprise compliance requirements (led by Randall Becker's concerns about CVE SLAs and LTS branches for regulated industries) and now this new licensing dimension. Jeff King's latest contribution raises important legal concerns about derivative works, noting that even conceptually similar Rust rewrites of C code may create licensing complications despite not being direct ports. He points out that truly permissive reuse would require either clean-room implementations or explicit relicensing permissions - both challenging to implement in practice.

While the technical implementation remains stable (with the `ivec` interop layer solidifying), the governance discussions are becoming more complex. Schindelin's licensing proposal aims to avoid the ecosystem fragmentation seen with libgit2, but King's analysis suggests the legal reality may be more nuanced. Meanwhile, Elijah Newren's maintainer proposal for the Rust transition continues to face operational challenges, particularly around enterprise requirements. The thread now awaits clearer documentation on both the maintainer structure and licensing strategy before these parallel policy tracks can converge.
