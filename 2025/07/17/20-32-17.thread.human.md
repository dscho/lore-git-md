This RFC thread continues to explore the complex policy and technical challenges around introducing Rust infrastructure into Git, now in its third iteration. The discussion has branched into three main tracks: enterprise compliance requirements, licensing concerns (particularly around xdiff's LGPL compatibility), and technical implementation details.

On the licensing front, Johannes Schindelin's latest email confirms that project-specific licensing exceptions - previously considered as a potential path forward - are no longer viable. He cites a prior SFC decision (referenced in brian m. carlson's email) that effectively closes this option, affecting not just GitOxide but also projects like Jujutsu. This development narrows the solution space, reinforcing the need to address licensing through formal PLC/SFC consultation channels.

The technical work remains on hold pending policy resolution, though the core architecture (including the `ivec` interop layer and module structure) remains stable. Meanwhile, new concerns have emerged about xdiff's license headers potentially creating LGPL inheritance issues that could impact compatibility with libgit2 and Vim/Neovim. 

Key open questions center on enterprise requirements (LTS branches, compliance validation), platform support (particularly NonStop's Rust toolchain timeline), and the mechanics of implementing any eventual licensing solution through the PLC and SFC. While there appears to be no fundamental opposition to relicensing conceptually, the practical challenges - now compounded by the elimination of the exceptions path - remain substantial.
