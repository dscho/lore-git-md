The RFC thread on introducing Rust infrastructure for Git's xdiff subsystem is moving toward consensus on a transition plan, with Phillip Wood's three-phase proposal gaining support. Wood's approach balances technical readiness (addressing Ezekiel Newren's arguments) with distributor concerns (raised by Patrick Steinhardt and Sam James), proposing a 6-month optional period with Rust enabled by default, followed by mandatory Rust adoption, and concluding with 3 years of security updates for the last Rust-optional version.

Steinhardt has now responded to Wood's proposal, expressing general agreement while suggesting additional documentation. He references his own "BreakingChanges" document as a potential framework for formally documenting this transition, recommending it be highlighted in release notes. This indicates the policy discussion is converging, with Steinhardt's response showing alignment on the timeline while focusing on governance aspects.

The technical implementation remains stable, with Rust modules and the `ivec` interop layer unchanged. Build system changes now include Wood's proposed NO_RUST=1 escape hatch, and parallel C/Rust implementations would be maintained during the transition. While some open questions remain (particularly around platform support and documentation), the thread shows policy consensus emerging around Wood's balanced timeline, allowing technical work to proceed with clearer governance guardrails.
