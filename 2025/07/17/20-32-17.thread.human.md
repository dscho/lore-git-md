This RFC thread continues to explore the complex policy questions around introducing Rust code into Git, with the technical implementation of the xdiff infrastructure now stable while licensing and enterprise compliance discussions intensify. 

The core debate has split into two tracks: enterprise compliance (led by Randall Becker's concerns about NonStop support and regulated industry requirements) and licensing (where Johannes Schindelin's GPLv2+exception proposal has drawn both support and skepticism). Jeff King has advocated for a general permissive licensing approach rather than GitOxide-specific exceptions, while Phillip Wood has raised practical concerns about license tracking and the risk of "laundering" through projects like GitOxide. Patrick Steinhardt has now proposed involving the Software Freedom Conservancy to evaluate LGPL or GPL-with-exception options, marking the first concrete step toward resolving the legal impasse.

The latest email from Phillip Wood highlights a key tension point - whether to adopt Schindelin's narrowly-scoped GitOxide exception (modeled after libgit2's historical approach) or King's broader permissive license. Wood questions the scalability of project-specific exceptions while acknowledging libgit2's git.git-authors file as a relevant precedent. As the discussion continues to reference these historical examples, the community appears to be awaiting SFC guidance before reaching consensus on a licensing path forward. Meanwhile, the technical work on Rust infrastructure continues unaffected, with the `ivec` interop layer stable and module structure solidified.
