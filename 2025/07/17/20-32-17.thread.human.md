This RFC thread continues to explore the technical and policy implications of introducing Rust infrastructure into Git's core, now in its third iteration. The discussion has shifted from whether to adopt Rust to how to manage the transition, with Ezekiel Newren and Patrick Steinhardt converging on an optional-feature approach. The technical implementation for xdiff (starting with simple subsystems like `varint.c`) is largely settled, with Rust modules and the `ivec` interop layer finalized.

The latest email from Phillip Wood brings a packaging perspective, questioning whether the v2.49.0 release notes provided sufficient notice of Rust's eventual mandatory adoption. He highlights the practical difference between optional Rust bindings (currently shipping) and future core requirements, particularly for platforms lacking Rust support. Wood's concerns about security updates and long-term maintenance align with Steinhardt's earlier distributor-focused arguments, while reinforcing that the mailing list's technical discussions may not adequately reach the ecosystem.

Key open questions now center on transition mechanics: how to handle backporting for non-Rust platforms, whether to require parallel C implementations for security fixes, and what constitutes proper ecosystem notification. Junio Hamano continues to guide the technical approach, endorsing a phased rollout starting with simple subsystems. While technical consensus has largely formed around the implementation, the policy debate remains active as contributors work to balance innovation with stability across Git's diverse platform support.
