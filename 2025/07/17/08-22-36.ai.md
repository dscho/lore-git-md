This is a performance optimization discussion in the ongoing thread about converting commit traversal to use priority queues. René Scharfe follows up on Jeff King's earlier mention of unpublished work converting `rev_info.commits` to a `prio_queue`, explaining his previous unsuccessful attempts to avoid this change. 

Sarfe had initially tried to keep `rev_info.commits` as a `commit_list` due to concerns about `prio_queue`'s memory overhead, lack of type safety, and dual stack/queue usage. However, his attempts resulted in an 8% performance regression in `get_revision_1()` compared to King's approach which showed no regression. After re-evaluating with the new prio_queue_replace()` helper from the current series, Scharfe confirms the performance issue persists and concludes there's no alternative to converting `rev_info.commits` to a `prio_queue`.

This email signals a potential future direction for the optimization work, suggesting Scharfe may propose extending the priority queue conversion to `rev_info.commits` after the current series lands. The discussion highlights the tradeoffs between type safety/abstraction and performance in Git's core data structures, with empirical measurements ultimately favoring the performance optimization despite the architectural concerns.
