Performance optimization follow-up for `git blame` commit parsing. Han Young responds to earlier feedback from Patrick Steinhardt and Junio Hamano about their patch that aimed to skip full commit parsing when only basic author data is needed. The author now shares concrete measurements showing the optimization provides negligible speed improvements (less than 1 second on a 180-second test case), likely within system noise. They also clarify that the call sites in question only access author information before discarding the commit data.

Most significantly, the author now agrees with Junio's suggestion to remove the partial parsing capability entirely, since all callers ultimately request full commit information anyway. They plan to submit a new patch simplifying the code path by eliminating the unused optimization rather than maintaining it. This represents a pivot from the original approach - instead of expanding the optimization's use, they'll remove it as unnecessary complexity. The thread demonstrates Git's performance culture where even small optimizations require empirical validation and may be removed if they don't provide measurable benefits.
