This is a response in the ongoing Rust integration discussion, focusing on platform support and dependency management concerns raised by Gentoo maintainers. Patrick Steinhardt engages with Sam James' specific points about Rust's ecosystem challenges, acknowledging the validity of the concerns while maintaining support for Rust adoption if certain conditions can be met.

The email addresses three key issues: (1) platform support limitations due to Rust's LLVM dependency, where Steinhardt suggests deferring Rustification if alternative implementations like gccrs might provide near-term solutions; (2) Rust's lack of LTS releases and version churn, which Steinhardt agrees is problematic for system-level software like Git; and (3) supply chain risks from crate dependencies, where he advocates for Git's traditional conservative approach to dependencies.

Steinhardt's position balances technical pragmatism with project direction - he's willing to consider delaying Rust adoption if platform support solutions are imminent, but also recognizes the impracticality of waiting indefinitely. His comments about version targeting and dependency selection provide concrete considerations for how Git might implement Rust integration while mitigating risks, though he doesn't propose specific solutions to these challenges. The response maintains the thread's focus on weighing Rust's benefits against real-world deployment constraints, particularly for distributions like Gentoo.
