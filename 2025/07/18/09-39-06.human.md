Performance optimization converting commit traversal to use priority queues. This is the first patch in a 3-part series that replaces commit_list operations with a more efficient prio_queue structure to handle merge-heavy histories better. The change provides dramatic speedups (92% faster) for pathological cases with many merges, though it has a negligible 1% slowdown on normal histories.

The patch converts pop_most_recent_commit() and its callers across multiple files (commit.c, fetch-pack.c, object-name.c, walker.c) to use the new queue structure. It includes a new performance test (t/perf/p1501-rev-parse-oneline.sh) that generates a synthetic merge-heavy history to demonstrate the improvement. The test creates a binary tree of merges 16 levels deep, providing a clear benchmark case.

This appears to be a well-considered optimization - the commit message clearly explains both the benefits and tradeoffs, and the test provides concrete performance measurements. The series has already gone through review iterations and appears ready for merging.
