Feature and refactoring patch series with Rust integration implications.

Phillip Wood responds to Ezekiel Newren's RFC series proposing Rust integration with xdiff optimizations, focusing on the policy implications rather than technical implementation. While supportive of making Rust a hard dependency for Git itself, Wood raises concerns about the impact on other projects using xdiff as a library (notably libgit2 and Neovim), having proactively notified their maintainers. This addresses a key gap in prior discussions that had focused primarily on Git's internal needs.

The response acknowledges the NonStop platform compatibility issue but considers it an acceptable tradeoff. Wood's pragmatic stance suggests the series is technically sound but needs broader ecosystem consideration, particularly regarding how external xdiff consumers will be accommodated. The thread context shows this concern was previously unaddressed despite extensive discussion of type safety, CI integration, and version policies.

This email advances the discussion by surfacing real-world adoption barriers beyond Git's control, though it doesn't propose specific solutions. The tone is constructive, recognizing the series' technical merits while ensuring downstream impact becomes part of the decision calculus. The proactive outreach to affected projects demonstrates responsible stewardship of xdiff as shared infrastructure.
