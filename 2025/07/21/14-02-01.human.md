This is a follow-up discussion to René Scharfe's performance optimization series that converts commit traversal to use priority queues. Lidong Yan offers an analysis of why the performance improvements might not be as significant as expected in some cases.

Yan suggests that the limited performance gains could be because the priority queue is primarily sorting a small number of sibling nodes (O(siblings)) in typical repository structures. They provide a complexity analysis showing that while the priority queue approach (O(W log W × N)) is theoretically better than commit lists (O(W² × N)), the practical difference may be small when the maximum sibling count W is not large.

This appears to be a thoughtful technical observation from a reader who has studied the patch series, though it comes after the series has already received final approval. The comment doesn't propose any changes but rather offers additional context about when the optimization might be most beneficial. The analysis seems reasonable but doesn't challenge the fundamental value of the optimization for worst-case scenarios.
