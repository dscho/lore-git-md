Performance investigation into xdiff hash optimizations. Phillip Wood shares benchmark results comparing the Rust-based xxhash implementation from the RFC series with a C-based xxhash approach. His tests show both implementations provide similar speedups (~15% for `git log --oneline --shortstat`), with about half coming from optimizing whitespace flag handling and half from the hash algorithm itself. 

Wood provides two experimental patches: one refactoring `xdl_hash_record()` to inline whitespace checks (8% speedup), and another switching to libxxhash's C implementation (additional 6% speedup matching Rust performance). The findings suggest the performance gains in the RFC series may come more from algorithmic improvements than Rust-specific optimizations.

This contributes important data to the ongoing discussion about whether Rust integration is necessary for these xdiff speedups, while demonstrating alternative approaches could achieve similar gains. The benchmarks are thorough and reproducible, though they don't address other potential benefits of Rust integration like safety or maintainability.
