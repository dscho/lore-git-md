This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing RFC discussion about Rust integration and xdiff optimizations. Junio Hamano responds to Phillip Wood's performance analysis showing that most of the speed improvements come from hash algorithm choices and optimization techniques rather than the Rust implementation itself.

Hamano highlights two key points from Wood's findings: (1) the significant 15% speedup from hoisting whitespace flag checks out of a loop, and (2) questions about how the xxhash algorithm performs on platforms without optimized implementations. His response suggests these are the most interesting aspects of Wood's analysis that warrant further investigation.

The email reinforces the emerging theme in this thread that the performance benefits may not require Rust as a hard dependency, since similar optimizations appear achievable in C. This continues the technical discussion while implicitly questioning the original proposal's justification for Rust integration. The exchange maintains a constructive tone while probing the underlying assumptions of the RFC series.
