This is a follow-up discussion to René Scharfe's performance optimization series that converts commit traversal to use priority queues. Lidong Yan analyzes why the performance improvements might not be as significant as expected in some cases.

The key technical observation is that the priority queue's O(W log W × N) complexity for merge-heavy histories (where W is the maximum number of siblings at any level) may not show dramatic speedups over the original O(W² × N) commit list implementation when W is small. Yan suggests that in many real-world repositories, the width (W) of merge histories may not be large enough to make the theoretical worst-case improvements practically noticeable.

This analysis builds on the thread's established technical context about the series' focus on improving worst-case performance while maintaining best-case behavior. The discussion doesn't propose any changes to the already-approved series but provides additional insight into when the optimizations would be most impactful. The observation about sibling count (W) being a limiting factor in practice could inform future performance work on commit traversal.
