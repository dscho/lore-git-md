This is a policy discussion about prohibiting AI-generated contributions in Git, building on an earlier documentation patch proposal. Christian Couder raises an important scope concern: the current draft forbids all "AI content generator" output, which is broader than QEMU's more specific prohibition of "AI code generator" output. He notes this could unnecessarily restrict non-code contributions like logos or commit messages.

Couder suggests consulting with the Software Freedom Conservancy (which both Git and QEMU are part of) to understand the legal reasoning behind QEMU's narrower wording. This reflects careful consideration of how to align Git's policy with both legal requirements and practical realities of contribution workflows. The discussion is moving from the initial "should we prohibit?" question to more nuanced questions about exactly what should be prohibited and why. The patch appears well-intentioned but may require refinement to avoid overreach while still addressing the core copyright and DCO compliance concerns.
