This is a contribution to the ongoing discussion about Git's `.clang-format` configuration and automated code style enforcement. Toon Claes weighs in with a strong opinion advocating for full adoption of `clang-format`'s output as the project standard, drawing parallels to Go's `gofmt` approach.

The email argues for a binary choice: either fully commit to automated formatting (accepting the tool's output as canonical, even if imperfect) or abandon formatting standardization entirely. Claes suggests the current middle ground - where formatting is partially automated but still requires human judgment - leads to endless bikeshedding and review friction. The perspective comes from experience with Go's opinionated formatting tool, where the standardization benefits outweigh any aesthetic preferences.

This contrasts with the emerging thread consensus (leaning toward removing problematic line-wrapping rules while keeping other formatting automation). Claes' position aligns more closely with Brian m. carlson's earlier arguments for comprehensive standardization, though stated more forcefully. The email doesn't propose specific technical changes but reinforces the philosophical divide in the discussion between those prioritizing standardization and those prioritizing readability.
