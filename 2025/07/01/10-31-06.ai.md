This is a discussion about making reftable the default Git backend, prompted by the ongoing case-insensitive filesystem issues with reference pruning. Patrick Steinhardt strongly advocates for reftable adoption, noting its technical advantages over the filesystem backend: better case-sensitivity handling, improved compression, efficiency gains, and maintenance benefits. He reports successful large-scale testing at GitLab and personal use for 1.5 years, suggesting it could be enabled via `features.experimental` as a step toward making it default in Git 3.0.

The key obstacle Patrick identifies is ecosystem compatibility - while JGit supports reftables, libgit2 and Gitoxide implementations are incomplete, partly due to his own time constraints on finishing the libgit2 integration. He also acknowledges the broader issue of scripts that directly access repository filesystem structures, though considers this an unavoidable transition cost. The email positions reftable as technically superior but blocked by adoption inertia, with Patrick expressing frustration about the situation while recognizing the practical constraints. This continues the thread's theme of reftable as the robust long-term answer to case-sensitivity issues, though now focusing on the broader transition challenges rather than the specific pruning problem.
