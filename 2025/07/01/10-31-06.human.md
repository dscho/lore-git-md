This is a discussion about making reftable the default Git backend, prompted by the ongoing case-sensitivity issues with the filesystem-based ref storage.

Patrick Steinhardt weighs in on Junio C Hamano's lighthearted endorsement of reftable adoption. He strongly advocates for reftables as the default backend, citing their advantages: they avoid filesystem-specific issues (like the current case-sensitivity problems), offer better compression, improved efficiency, and better maintenance properties. He shares real-world experience from GitLab's staging environments and his personal use, noting reftables have been stable after initial teething problems.

However, Patrick identifies a major blocker - ecosystem compatibility. While JGit supports reftables, other implementations like libgit2 and Gitoxide don't, and he acknowledges his own stalled work on the libgit2 integration. This incompatibility makes a default switch currently impractical, as it would break many existing tools. He also notes the problem of scripts that directly access the filesystem structure, though considers this unavoidable technical debt.

The email reflects both enthusiasm for reftables' technical merits and pragmatic concerns about ecosystem readiness, suggesting this might be a longer-term transition rather than an immediate change. It advances the thread by bringing concrete deployment experience and implementation realities to what had been a more theoretical discussion of solutions to the case-sensitivity issues.
