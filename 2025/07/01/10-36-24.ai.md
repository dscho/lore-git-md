Documentation policy proposal regarding AI-generated contributions, with discussion of scope and wording refinements.

Christian Couder responds to Junio Hamano's initial patch proposing to forbid AI-generated contributions in Git, similar to QEMU's policy. While agreeing with the general direction, Couder identifies several areas where the current wording may be overly broad or unclear compared to QEMU's more specific prohibition.

Key observations include that Git's proposed policy would forbid all "AI content generator" output (potentially including non-code elements like logos or commit messages), whereas QEMU specifically targets "AI code generator output." Couder also notes the current phrasing could be interpreted as prohibiting even using AI tools to check contributions, which may not be the intended scope.

The email suggests rewording to clarify these boundaries, though specific alternative phrasing isn't proposed. This continues the thread's focus on precise legal language given the DCO requirements, with particular attention to distinguishing between AI-assisted validation versus AI-generated content. The discussion remains focused on documentation policy rather than implementation or enforcement mechanisms.
