This is a follow-up discussion in the SHA-256 transition thread, specifically about the naming of hash algorithm constants. Patrick Steinhardt responds to the choice of `GIT_HASH_LEGACY_SHA1` as the constant name for cases requiring SHA-1 for backward compatibility.

The email confirms agreement with Junio C Hamano's approval of the `GIT_HASH_LEGACY_SHA1` name, which was proposed by brian m. carlson as an alternative to the original `GIT_HASH_ORIGINAL` suggestion. Patrick notes they considered suggesting `GIT_HASH_SHA1_HISTORICAL` but finds "legacy" even better, reinforcing the consensus around this naming choice.

This discussion resolves one of the open questions from the series - the naming of constants to distinguish between explicit SHA-1 usage (`GIT_HASH_SHA1`) and legacy SHA-1 fallback behavior (`GIT_HASH_LEGACY_SHA1`). The thread shows alignment between all three key participants (brian m. carlson, Junio C Hamano, and Patrick Steinhardt) on this naming approach, which maintains clear semantic distinction while keeping SHA-1 in the name as requested by Junio.

The email doesn't introduce new technical changes but solidifies the naming convention that will be used throughout the SHA-256 transition work, particularly for handling legacy repository formats. This consensus helps prevent future confusion between cases where SHA-1 is explicitly requested versus cases where it's used for backward compatibility with older repositories.
