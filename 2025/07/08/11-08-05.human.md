This is a process discussion email in the ongoing thread about improving signature handling in Git's fast-export/fast-import functionality. Christian Couder responds to Patrick Steinhardt's emphasis on fast response cycles in code reviews, offering a nuanced perspective on review timing.

Christian argues that while quick responses can be valuable (especially for newcomers), they shouldn't be considered an absolute requirement for good collaboration. He notes that delayed responses can sometimes lead to higher-quality discussions when contributors take time to deeply consider feedback and return with well-thought-out patches. He also points out that real-world constraints (vacations, personal issues, focus on other work) can naturally delay responses.

The email highlights different expectations between newer and established community members, with Christian suggesting that long-time contributors should have some flexibility in their response patterns. He emphasizes that different working styles (like preferring to stay focused on one topic rather than context-switching) should be accommodated.

This discussion doesn't advance the technical aspects of the signature handling feature, but rather explores the social dynamics of Git's review process. Christian's position suggests valuing thoughtful, substantive responses over immediate acknowledgments, particularly for complex topics where deep consideration is beneficial. The exchange provides insight into how different contributors balance responsiveness with thoughtful development in an open-source context.
