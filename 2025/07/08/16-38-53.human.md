This is a process discussion email in the ongoing thread about improving signature handling in Git's fast-export/fast-import functionality. Junio Hamano responds to Christian Couder's defense of his patch review methodology, where Christian prefers to bundle responses with new patch versions rather than providing immediate acknowledgments.

The exchange highlights differing perspectives on review responsiveness norms. Junio suggests that even senior contributors should provide interim feedback (like agreement or reservations) within a few days, rather than waiting to respond with a full patch revision. He notes that Christian's pattern of minimal interim communication is unusual compared to most contributors, and argues for establishing clearer community norms that don't require others to learn individual working styles.

Christian had previously argued that his approach of delivering concrete solutions in new versions shows deeper engagement than quick acknowledgments, and that experienced contributors should have flexibility in their workflow. Junio counters that this risks appearing like special privileges for senior contributors, referencing GitLab's team handbook guidelines that Christian had dismissed as only applying to newcomers.

The discussion remains cordial but reveals fundamental tensions in open source collaboration - between individual working styles and shared expectations, between deep work and timely communication, and between the flexibility granted to experienced contributors versus establishing clear norms for all. While the technical work on signature handling continues to progress (now at v5), this side discussion about process norms has become a significant sub-thread.
