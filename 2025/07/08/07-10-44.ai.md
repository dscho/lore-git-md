This is a legal compliance discussion in the ongoing documentation patch series about allowing pseudonyms in Signed-off-by trailers. Martin Ågren responds to Jeff King's analysis of legal precedents, specifically the Linux kernel's policy change (commit d4563201f33a) and CNCF DCO guidelines that Jeff cited as justification for relaxing real-name requirements.

Martin points out that the Linux kernel commit was Acked-by Michael Dolan (a Linux Foundation lawyer), which provides implicit legal validation. This addresses Jeff's question about whether there was lawyer vetting of the kernel's approach. The email also notes Theodore Ts'o's earlier confirmation of Dolan's legal role at LF, reinforcing the credibility of this precedent.

The discussion continues to focus on building a legally defensible position for Git's policy change, with Martin's contribution helping establish that the Linux kernel's similar policy shift had proper legal review. This moves the thread closer to consensus on citing these precedents in Git's documentation updates, though Martin defers to the Project Leadership Committee (PLC) on whether additional consultation with Software Freedom Conservancy is needed - a point previously raised by Phillip Wood and Jeff King. The email doesn't introduce new arguments but strengthens the evidentiary basis for the proposed changes by confirming legal vetting of the key precedent.
