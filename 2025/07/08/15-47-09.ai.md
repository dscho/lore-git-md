This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about fixing comment character selection when `core.commentChar="auto"` encounters conflict markers. Ayush Chandekar responds to Phillip Wood's concerns about their proposed solution to skip comment character scanning when conflict markers are detected.

Chandekar raises two new points: (1) since the deprecation of `core.commentChar="auto"` is planned anyway, perhaps it's acceptable to simply ignore the auto-selection when conflicts are detected, and (2) questions how legitimate commit message lines would realistically begin with conflict marker characters. This suggests Chandekar sees their localized solution as a reasonable stopgap given the impending deprecation.

The email references Wood's parallel work to deprecate the auto keyword (linked in [1]), connecting this specific fix to broader architectural changes. Chandekar's position appears to be that with the deprecation coming, a simpler solution that just preserves the default '#' character when conflicts exist may be sufficient, though they seem open to Wood's judgment on this tradeoff.

The technical discussion now centers on whether to:
1. Proceed with Chandekar's localized solution (skip scanning when conflicts detected)
2. Implement Wood's config-parsing solution (set '#' earlier in config processing)
3. Accept a temporary solution given the planned deprecation

Wood had previously expressed concerns about false positives in Chandekar's approach potentially skipping legitimate comments, but acknowledged neither solution is clearly superior. Chandekar's latest response attempts to address these concerns by framing the solution in light of the upcoming deprecation.
