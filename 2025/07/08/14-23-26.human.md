This is a policy discussion about AI-generated content in Git contributions, specifically focusing on commit messages and assistance tools.

Christian Couder raises nuanced concerns about the proposed blanket prohibition on AI-generated content. He questions whether we should allow some AI-assisted improvements to commit messages, particularly for non-native English speakers who might use AI for translation or wording refinement. The example given is someone writing a draft in their native language or poor English and using AI to polish it - a case where the output might closely resemble fully AI-generated text but stems from genuine human authorship.

The email also revisits the distinction between generative AI (which would be prohibited) and assistive tools (which would be allowed). Couder points out the practical difficulty in drawing this line, especially when an AI's "suggestions" might effectively rewrite most of a human's original input. He suggests seeking input from others who have deeper expertise in this area rather than attempting to craft precise wording immediately.

This continues the thread's trend of refining the policy's scope, now focusing on the grey area between generation and assistance. The discussion remains constructive, with Junio Hamano having previously acknowledged these as valid concerns. The key open question is how to define acceptable AI assistance without creating loopholes for de facto AI generation.
