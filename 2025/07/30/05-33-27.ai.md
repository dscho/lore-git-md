This is a follow-up discussion in the completed reflog migration series, specifically addressing the design of the new `git reflog write` subcommand's refname validation behavior. The key points:

Patrick Steinhardt and Junio Hamano are discussing whether the new `git reflog write` command should use DWIM (Do What I Mean) behavior for refname resolution. Patrick argues against DWIM for this plumbing-like command, especially since it needs to handle non-existent reflogs where DWIM resolution wouldn't work reliably. He proposes stricter validation by dropping the `REFNAME_ALLOW_ONELEVEL` flag.

Junio agrees with this approach, comparing it to `update-ref`'s behavior, and muses about whether the broader `git reflog` command's DWIM behavior was a UI mistake that could be corrected by moving reflog operations to branch/tag commands. However, both conclude it's too late for such a fundamental change and likely not worth the effort given the infrequency of these operations.

The discussion confirms the technical decision to require fully-qualified refnames for `git reflog write` while acknowledging the historical context of DWIM behavior in other reflog operations. This aligns with the series' goal of creating robust, predictable plumbing for reflog operations during the files-to-reftable migration.
