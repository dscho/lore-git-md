This is a technical follow-up in the thread about making `git rebase` more transparent when skipping commits during conflict resolution. Phillip Wood responds to Junio C Hamano's proposed implementation with specific implementation details and edge cases to consider.

Phillip identifies two potential issues with the current patch: it might incorrectly trigger warnings during `break` commands or failed `exec` commands, and suggests checking for `rebase_path_message()` as a guard. He also proposes reading `REBASE_HEAD` to provide more detailed commit information in the warning message. The email notes that the warning would logically apply to both `--continue` and `--skip` operations when changes are removed.

Key technical considerations raised include:
- The need to distinguish between legitimate empty-step skips and other rebase operations
- Potential use of `REBASE_HEAD` to enrich warning messages
- The shared behavior between `--continue` and `--skip` operations
- The suggestion to check for `rebase_path_message()` as a condition for emitting warnings

This moves the discussion from initial concept to concrete implementation concerns, particularly around when and how the warning should trigger. The technical depth suggests this will require careful review of the sequencer's state machine to avoid false positives in the warning system.
