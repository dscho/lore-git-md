This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing thread about improving `git rebase` to show warnings when commits are skipped during conflict resolution. 

Cameron Steffen clarifies their position on the warning mechanism, explaining that the goal isn't to determine whether a commit was previously applied (as in the existing automatic skip case), but simply to detect when a commit in the rebase plan isn't being committed due to becoming empty during conflict resolution. This suggests a simpler detection approach than Junio Hamano had proposed - just checking for the absence of staged changes when a commit is being skipped.

The exchange reveals a key implementation question: how reliably can Git detect this skip condition, particularly after user intervention during conflict resolution? While there's agreement on reusing the existing warning message pattern ("skipped previously applied commit <hash>"), the technical challenge lies in accurately identifying this specific skip scenario without false positives.

The discussion remains focused and technical, with both participants working toward a solution that maintains rebase's reliability while adding this new warning capability. The patch appears to be making progress toward consensus, though the final implementation will need to carefully handle edge cases around user-controlled conflict states.
