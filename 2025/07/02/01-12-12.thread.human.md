This thread addresses a performance optimization for `git fetch --prune` that eliminates quadratic time complexity when reporting dangling refs. The current implementation can take 470 CPU seconds in repositories with 174k refs (pruning 15k), while the new approach using sorted lists and binary search reduces this to under 1 second. The 3-patch series (now at v4) restructures the prune operation across `builtin/fetch.c`, `builtin/remote.c`, and `refs.c`, while maintaining the same user-visible behavior.

The optimization has already received a Reviewed-by from Jacob Keller for the main patch, with the other two being mechanical cleanups of now-obsolete code paths. Junio Hamano recently checked on the thread's status, prompting Phil Hord (the original author) to confirm the series looks good to him. With no outstanding objections and demonstrated performance improvements from 470s to 1s in test cases, the series appears ready for final maintainer review and potential inclusion. The changes are focused and well-contained, touching the prune logic while keeping the same output format, just much more efficiently.
