This is a technical follow-up in the HTTP Accept-Language header filtering discussion, focusing on validation approaches. Collin Funk responds to Justin Tobler's suggestion about length-based validation of language tags, agreeing it would be a better heuristic than the current patch's explicit "C"/"POSIX" filtering.

Collin sees two advantages to length-based validation: (1) it would automatically catch invalid tags like "C" and "POSIX" without special cases, and (2) it avoids the maintenance burden of tracking BCP 47 language tag updates. The proposal would validate that the language portion (before any '_') is 2-3 characters, matching ISO 639 requirements.

This continues the thread's exploration of alternative validation strategies while maintaining the core goal of IANA compliance. The length-based approach represents a middle ground between the current minimal filtering and more complex locale mapping solutions previously discussed. Collin's endorsement suggests this could be a viable direction for a v2 patch, though the practical value over the current approach remains an open question as noted by Justin.
