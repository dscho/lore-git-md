This thread continues the deep technical discussion around Patrick Steinhardt's reflog migration infrastructure work, particularly focusing on the behavior of the `REF_HAVE_OLD` flag during HEAD updates. 

Jeff King (Peff) had raised concerns about potential race conditions when `REF_HAVE_OLD` is no longer stripped for log-only operations. Patrick has now responded with a detailed analysis confirming that the behavior change for split HEAD updates is intentional, while uncovering a more fundamental race condition in the current implementation. He explains how HEAD's target ref could change between resolution and update, potentially creating inconsistent reflog entries - particularly problematic when HEAD is unborn or points to a ref not being updated.

Patrick proposes partial solutions involving locking parent refs earlier and propagating old OIDs when `REF_HAVE_OLD` is unset, though he acknowledges some race conditions (like HEAD rapidly changing targets) may remain unfixable without major architectural changes. The discussion highlights the delicate balance between maintaining atomic operations and avoiding overly restrictive locking in Git's ref handling.
