This thread documents the completion of Patrick Steinhardt's 9-patch series that overhauled Git's reflog infrastructure, particularly focusing on migration between the traditional files-based storage and the newer reftable format. The work addressed several technical challenges including committer identity handling, atomic updates, race conditions, and OID integrity during migration. The final patch fixed a subtle issue with all-zero OIDs in migrated reflogs by introducing a new `REF_LOG_USE_PROVIDED_OIDS` flag.

The series standardized documentation, reorganized commands, added a new `git reflog write` subcommand (requiring fully-qualified refnames), and made various technical improvements to type safety and committer identity handling. Junio Hamano merged the complete series after extensive review from Jeff King, Justin Tobler, and others. 

A follow-up discussion between Patrick and Junio clarified the design decision to avoid DWIM (Do What I Mean) behavior in the new `git reflog write` subcommand when dealing with non-existent reflogs. Junio agreed that DWIM would be inappropriate for this plumbing-like command, comparing it to `update-ref` which also requires explicit reference names. While briefly considering a more radical change to Git's UI by removing DWIM from reflog commands entirely, Junio recognized this would be too disruptive at this stage. The thread concludes with the infrastructure now complete and merged, providing robust reflog migration capabilities while maintaining Git's pragmatic approach to concurrency challenges.
