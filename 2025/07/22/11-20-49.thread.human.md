Patrick Steinhardt's reflog migration series has been merged after extensive discussion, bringing several important improvements to Git's reflog handling. The 9-patch series standardized documentation, reorganized commands, added a strict `git reflog write` subcommand, and fixed issues around committer identity, race conditions, and OID handling during migration between files and reftable backends.

Key contributors including Jeff King, Justin Tobler, and Karthik Nayak provided thorough review, with Junio Hamano ultimately validating the technical approach. The implementation introduces flags like `REF_LOG_VIA_SPLIT` to mitigate HEAD reflog race conditions and `REF_LOG_USE_PROVIDED_OIDS` to ensure proper OID preservation during migration. One remaining inconsistency was noted - while `reflog write` requires fully-qualified refnames, other subcommands like `drop` and `expire` still use DWIM (Do What I Mean) resolution.

After merging, Toon Claes has continued to spot minor documentation improvements, first noting a test case ambiguity and now catching an awkward phrase in the race condition commit message. His latest observation about the redundant "point to not point to" phrasing doesn't affect the code, but helps clarify the explanation of edge cases in concurrent HEAD updates. These follow-ups demonstrate the community's attention to both technical correctness and clear communication, even after a series is complete.
