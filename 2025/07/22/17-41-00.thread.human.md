This thread addresses file descriptor leaks in Git's test helper programs, with the latest iteration focusing on `test-delta`. The series began by fixing `test-truncate`, though Junio questioned the practical need since the process exits immediately. The `test-delta` portion proved more contentious - Eric Sunshine identified serious flaws in the initial approach, including a double-close bug.

Jeff King has now proposed a philosophical shift in the solution. Rather than meticulously managing cleanup paths, he suggests embracing the test helper's throwaway nature by using `die_errno()` on errors, letting process termination handle cleanup. His patch simplifies `test-delta.c` by 33 lines while also fixing two subtle bugs (integer truncation and unchecked reads). The changes use `xopen()` consistently and could be extended further with `strbuf` APIs.

While this differs from the original series' approach, it appears technically sound and avoids the complexity that caused earlier problems. The `test-truncate` portion remains accepted despite questions about its necessity, while the `test-delta` solution has evolved significantly through review. King's proposal offers a pragmatic way forward that maintains reliability while reducing code complexity.
