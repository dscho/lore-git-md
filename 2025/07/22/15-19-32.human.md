This is a discussion about protecting VCS metadata directories (like `.jj` and `.sl`) from accidental deletion during `git clean` operations, building on the broader "precious files" concept.

Elijah Newren responds to Jade Lovelace's concerns about `git clean -ixd` potentially deleting gitignored VCS directories. He clarifies Git's current behavior: ignored files are considered expendable and may be deleted during operations like branch switching, while untracked files are generally protected unless forced. The discussion highlights how this becomes problematic when VCS metadata directories (which contain critical version control data) get caught in this behavior.

Newren analyzes the specific risk scenario where these directories might transition between tracked/untracked/ignored states across different developers' workflows, creating situations where Git's normal behavior could lead to data loss. While acknowledging the severity of accidentally deleting VCS metadata (comparable to deleting `.git`), he expresses a preference for solving this through a general precious-files mechanism rather than special-casing specific directories.

The email provides useful technical context about Git's current file handling semantics while keeping the focus on the core issue - how to best protect these critical directories without resorting to one-off exceptions.
