He also questions the wisdom of splitting the option definitions between commands when they share the same underlying implementation.

The exchange reveals a tension between cleaning up the experimental interface and preserving established workflows. While the original proposal frames removing `-m` as making room for future extensions, Junio argues the flag is actually quite useful in practice. His response suggests the experimental period may have lasted long enough that some behaviors became entrenched despite initial intentions.

Junio also makes a technical point about code organization - since `--merge` and `--conflict` are used together in shared code paths, keeping their definitions together might make the implementation clearer. This hints that the patch might need revision to maintain consistency across the related commands.
