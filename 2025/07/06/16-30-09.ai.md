This is a documentation patch proposing changes to Git's contribution guidelines regarding name requirements in commit signatures. The patch seeks to relax the current requirement for real names in `Signed-off-by` trailers, allowing pseudonyms or preferred names in cases where contributors have valid reasons for not using their legal names.

The author presents several compelling cases where real name requirements create barriers: for transgender/non-binary contributors who may have "deadnames" permanently recorded in history, for well-known pseudonymous developers, and for those concerned about privacy/safety due to harassment or government attention. The proposal maintains the prohibition on truly anonymous contributions (requiring a "known identity") while making the policy more inclusive.

The technical change modifies Documentation/SubmittingPatches to replace the strict "real name" language with more flexible wording that encourages but does not mandate real names. The diff shows the precise text modifications, which retain the same section header ID for linking purposes. The author notes this is open to wording improvements and references Linux kernel's "known identity" terminology as a model.

This touches on sensitive identity/policy matters likely to generate discussion about community norms, inclusivity vs. accountability, and the balance between welcoming contributors while maintaining traceability. The patch appears carefully considered with clear motivations and maintains the ability to attribute contributions while accommodating diverse circumstances.
