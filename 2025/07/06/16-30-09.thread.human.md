This thread discusses a long-standing Git policy that requires real names in Signed-off-by trailers, with brian m. carlson proposing to relax this requirement to allow pseudonyms while maintaining legal traceability. The discussion has evolved into a thorough examination of the legal implications across multiple dimensions: DCO enforceability, OFAC sanctions compliance, GPL license enforcement, and alignment with legal precedent.

The proposal has gained significant support, with Jacob Keller's Reviewed-by and positive feedback from key contributors including Phillip Wood and Theodore Ts'o, who brought important perspectives on license enforcement and sanctions compliance respectively. The Linux kernel's similar policy change (commit d4563201f33a) serves as a key precedent, with Martin Ågren confirming it was legally vetted by Linux Foundation lawyer Michael Dolan. Jeff King (peff) has reinforced this by analyzing the legal viability of pseudonyms with verifiable persistence, citing both the kernel precedent and CNCF DCO guidelines.

Junio Hamano agrees with the direction but insists on stronger language prohibiting complete anonymity and clearer legal justifications. The discussion has now reached consensus that pseudonyms with verifiable persistence are the only legally viable path forward that satisfies all compliance requirements. Remaining work includes incorporating Junio's feedback, documenting the legal precedents more explicitly, and potentially consulting with the Software Freedom Conservancy as suggested by Wood and King. The patch appears ready to move forward once these final elements are addressed.
