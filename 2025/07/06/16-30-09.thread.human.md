This thread discusses proposed changes to Git's contribution guidelines regarding the use of pseudonyms in Signed-off-by trailers. The original proposal sought to relax real-name requirements while maintaining legal compliance across multiple dimensions: DCO enforceability, OFAC sanctions, GPL license enforcement, and legal precedent alignment.

The discussion has evolved through several rounds of review, with key contributions from brian m. carlson (original proposal), Jacob Keller (Reviewed-by), Junio Hamano (maintainer feedback), Theodore Ts'o (OFAC compliance), Phillip Wood (license considerations), and Jeff King (legal precedent analysis). The current consensus supports allowing pseudonyms but requires verifiable persistent identities, with Junio insisting on stronger language prohibiting complete anonymity.

The latest contribution from Martin Ågren strengthens the case by confirming that the Linux kernel's similar policy change (commit d4563201f33a) was Acked-by a Linux Foundation lawyer, providing legal validation of this approach. This addresses a key question about precedent credibility that Jeff King had raised earlier.

While technical implementation details around identity verification remain to be worked out, the thread is moving toward documenting these legal precedents (Linux kernel policy and CNCF DCO guidelines) in Git's updated contribution guidelines. The Project Leadership Committee may still need to consult with the Software Freedom Conservancy, as previously suggested by multiple participants, before finalizing the changes.
