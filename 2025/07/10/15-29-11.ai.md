This is a process discussion email in the signature handling improvements thread, focusing on community norms around response times and expectations for contributors.

Christian Couder responds to Patrick Steinhardt's suggestions about response time norms, arguing against establishing rigid expectations for reply speed. The discussion centers on balancing community health with individual contributor circumstances. Christian raises several concerns: that personal situations vary widely (health, caregiving responsibilities, employment changes), that measuring response times could create unhealthy pressure, and that senior contributors may be at higher risk of burnout from such expectations.

Key points in Christian's response include:
- Questioning how exceptions would be handled without invasive personal inquiries
- Arguing that even employed contributors face similar life challenges as volunteers
- Suggesting response speed is a poor metric for discussion quality compared to technical merit and tone
- Noting that different contributors have different strengths that shouldn't be standardized
- Maintaining that no single norm can accommodate all contributors' situations

The email represents a pushback against establishing formal response expectations, particularly for long-time contributors, on grounds that it could harm community health it aims to improve. This continues the thread's process discussion but doesn't advance the technical signature handling work, which was already finalized in previous emails. The exchange highlights differing views on how to maintain productive collaboration while respecting individual contributors' circumstances.
