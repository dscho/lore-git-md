This is a proposal about Git's release schedule and maintenance strategy, aimed at helping distribution maintainers. Junio Hamano suggests changing from the current version-tagged release model to a time-based monthly tagging of whatever is at the tip of `master`, with version names like "Git 2.50.202508" for August 2025. The motivation comes from observing that some distros struggle with backporting security fixes to their maintained versions, often sticking to outdated major versions longer than Git's official maintenance period (currently supporting 8 parallel versions, which may be excessive).

The proposal questions whether making releases more frequent and less "special" would help break distros' habit of clinging to specific major versions. Hamano notes that Git maintains backward compatibility at the user level, so there's little technical reason for distros to avoid updating. The current situation where distros maintain ancient versions appears to be causing more harm than good to both the distros and their users.

The email frames this as an RFC (Request For Comments), seeking discussion on whether this model would actually help distributors and whether the Git project should adopt it. No specific implementation details or transition plans are provided yet - this is purely a conceptual proposal to gauge community interest. The suggestion implies that more frequent, time-based releases might reduce the perceived significance of version upgrades, making it psychologically easier for distros to stay current.
