This thread explores performance optimizations for Git's xdiff string hashing, building on Phillip Wood's earlier refactoring work. Alexander Monakov proposed two key changes: splitting the hash function into verbatim and whitespace-aware variants (8% speedup) and implementing an optimized djb2 hash (targeting 1.5x speedup), inspired by but independently implemented from Glibc's approach. The changes focus on xdiff/xutils.{c,h} with minimal code impact.

After initial positive reception from Junio Hamano and license verification by Eli Schwartz, Phillip Wood's benchmarking showed more modest gains - just 2% over the previous patch and 11% over master for `git log`, with his xxhash implementation still 3% faster. Monakov attributes the discrepancy to compiler/CPU differences and maintains the optimization should help even without architecture-specific tuning. He's adjusting his measurements to be clearer (now showing explicit speedup percentages) and plans Skylake testing to better understand the variance.

The discussion has narrowed to practical considerations: whether the assembly optimization's benefits justify its maintenance cost, with Monakov suggesting restricting it to x86_64 with GCC/Clang if needed. Technical debates continue around measurement methodology (Monakov prefers perf stat over hyperfine) and the suitability of C11's atomic_signal_fence versus their custom solution. While the approach is technically sound, the thread remains open pending further performance validation across different build environments.
