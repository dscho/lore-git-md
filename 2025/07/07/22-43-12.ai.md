This is a status check on a performance optimization series for `git fetch --prune`. The series (now at v4) addresses a severe O(N^2) bottleneck in dangling ref reporting, where pruning 15k refs from a 174k-ref repository previously took 470 CPU seconds due to 2.6 billion strcmp calls. The main optimization in patch 1 restructures the prune logic to sort refs first and use binary search, reducing the same operation to under 1 second.

The series has three parts: (1) the core optimization (already reviewed by Jacob Keller), (2) removal of obsolete helper functions, and (3) cleanup of the warning interface. Changes span `builtin/fetch.c`, `builtin/remote.c`, and refs.c/h`. The author notes v3 accidentally omitted the first commit, making v4 the complete series.

Junio is checking on the thread's status as discussion has gone quiet. The series appears technically sound with clear performance gains demonstrated, and the first patch already has a Reviewed-by. The remaining patches are mechanical follow-ups to the now-obsolete code paths. This looks ready for potential inclusion pending any final maintainer review.
