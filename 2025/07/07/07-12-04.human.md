This is a discussion about metadata handling in Git's email workflow, specifically around commit signatures and change IDs. Drew DeVault weighs in on an ongoing thread about where to place metadata like change IDs and signatures in patch emails.

The discussion has evolved from a specific proposal about X-Change-ID headers to a broader consideration of how Git should handle various types of metadata in its patch workflow. Drew makes several key points:

1. Commit signatures currently don't survive the format-patch/am process, and there's no active work to change this. He clarifies that commit signatures are distinct from PGP signatures.

2. He strongly advocates for using email headers rather than commit trailers or body placement for metadata, arguing headers are the proper structured format for this purpose. This aligns with Junio Hamano's earlier proposal for a generic header preservation mechanism.

3. The email responds to Martin von Zweigbergk's suggestions about alternative metadata placement (between the `---` separator and diff, or after the final `--` line), dismissing these in favor of headers.

The thread shows growing consensus around a generic solution rather than change-ID-specific headers, though questions remain about signature handling and whether header preservation should be opt-in or always-on. Drew's contribution reinforces the technical direction while highlighting the still-unresolved signature preservation issue.
