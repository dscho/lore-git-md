This is a legal discussion follow-up in the ongoing thread about relaxing Git's real-name requirements for Signed-off-by trailers. Jeff King (peff) responds to brian m. carlson's proposal, focusing on the legal foundations for allowing pseudonyms while maintaining traceability.

Peff agrees with the direction but suggests strengthening the legal justification by referencing the Linux kernel's similar policy change and its underlying legal vetting. He points to two key resources: the kernel's commit adopting pseudonyms (which cites CNCF's DCO guidelines) and the CNCF guidelines themselves. While supportive, he notes the lack of public legal analysis behind these decisions and suggests the Git project might want its own legal review through the Software Freedom Conservancy.

The email doesn't propose specific text changes but provides useful context for the policy discussion, highlighting that while the technical solution (allowing pseudonyms with persistent identities) seems sound, the project should ensure its legal footing is as robust as the kernel's. This keeps the conversation moving forward while flagging an important due diligence step.
