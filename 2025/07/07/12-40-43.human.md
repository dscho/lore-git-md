This is a design discussion email in the ongoing thread about change ID tracking in Git's email workflow, with new focus on commit signature preservation.

Junio Hamano responds to Martin von Zweigbergk's question about why commit signatures are currently omitted from `git format-patch` output. He explains this was a deliberate early design choice, as the email workflow was meant for conveying change essence rather than bit-for-bit reproduction, since patches often get modified (whitespace fixes, different committer info) before application. However, he acknowledges this assumption may warrant reevaluation.

Junio proposes two potential approaches for preserving commit metadata (including signatures) in email workflows: either including the full `git cat-file commit` output as base85-encoded data (suggesting placement between the `---` separator and diff), or as an email header. He advises against using the area after the final `--` line due to email client handling issues.

This email shifts the thread's technical focus from change IDs specifically to the broader challenge of commit metadata preservation in email workflows. Junio's response suggests openness to enhancing Git's email capabilities while maintaining careful consideration of real-world email client behaviors. The discussion remains exploratory rather than prescriptive, inviting further input on metadata handling strategies.
