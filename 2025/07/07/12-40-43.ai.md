This is a technical discussion about commit signature preservation in Git's email workflow, part of the ongoing thread about change ID tracking and metadata preservation.

Junio Hamano responds to Martin von Zweigbergk's question about why commit signatures are currently omitted from `git format-patch` output. He confirms this was a deliberate design choice based on the original assumption that email patches are meant for conveying change essence rather than exact bit-for-bit reproduction. The rationale includes that timestamps would be invalidated and recipients often modify patches (e.g., via `git am -s --whitespace=fix`).

However, Junio acknowledges this assumption may need reevaluation, suggesting Git could support both workflows: the current human-readable mode and a new exact-reproduction mode. For the latter case, he proposes including the full `git cat-file commit` output, either between the `---` separator and diff or as email headers (though notes the final `--` line is unreliable due to email client behaviors). This would preserve not just signatures but all commit metadata including tree/parent references.

The email reinforces the thread's broader theme of metadata preservation while introducing new technical specifics about signature handling. It also implicitly connects to the change-ID discussion by showing how a generic metadata preservation mechanism (like Junio's earlier proposed `--extra-headers`) could solve multiple problems at once. The response maintains Junio's consistent position favoring flexible, general solutions over case-specific ones.
