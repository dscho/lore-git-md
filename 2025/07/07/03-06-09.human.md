The concern isn't just about copyright, but also preventing sanctioned entities from circumventing restrictions through anonymous contributions.

Ts'o notes this affects not just U.S. sanctions but similar regulations in many countries, suggesting contributors consult their legal departments if affiliated with Linux Foundation member companies. His message reinforces Junio Hamano's earlier point about needing strong language against anonymity, while providing concrete legal rationale beyond just traceability concerns.

The email doesn't propose specific wording changes but strengthens the case for maintaining strict identity requirements by highlighting real-world legal constraints the project must navigate. It suggests the Git project may want to consider the Linux kernel's hard-won lessons in this area rather than reinventing policy from scratch.
