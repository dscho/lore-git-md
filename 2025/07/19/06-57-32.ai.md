This is a technical discussion email in the performance optimization series about converting commit traversal to use priority queues. Jeff King (Peff) responds to René Scharfe's concerns about memory overhead and dual-use behavior of prio_queue versus commit_list.

Peff analyzes the memory tradeoffs, noting prio_queue actually uses fewer pointers per entry than commit_list nodes, though it doesn't shrink dynamically. He highlights how prio_queue's stack-like behavior proved useful in his own conversion work, allowing FIFO ordering when needed. The key performance difference appears to stem from Scharfe keeping rev_info.commits as a commit_list while Peff's approach made prio_queue the hot-path structure during traversal.

The email provides insight into implementation tradeoffs between the two approaches, with Peff suggesting Scharfe's performance hit may come from conversion overhead between representations. This discussion of internal data structure choices and their performance implications is valuable context for future work in this area, though it doesn't introduce new changes to the already-complete patch series.
