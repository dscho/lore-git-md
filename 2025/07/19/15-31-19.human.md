Bug report about `git bisect` behavior with skipped commits. Begad Habib observes that when a commit is explicitly skipped during bisection, the final output may misleadingly include already-identified good commits in the list of potential bad commits. 

The reproduction scenario shows a simple 3-commit history where the first commit is marked good, the middle commit is skipped, and the last commit is marked bad. The bisect output then lists both the good commit and skipped commit as potential bad candidates, which seems counterintuitive since the bad commit was already identified.

The reporter questions whether this is intentional behavior or if the output could be improved to exclude commits already marked as good. The report is well-structured with clear reproduction steps and focuses on potential user confusion rather than proposing specific changes. This appears to be a legitimate usability concern that might warrant discussion about refining bisect's output in edge cases.
