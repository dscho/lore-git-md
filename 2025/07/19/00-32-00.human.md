This is a response in the ongoing RFC discussion about Rustifying parts of Git's xdiff implementation, specifically addressing concerns about the scope and approach of the changes.

Elijah Newren clarifies that the current RFC series (which introduces Rust-based optimizations for xdiff's line hashing) is just the beginning of a larger exploration, not a wholesale replacement of xdiff's C code. He explains that the work started with Ezekiel Newren examining xhistogram.c and then expanding to other xdiff components, with both cleanup and performance improvements in mind. The DJB2a hash optimization that yielded 5-19% speedups emerged from this investigation.

Newren notes the work involves difficult tradeoffs between maintainability and performance, and that more questions about these tradeoffs will likely surface as the work continues. He positions this initial series as a way to gauge community sentiment about Rust in xdiff, while acknowledging the codebase's complexity ("how arcane xdiff sometimes feels"). The response also clarifies terminology around "xdiff" vs specific files within the xdiff directory, showing attention to technical precision in the discussion.

This email reinforces that the RFC remains exploratory, with no consensus yet on Rust's role in xdiff's future, and hints at deeper technical discussions to come about optimization/maintainability balances in this critical diff component.
