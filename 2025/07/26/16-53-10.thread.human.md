This thread discusses a patch series that aims to make `-h` and `--help-all` more flexible across Git commands, particularly by allowing them to work outside repositories and coexist with other arguments. The series, now at version 1, includes refactoring of `parse-options.c`, test fixes from D. Ben Knoble, and adjustments to preserve special cases like `ls-remote -h` and `grep -h`.

The primary author has completed all planned changes, including a potentially controversial final patch that allows `-h`/`--help-all` to work with additional arguments. While the author is open to feedback, they prefer squashing test fixes rather than keeping them as separate commits. So far, D. Ben Knoble has contributed test fixes, but no other substantive reviews had been received—until now.

Junio C Hamano, the Git maintainer, has raised serious concerns about the final patch. He argues that allowing `-h` to work alongside other arguments could introduce dangerous behavior, especially with aliases that might misinterpret `-h` destructively (e.g., `!echo rm -rf .`). He suggests the opposite approach—making `-h` fail when given arguments—would be safer. Additionally, he questions whether supporting `-h` mid-command is even desirable, as it goes against Git's CLI guidelines. He also points out that `grep -h`'s current behavior might be buggy rather than something to emulate.

This review casts significant doubt on the final patch's viability, as Junio's concerns touch on both safety and design philosophy. The author will likely need to either revise the patch substantially or drop it entirely, despite it being the culmination of the series. The discussion now hinges on whether the convenience of `-h` flexibility outweighs the potential risks to Git's CLI consistency and security.
