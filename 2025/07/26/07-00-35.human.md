This is a discussion about the proposed `git rebase` warning feature for skipped commits during conflict resolution. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) raises important concerns about the fundamental assumptions behind the warning mechanism.

Hannes questions whether Git can reliably determine why a commit becomes empty during rebase conflict resolution. He points out that users have many legitimate ways to modify the working state during conflict resolution (manual commits, resets, splitting changes, etc.), making any automated warning about skipped commits potentially misleading. The current proposal to warn whenever a commit is skipped due to becoming empty would frequently generate false positives in real-world workflows.

This email shifts the discussion from implementation details to a more philosophical question about whether such warnings can ever be accurate enough to be useful. Hannes suggests that in his typical usage, most "commit skipped" warnings would actually be incorrect, as the skipped state could result from intentional user actions rather than the commit being truly empty or previously applied.

The exchange highlights a tension between the desire to inform users about potentially surprising behavior and the reality that rebase conflict resolution allows for too many legitimate state modifications to reliably classify skipped commits. This may require the feature to either be significantly narrowed in scope or reconsidered entirely.
