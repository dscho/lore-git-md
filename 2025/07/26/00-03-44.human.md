This is a discussion about the limitations of the proposed `commit.requireIgnoreFirst` config option in preventing problematic `.gitignore` situations. Junio Hamano responds to Torsten Bögershausen's skepticism by pointing out that merely requiring `.gitignore` to exist before other commits doesn't actually ensure good ignore patterns are in place.

Hamano shares a concrete example from his own experience where a broad `*.txt` ignore pattern caused him to miss adding important template files from a new tool directory. He argues the real problem is detecting when `git add` operations with wide pathspecs might be excluding files that should be tracked, based on historical project patterns.

The email shifts the discussion from the original proposal's focus on commit ordering to a more fundamental challenge: how to warn users when their ignore patterns might be causing them to miss important files during adds. This suggests the solution space may need to expand beyond simple config options to include more sophisticated pattern analysis or interactive warnings during `git add`. The tone remains constructive but highlights deeper issues with the original approach.
