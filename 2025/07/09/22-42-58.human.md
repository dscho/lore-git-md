This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about relaxing real-name requirements in Git's contribution guidelines. Brian Carlson responds to Jeff King's suggestion to incorporate CNCF-style wording into the documentation changes, explaining why they find that approach problematic.

Carlson plans to send a v2 of the patch this weekend with updated wording, but expresses reservations about the CNCF's ambiguous definition of "real name" as something recognized by a "community." They argue this could confuse contributors, especially non-native English speakers, and prefers language that more clearly allows pseudonyms while avoiding misleading terminology. The response cites Wikipedia's definition of "name" as a more inclusive model, while acknowledging the need to draw some inspiration from CNCF's approach.

The exchange highlights the careful balance required in documentation wording - maintaining legal compliance while ensuring clarity and inclusivity. Carlson's response suggests the v2 patch will aim for more precise language that accommodates pseudonyms without resorting to legalistic redefinitions of common terms. This continues the thread's trend of thoughtful consideration around both technical identity persistence and contributor experience.
