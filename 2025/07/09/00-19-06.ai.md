This is a process discussion email in the ongoing thread about signature handling improvements in Git's fast-export/fast-import functionality. Christian Couder responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about review response timing norms in the Git development community.

Christian argues against establishing rigid response time expectations, noting contributors have varying personal circumstances (health issues, caregiving responsibilities, limited free time) that make fixed norms impractical. He cites an example of a former Outreachy intern who contributed valuable work despite only being able to engage every few months. The email makes two key points: (1) senior contributors are less likely to abandon work mid-review, making fast responses less critical for them, and (2) imposing response pressure on experienced contributors could increase burnout risk without clear benefit.

The discussion represents a meta-conversation about community norms that emerged alongside the technical work on signature handling. While the technical implementation (v5) has reached consensus and been queued, this email focuses on the human processes surrounding code review. Christian's position contrasts with Junio's preference for more consistent interim communication during review cycles, though both agree thoughtful responses are more valuable than quick superficial ones. The exchange highlights tensions between maintaining productive review workflows and accommodating contributor diversity.
