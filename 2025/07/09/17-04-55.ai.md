This is a technical discussion email in the ongoing git-daemon signal handling thread, focusing on Windows platform considerations for the pipe-based child process tracking approach.

Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón responds to Phillip Wood's concerns about SA_RESTART handling complexity by arguing that non-Windows platforms without SA_RESTART have few users and thus lower risk. He defends his approach of toggling SA_RESTART around poll() calls as addressing the original problem of zombie process cleanup delays on OpenBSD, despite acknowledging it may appear "silly".

The email highlights key technical distinctions in Windows behavior:
- Windows doesn't fail system calls with EINTR (making SA_RESTART less relevant)
- The platform has different signal handling characteristics than Unix-like systems
- Current testing shows signal number 20 behaves as documented on Windows

This continues the core debate between:
1. Phillip's preference for POSIX-consistent behavior (setting errno=EINVAL on Windows)
2. Carlo's transitional approach maintaining backward compatibility (no errno setting)

The exchange shows Carlo maintaining his position that Windows-specific considerations justify keeping some SA_RESTART-related code, while Phillip advocates for simpler, more portable solutions. The discussion remains focused on implementation details rather than fundamental disagreement about the pipe-based tracking approach itself.
