This is a discussion about the proposed deprecation of `core.commentChar=auto`, part of an ongoing thread about whether to remove or fix this problematic feature. Phillip Wood responds to Junio C Hamano's concerns about test failures and alternative approaches to deprecation.

Phillip acknowledges the test failures Junio pointed out but argues they're expected - the tests should be marked to skip when breaking changes are enabled. He then explains why he believes fixing the feature's fundamental design flaws would be impractical. The core issue is that hooks and templates can't reliably communicate which lines should be treated as comments, making the "auto" heuristic inherently unreliable. While the rebase case might be fixable, Phillip argues it's not worth addressing in isolation when other workflows would remain broken.

The email reinforces Phillip's position that deprecation is the right path, noting that Git's expanded support for multi-character comment strings provides users with better alternatives. The discussion remains unresolved, with Phillip providing technical rationale for deprecation while Junio had previously suggested either simplifying the feature or documenting its limitations instead.
