This is a final approval notice for a documentation patch establishing Git's policy on pseudonyms in Signed-off-by trailers. Patrick Steinhardt adds his support to Jeff King's approval of the v2 patch, which has already gone through six rounds of review and achieved consensus.

The patch updates Documentation/SubmittingPatches to explicitly allow non-real names while maintaining legal compliance, requiring that identities be distinctive, identifying, and not misleading. It balances privacy needs with the project's requirements for verifiable contributor identities, drawing on precedents from the Linux kernel and other open source projects.

All substantive concerns have been addressed in this version, including strengthened language about anonymity prohibition (per Junio Hamano's request), identity persistence mechanisms, and consolidated legal citations. The patch has received unanimous approval from all key reviewers, with Jeff King and brian m. carlson finalizing the wording to meet all legal requirements.

This marks the conclusion of a thorough review process that examined DCO compliance, OFAC sanctions, GPL enforcement, and alignment with Linux kernel practices. The policy now clearly accommodates valid pseudonym use cases (like gender identity or privacy concerns) while preventing anonymous or disposable identities.

With this final approval from Patrick Steinhardt, the patch is ready for immediate merging, closing the last gap in Git's contributor documentation regarding identity requirements. The change represents a carefully balanced policy that respects contributor privacy while maintaining the project's legal and practical needs for identifiable contributions.
