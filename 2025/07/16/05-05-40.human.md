Performance optimization follow-up. Jeff King (Peff) responds to René Scharfe's patch converting commit traversal to use priority queues, offering additional review and context.

Peff confirms the patch addresses a known quadratic performance issue he's previously raised, though notes the specific use cases affected (like ":/" rev-parse patterns and fetch's mark_recent_complete_commits()) may be narrower than initially apparent. He shares that he has a related long-running local branch converting rev_info.commits to prio_queue, though it doesn't overlap with this change.

The review highlights two technical points: (1) the patch could potentially avoid list allocation by having callers pass prio_queues directly, though Peff acknowledges this might complicate ONELINE_SEEN mark clearing, and (2) the test case's recursive build_history function could benefit from explanatory comments to clarify its binary tree construction approach.

Peff also notes a minor test script style consideration about command interpolation in test_perf titles, though considers it acceptable in this case. Overall the review is supportive, with the technical suggestions being refinements rather than fundamental objections to the approach. This continues the positive reception the series has received from other reviewers (Junio Hamano and Justin Tobler) while adding implementation insights from someone with deep commit traversal optimization experience.
