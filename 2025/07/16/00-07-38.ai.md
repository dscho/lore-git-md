This is a performance optimization thread discussing the conversion of commit traversal to use priority queues. Junio Hamano raises a follow-up question about potential behavioral changes in the tie-breaking behavior (sort stability) when switching from sorted linked lists to priority queues. 

The thread has already established that René Scharfe's 3-patch series successfully improves worst-case performance from O(n²) to O(log n) for pathological commit histories (like Git's own history with many merges), while maintaining near-identical performance for normal cases. The key technical components involve replacing commit_list_insert_by_date() with prio_queue in pop_most_recent_commit(), introducing a new prio_queue_replace() helper, and applying it to first-parent processing.

Junio's new question probes an important semantic consideration - whether the change from sorted linked lists to priority queues might affect the stability of commit ordering when commits have identical timestamps. This is a subtle but potentially significant detail for operations that depend on deterministic traversal order. The question suggests looking beyond just performance metrics to verify the behavioral equivalence of the new implementation.
