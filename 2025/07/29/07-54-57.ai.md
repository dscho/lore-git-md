This is a technical follow-up in the reflog migration infrastructure thread, focusing on the `REF_HAVE_OLD` flag behavior for HEAD updates. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King's concerns about potential race conditions in split HEAD updates when `REF_HAVE_OLD` is no longer stripped for log-only operations.

The discussion reveals a deeper race condition in the current implementation where HEAD's target ref can change between resolution and update, potentially causing inconsistent reflog entries. Patrick analyzes several scenarios where this could occur, particularly when HEAD is unborn or points to a ref not being updated. He proposes partial solutions: locking the parent ref before splitting HEAD updates and propagating the old OID from parent to reflog entry when `REF_HAVE_OLD` is unset.

Key technical points:
- Confirms the behavior change for split HEAD updates (no longer doing old-oid checks) is intentional
- Identifies a fundamental race in HEAD target resolution that could lead to broken reflog entries
- Proposes mitigation by locking parent refs earlier and propagating old OIDs
- Acknowledges some races (like HEAD flip-flopping) may be unfixable without major changes

The email continues the thread's pattern of deep technical scrutiny, now focusing on atomicity and race conditions in HEAD updates. Patrick's response shows the complexity of maintaining consistency while avoiding overly restrictive locking, leaving some edge cases unresolved. The discussion remains focused on implementation correctness rather than user-facing behavior.
