This is a performance-oriented refactoring of Git's byte-swapping macros in `compat/bswap.h`, part of an ongoing effort to simplify and optimize endianness handling. Sebastian Andrzej Siewior proposes removing custom x86-optimized versions of byte-swapping functions in favor of letting modern compilers optimize simpler code, while maintaining compatibility with systems like musl libc and Windows.

The series focuses on three main changes: standardizing endianness macros across platforms, removing hand-optimized x86 assembly that compilers now handle better, and providing built-in compiler intrinsics where available. The author notes that glibc already provides optimized `ntohl()` implementations, making Git's custom versions largely redundant, though musl libc still benefits from the macro approach. Windows support receives special attention for `ntohll()` which isn't provided by standard libraries there.

The changes are narrowly scoped to `compat/bswap.h` and appear well-motivated by compiler advancements. The author has iterated on the series (now at v3) with typo fixes from previous feedback. This looks like a solid low-risk cleanup that could marginally improve portability and maintenance, though the performance impact is likely minimal on most modern systems.
