Performance optimization follow-up from Junio Hamano on René Scharfe's commit traversal improvements. This is a brief response to the first patch in the series that converted commit list handling to use a priority queue for better worst-case performance.

Hamano acknowledges the impressive 92% speedup shown in the pathological case benchmark (from 2.48s to 0.20s for deep history traversal), while noting with a smiley the modest 1% regression in normal cases (1.071s to 1.079s for Git's own history). The response serves as a quick validation of the maintainer's initial reaction to the performance tradeoffs presented in the patch.

The email doesn't raise any concerns or suggest changes - it appears to be a positive acknowledgment of the demonstrated improvements while recognizing the minor tradeoff for common cases. This suggests the patch is on track for acceptance, though we might expect more technical discussion to follow from other contributors.
