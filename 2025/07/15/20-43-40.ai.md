Performance optimization completing René Scharfe's series converting commit traversal to use priority queues. This third patch introduces `prio_queue_replace()` to optimize the common case of processing first parents in `pop_most_recent_commit()`, recovering the 1% performance regression seen in normal repository traversal after the initial conversion to prio_queue.

The change replaces the sequence of `prio_queue_get()` followed by `prio_queue_put()` for first parents with a single `prio_queue_replace()` operation, avoiding the overhead of removing and reinserting the same element. Junio Hamano notes this optimization particularly benefits Git's own history which contains many merges, though it helps most with linear histories. He approves the approach while observing the `delete_pending` variable name could be more descriptive of its first-parent-specific role.

Key technical points:
- Adds `prio_queue_replace()` operation combining peek+put
- Modifies `pop_most_recent_commit()` to use it for first parent processing
- Maintains same behavior while restoring original performance
- Benchmarks show neutralized regression (1.069s vs 1.073s for `rev-parse`)

This completes the series' goal of improving worst-case performance while preserving best-case behavior through careful optimization of core commit handling. Junio's review indicates likely acceptance of the completed approach.
