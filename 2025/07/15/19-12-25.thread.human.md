This thread discusses Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón's patch series optimizing byte-swapping operations in Git's codebase, with contributions from Sebastian Andrzej Siewior. The series standardizes and improves performance of ntohl() and related functions across different compilers (gcc, clang, MSVC) and platforms (Linux/musl, Windows).

The current v3 iteration has made several improvements: adding MSVC support through endianness constants, removing x86-specific assembly in favor of compiler optimizations, and refactoring macro definitions for consistency. The latest patch reintroduces compiler built-in optimizations (bswap32/bswap64) when available, particularly benefiting architectures like RISC-V and MIPS where manual shift operations would be inefficient. The implementation maintains compatibility across all architectures while improving code generation efficiency.

The series has shown good progress in moving from platform-specific optimizations to more maintainable compiler-assisted approaches. While MSVC still has some optimization challenges with byte operations in put/get_be*() macros, the current changes are behavior-preserving and show measurable improvements. The series appears ready for review, though no feedback has been provided yet on this v3 iteration. The work builds on Git's long-standing platform optimization efforts and represents a thoughtful balance between performance and maintainability.
