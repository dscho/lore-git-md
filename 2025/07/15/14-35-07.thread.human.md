René Scharfe's series optimizing commit traversal by converting from commit lists to priority queues has completed technical review and received final sign-off from Jeff King (Peff). The 3-patch series improves worst-case performance for merge-heavy histories while maintaining or slightly enhancing best-case performance. Key changes include new prio_queue operations, comprehensive test coverage, and careful attention to memory characteristics.

While the series is ready for merging, Lidong Yan's follow-up analysis provides useful context about when these optimizations will be most noticeable. Yan observes that the theoretical speedups (92% improvement in worst-case scenarios) may not always materialize in practice because many real-world repositories don't have merge histories wide enough to trigger the worst-case behavior of the original implementation. The priority queue's O(W log W × N) complexity only shows dramatic improvements over the O(W² × N) commit list approach when W (the maximum number of siblings at any level) is sufficiently large. This insight doesn't require changes to the current series but helps set expectations about where the optimizations will have the most impact.

The discussion highlights Git's careful approach to performance work - even approved optimizations receive continued scrutiny to understand their real-world implications. Both Scharfe's implementation and Yan's analysis demonstrate the project's commitment to thorough, data-driven improvements.
