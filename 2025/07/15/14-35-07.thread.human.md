René Scharfe has proposed a performance optimization series converting Git's commit traversal to use priority queues (prio_queue) for better worst-case behavior. The three-part series first showed a 92% speedup for pathological histories (with 50% merges) while introducing a minor 1% regression in normal cases. Junio Hamano reviewed the initial approach favorably, validating the performance measurements.

The just-arrived third patch completes the optimization by introducing `prio_queue_replace()` to handle first-parent processing more efficiently, eliminating the earlier regression. This change is particularly beneficial for Git's own history which contains many merges. Junio approved the technical approach while suggesting a minor variable name improvement.

The series touches commit.c, prio-queue.[ch], and several other files, with comprehensive performance tests in t/perf/p1501-rev-parse-oneline.sh. It achieves its goal of improving worst-case performance from quadratic to log-linear while maintaining best-case behavior through careful optimization. With Junio's positive review of all three patches, this series appears ready for merging.
