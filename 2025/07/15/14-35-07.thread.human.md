René Scharfe's series optimizing commit traversal using priority queues has reached completion, with all technical feedback addressed and the patches ready for merging. The 3-patch series refactors various commit list operations to use the more efficient prio_queue data structure, particularly improving performance in merge-heavy histories while maintaining or slightly enhancing normal-case speeds.

The key optimization comes from a new `prio_queue_replace()` function that efficiently swaps queue elements while maintaining proper ordering, avoiding the overhead of separate removal and insert operations. This change, along with other improvements in the series, yields a 92% speedup in pathological cases while keeping first-parent traversals slightly faster than the original implementation. The patches touch multiple files including commit.c, walker.c, and fetch-pack.c, with comprehensive unit tests added to verify the new behavior.

Reviewers including Jeff King (Peff) and Junio Hamano have signed off on the changes after several iterations. The series now includes complete meson build system updates, clearer variable naming, and thorough test coverage. With no outstanding objections remaining, this performance improvement is poised to land in the next integration cycle. Scharfe has hinted at potential future work to convert rev_info.commits to prio_queue as well, building on these foundational optimizations.
