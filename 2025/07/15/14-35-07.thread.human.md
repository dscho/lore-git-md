René Scharfe has proposed a performance optimization series that refactors Git's commit traversal to use priority queues (prio_queue) instead of linked lists, significantly improving worst-case performance (92% faster for pathological histories) while maintaining normal-case speeds. The three-part series has received positive technical reviews from Junio Hamano and Justin Tobler, with Junio validating the performance measurements and Justin confirming the soundness of the approach while suggesting minor documentation clarifications.

Jeff King (Peff) has now weighed in, sharing his long-standing concerns about quadratic commit list operations and confirming that René's changes align with the performance bottlenecks he's observed, particularly for ":/" rev-parse operations and fetch operations with many refs. Notably, Peff revealed he's been using his own unpublished prio_queue conversion in production for years, lending additional credibility to the general approach. He offered two minor suggestions for improving test clarity but raised no substantive objections.

The only remaining open question comes from Junio, who is verifying whether the priority queue preserves deterministic traversal order semantics for commits with identical timestamps. With the core technical approach validated by multiple experts and only minor polish items outstanding, this optimization appears ready for merging once the final behavioral verification is complete. The changes particularly benefit repositories with complex histories like Git's own codebase.
