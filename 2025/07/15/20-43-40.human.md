Performance optimization completing René Scharfe's series improving commit traversal with priority queues. This third patch introduces `prio_queue_replace()` to optimize the common case of processing a commit's first parent, recovering the small performance regression seen in normal repository traversal after the initial conversion to priority queues.

Junio Hamano reviews the patch, noting it effectively neutralizes the 1% performance hit measured in Git's own repository (which has more merges than typical projects). The optimization works by peeking at the queue rather than removing and re-adding items when processing first parents. While briefly puzzled by the `delete_pending` variable name, Junio finds it ultimately clear in signaling when queue removal is needed.

The change maintains the series' key achievement - transforming worst-case performance from quadratic to log-linear while preserving best-case behavior. With Junio's positive feedback on both the approach and implementation, this appears to be the final piece of a well-executed optimization effort that will soon land in Git's codebase.
