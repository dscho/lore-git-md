This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about enabling `-Wunreachable-code` warnings in developer builds. The thread has revealed platform-specific complications, particularly with OSX builds failing due to `sigfillset()` in `run-command.c` being flagged as unreachable code when the compiler statically determines it cannot fail.

Jeff King responds to Junio Hamano's frustration with the warning's maintenance burden, acknowledging the tradeoffs. He notes the warning has only found one real issue (in the refs code) so far, but there's uncertainty about how many more false positives might emerge. As a middle ground, he suggests enabling the warning in just one CI job (linux-sha256) to gather more data on its usefulness versus noise.

Key technical points:
- The OSX-specific failure remains unresolved
- The proposed `errno` workaround is considered too subtle
- Discussion has shifted from implementation to policy - whether the warning's benefits outweigh its costs
- The linux-sha256 CI job is proposed as a testbed for further evaluation

The thread shows the maintainer's growing skepticism about strict warning enforcement when it creates maintenance headaches, with Jeff King offering a more measured approach to gather data before making a final decision. The core tension remains between code quality benefits and practical development workflow concerns.
