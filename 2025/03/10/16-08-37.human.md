This is a discussion about index handling behavior in Git's inspection commands, continuing the thread about `--no-optional-locks` implementation.

Jeff King (peff) responds to Junio Hamano's suggestion that read-only commands like `git diff` and `git status` might benefit from opportunistically writing the index back by default. While acknowledging the potential optimization benefits, peff expresses caution about changing this long-standing behavior. He notes that such a change would be a significant departure from current practice and could introduce new complexities, particularly around lock contention and stat cache behavior.

The discussion highlights an interesting tension between optimization opportunities and stability concerns. Peff suggests the optimization might be more for `git status` (which frequently runs in interactive contexts) than for `git diff`, but maintains that the current conservative approach of not automatically writing the index is probably the safer path forward. This exchange represents the final philosophical discussion point in what has otherwise reached technical consensus on the `--no-optional-locks` feature.
