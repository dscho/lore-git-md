This is a thoughtful review response from Junio Hamano regarding the recently completed `the_repository` removal work for core.attributesfile configuration. 

Hamano acknowledges Patrick Steinhardt's earlier review points about commit message structure and the questionable utility of `git_attr_system_is_enabled()` after the refactoring. However, he raises a more fundamental architectural concern about whether attributes should be tied to repository instances at all.

The key observation is that Git's attribute system was designed around multiple active attribute sets (working tree vs index) that can switch dynamically, similar to how multiple index_state instances can exist for a single repository. Hamano suggests that attributes might better follow the index_state model - passed explicitly through call chains rather than being repository-scoped, since a repository can't meaningfully represent multiple concurrent attribute sets.

This appears to be a significant conceptual challenge to the approach taken in the patch series, suggesting that while removing globals is good, the repository-scoping abstraction may not be the most natural fit for attributes. The comparison to index_state handling provides a concrete alternative model to consider. The feedback doesn't reject the work outright but invites deeper consideration of how attributes should be modeled in a multi-repo context.
