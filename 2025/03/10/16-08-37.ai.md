This is a discussion follow-up in the `--no-optional-locks` feature thread, focusing on the philosophical implications of index handling behavior in Git's inspection commands.

Jeff King (peff) responds to Junio Hamano's suggestion that read-only commands like `git diff` and `git status` might benefit from always opportunistically writing updated indexes. While acknowledging the conceptual oddity of read operations modifying repository state, peff expresses skepticism about the practical value of such a change. He notes that while it could optimize stat cache usage, the performance impact may be negligible compared to the actual file reading required for diffs. The discussion highlights a tension between theoretical purity and practical considerations, with peff advocating for maintaining the status quo due to both uncertainty about benefits and the risk of destabilizing long-standing behavior.

The exchange reveals deeper architectural questions about Git's approach to index management that go beyond the immediate `--no-optional-locks` feature. Peff's response suggests these broader questions may warrant separate consideration rather than being addressed as part of the current patch series. The thread continues to balance between solving immediate lock contention issues and preserving Git's fundamental design principles around repository state management.
