This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing thread about unit test isolation in Git's test infrastructure. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio Hamano's preference for actively constructing test environments rather than just preventing config reads.

The exchange clarifies two approaches to test isolation:
1. Junio's preferred method of actively setting controlled values ($HOME, $AUTHOR_NAME etc.)
2. Patrick's more minimal approach of just sanitizing the environment, which he argues may be sufficient for most unit tests

Patrick acknowledges that while Junio's comprehensive approach has merit, he sees value in starting with simpler sanitization as a first step, with the option to add controlled environment variables later if needed. He leaves the door open for implementing both approaches now if the original author prefers.

The discussion continues to refine the technical direction for test isolation, with Patrick's response suggesting a pragmatic middle ground between minimal sanitization and full environment construction. The thread remains focused on finding the right balance between thoroughness and simplicity for the unit test framework.
