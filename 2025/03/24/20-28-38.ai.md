This is a discussion about the security implications of the git-shell command override proposal. Jeff King (peff@peff.net) responds to Junio Hamano's approval of the architectural direction while expressing broader concerns about git-shell's security posture.

The email focuses on three key points about git-shell's role in production environments:
1. Feature-wise, gitolite is likely a better choice for admins needing more than basic functionality
2. Security-wise, http service is preferable for untrusted client scenarios due to git-shell's history of vulnerabilities
3. More general restricted shell implementations may exist that are better hardened for this use case

Jeff acknowledges he hasn't researched alternative restricted shells but maintains his position that git-over-http is the most robust solution for security-sensitive deployments. The response doesn't directly address the technical implementation issues from his previous review, instead providing context about why he views git-shell as a niche solution regardless of the override mechanism's implementation quality.
