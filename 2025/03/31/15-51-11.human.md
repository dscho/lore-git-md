This is a major refactoring and cleanup series targeting the merge machinery, specifically the removal of the legacy merge-recursive implementation in favor of merge-ort.

Elijah Newren proposes to finally remove merge-recursive.[ch] after a long migration period where most callers were switched to the newer merge-ort implementation. The series consists of three main phases: first switching the remaining callers to use ort equivalents (patches 1-5), then deleting merge-recursive entirely (patch 6), and finally cleaning up test infrastructure that was maintaining compatibility with both algorithms (patches 7-8).

The changes are extensive in terms of lines touched (over 5000 deletions), but the author notes the actual code modifications are relatively small - most of the churn comes from moving shared code to merge-ort and deleting the now-unused files. The series completes the long-running effort to make merge-ort the sole merge strategy implementation, which has been in progress for several years.

This appears to be a well-planned and executed cleanup, building on previous work to ensure all functionality (including diff algorithm selection) is properly supported in merge-ort before removing the old implementation. The test changes show careful attention to maintaining coverage while removing now-redundant infrastructure. Given the thorough preparation and the author's deep expertise in Git's merge machinery, this series seems ready for review and likely to be uncontroversial.
