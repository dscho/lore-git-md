Johannes Schindelin kicked off this thread with a straightforward cleanup effort: removing unnecessary comma operators in Git's codebase, starting with instances in `remote-curl.c` and `builtin/rebase.c`. These purely stylistic changes, identified via GitHub's CodeQL tool, quickly gained support from reviewers like Phillip Wood (who confirmed one case was clearly a typo) and Patrick Steinhardt. The discussion then expanded when Phillip found another similar case in `remote-curl.c` and tested Clang's `-Wcomma` flag as a detection method.

Jeff King has now weighed in with a more nuanced perspective. While agreeing that many comma operators can be safely replaced with semicolons (demonstrating clean replacements in `kwset.c` and `xdiff/xdiffi.c`), he points out legitimate uses in performance-sensitive areas like delta encoding and wildcard matching. His analysis shows that some comma operators in loop conditions and complex expressions serve deliberate sequencing purposes, particularly in `diff-delta.c` and `wildmatch.c`. This suggests the cleanup may need careful case-by-case evaluation rather than blanket application.

The thread has evolved from simple mechanical fixes to a more considered discussion about code style in edge cases, with key contributors agreeing on the principle of removing accidental comma usage while recognizing intentional patterns that should remain. No functional changes are proposed—just ongoing refinement of how Git expresses sequencing in its code.
