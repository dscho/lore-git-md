This is a cover letter for a 3-patch series addressing assertion safety in Git's codebase. The series introduces infrastructure to detect and replace potentially dangerous assertions that might contain side effects.

The series builds on previous discussion that validated both the technical approach and resolved licensing concerns. The key components are:
1. A new BUG_IF_NOT() macro that remains active even in production builds (unlike assert())
2. A CI job that detects assertions likely to have side effects using a clever compiler trick (now confirmed public domain)
3. Conversion of the 9 flagged assertions across merge machinery, object storage, and parallel checkout subsystems

The cover letter explains the motivation is to prevent subtle bugs from assertions with side effects that would disappear in production builds. The CI approach is preferred over just documentation since it actively prevents the issue. The implementation has been vetted by multiple reviewers (Junio Hamano, brian m. carlson) and the original technique author (Bruno De Fraine) has placed the method in public domain.

The changes are mechanical conversions of existing assertions, with the most interesting being in merge-ort.c, merge-recursive.c, and object-file.c. The series appears ready for merging with all technical and licensing concerns addressed. The author indicates this is a v2 revision, with the main change being updated documentation about the licensing status of the CI detection method.
