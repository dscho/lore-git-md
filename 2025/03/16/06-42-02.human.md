This is the final patch in a 3-part series addressing potentially unsafe assertions in Git's codebase. The patch systematically replaces `assert()` calls with the new `BUG_IF_NOT()` macro in cases where the compiler cannot verify the assertions are side-effect free, as identified by the CI detection infrastructure added in patch 2.

The changes are mechanical conversions across 7 files, primarily affecting the merge machinery (merge-ort.c, merge-recursive.c), object storage (object-file.c), and parallel checkout. Each replacement maintains the same logical checks but ensures they'll execute in production builds rather than being compiled out with NDEBUG`. The changes are straightforward - for example, converting `assert(!merge_remote_util(commit))` to `BUG_IF_NOT(!merge_remote_util(commit))` in merge-recursive.c.

This patch completes the series' goal of making these important checks more robust by ensuring they remain active in all builds. The changes have been vetted by Junio Hamano and brian m. carlson, with all technical and licensing concerns addressed in earlier patches. The impact is primarily internal safety improvements rather than user-facing changes.
