This is a mentoring discussion about Git's GSoC onboarding process. Junio C Hamano responds to Mahendra Dani's guidance for a new contributor, emphasizing that microprojects don't require permission to start - they're about learning the contribution workflow through small, self-directed improvements. 

The email clarifies that while predefined microprojects are good starting points, contributors can also propose their own small changes. The key is being specific about what needs updating rather than asking open-ended permission ("this documentation says X but should say Y" is better than "can I fix docs?"). 

Junio outlines the range of possible responses to such specific proposals, from outright rejection to collaborative refinement. The underlying message is that the community values initiative and concrete suggestions over vague requests for assignment. This continues the thread's theme of balancing structured onboarding with encouraging contributor agency, though no specific documentation changes have been proposed yet.
