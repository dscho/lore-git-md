This thread centers on the architectural direction for the new `git-blame-tree` feature, where a nearly complete pathspec trie implementation (by Toon Claes) now faces a potential pivot to a Bloom filter–based approach championed by Taylor Blau and Derrick Stolee. 

Jeff King (Peff) recently tipped the scales by endorsing the Bloom filter alternative, noting its production validation at GitHub and likely performance advantages. Derrick Stolee's latest email reinforces this momentum, clarifying that the modern Bloom filter implementation differs fundamentally from a 2011 prototype by introducing a novel "pass un-blamed paths to parents" traversal strategy. This enables persistent result caching—a capability the pathspec trie cannot match. Stolee explicitly dismisses the old version as obsolete and urges Taylor to share their current code with Toon for evaluation.

The discussion now hinges on two concrete next steps: Taylor providing the Bloom filter implementation for comparison, and the community assessing whether its advantages justify abandoning the nearly complete pathspec trie work. While the Bloom filter approach appears technically superior, the pathspec trie series represents significant investment (26 files, 1200+ lines) with some salvageable components. Key participants—Toon, Taylor, Peff, and Stolee—await the code sharing that will determine the feature's final architecture.
