This is the final patch in the `git-blame-tree` series, implementing a performance optimization for tree-level blame. The patch introduces dynamic pathspec narrowing during traversal - as each path's blame is resolved, it's removed from the pathspec to avoid unnecessary future checks. 

The key insight is that while diff operations themselves are cheap, maintaining a full pathspec prevents aggressive history pruning. By progressively narrowing the pathspec, the revision walker can skip entire branches of history that no longer contain any interesting paths. The results are dramatic: 2.35x faster for git.git and 21.68x faster for linux.git in the `--max-depth=0` case.

The implementation adds new pathspec manipulation functions (`pathspec_setup`, `pathspec_drop`) and integrates them with the blame-tree machinery. While technically munging pathspecs mid-traversal isn't officially supported, the approach works reliably with the current codebase and leverages Duy's recent pathspec refactoring.

This patch completes the series' performance story, building on the earlier pathspec trie optimization (patch 7/8) to deliver end-to-end speedups. The only remaining work appears to be finalizing porcelain output formatting and resolving one test case - the core functionality now looks production-ready with comprehensive test coverage and measurable performance benefits.
