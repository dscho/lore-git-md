This is a technical discussion about the CI infrastructure for detecting unsafe assertions, part of the ongoing series to make Git's assertion handling safer.

Brian m. carlson responds to Junio Hamano's review of the clever compiler trick used in patch 2/3 to detect assertions with potential side effects. The mechanism works by redefining `assert()` to reference an undefined symbol in a way that only compiles if the expression can be proven side-effect-free by the optimizer. Carlsson confirms this approach is valid under C99 and notes it's acceptable for CI environments where compiler quality and optimization flags are controlled, mentioning GCC, Clang and MSVC as known-good compilers that should handle it correctly. He asks if Junio has additional concerns beyond the optimization behavior discussed.

The exchange focuses on validating the technical soundness of the detection mechanism, particularly around compiler optimization behavior and compatibility. Carlsson reassures that while less capable compilers might produce false positives, this isn't a concern since the check only runs in CI with known compilers. The discussion remains focused on implementation details rather than proposing changes to the approach.
