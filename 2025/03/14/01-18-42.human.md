This is a technical discussion about compiler optimization behavior in the ongoing series to make Git's assertions safer. The conversation focuses on the clever compiler trick used in patch 2/3 to detect assertions with potential side effects.

Brian Carlson responds to Junio Hamano's review of the CI check mechanism that redefines `assert()` to reference an undefined symbol during static analysis builds. Junio had noted this approach relies on compiler optimization behavior to detect side effects, where the compiler will only optimize away the check if it can prove the expression has no side effects.

Carlson agrees the technique is clever and confirms it should work well in CI environments where compiler quality and optimization flags are controlled. He specifically mentions it should work with major compilers (GCC, Clang, MSVC) and notes the check won't enabled by default, so users with less capable compilers won't be affected. The exchange shows the reviewers carefully considering edge cases and implementation details for this safety mechanism.
