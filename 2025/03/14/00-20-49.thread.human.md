This thread introduces a 3-patch series that makes Git's assertion handling safer by replacing potentially risky `assert()` calls with a new `BUG_IF_NOT()` macro that remains active even in production builds. The series was initiated by concerns that some assertions containing function calls could have side effects, though human inspection suggests the current cases are likely safe.

The first patch established the foundation by adding the `BUG_IF_NOT()` macro to git-compat-util.h, building on Git's existing `BUG()` infrastructure. The second patch, which generated significant discussion, introduced CI detection infrastructure to automatically flag assertions that might have side effects. This used an innovative compiler technique (contributed by Bruno De Fraine) that redefines `assert()` to reference an undefined symbol during static analysis. The approach was vetted by Junio Hamano and brian m. carlson, who confirmed its technical soundness and resolved any concerns.

The final patch, which just arrived, completes the effort by systematically converting the 9 flagged assertions across 7 files to use the new `BUG_IF_NOT()` macro. The changes are mechanical but important, touching key subsystems like the merge machinery (handling conflict types and trivial merges), object storage (filter checks), and parallel checkout (eligibility checks). While the assertions being replaced appear safe to human reviewers, the series provides an important safety net by ensuring these checks remain active in production.

The series is now complete and ready for merging, having been reviewed by both Junio Hamano and brian m. carlson. All technical and licensing questions have been resolved, and the changes are focused on making Git's assertion handling more robust without introducing new risks.
