This thread introduces a 3-patch series to make Git's assertions safer by replacing potentially dangerous `assert()` calls with a new `BUG_IF_NOT()` macro that remains active in production builds. The series was initiated by Elijah Newren and has been reviewed by Junio Hamano and brian m. carlson, who confirmed the technical approach.

The first patch (just received) adds the core infrastructure - a `BUG_IF_NOT()` macro that uses Git's existing BUG() mechanism to ensure assertions remain active, even when standard assertions would be compiled out. This addresses cases where assertions containing function calls could silently disappear in production builds.

The full series will also introduce CI infrastructure to detect risky assertions (using a clever compiler trick that has been vetted for both technical soundness and licensing), then systematically convert the identified cases. Key subsystems affected include merge machinery (merge-ort.c, merge-recursive.c) and object storage (object-file.c), with minor changes to parallel checkout, sequencer, and scalar. 

All concerns about the approach have been resolved - the detection mechanism works reliably across GCC, Clang and MSVC, and licensing questions about the technique have been cleared by contacting the original author. The series appears ready for merging with its combination of new infrastructure, automated detection, and mechanical conversions of the identified cases.
