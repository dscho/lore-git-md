This is a CI/build system patch series introducing static analysis to catch potentially dangerous assertions in Git's codebase. Elijah Newren proposes adding a CI job to detect `assert()` calls that might contain side effects, which could cause subtle bugs when assertions are compiled out in production builds.

The series introduces a new `BUG_IF_NOT()` macro as a safer alternative for assertions that might need side effects, converts the 9 existing `assert()` calls that the analysis flags as potentially problematic, and adds a CI job to catch future violations. The approach uses a compiler/linker hack to identify most side-effect-free assertions while requiring manual review for the few ambiguous cases.

The motivation is to proactively prevent a class of hard-to-debug issues rather than just documenting the risk in CodingGuidelines. The implementation appears thorough, with a new test script and conversions of all flagged assertions. The patch touches several subsystems (merge machinery, object storage, parallel checkout) but the changes are mechanical replacements of assertion macros.

This looks like a well-considered quality improvement that could prevent subtle bugs, though reviewers may want to verify the compiler technique's reliability and whether the 9 converted cases truly needed side effects. The approach of automated enforcement rather than documentation aligns with Git's recent CI-focused development culture.
