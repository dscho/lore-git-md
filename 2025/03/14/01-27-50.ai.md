This is a licensing clarification in the ongoing series about safer assertion handling in Git. The thread had previously established technical validation of the compiler-based assertion detection mechanism, with brian m. carlson confirming it works reliably under C99 with major compilers in CI environments. 

Junio Hamano raises a new concern about whether the clever compiler trick (using an undefined symbol in a redefined assert() macro) might have licensing implications, as it appears similar to a StackOverflow solution under CC-BY-SA. Elijah Newren confirms the core trick was indeed inspired by StackOverflow (though the rest of the patch is original work) and asks for alternative approaches to avoid licensing issues.

The technical aspects of the series remain sound - the CI infrastructure in patch 2/3 successfully identified 9 assertions across merge, object storage and parallel checkout code that were converted to safer BUG_IF_NOT() in patch 3/3. But the discussion now shifts to finding a clean-room implementation of the assertion detection mechanism that doesn't carry potential licensing baggage from StackOverflow.

Key elements at stake are the assert() redefinition trick that makes the CI check work, and the broader goal of preventing debug/production divergence by catching assertions with potential side effects. The merge machinery (merge-ort.c, merge-recursive.c), object storage (object-file.c) and parallel checkout were the main subsystems flagged by this check, though the actual assertions appeared side-effect-free to human review.
