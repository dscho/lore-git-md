This is a performance optimization discussion in the ongoing cruft pack series, specifically addressing the behavior of `--max-pack-size` limits. The thread has reached consensus on most technical aspects, with this being the final open design question.

Taylor Blau proposes modifying cruft pack generation to allow exceeding the size limit by one object, arguing the current strict cutoff causes inefficient repeated repacking of nearly-full packs. The implementation would change `pack-objects.c`'s size handling when `--cruft` is active, letting packs grow slightly beyond configured thresholds.

Junio Hamano raises two key concerns: First, that this diverges from Git's established behavior for regular object packing where limits are strict. Second, that the "one object" allowance could unpredictably violate size expectations since large objects might blow past the limit. He suggests an alternative approach - having the repack logic recognize when a nearly-full pack requires no updates, thus avoiding unnecessary repacks without changing size semantics.

The discussion centers on tradeoffs between:
- Consistency with existing pack behavior vs. practical repack efficiency
- Predictable size limits vs. flexibility for cruft's unique characteristics
- Whether the solution belongs in pack-objects logic or higher-level repack heuristics

Technical context includes:
- `--max-pack-size`/`--max-cruft-size` interaction
- Object selection in `builtin/pack-objects.c`
- Cruft pack aggregation in `builtin/repack.c`

This represents the final design decision needed before the series can proceed, with the maintainer seeking stronger justification for changing established size limit behavior. The resolution will determine whether the patch proceeds as-is or requires redesign to maintain consistency with Git's packing conventions.
