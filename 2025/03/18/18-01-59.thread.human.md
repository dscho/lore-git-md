Tuomas Ahola sent v3 of his four-patch series fixing long-standing edge cases in Git's date approximation ("approxidate") handler. The series has evolved well since v2, with the "today" alias pulled into its own patch (now 2/4) so it can be debated separately from the core fix.

**Patches 1/4 and 3/4 are clean and ready.** Patch 1/4 reworks the test infrastructure to accept human-readable offset strings (like "+2 days") instead of opaque integers, addressing all of Jeff King's and Junio's v2 feedback. Patch 3/4 is the behavioral fix that everyone cares about -- a three-line change checking if `tm_mday` has already been set to a non-negative value before triggering the "most recent" rollback. Jeff King reviewed the v2 version and his suggestions are incorporated. Junio's requests for clearer commit messages and better comments have been addressed.

**Patch 2/4 (the "today" alias) is the only open item.** Junio's concern is that `--since=today` would exclude earlier commits from today because `--since` is "strictly after" -- not a design that `--since=yesterday` or `--since=today` can easily paper over. Tuomas Ahola's proposed fix suggests he may not fully grasp the semantics. This patch is the most likely to be dropped, and dropping it would not affect patches 3/4 or 4/4.

**Patch 4/4 is the newest addition** and the only one Junio hasn't yet reviewed. It introduces a deferred-adjustment scheme for "now" and "yesterday," using negative `tm_mday` values as markers that get resolved later, fixing a consistency problem where "tea yesterday" would produce a date two days ago. The approach is clean and well-explained. It awaits Junio's first look.

The series is nearly there. Only the "today" alias controversy and the new patch 4/4 need attention -- the core fix that everyone has been waiting for is solid and ready.
