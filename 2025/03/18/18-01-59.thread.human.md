Tuomas Ahola sent v2 of his three-patch series fixing long-standing bugs in Git's date approximation (approxidate) engine — some of them dating back to a 2006 bug report by Linus Torvalds. The series addresses several edge cases where special date/time words ("now", "yesterday", "noon", "tea") interacted incorrectly with explicit day-of-month specifications. The root cause was that the "most recent" rollback logic in `date_time()` would fire even after the user had already supplied a specific day, producing contradictory dates.

The series now has all three patches. Patch 2/3, the core fix, adds a three-line guard in `date_time()` that skips the rollback if `tm_mday` has already been set. The key insight, delivered in the new patch 3/3, is a deferred-adjustment scheme: "now" and "yesterday" set `tm_mday` to negative sentinel values (-1 and -2 respectively) that `update_tm()` resolves later. This allows an explicit day-of-month to overwrite the sentinel before resolution, making phrases like "tea yesterday" work correctly. The approach also simplifies `date_time()` by removing its dependency on the current clock time.

Patch 1/3 (seen previously) added infrastructure to control the test clock, and the two substantive patches together add 45 lines of changes spread across `date.c` and `t/t0006-date.sh`. Jeff King, who reviewed v1, raised questions about test methodology, month/year rollover edge cases, and field management — all of which appear to be addressed in v2. No further review of v2 has been posted yet, and the series remains unmerged.
