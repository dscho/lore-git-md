This thread addressed long-standing edge cases in Git's date approximation logic, specifically around how special date/time formats like "today" and "yesterday" interact with explicit day specifications. The five-patch series from Tuomas Ahola fixed several quirks, most notably ensuring "today" consistently means midnight (rather than the previous accidental behavior of "now") and properly handling combinations like "January 5th today pm".

After multiple iterations (v1-v5), the series reached consensus with Junio C Hamano indicating it was ready to merge, pending one minor clarification about the `date_today()` function's handling of its `num` parameter. Tuomas explained this was necessary for consistency with other date functions, demonstrated by a new test case. The changes included both core fixes to the date parsing logic and comprehensive test coverage to prevent regression.

Key participants were Tuomas as the implementer and Junio as the maintainer, with earlier input from Jeff King. The series resolves a bug originally reported by Linus Torvalds in 2006, demonstrating Git's careful approach to changing even long-standing behaviors when they're clearly unintentional. With all technical questions resolved and test coverage in place, the changes are now queued for inclusion in the next release.
