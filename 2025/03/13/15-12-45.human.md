This is a conceptual refinement in the ongoing cruft pack discussion, focusing on the interaction between pack size limits and object freshening behavior.

Junio Hamano responds to Jeff King's suggestion that the size limit considerations might be orthogonal to the core problem of properly freshening objects in cruft packs. The key insight is that the primary goal is to ensure objects that become fresh (via loose copies) are properly rescued from cruft packs, which is handled by the `want_cruft_object_mtime()` logic already implemented.

Hamano agrees with King's perspective that output size limits may be the wrong place to enforce the policy, and that the more important consideration is making sensible decisions about when to roll up existing cruft packs. He notes that previous confusion stemmed from trying to apply thresholds on the output side rather than focusing on the input-side decision making.

This exchange doesn't propose any code changes to the settled v3 implementation, but rather refines the conceptual understanding of how size limits interact with the core freshening behavior. The discussion helps clarify that the size enforcement mechanics are somewhat separable from the policy intent of avoiding fruitless repacks of small cruft packs.
