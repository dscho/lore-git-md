This is a security-focused follow-up in the thread about reproducible Git bundles. Kyle Lippincott questions the threat model behind Simon Josefsson's pursuit of bit-for-bit identical bundles, suggesting that SHA-1 collisions (the scenario bit-for-bit matching would detect) are both extremely difficult to produce and less likely than simpler attacks like compromised validation instructions. The email implies that content-equivalence verification (via commit hashes) may provide sufficient security with less complexity than the current `pack.threads=1 repack` solution.

The response challenges the core motivation for the technical solution that was just validated, proposing that the security benefits of bit-for-bit reproducibility may not justify its implementation costs compared to simpler hash-based verification. This introduces a new dimension to the discussion beyond the technical feasibility already demonstrated - whether the security goal itself is properly scoped.
