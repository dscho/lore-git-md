This is a continuation of the ongoing discussion about assertion patterns in Git's codebase, specifically debating the merits of a proposed BUG_ON() macro versus explicit BUG() calls with descriptive messages.

Junio Hamano responds to Elijah Newren's arguments about assertion ergonomics, maintaining his position that descriptive error messages in BUG() calls provide valuable context that raw conditions cannot. He illustrates this with an example where a BUG() message reveals the developer's intent (array bounds checking) that the condition alone would not convey, helping catch potential logic errors. While agreeing that removing NDEBUG from any assertion-like mechanism addresses the core safety concern, Junio disagrees that assert()-style ergonomics are desirable, preferring explicit explanations.

The email introduces a potential compromise - a BUG_ON() variant that accepts both a condition and message parameter (BUG_ON(<condition>, <message>)), which would prevent misuse while preserving descriptive capability. This represents a middle ground between their positions, though Junio still expresses philosophical reservations about assertion patterns that might discourage thorough error explanations.

Key technical points:
- Debate centers on whether conditions alone (BUG_ON) or messages (BUG) better serve future developers
- Junio demonstrates how messages can reveal intent that conditions obscure
- Both agree NDEBUG must be part of any solution
- New BUG_ON(cond,msg) syntax proposed as potential compromise
- Underlying divide remains between development-time checks (Elijah) and production reliability with documentation (Junio)

The discussion continues refining the error handling proposal while the core philosophical difference persists about the role and form of developer-facing assertions in the codebase.
