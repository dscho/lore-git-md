This is a follow-up discussion in the settled cruft pack implementation thread, focusing on the naming and semantics of size thresholds for cruft pack aggregation.

Jeff King (peff) weighs in on the recent discussion about cruft pack size limits, arguing that the core issue is one of naming rather than technical implementation. He suggests that Taylor Blau's current approach of using `--max-pack-size` may be misleading, since the real goal is to avoid repeatedly rewriting small cruft packs rather than strictly enforcing size limits. 

Peff proposes that the parameter should instead be named something like `--combine-cruft-below-size` to better reflect its advisory nature - indicating when packs should be combined rather than setting a hard maximum. He illustrates with an example showing how the current behavior might create suboptimal pack sizes, and suggests that completely omitting the size limit might actually produce better long-term behavior by creating larger consolidated packs that won't need rewriting.

The email also clarifies that the core functionality around rescuing freshened objects from cruft packs (via `want_cruft_object_mtime()`) is orthogonal to the size limit discussion and appears to be working as intended. This suggests the naming debate doesn't affect the already-settled technical implementation, but rather the user-facing interface and documentation.

This appears to be a late-stage refinement suggestion rather than a fundamental challenge to the implementation, continuing the thread's pattern of polishing the conceptual model while maintaining the working code.
