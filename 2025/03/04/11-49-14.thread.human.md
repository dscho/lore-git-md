The discussion around language-specific `.gitignore` support in `git init` continues to evolve, with the maintainer Junio Hamano reinforcing Git's design philosophy of neutrality. The original proposal sought built-in language awareness, which was firmly rejected as violating Git's principle of not favoring specific ecosystems. Alternatives like Randall Becker's regex-based system and Oleg Taranenko's `--only-gitignores` flag have since emerged, attempting to find middle ground.

Hamano's latest clarification narrows the scope further, emphasizing that Git's template mechanism was designed only for Git-specific files like `.git/info/excludes` and hooks—not for language-specific `.gitignore` patterns. This pushes back against the `--only-gitignores` proposal, suggesting even selective copying of ignore files may overstep Git's intended boundaries. The thread's trajectory now suggests that unless proponents can demonstrate a solution that strictly adheres to Git's neutrality while solving a real need not addressable via existing tools, core is unlikely to change. The next steps likely require either a revised proposal that fits within Git's existing template philosophy or acceptance that users should handle language-specific ignores manually or through external tooling.
