This is a follow-up discussion about the clone advice suppression bugfix series. Phillip Wood raises an important architectural question about Git's advice system that goes beyond the immediate fix.

Phillip points out that the root issue may be more fundamental - the `advise()` function currently ignores `GIT_ADVICE` and `--no-advice` flags, only respecting advice that's explicitly guarded by `advice_enabled()`. This contradicts the documented behavior that these flags should disable all advice hints. His observation suggests the current patch series might be treating a symptom rather than the underlying design issue.

This email shifts the discussion from the specific clone/bundle case to a broader consideration of how Git's advice system should work. Phillip's question implies that a more comprehensive solution might involve modifying `advise()` itself to respect the global advice flags, rather than (or in addition to) the current approach of adding suppression parameters to individual functions.

The thread had previously reached consensus on the technical solution for the clone case, but this new perspective may require revisiting the approach. It's a potential design discussion that could affect how advice is handled throughout Git, not just in the clone operation.
