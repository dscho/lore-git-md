This is a reply in the ongoing thread about defensive array access checks, specifically addressing the second patch in the series which modifies `read-cache.c`.

Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on Johannes Schindelin's v2 patch that reorders array bounds checking in `ce_write_entry()`'s name comparison logic. The patch moves the bounds check before the array access while keeping what the author acknowledges is a redundant NUL check, primarily to reduce cognitive overhead for maintainers.

Peff explicitly approves this approach for the `read-cache.c` changes, finding the justification sound. He also concurs with Junio Hamano's earlier rejection of the first patch (for `diff.c`) as addressing a false positive, though he notes he didn't examine it himself. This leaves the series likely to proceed as a single-patch change focused solely on the index subsystem.

The reply confirms the thread's emerging consensus: while the original CodeQL findings revealed technically safe code, the project appears willing to accept defensive-but-redundant checks when they demonstrably improve maintainability, as in this `read-cache.c` case. The exchange establishes a practical boundary for applying such changes - they're acceptable when they aid comprehension without contradicting subsystem invariants (as the `diff.c` changes did).
