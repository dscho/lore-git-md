This is the final patch in a 3-part series introducing safer assertion infrastructure in Git. The series adds a new `ASSERT()` macro that remains active in production builds (unlike standard `assert()` which is compiled out) and includes CI detection for problematic assertions with side effects.

This patch specifically converts several existing `assert()` calls to the new `ASSERT()` macro in cases where the compiler/linker can't verify the assertions are side-effect free. The changes touch 7 files across various subsystems including merge machinery (`merge-ort.c`, `merge-recursive.c`), object storage (`object-file.c`), parallel checkout, and scalar command.

The patch appears straightforward - it's a mechanical conversion of existing assertions that were flagged by the new CI detection added in patch 2 of the series. The changes are minimal (9 lines changed total) and focused on safety-critical areas where we want assertions to remain active in production builds.

The series has already received positive reviews with all feedback addressed in v3, including renaming the macro from `BUG_IF_NOT()` to `ASSERT()` per Taylor Blau's suggestion. With technical consensus established and all open items resolved, this final patch completes the series which now appears ready for merging.
