This is a follow-up in the ongoing Windows reftable backend compatibility discussion, where Johannes Schindelin responds to Patrick Steinhardt's v2 patch about handling file-in-use errors during unlink operations on Windows.

Schindelin acknowledges the patch as a pragmatic solution to the immediate problem of annoying user prompts during reftable operations, but critiques the underlying architectural approach. He notes that Git's lack of proper OS abstraction leads to these workarounds, comparing unfavorably to Subversion's cleaner design. While he suggests a theoretically better solution would involve introducing a new "best-effort" deletion function, he recognizes the impracticality of such a fundamental change at this stage due to the extensive downstream impact on Git for Windows.

The email serves as a reluctant approval of the current approach, with Schindelin explicitly stating he'll "live with this patch" despite his architectural concerns. This maintains the thread's trajectory of accepting Patrick's reftable-specific solution while acknowledging the prerequisite patch still needs to land first. The discussion highlights the tension between ideal system design and practical constraints in maintaining cross-platform compatibility.
