Performance optimization discussion in the bash completion thread.

Phillip Wood responds to David Mandelberg's benchmark results regarding potential algorithmic improvements to remote ref filtering in Git's bash completion. The discussion centers on whether to implement an O(n+m) algorithm for matching remote refs versus the current O(n*m) approach. 

Key technical points:
- David's benchmark shows the current approach takes ~0.1s for 1000 remotes × 1000 branches, which he considers acceptable
- Phillip had proposed sorting remotes and refs to enable single-pass matching
- Collation handling (LC_COLLATE=C) would be needed to ensure consistent sorting between `git for-each-ref` and `sort`
- David argues the complexity may not justify the optimization given real-world scale
- Phillip agrees to keep the simpler current implementation, noting it could be revisited if performance becomes an issue

The exchange also clarifies behavior differences between `sort -u` and `uniq -u` that affect the implementation.
