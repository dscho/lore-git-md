This is a discussion about assertion practices in Git's codebase, sparked by a bugfix in merge-ort's rename handling. Taylor Blau had suggested documenting a preference for `if (foo) BUG("bar")` over `assert(foo)` in CodingGuidelines, referencing past discussions about edge cases like nedmalloc and Windows builds. 

Elijah Newren pushes back, noting there was no clear project-wide directive to remove assertions, and he's continued using them in merge-ort. He argues that assertions serve a useful middle ground between code comments and full `BUG()` checks - they're more maintainable than comments, catch bugs during development, and are more ergonomic than the `if/BUG` pattern. He does agree that assertions with side effects are problematic but maintains that well-used assertions have value.

The exchange highlights an ongoing tension in the codebase between different error-checking styles. While not blocking the immediate merge-ort fix (which both agree is correct), it surfaces a maintainer-level discussion about coding standards that may warrant documentation. Newren suggests he might add guidance to CodingGuidelines to clarify appropriate assertion patterns, particularly warning against side effects in assertions.
