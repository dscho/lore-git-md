Performance optimization patch in the refs subsystem series, implementing iterator reseek capability for merged iterators. This is patch 11/16 in v6 of Patrick Steinhardt's series addressing refname availability checks.

The patch extends the merged iterator implementation to support seeking (jumping to a specific prefix) by adding a new `merge_ref_iterator_seek()` function and modifying the iterator structure to track ownership separately from active iteration. The key change is preserving the underlying iterators even after exhaustion so they can be reused for subsequent seeks, rather than freeing them immediately. This builds on the iterator lifecycle management introduced in patch 9/16.

The implementation appears straightforward and focused - it adds the new seek capability while maintaining the existing merge iterator behavior. The changes are limited to `refs/iterator.c` with 29 lines added and 9 removed. This continues the series' pattern of incremental improvements to the refs iterator infrastructure, with this change enabling more efficient repeated queries against merged ref sources.

As part of the larger performance optimization effort, this patch helps reduce overhead for operations that need to check refname availability across multiple sources (like during ref updates). The series has been through multiple rounds of review and benchmarking, with this patch representing a logical next step after the iterator lifecycle changes in earlier patches.
