This is a performance discussion in the ongoing path-walk delta compression series. Taylor Blau responds to Junio Hamano's review of the performance data from Derrick Stolee's patch series introducing the --path-walk option for pack-objects.

Taylor raises important questions about the trade-offs between pack size reduction and runtime performance. He presents new experimental data comparing path-walk with name-hash-v2 at different pack.window sizes, showing that some of path-walk's benefits might be achievable through simpler configuration tuning. However, his timing results differ significantly from Stolee's, making direct comparison difficult.

Key technical points discussed:
- Path-walk shows pack size reductions (11.8% in fluentui) but with runtime costs (30.9% slower in Stolee's tests)
- Alternative approach of increasing pack.window with name-hash-v2 achieves similar size reductions (10.3% at window=50) but with varying runtime impacts
- Significant discrepancy in absolute timing measurements between test environments (Stolee's 87s vs Taylor's 932s for same operation)
- Open question whether path-walk's complexity is justified given potential alternatives
- Note that path-walk's primary use case (push operations) may differ from these repack scenarios

The discussion highlights the need for:
- Better understanding of environment differences affecting performance measurements
- Clearer comparison between path-walk and simpler alternatives like adjusted pack.window
- Evaluation of whether the benefits hold for push operations (the primary use case)
- Assessment of whether the complexity is justified given alternative approaches

This email advances the thread by introducing new experimental data and raising important questions about the feature's value proposition, while acknowledging the need for further investigation to draw definitive conclusions.
