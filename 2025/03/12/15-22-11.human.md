This is a performance optimization patch in the ongoing cruft pack size limit discussion, part of Taylor Blau's v4 series. The thread has largely settled other technical aspects of cruft pack handling, with this being the final design decision point.

The patch proposes modifying `pack-objects` to allow cruft packs to exceed the `--max-pack-size` threshold by at most one object, addressing a repacking inefficiency in the current implementation. Taylor argues that the current strict size limit causes repeated repacking of the same objects when packs hover just below the threshold, creating unnecessary work. He provides a concrete example where two packs (99MB + 1MB) would be constantly repacked and resplit at a 100MB limit.

Junio had raised concerns about potentially large size violations if the extra object is itself large, and suggested alternative approaches via heuristics in `repack`. Taylor responds that Git already handles oversized single objects today (a 100MB object in a 50MB limit pack), and that the primary goal here is repacking efficiency rather than strict filesystem constraints. The discussion reveals differing perspectives on whether the solution belongs in `pack-objects` (Taylor's approach) or higher-level repack logic (Junio's suggestion).

The patch appears technically sound but represents a policy decision about where to draw the line between pack size limits and repacking efficiency. It's likely to require maintainer approval given Junio's expressed preference for maintaining consistency with existing pack behavior. The commit message clearly explains the motivation and tradeoffs, making it easy for readers to understand the proposed change.
