This is a discussion about assertion practices in Git's codebase, sparked by a bugfix in merge-ort's rename handling. Elijah Newren responds to Taylor Blau's suggestion about preferring `if (foo) BUG(bar)` over `assert(foo)` patterns in the codebase.

Elijah disagrees with the characterization that the project has settled on avoiding `assert()`, pointing to his own continued use of assertions in merged patches. He outlines a nuanced view of developer checks, positioning assertions as a middle ground between code comments (lightweight) and `BUG()` calls (production-critical). He notes merge-ort's heavier use of assertions stems from its complex optimization preconditions, where assertions help maintain invariants more effectively than comments while being less verbose than full `BUG()` checks.

The email references past discussions about assertions (linking to a 2021 thread) and suggests the real anti-pattern is assertions with side effects, not assertions generally. Elijah considers documenting these distinctions in CodingGuidelines to preempt future discussions, while maintaining his position that assertions serve a valuable role in development-time checking. The technical context remains merge-ort's directory rename handling where a file can be transitively renamed to itself, but the focus here shifts to broader coding standards implications rather than the specific bugfix.
