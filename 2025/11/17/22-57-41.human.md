This is a maintainer response in the ongoing `git worktree` documentation thread, weighing in on Johannes Schindelin's real-world example of data loss from non-nested worktrees. 

Junio Hamano acknowledges the severity of Schindelin's experience where deleting the main worktree orphaned a secondary worktree containing important work. He notes the technical challenge of preventing such `rm -fr` operations through Git mechanisms, while also considering whether `git worktree list` information should be integrated into `git status` output. 

The response thoughtfully balances two competing concerns: the safety benefits of nested worktrees (which maintain clear ownership relationships) versus the traditional recommendation to keep worktrees separate to avoid accidental inclusion in operations like `git add .`. Hamano suggests that while accidental inclusion is problematic, data loss from deletion is far more serious, subtly indicating he may favor revising the documentation to acknowledge nested worktrees as a valid approach.

This moves the thread closer to resolution on the nested worktree question, with the maintainer recognizing that real-world safety concerns may outweigh the theoretical cleanliness of separate worktree directories.
