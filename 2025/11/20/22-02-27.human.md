This is a discussion about the ongoing `git-history` command proposal, specifically addressing concerns about the implementation scope and design decisions.

Elijah Newren responds to Junio Hamano's suggestion to limit the initial scope of the `git-history` command to single-branch cases. While acknowledging the intent to start small, Elijah argues that this approach might actually complicate matters by not properly handling edge cases from the beginning. He points out that failing to verify the single-branch condition could lead to buggy behavior when the command is used in more complex scenarios.

The email highlights several technical concerns that remain unresolved. These include potential issues with index preservation during commit splitting, handling of extended commit headers in edited commits, and the somewhat surprising behavior of automatically associating original commit messages with the second commit rather than the first. Elijah also revisits his earlier feedback about patches 5 and 9, suggesting the changes needed might be more substantial than Junio anticipated.

While recognizing the progress made, Elijah expresses mild surprise at Junio's characterization of the remaining work as requiring only a "small and final reroll." The discussion represents a key moment in the series' review, where maintainers are negotiating the appropriate balance between initial scope limitation and comprehensive solution design.
