This thread discusses adding a new "lisp" userdiff driver to better handle Common Lisp syntax, complementing the existing "scheme" driver. Johannes Sixt initially questioned whether a separate driver was truly necessary versus extending the scheme driver, noting similarities in their patterns and suggesting potential unification.

Scott L. Burson has now provided a detailed justification for the new driver, explaining three key differences in Common Lisp syntax that the current scheme driver doesn't handle well: its 'defun'/'deftype' forms, macro naming conventions, and distinctive indentation patterns. He notes this approach was vetted with other Common Lisp developers. While acknowledging the patterns could theoretically be merged into the scheme driver, Burson suggests a separate driver might improve discoverability and defers to Atharva Raykar (the scheme driver author) on whether the lisp patterns would cause false positives in scheme code.

The technical discussion continues around edge cases like vertical bar escape sequences, which Burson explains are technically correct for Common Lisp but unlikely to appear in practice. The thread now awaits input from the scheme driver maintainer to determine whether to proceed with a separate driver or attempt pattern unification.
