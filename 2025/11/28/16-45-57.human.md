This is a bugfix for the `git last-modified` command, addressing cross-platform issues that were causing incorrect modification attribution on big-endian systems like s390x and HP NonStop. The problem was first identified in tests 16 and 19 of t8020-last-modified.sh, where merge commits were incorrectly being blamed for file modifications instead of the actual source commits.

Toon Claes has now identified and fixed the root cause, which turns out to affect more than just big-endian systems - the issue was simply more visible there. The fix has been submitted as a separate patch series. This follows earlier investigation by Jeff King and Anders Kaseorg who helped reproduce and diagnose the platform-specific failures.

The core issue appears to relate to how blame information was being propagated through merge commits in the performance-optimized algorithm. While the optimization provided significant speedups (2.5x-111.7x) on little-endian systems, this edge case wasn't caught until testing on big-endian architectures. The fix should make the behavior consistent across all platforms while maintaining the performance benefits.

This is an important correction for the recently merged optimization series, ensuring the faster algorithm works correctly everywhere. The thorough cross-platform testing and quick response to the reported issues demonstrate good maintenance practices around the new feature.
