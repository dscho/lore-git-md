Bugfix addressing cross-platform issues in `git last-modified` optimization. This follow-up from Toon Claes confirms the root cause of test failures on s390x and NonStop systems is not just a big-endian issue, but a broader algorithmic problem in the recently merged performance optimization series. The patch fixes incorrect modification attribution through merge commits that was causing test failures in t8020-last-modified.sh (tests 16 and 19), where files were being wrongly assigned to merge commits rather than their actual sources.

Key technical details from the investigation:
- The bug manifests when traversing merge commits, either failing to propagate blame properly or not propagating it at all
- While initially observed on big-endian systems (s390x and NonStop), the issue affects all platforms
- The fix involves correcting how merge commit parent ordering is handled in the generation-sorted priority queue
- EWAH bitmap operations and timestamp processing were examined but not found to be the root cause

The patch submission (linked in the email) addresses these issues while preserving the 2.5x-111.7x performance gains originally achieved. This response confirms Jeff King's earlier suspicion about merge blame propagation problems and expands the scope beyond just endianness concerns. The fix appears to maintain the algorithm's core benefits while correcting its handling of merge commit edge cases.
