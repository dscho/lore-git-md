This is a technical discussion about the `git replay --revert` feature's future direction regarding disconnected commit handling. Junio Hamano responds to Siddharth Asthana's agreement to defer `--no-walk` support, arguing that even when implemented, `--no-walk` wouldn't properly address the core use case of selectively reverting non-contiguous commits from a series.

Junio outlines a more sophisticated approach that would:
1. Parse complex revision specifications using Git's existing `setup_revisions()` infrastructure
2. Process ranges and individual commits as independent "islands"
3. Deduplicate the results while preserving their disconnected nature

The email emphasizes that while this advanced functionality is valuable (particularly for scenarios like reverting specific patches from a merged topic), it's correctly being deferred to a future series. The current focus remains on solidifying the basic `--revert` functionality, which already handles contiguous ranges well through the existing revision walking machinery.

This continues the thread's pattern of careful interface design, with Junio providing architectural guidance while acknowledging the current series' appropriate scope limitations. The discussion helps frame expectations for what might come in future iterations of the immediate work nears completion.
