This is a technical discussion about the `git replay --revert` interface design, specifically around handling complex revision ranges. Elijah Newren responds to Junio Hamano's suggestions about parsing negative refs (like `^topic~7`) and ancestry-path queries in the context of the new `--revert` mode.

Elijah argues against simply rejecting negative refs, noting they're a key feature for `git replay --onto` (allowing multi-branch rebases) and are explicitly documented in the manual. He points out the inconsistency that would arise if `--revert` treated revision ranges differently from other modes. The discussion reveals a tension between maintaining Git's standard revision parsing behavior and adding special handling for disconnected commits.

The email suggests a broader solution: adding a new revision-walking flag that could benefit both `git log` and `git replay`, allowing selective treatment of commits as individual "islands" rather than connected ranges. This would provide a more consistent approach across Git commands rather than having mode-specific parsing rules in `git replay`.

Elijah also questions whether the complexity is justified when users could simply invoke `git replay` multiple times for truly disconnected commits. The exchange highlights the careful balance needed between powerful flexibility and implementation complexity in Git's revision parsing system.
