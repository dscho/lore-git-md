This thread centers on extending Git's `git replay` command to support for reverting commits, particularly for GitLab's Gitaly service. The initial approach proposed using a `--revert` flag that reused cherry-pick infrastructure, but this ran into both technical issues (test failures in core functionality) and design concerns from key reviewers. Phillip Wood and Elijah Newren, with Junio Hamano's agreement, argued that revert should be a distinct operation mode rather than a flag combination, citing confusing interactions and misalignment with real-world use cases.

The latest email from Siddharth Asthana responds to Johannes Schindelin's review, acknowledging two distinct revert needs in Gitaly: commit-by-commit reverts (for auditability) and bulk reverts via `merge-tree` (for efficiency). While the response focuses on test suite improvements and documenting alternative approaches, it doesn't yet address the broader architectural consensus around separating operation modes. The thread shows clear agreement on improving test quality and operation semantics, with the main open question being how to balance individual versus bulk reversal approaches in the final implementation. Junio Hamano remains actively involved, providing both technical guidance and coordination with parallel work.
