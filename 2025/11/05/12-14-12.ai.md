This is a new participant joining the ongoing discussion about extended attribute reporting in `git diff`. Ben Knoble weighs in on the output format debate, offering practical considerations for script parsing.

The email focuses on the technical tradeoffs between two proposed formats for reporting file attributes in raw diffs. Ben considers both the letter-code approach ("tt,ic,cl") and key-value pairs ("binary=tt,incomplete=ic") that were previously proposed by Junio and Justin respectively. While acknowledging the appeal of self-documenting key-value pairs, Ben raises concerns about their parseability compared to line-oriented formats, suggesting that processing comma-separated key-value pairs may be more complex for scripts than handling one attribute per line.

Ben's contribution introduces concrete implementation considerations to the format discussion, particularly around script consumption - a point Junio had recently emphasized. The email doesn't propose a definitive solution but rather highlights the tradeoff between human readability (favored by key-value pairs) and script processing simplicity (potentially better served by line-oriented formats). This moves the discussion toward more practical evaluation of the competing proposals while maintaining the thread's focus on creating a machine-parseable attribute reporting system.
