This is a technical discussion about memory safety and ASan (AddressSanitizer) behavior, specifically regarding patch 3/9 in Jeff King's series that enables NO_MMAP when building with ASan.

Collin Funk follows up on Jeff's earlier demonstration that mmap-based ASan detection is unreliable compared to malloc-based detection. Jeff had shown that ASan inconsistently catches page-sized overflows with mmap and completely misses sub-page overflows, while malloc-based detection reliably catches all buffer overflows. Collin acknowledges Jeff's more thorough test case that maps actual files of different sizes, which clearly demonstrates the limitations of mmap-based ASan checking.

The key takeaway is that Jeff's approach of using NO_MMAP with ASan (forcing malloc-based allocation) provides more reliable detection of buffer overflows, especially for small buffers that don't fill a whole page. Collin, who had initially questioned whether modern compilers might handle this better, now agrees the patch makes sense after seeing the concrete evidence.
