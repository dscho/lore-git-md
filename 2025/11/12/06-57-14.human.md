This is a security discussion follow-up regarding the attribute macro expansion fix. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King's proposed iterative solution for preventing stack overflow in Git's attribute system.

Patrick acknowledges the iterative approach solves the immediate stack overflow risk but raises concerns about trading it for potential heap exhaustion from malicious input. He notes that while extremely large attribute files would be needed to trigger OOM (likely exceeding hosting provider limits anyway), he personally would have preferred implementing a hard recursion limit instead. His reasoning is that a limit would prevent both compute and memory growth while allowing for proper error messages.

The email presents a thoughtful counterpoint to the current approach, though Patrick ultimately concedes the iterative solution is acceptable. He's seeking confirmation that his understanding of the tradeoffs aligns with Jeff's thinking. The discussion remains technical and constructive, focusing on the practical implications of different mitigation strategies rather than rejecting the proposed fix outright.
