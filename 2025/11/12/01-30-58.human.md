This is a security fix discussion for the attribute macro expansion system in Git. Ben Knoble is responding to Jeff King's initial patch that converts the recursive macro expansion to an iterative approach to prevent stack overflow attacks.

The conversation focuses on the memory implications of the fix. While Jeff's patch successfully converts the stack-based recursion into heap-allocated iteration, Ben raises a thoughtful question about whether we're simply trading one resource exhaustion scenario (stack overflow) for another (heap OOM). He acknowledges this is likely an acceptable tradeoff, noting that heap exhaustion is generally more graceful than stack crashes and that optimizing further would require significant complexity for a rarely-used feature.

Ben shares his positive experience with attribute macros for custom diff drivers while acknowledging their limited adoption due to sharing difficulties. The discussion remains technical and constructive, with no objections to the core solution - just clarifying questions about its implications. The patch appears sound and ready for merging once this memory consideration is addressed.
