This is a technical discussion about Patch 3 in Jeff King's ASan series, specifically addressing whether modern compilers' ASan implementations can detect mmap bounds violations without the NO_MMAP workaround.

Jeff King responds to Collin Funk's findings about modern compiler support for mmap bounds checking under ASan. While acknowledging the LLVM interceptor exists, Jeff demonstrates through test cases that ASan's mmap detection is inconsistent - sometimes catching page-sized overflows but missing sub-page violations, and producing different results across runs. He shows that malloc-based detection (what NO_MMAP uses) provides more reliable and consistent overflow detection, especially for small buffers.

Key technical points:
- The discussion centers around test programs demonstrating mmap vs malloc behavior under ASan
- ASan sometimes detects page-sized mmap overflows but inconsistently labels them as "unknown crash"
- Sub-page mmap overflows (like 372-byte file) go completely undetected
- Malloc-based access consistently triggers ASan's heap-buffer-overflow detection
- The test cases suggest NO_MMAP may still provide value for thorough bounds checking

This email doesn't propose changes to Patch 3 but provides empirical evidence supporting its approach. The discussion highlights the tradeoffs between mmap efficiency and ASan detection reliability, with Jeff maintaining that the NO_MMAP workaround offers more comprehensive safety checking despite compiler improvements.
