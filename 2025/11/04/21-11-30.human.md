This is a follow-up discussion about a recently fixed bug in ref-filter's object parsing logic. Jeff King (peff) responds to Junio Hamano's question about whether the performance optimization from commit 054f5f457e ("ref-filter: parse objects on demand") needs new benchmarks.

Peff confirms that the optimization specifically benefits formats like %(raw) that need object contents without parsing, and that this case wouldn't be affected by the recent fix. He notes that most other placeholders either don't need the object at all or require parsing anyway, so the optimization's impact is limited to %(raw)-like cases.

The discussion then considers potential future improvements, suggesting that more granular parsing could help additional formats by skipping unneeded parts of objects. Peff mentions that pretty.c's parser already does something similar, and raises the idea of unifying these parsing approaches, though this is noted as a separate consideration from the current fix.

The exchange serves as a) confirmation that the fix maintains the intended performance benefits, and b) brainstorming about potential future optimizations in object parsing, while making clear these are distinct from the regression fix.
