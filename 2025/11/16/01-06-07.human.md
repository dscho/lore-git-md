This is a maintainer response in the ongoing discussion about adding a `--committer` option to `git commit`. Junio Hamano reiterates his position that the asymmetry between author and committer fields is intentional and reflects their different purposes in Git's data model. 

He explains that the committer field records who physically made the commit, while the author field records who wrote the change, noting this distinction naturally leads to different use patterns. The response emphasizes that in typical workflows (like patch submission), there's frequent need to adjust authorship but almost never a need to manually specify the committer, since the person at the terminal is inherently the committer.

The email serves as a philosophical counterpoint to ZheNing Hu's argument for interface symmetry, suggesting that while the implementation may be sound, the feature itself doesn't align with Git's design principles or real-world usage patterns. This appears to be the core disagreement blocking the patch's acceptance, with Junio's position remaining firm after four iterations of the proposal.
