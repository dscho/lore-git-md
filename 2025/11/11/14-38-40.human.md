This is a discussion in the ongoing thread about adding a `--committer` option to `git commit`. Phillip Wood questions the practical need for this feature, pushing back on ZheNing Hu's justification about fixing misconfigured committer information.

Phillip makes two key points: First, if you're amending commits to fix authorship, the committer info will automatically update to your current config. Second, if you haven't yet realized your config is wrong when making the initial commit, the `--committer` option wouldn't help since you wouldn't know to use it. He suggests the proposed solution doesn't actually address the stated problem of forgotten configuration.

This continues the thread's central tension between those who see value in command-line symmetry with `--author` (ZheNing, Patrick Steinhardt) and those who question whether existing methods (environment variables, config) are sufficient (Phillip, Junio Hamano). Phillip's response focuses narrowly on whether the feature solves the specific "forgotten config" use case that ZheNing presented earlier in the thread.

The exchange highlights how even well-motivated features need to demonstrate they solve problems that existing mechanisms can't. Phillip's technical pushback forces more precise justification of the feature's value proposition.
