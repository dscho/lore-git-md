Critical response to gitignore redesign proposal from Brian M. Carlson. This is a substantive technical counterpoint to Ryan Johnson's earlier proposal for overhauling gitignore functionality.

Carlson raises several concerns about the proposed changes:
1. Questions the need for `.gitignore.local` when existing mechanisms (`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/git/ignore` and `.git/info/exclude`) already cover both global and per-repository ignore needs
2. Strongly opposes removing the dot prefix from gitignore files, citing historical VCS practices and tooling expectations
3. Objects to GitHub-specific template links in new gitignore files, noting Git's forge-agnostic stance and the bloat of GitHub's templates
4. Challenges the YAML format proposal due to parser inconsistencies, Unicode handling issues, and lack of clear advantage over the current format

The response comes from an experienced developer (13+ years) who prefers manually curated ignore files and sees the current system as sufficient. Technical concerns about YAML parsing edge cases (particularly around non-UTF-8 filenames) appear well-founded. The critique suggests the proposal may be solving non-problems while introducing new technical debt.

This sets up key debate points that future replies will need to address: backward compatibility, cross-platform consistency, and whether the complexity justifies the benefits. Carlson's objections carry weight given their experience and technical specifics, though some points (like dot-file conventions) may be more subjective.
