This thread discusses a regression in `git clone` where whitespace in `-c`/`--config` arguments (like `'-c KEY=VALUE'`) stopped working in v2.52.0+. The behavior dated back to 2010 but was removed in commit dcecac2580ef, breaking some existing workflows.

Jeff King (peff) and Junio Hamano analyzed the history, finding the whitespace-trimming was an accidental feature that violated Git's config format. While widely used, it was inconsistent - working in `git clone` but not `git -c`, and already partially broken since 2021. Peff argues this should be treated as a bugfix rather than preserving problematic behavior.

Two fixes emerged: Johannes Schindelin (Dscho) proposed modifying string-list infrastructure to selectively trim keys, while Junio suggested a simpler, temporary fix just for `clone.c`. Peff's latest email notes the original behavior was broader than just clone - it also affected `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` parsing, trimming whitespace on both sides of the equals sign. He points out this was already broken for `git -c` since 2021 without causing issues, suggesting the impact may be limited.

The discussion now centers on whether to implement any fix or leave the change as an intentional correction. Peff leans toward the latter, noting third-party tools should adapt to Git's proper syntax rather than maintain backward compatibility for what was effectively a bug. The thread has clarified the exact scope of the original behavior while questioning if preserving it is worth the complexity.
