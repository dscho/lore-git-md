This is a design discussion response in the ongoing `git replay --revert` feature thread. Elijah Newren weighs in on Phillip Wood's critique of the proposed revert functionality, agreeing with the core concerns while offering additional perspective.

Elijah frames the issue in terms of Git's existing sequencer operations, noting that reverting logically belongs as a distinct mode alongside `--onto` and `--advance` rather than as a modifier flag. He questions the practical utility of the current implementation, suggesting most users would prefer bulk reverts (as Johannes Schindelin proposed) over commit-by-commit reversal. The technical feedback focuses on command-line semantics, pointing out that `--revert` combined with `--advance` creates confusing terminology ("revert" implying backward movement vs "advance" implying forward).

The response identifies three key design problems: 1) The current `--revert` flag implementation doesn't align with user mental models of version control operations, 2) The feature's interaction model with other flags needs restructuring to properly reflect operation modes, and 3) The test cases don't reflect realistic usage scenarios. Elijah suggests making `--revert` a primary operation mode incompatible with both `--onto` and `--advance`, while maintaining `--contained` as a modifier specific to `--onto`.

This email moves the discussion toward a potential redesign by validating Phillip Wood's concerns from a different architectural perspective, while stopping short of proposing specific implementation changes. It leaves open whether to pursue the current approach with fixes or reconsider the fundamental design, noting the tension between supporting individual commit reversal versus bulk operations.
