This is a technical discussion about cache-tree parsing safety and potential format changes, part of Jeff King's ASan hardening series.

Jeff King (Peff) responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about the current cache-tree parsing implementation in Patch 4/9. The discussion has evolved to consider four approaches: (1) Junio's more thorough validation implementation, (2) copying to NUL-terminated buffers, (3) keeping current strtol() with newline checks, or (4) introducing a binary cache-tree v2 format.

Peff defends the current approach's cleverness in handling unsigned wraparound and signed range validation, though acknowledges alternatives like checked-operation functions or a global strntoi() utility. He notes Junio has already merged the current version into 'next' but remains open to alternatives.

Regarding Junio's cache-tree v2 proposal, Peff agrees with the technical merits but expresses concern about the engineering effort required versus the relatively small parsing problem being solved. He notes the lack of other compelling reasons to change the format makes the transition harder to justify, though doesn't oppose the idea outright.

The exchange shows the series is effectively approved but leaves open whether to stick with the current parsing approach or pursue the more fundamental v2 format change. Peff's position favors the simpler solution for now while acknowledging the v2 approach's technical soundness.
