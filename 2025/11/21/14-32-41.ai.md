This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about `git cp` functionality and Git's content-based tracking mechanisms. Martin Guy responds to Randall Becker's question about SHA-1 collision detection during copy/rename operations, confirming that since `git mv` is implemented as `rm`+`add` with retrospective line comparison, the same collision detection would theoretically apply. He references Linus Torvalds' design philosophy again, noting how Git's post-hoc analysis would correctly track content movement even in complex cases like splitting utility functions across files.

Martin expresses admiration for Git's retrospective analysis capabilities but voices a personal wish for word-based rather than line-based diffing, suggesting this could enable more semantic change analysis while remaining compatible with Git's existing architecture. The email doesn't advance the core `git cp` proposal but reinforces understanding of Git's content-tracking mechanics and hints at potential future directions for diff granularity. No new technical proposals or objections are introduced.
