This is a follow-up in the ongoing thread about test failures in t8020.16 and t8020.19 involving `git last-modified` during merges on the NonStop platform. Randall Becker, the original reporter, responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk's reference to a potentially related report by shifting focus to the practical implications for shipping Git 2.52.0 on NonStop.

The email reveals two key new developments: First, Becker's team is actively evaluating whether to ship 2.52.0 given these test issues, indicating the problem has real-world deployment consequences. Second, they're seeking clarity on whether the root cause lies in the test code itself or in Git's merge functionality - a distinction that would determine the severity and required response time for a fix.

Notably, Becker does not engage with the technical content of the linked report that Haugsbakk referenced, instead focusing on the packaging timeline concerns. The email underscores the reproducibility of the failures (previously established as 100% on NonStop) but provides no new technical analysis of the underlying `git name-rev --tags` behavior that appears to be at fault.

The thread remains open on several fronts: the original technical mystery of why tag resolution fails during merges, the practical question of whether to ship 2.52.0 with known test failures, and the unresolved connection to the other report about similar tag resolution issues. Becker's position as a packager rather than a core developer may explain the shift toward deployment concerns rather than technical investigation in this response.
