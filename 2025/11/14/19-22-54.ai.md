This is a maintainer response in the ongoing discussion about AI tool attribution in commit messages, separate from the technical UTF-8 alignment fixes in the patch series. Junio C Hamano raises several concerns about the proposed `Co-developed-by` trailer for AI tools, questioning what value it provides to the Git project beyond corporate tracking needs. 

The response makes several key points: disclosure of tool usage (whether AI or traditional scripts) is already common practice in commit messages without special trailers; the submitter's obligations under DCO remain unchanged regardless of tool usage; and there are particular legal risks with AI-generated code regarding copyright and licensing that may not be immediately apparent to submitters. Hamano notes these risks primarily affect the submitter rather than the project, ironically suggesting the project might be better off not knowing about AI tool usage to avoid potential liability.

The email continues the meta-discussion about commit message conventions while maintaining clear separation from the technical merits of Jiang Xin's UTF-8 alignment fixes. It reflects broader community concerns about how to handle AI-assisted development transparently while maintaining project integrity and legal safety. The technical portion of the original patch series appears unaffected by this discussion and remains ready for merging based on prior reviews.
