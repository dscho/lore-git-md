This is a discussion thread about symref usage patterns in Git, continuing a conversation that started in October 2024. Kristoffer Haugsbakk shares their evolving approach to using symbolic references (symrefs) after encountering some pitfalls.

The thread documents a learning journey where the author initially tried creating symrefs under `refs/heads/` pointing to remote-tracking branches, but found this problematic when committing (since it would move the remote-tracking branch). They then experimented with top-level symrefs (like `H`), but discovered these are worktree-specific. Their current solution uses symrefs under `refs/` (like `refs/C` and `refs/O`) pointing to local and remote branches respectively, which they've found stable for about six months.

The discussion provides practical insights into symref behavior that might not be immediately obvious from the man pages, particularly around worktree scope and the interaction between symrefs and remote-tracking branches. While not proposing any code changes, it offers valuable real-world experience that could inform future documentation improvements or tooling around symref usage.
