The thread began as a straightforward bugfix for the git-credential-netrc helper, which was incorrectly rejecting symbolic port names (like "smtps") despite git-send-email's support for them. Maxim Cournoyer's initial solution - updating the port validation regex to match RFC6335 standards - received technical approval from Junio Hamano after Andreas Schwab provided the relevant standards reference. The patch was nearly ready for merging, needing only minor cleanup.

However, Brian Carlson has now raised deeper architectural concerns about credential manager behavior with symbolic ports. They question whether accepting symbolic ports in the netrc helper is the right approach, pointing out potential inconsistencies in how different credential managers might match symbolic vs numeric ports across various protocols (not just email). Their message suggests the port resolution might better belong in git-send-email itself rather than individual credential helpers, and that the credential system's documentation may need updates to clarify expected behavior.

This shifts the discussion from a specific regex fix to broader design questions about the credential system's handling of port representations. While the RFC6335-based technical solution remains valid, the thread now needs to address whether this is architecturally the right place to solve the problem, and whether changes might be needed in git-send-email or credential system documentation to ensure consistent behavior. Junio's earlier approval was based on the narrow technical merits; the new questions may require revisiting the solution approach.
