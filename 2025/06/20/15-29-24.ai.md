Thread continuation about symref usage patterns and pitfalls. This email from Kristoffer Haugsbakk shares practical experiences with symbolic references (symrefs) in Git, building on previous discussion about their intended usage.

The author describes their evolving approach to using symrefs after encountering several pitfalls. They initially tried creating symrefs under `refs/heads/` pointing to remote-tracking branches, but found this problematic when committing (as it would move the remote-tracking branch). They then switched to root-level symrefs (like `H`), but discovered these are worktree-specific. Their current solution uses `refs/H` and `refs/O` symrefs pointing to local and remote branches respectively, which work across worktrees and avoid the earlier issues.

Key technical details include:
- Specific symref patterns used: `refs/C` for local branches and `refs/O` for remote branches
- Mention of `git symbolic-ref` command usage
- Worktree behavior differences between root-level and `refs/`-prefixed symrefs
- Practical use case for rebasing on remote branches via symrefs

The email provides real-world validation of symref usage patterns discussed earlier in the thread, showing how the theoretical advice plays out in practice with concrete examples and discovered edge cases. The author reports these patterns have been stable in their workflow for over six months.
