This is a policy discussion about maintaining niche contrib scripts, specifically the `thunderbird-patch-inline` tool in Git's contrib directory. Junio C Hamano weighs in on Phillip Wood's v2 patch to update the script, which had been proposed for removal by Patrick Steinhardt due to lack of maintenance.

The discussion has reached an impasse - Phillip's technical fixes are complete but he doesn't personally use the script, while Junio introduces the possibility of silent users who may have locally fixed issues without reporting them. This complicates the removal rationale, as the script may have more users than visible in the mailing list archives.

Junio suggests dropping the patch from his tree since the discussion has stalled, while noting that unrelated documentation updates in `git format-patch`'s manpage are still needed to reflect current Thunderbird extensions. The thread highlights the broader policy question of how to handle niche contrib tools with low visibility but potential silent users.

The patch itself appears technically sound but lacks a clear advocate - Phillip implemented the fixes but doesn't use the tool, while opponents of keeping it cite years of inactivity. This leaves the script's fate uncertain, with Junio's intervention suggesting contrib may retain such scripts unless proven harmful rather than just based on visible usage metrics.
