This is a response to a bug report about fsck warnings during git.git cloning. Jeff King explains that the "badFilemode" and "missingTaggerEntry" warnings are expected artifacts from Git's early history - specifically an old tag format and non-standard file modes from before conventions were established. He notes these are harmless and don't affect repository functionality.

The response provides two key pieces of information for users encountering these warnings: 1) they can be safely ignored as historical quirks, and 2) specific config settings to suppress them if desired. King also observes an inconsistency where `git fsck` behaves differently from fetch/push operations regarding strict checking, suggesting this area could use cleanup but isn't urgent.

The explanation is thorough yet accessible, putting the warnings in historical context while giving practical advice. The PS about fsck behavior differences adds useful technical depth for maintainers who might want to standardize these checks in the future.
