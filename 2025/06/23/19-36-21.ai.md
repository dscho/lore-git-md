Performance analysis of `git rev-list --changed-paths` multi-path filtering. Junio C Hamano explains why the performance anomaly Kai Koponen observed (where querying two paths takes 3x longer than their individual queries) is expected behavior rather than a bug. The commit-graph's bloom filters are intentionally disabled for multiple pathspecs as part of their design constraints, as confirmed by Git's internal documentation. This explains why the performance doesn't benefit from early termination optimizations when multiple paths are specified - the bloom filter optimization simply isn't applied in this case. The response clarifies this is working as designed, though the behavior may be surprising to users expecting linear scaling. The explanation points to `forbid_bloom_filters()` in revision.c (lines 674-686) as the key implementation detail enforcing this single-path restriction. This settles the immediate question while implicitly raising the question of whether the design could/should be extended to handle multiple paths more efficiently in the future.
