This is patch 4/6 in the bswap.h refactoring series, focusing on standardizing the ntohl/ntohll macros. The discussion confirms that modern compilers (GCC/Clang with -O2) already optimize shift-based byte swaps to bswap instructions on x86, making the performance motivation for redefining these macros less critical than initially thought. However, Junio Hamano notes the architectural cleanup value in separating the concerns of (1) optimal bswap implementation and (2) host-network byte order helpers, maintaining support for the change on those grounds.

The patch represents a logical step in the series' progression toward cleaner byte-order handling, following the foundational work in patches 1-3 that addressed platform-specific detection and MSVC support. While the performance angle turns out to be less compelling due to compiler optimizations, the code organization benefits remain valid. The exchange demonstrates careful consideration of both technical realities (compiler behavior) and codebase maintainability, with Junio indicating he'll likely accept the change for its structural improvements rather than performance gains. This positions patch 4 as a non-controversial step toward the series' goal of modernizing endian handling.
