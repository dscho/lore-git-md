This is patch 4/6 in Sebastian Andrzej Siewior's performance-oriented refactoring of Git's bswap.h, focusing on standardizing the ntohl/ntohll macros.

The patch originally proposed redefining these macros to use bswap operations for better performance on x86, but Brian m. carlson points out that modern compilers already optimize the shift-based implementations to use bswap instructions at -O2 level. Junio Hamano agrees with this analysis, noting that while the performance motivation may not hold, the reorganization still provides cleaner separation of concerns in the file - keeping byte-swapping optimizations separate from host-network byte order helpers.

The change is part of a larger effort to modernize Git's endianness handling while maintaining compatibility across platforms. While this particular optimization may not yield measurable benefits, the structural improvements are still worthwhile. The discussion shows the careful consideration being given to each change in this series, with Junio's involvement suggesting these changes are on track for inclusion.

This patch follows three already-accepted changes (fixing big-endian behavior, adding compiler-specific defines, and handling MSVC) and precedes two more that will remove redundant x86 optimizations and reintroduce built-in bswap support. The series demonstrates Git's ongoing effort to balance performance, readability and cross-platform compatibility in low-level code.
