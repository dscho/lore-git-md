Feature refinement for Git's fast-export/import signature handling. This email from Elijah Newren raises several substantive questions about the v4 patch's approach to representing and handling commit signatures during repository conversion.

Key technical questions center on:
1. The proposed "gpgsig <hash-algo> <format>" syntax's necessity versus simply preserving ASCII armor lines
2. Whether the hash algorithm field serves as a correctness constraint (potentially problematic) or just a performance hint
3. The rationale behind limiting to one signature per hash algorithm rather than handling multiple signatures generically
4. Performance implications of requiring dual hash algorithm traversal during export
5. Missing test strategy for commits with both SHA-1 and SHA-256 signatures

Newren suggests the format additions may be over-engineering if the goal is simply signature preservation across conversions, proposing instead to directly output signature headers with their original armor lines. He questions whether the current implementation's restrictions reflect Git limitations or just temporary implementation gaps that shouldn't be encoded in the interchange format.

The email also highlights documentation concerns, particularly around clearly communicating current limitations versus intended future behavior. Newren requests clarification on what actually happens to signatures during import (are they preserved verbatim despite not being checked?) and suggests softening language that presents implementation limits as protocol constraints.

This represents a significant design critique from an experienced contributor, suggesting the current approach may be more complex than necessary while missing some key use cases. The questions imply the patch may need reworking to either:
- Simplify to a direct signature passthrough model, or
- Better justify the additional metadata fields and their planned uses

The lack of dual-signature test cases remains a notable gap, with Newren questioning whether the feature should ship without this verification. The discussion appears headed toward either finding a way to generate test commits with dual signatures or reconsidering the feature's scope.
