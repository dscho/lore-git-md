This is a follow-up discussion in the performance optimization thread for `git fetch --prune`. 

Junio C Hamano chimes in to agree with Jeff King's earlier observation about the trade-off in the new implementation. The optimized version changes the output ordering of dangling ref warnings, making it harder to see exactly which ref deletion caused a symref to dangle. While the old output showed the relationship clearly (with the warning appearing immediately after the relevant deletion), the new approach batches all warnings at the end for performance reasons.

Both maintainers acknowledge this is a minor regression in diagnostic clarity, but neither considers it particularly important - Junio notes he wasn't even aware this warning existed before, and Jeff had previously pointed out it's been around for 15 years without being a user pain point. They agree that users who really need to debug dangling symrefs can always use `git symbolic-ref` to investigate manually.

This exchange effectively closes the one remaining question about the patch series' trade-offs, confirming the maintainers are comfortable accepting the output ordering change in exchange for the dramatic performance improvement (from 410 seconds to 4 seconds in the test case). The discussion reinforces that the patches are likely to be accepted as-is.
