Junio Hamano responds to Aditya Garg's proposal for interactive confirmation on `git push -f` with skepticism. He argues that adding confirmations to individual commands like `push -f` would be too narrow and annoy users who deliberately use force flags. Instead, he suggests a more general solution at the shell level that would apply to all potentially destructive commands (like `rm` or file redirections) by introducing a delay before execution. 

The response indicates this proposal may face resistance in the Git community, as it touches on the longstanding design philosophy that force flags are already explicit enough indicators of potentially dangerous operations. Hamano's position suggests he would prefer solutions that don't special-case Git commands, but rather address the workflow issue more holistically. This may steer the discussion toward either abandoning the idea or exploring shell-level solutions rather than Git-specific ones.
