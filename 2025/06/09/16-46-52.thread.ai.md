Thread summary:

Aditya Garg proposes adding an interactive confirmation prompt for `git push -f` operations to prevent accidental destructive pushes. The initial suggestion left open questions about configurability (`push.confirmForce`), scope (coverage of `--force-with-lease`), and scripting compatibility. Junio Hamano responds with skepticism about command-specific safety prompts, arguing that selectively adding confirmations to individual commands is the wrong approach given Git's many destructive operations. He suggests a shell-level delay mechanism would be more appropriate but doesn't propose implementing it. 

Key technical points center on `--force` flags already marking explicit user intent, making additional confirmations potentially redundant and annoying for experienced users. Hamano implies this violates Git's principle of doing what the user explicitly asks. The response challenges the proposal's fundamental premise rather than engaging with its implementation questions, setting up a thread direction exploring where safety mechanisms should live (Git command level vs. environment) and what constitutes appropriate friction for destructive operations. The discussion now has two clear positions: Garg's targeted safety improvement versus Hamano's preference for either comprehensive solutions or none at all. Open questions include whether alternative approaches might satisfy both concerns and whether other contributors will weigh in on either side.
