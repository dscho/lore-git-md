The thread discusses establishing Git's policy on AI-generated contributions, with consensus forming around prohibiting AI-generated code and commit messages while permitting non-generative assistance tools. Christian Couder has submitted a second version of his proposal to document this in SubmittingPatches, adding a new "Use of Artificial Intelligence" section.

The latest proposal outlines two key concerns - uncertain copyright status of AI output and quality control - while attempting to draw clear lines between acceptable and prohibited uses. It would reject obviously AI-generated content ("AI slop", overly formal language) but allow tools that provide guidance or style checking without direct generation. The text references existing mentoring guidelines and gives examples like permitting step-by-step debugging help while banning copy-pasted AI output.

Junio Hamano and others have pointed to QEMU's similar policy as a reference, with broad agreement on the core principle against AI generation. The discussion has now shifted to refining the language to: (1) better distinguish between generation and assistance, particularly for commit message tools, (2) address concerns about non-native English speakers, and (3) ensure the policy doesn't inadvertently ban useful non-generative aids. The current draft moves toward these goals but may still need adjustment to avoid being overly broad in rejecting "anything that looks AI generated."

Key open questions include whether to adopt QEMU's wording more directly and how to handle edge cases like translation tools. The proposal appears headed toward acceptance once these finer points are resolved, with legal review likely before finalization.
