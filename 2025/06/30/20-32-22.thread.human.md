Junio Hamano has made the call to adopt an AI contribution policy for Git, choosing his SFC-vetted version that prohibits AI-generated content while acknowledging the practical difficulties in enforcement. The decision comes after extensive debate weighing legal risks against practical concerns, with Christian Couder having proposed an alternative approach more aligned with mentoring programs. 

Key aspects of the resolution include: AI-assisted contributions will be prohibited going forward, modeled after QEMU's policy but with Git-specific adjustments like non-retroactive application. Problematic contributions - whether AI-generated or not - will be handled case-by-case, addressing Elijah Newren's concern about his previously accepted AI-assisted documentation work. Junio emphasizes that having any policy in place is more important than perfect wording, with the understanding that issues can be addressed through reverts if needed.

The policy will treat "materials of unknown origin" as the broader concern beyond just AI, reflecting Junio's risk-management approach as maintainer. While enforcement challenges remain - particularly for simple fixes where contributors may not realize they're using AI tools - the decision moves the discussion from debate to implementation, with the policy heading to the 'next' branch while remaining open to future wording refinements. This maintains Git project's legal protections while pragmatically acknowledging the realities of contribution review.
