This thread continues to refine Git's policy on AI-generated contributions, now with a new proposal from Randall Becker that shifts the focus toward legal attestations. The discussion began with Christian Couder's v2 proposal establishing guidelines that balance DCO compliance with practical enforcement, incorporating both explanation requirements and stylistic pattern recognition. Junio Hamano has since pushed back against the pattern recognition approach, advocating instead for relying solely on contributors' ability to explain their code.

Becker's latest email introduces a third path: requiring explicit legal declarations in cover letters or commit messages to address copyright concerns. His proposal would have contributors attest to their legal authority over patches, originality, and AI generation status, potentially involving the Conservancy in maintaining standard agreements. This approach sidesteps the ongoing debate about detection methods by shifting responsibility to contributors through formal statements.

Key open questions now include how this legal documentation approach interacts with Junio's explanation standard and Couder's hybrid model, whether the project wants to involve the Conservancy in enforcement, and how to reconcile these proposals with the DCO's existing requirements. The thread remains focused on finding a legally sound yet practical policy, with Junio's maintainer perspective carrying particular weight in shaping the final outcome.
