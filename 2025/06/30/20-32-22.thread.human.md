Christian Couder's proposal to formally prohibit AI-generated contributions in Git has drawn thoughtful feedback from Chuck Wolber, who raises concerns about fairness and subjectivity in the current draft. While there's broad agreement on rejecting outright AI-generated code and commit messages, Wolber argues the v2 wording could create problematic double standards. His key objection centers on the "looks AI generated" criterion, which might unfairly flag non-native English speakers or those with formal writing styles, moving away from Git's traditional focus on technical merit.

Wolber identifies three specific issues: the legal section's vagueness about DCO compliance, the subjectivity of detection methods, and a perceived contradiction between strict prohibitions and encouragement of responsible AI use. He suggests alternative approaches, including requiring contributors to explicitly declare AI assistance (pointing to declare-ai.org as a model) and reframing the guidelines around principles of self-reliance rather than generation bans. Notably, he supports the positive examples of acceptable AI use - like step-by-step debugging assistance - which align with Git's existing mentoring philosophy.

This response adds nuance to the ongoing discussion, particularly around enforcement fairness and non-native speaker impacts. While the core consensus against AI generation remains, Wolber's intervention highlights the need for careful phrasing to avoid subjective judgments. The suggestion of mandatory declarations presents a new alternative to explore as the policy takes shape, alongside the existing QEMU-inspired approach. The thread now awaits either specific wording proposals from Wolber or a revised version from Christian Couder addressing these concerns.
