This thread has been discussing a proposed policy to prohibit AI-generated contributions in Git, following QEMU's precedent while addressing Git's specific needs. The discussion has evolved through several key phases:

Christian Couder's v2 proposal established the core framework, prohibiting AI-generated content while allowing assistive tools (like debugging aids or style checkers). Maintainer Junio Hamano guided the discussion toward replacing subjective "looks AI-generated" detection with an objective standard requiring contributors to explain their work when questioned. This addressed fairness concerns raised by Chuck Wolber regarding non-native English speakers.

Brian M. Carlson's latest contribution pushes for even clearer language in the policy text. He advocates for explicit prohibitions ("Contributors may not use AI to write contributions") rather than leaving interpretation to contributors, comparing vague guidance to unenforceable speed limits. Carlson suggests modeling the policy's clarity after Git's code of conduct, with sharp do/don't boundaries. He also raises the possibility of narrow exceptions where copyright provenance is fully documented, though this remains a secondary consideration.

The thread shows strong agreement on the core principle - rejecting content the sender cannot explain - but continues refining how to operationalize this. Key unresolved points include whether to adapt QEMU's wording verbatim or maintain Git-specific language, how to phrase the "explain upon request" standard, and whether to incorporate a declaration-based alternative (declare-ai.org). A minor formatting correction from v1 also remains pending.

With the prohibition-plus-exceptions framework now including fairness safeguards through objective standards, the policy appears to be approaching its final form. Legal review is expected before finalization but isn't blocking the next revision. The discussion has productively shifted from whether to prohibit AI generation to precisely how the prohibition should be worded and enforced.
