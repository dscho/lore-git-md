This thread continues to refine Git's policy on AI-generated contributions, now focusing on practical enforcement after Junio Hamano proposed adopting QEMU's prohibition model. Christian Couder's latest response argues that existing review mechanisms - where maintainers use judgment and the community reaches consensus, with Junio as final arbiter - can handle AI detection without special procedures. He compares this to Code of Conduct enforcement, noting that questioning submitters may not work since AI tools can generate plausible explanations that contributors might unknowingly provide.

The discussion has split between legal concerns (led by Junio and Brian M. Carlson, who emphasize copyright risks) and practical implementation (championed by Couder and Elijah Newren, who point to real-world cases like Newren's already-merged AI-assisted documentation). Newren's six edge cases - including IDE autocompletion and documentation tweaks - remain unresolved, as does the retroactivity question for his merged commit. While the policy is coalescing around a prohibition model, key questions linger about granular exceptions, enforcement thresholds, and how to handle past AI-assisted contributions that passed review. The thread shows the project carefully balancing legal prudence against maintaining its pragmatic review culture.
