This is a response in the credential helper behavior thread, providing Git maintainer perspective on the reported issue.

Brian M. Carlson confirms that Git's current behavior of clearing credentials after failed operations is intentional design, not a bug. They explain the security rationale - preserving bad credentials would prevent users from ever being re-prompted for correct ones, potentially locking them out of successful authentication. The response suggests this behavior affects all credential helpers uniformly, not just the OAuth case mentioned in the original report.

For users wanting different behavior, Carlson proposes a workaround: writing a custom credential helper that intercepts and ignores erase commands while passing other operations through to the real helper. This would preserve credentials across failed attempts while maintaining the rest of the credential helper functionality.

The response establishes this as a security-conscious design choice rather than an oversight, though it doesn't address potential usability improvements for OAuth flows specifically. The suggestion of a custom helper implies the core Git behavior is unlikely to change, pushing any modifications to individual helper implementations. This positions the thread at a potential impasse between security design and user experience concerns.
