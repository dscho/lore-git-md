This is a follow-up in the thread about credential helper behavior during failed operations. Michal Suchánek clarifies their position in response to Brian M. Carlson's explanation that Git's credential-clearing behavior is intentional for security reasons.

The key point is that the credentials themselves are valid - it's the operation that fails (in this case, an AGit push attempt). They argue that erasing working credentials creates unnecessary friction, especially compared to non-Git-managed auth methods like SSH. While Brian suggested a workaround involving a custom credential helper, Michal explicitly rejects this approach, wanting instead to preserve valid credentials natively in Git's behavior.

The exchange highlights a tension between Git's security model (which prioritizes preventing credential lockout) and real-world usability in OAuth workflows. No new technical solution is proposed here - just a restatement of the user experience pain point that motivated the original report. The thread remains at an impasse between security design and workflow convenience.
