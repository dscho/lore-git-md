This is a documentation patch proposing to forbid AI-generated contributions in Git, following QEMU's lead. The patch adds a new section to SubmittingPatches that explains the rationale and establishes the policy.

The change cites legal uncertainty around AI-generated content as incompatible with the Developer's Certificate of Origin (DCO) requirements. The key concern is that contributors cannot reliably certify the provenance and licensing of AI-generated code, making DCO compliance impossible to verify. The new section explicitly states that AI-assisted contributions may be rejected.

The patch follows QEMU's precedent nearly verbatim, adopting their well-articulated legal reasoning. It positions this as a temporary measure until the copyright status of AI output becomes clearer. The change is straightforward - adding a single new section to the contribution guidelines with clear prohibitive language.

This appears to be a proactive policy change rather than a reaction to any specific incident. The patch is cleanly implemented and well-motivated, though it may spark discussion about how to detect AI contributions and whether the prohibition should extend beyond code to documentation. The legal rationale is sound but leaves open questions about enforcement mechanisms.
