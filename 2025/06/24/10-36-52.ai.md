This is a performance discussion follow-up in the test infrastructure series adding a `-f` format option to `test_seq`. Jeff King responds to Eric Sunshine's concerns about shell vs awk performance for large sequences, particularly referencing Patrick Steinhardt's prior rejection of shell-based approaches in t0610 due to speed issues with 10,000+ items.

Jeff shares timing data showing the shell implementation actually outperforms awk at 10,000 items on his system, though acknowledges awk may win at 50,000 items or on loaded systems. He investigates potential causes, ruling out buffering issues and attributing it to shell command overhead. The discussion reveals implementation details: awk makes per-item write() calls while the shell version uses built-in echo commands.

The key tradeoff is between readability (favoring the shell implementation) and performance (where awk may win for very large sequences). Jeff advocates prioritizing code clarity, suggesting test_seq's current design is reasonable given the small absolute time differences (milliseconds) and that test suite optimization would be better focused elsewhere. This maintains the series' minimalist philosophy while acknowledging the performance considerations as a known limitation for extreme cases.
