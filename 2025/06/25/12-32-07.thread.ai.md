Bug report and proposed fix for `git pull --prune` failure on case-insensitive filesystems when pruning case-conflicting references. Originated by ryenus, the issue occurs in refs.c (lines 2863-2895) where batch reference deletions fail entirely if any case-conflicting refs (e.g. `origin/TOM/b2` vs `origin/tom/b2`) are present on case-insensitive filesystems (macOS/Windows default). 

The proposed solutions have evolved through technical review: the initial approach of individual transactions was rejected due to performance concerns from Karthik Nayak (particularly for reftable backend). Karthik's alternative solution - filtering adjacent case-conflicting refs - was invalidated by brian m. carlson who demonstrated that case conflicts can be non-contiguous in the ref list due to bytewise sorting. Brian suggests reftable backend as a natural solution for case-insensitive systems, given its design handles case preservation without filesystem limitations.

Technical context includes: filesystem behavior where differently-cased refs map to same underlying file, current transaction model limitations, and the performance tradeoffs between correctness and throughput. Testing methods for case-insensitive environments (via JFS/APFS/HFS) were previously established by brian m. carlson and acknowledged by Jayatheerth K.

Current status: the discussion on two fronts - short-term workarounds are narrowing (with adjacent-element filtering now invalid), while reftable emerges as a more robust solution. The conversation remains tied to broader ref transaction improvements (Karthik's `kn/fetch-push-bulk-ref-update` series). Key open questions are whether to pursue alternative filtering approaches, accelerate reftable adoption for affected systems, or wait for comprehensive transaction handling improvements. Performance impact on different backends remains the central design constraint, with reftable's advantages in this specific case becoming more apparent.
