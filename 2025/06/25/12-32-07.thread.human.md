This thread began as a bug report about `git pull --prune` failing on case-insensitive filesystems when encountering references with case conflicts (e.g., `origin/TOM/b2` vs `origin/tom/b2`). The discussion explored several solutions, with Jayatheerth K proposing a strmap-based approach to detect conflicts while preserving batch operations, and brian m. carlson advocating for reftable as the more robust long-term solution. Junio C Hamano has indicated support for reftable adoption, though without closing the door to interim fixes.

The latest contribution from Patrick Steinhardt shifts the focus to making reftable the default backend, highlighting its technical advantages including proper case-sensitivity handling, compression benefits, and performance improvements. He reports successful large-scale use at GitLab and suggests enabling it via `features.experimental` as a stepping stone toward Git 3.0. However, he acknowledges hurdles including incomplete support in libgit2 and Gitoxide, along with ecosystem scripts that assume direct filesystem access. While reftable appears to be the consensus solution, the discussion now centers on the practical challenges of transitioning the broader ecosystem rather than just solving the immediate pruning issue. The thread remains open with no clear resolution yet on timing or transition strategy.
