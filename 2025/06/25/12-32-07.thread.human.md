This thread addresses a bug in `git pull --prune` where case-conflicting references (like `origin/TOM/b2` and `origin/tom/b2`) cause failures on case-insensitive filesystems (common on macOS/Windows). The discussion has evolved through several technical approaches, with key contributions from ryenus (who reported the issue), Karthik Nayak, brian m. carlson, and Jayatheerth K.

Initially, Karthik proposed filtering adjacent case conflicts, but brian m. carlson demonstrated this wouldn't catch all cases. Jayatheerth K then suggested a more robust strmap-based solution that tracks lowercase ref names to detect conflicts while preserving the original casing, warning about skipped refs rather than failing the entire operation. Meanwhile, brian m. carlson advocates for the reftable backend as a more fundamental solution, with Junio C Hamano expressing support for reftable adoption in general.

In the latest email, Karthik acknowledges that his adjacent-case approach is insufficient and agrees a hashmap (like Jayatheerth's strmap) would be needed for proper conflict detection. The discussion now centers on balancing immediate fixes for the files backend (via strmap) against the longer-term reftable solution. While reftable would resolve the issue completely, there's recognition that the files backend needs maintenance in the interim. The thread remains focused on these two paths forward, with no new approaches being introduced at this stage.
