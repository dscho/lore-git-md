Performance investigation of `git log --follow` slowness. Kai Koponen reports that `--follow` appears to perform unnecessary rename/copy detection work even for simple cases where it shouldn't be needed. The test case shows a 30x slowdown (0.07s vs 2.3s) when using `--follow` to find the last commit touching a small file, despite there being no renames or complex history involved.

The email suggests several possible explanations: that `--follow` might not be leveraging the commit-graph, might be performing expensive checks unnecessarily, or might not properly respect output-limiting flags like `--max-count`. The example uses a small 12-line file with a single-line modification to demonstrate that the slowdown occurs even in trivial cases.

This appears to be a genuine performance issue worth investigating, as the current behavior seems to defeat optimizations that should theoretically be possible. The report is well-structured with clear reproduction steps and thoughtful analysis of where the overhead might be coming from.
