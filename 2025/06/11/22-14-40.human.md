This is part 4 of a performance-oriented refactoring series addressing byte-order handling in Git's bswap.h, specifically focusing on making ntohl/ntohll macro behavior consistent across platforms.

The patch standardizes how Git handles network-to-host byte order conversion macros (ntohl/htonl and ntohll/htonll) by always redefining them after determining the system's endianness. The key change is moving from a piecemeal approach (where macros were conditionally overwritten) to a systematic one where all four macros are consistently defined based on the platform's byte order and available optimization capabilities.

On big-endian systems, the macros now become no-ops (just returning the input), while on little-endian systems they use either optimized bswap operations (when available) or fall back to Git's default swapping implementations. The change particularly benefits x86 systems where the bswap instruction offers better performance than manual shifting.

The patch continues the series' theme of cleaning up byte-order handling while maintaining performance, though like the rest of the series, no specific benchmarks are provided. The changes are focused entirely in compat/bswap.h, with a net reduction of 2 lines (-28 +26). The approach appears well-considered, building logically on the previous patches' groundwork for endianness detection.
