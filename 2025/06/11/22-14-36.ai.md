Cover letter for a performance-oriented refactoring series targeting byte-order handling in Git's bswap.h.

This 6-patch series from Sebastian Andrzej Siewior revisits Git's byte-swapping infrastructure after a regression was introduced for big-endian systems. The core issue stems from how ntohl() macros interact with Git's built-in bswap functions, particularly on big-endian platforms where the macros incorrectly force byte swapping. The series first reverts the problematic change (patch 1), then systematically improves the situation with several follow-up changes.

Key technical points include: restoring proper endianness handling via __BYTE_ORDER__ (patch 2), fixing MSVC endian detection (patch 3), making ntohl/ntohll behavior consistent (patch 4), removing x86-specific optimizations that compilers now handle better (patch 5), and reintroducing built-in bswap support more carefully (patch 6). The author notes that modern compilers (gcc/clang) handle manual byte swapping efficiently, making some optimizations redundant, though musl libc's function-call approach to ntohl() may still warrant keeping Git's macros.

The changes focus on compat/bswap.h, touching 118 lines with a net reduction of 30 lines. The series appears technically sound with clear progression from problem identification to solution. The author demonstrates awareness of platform differences (glibc vs musl vs Windows) and compiler capabilities. While performance impact isn't quantified, the changes aim to maintain efficiency while fixing correctness issues, particularly for big-endian systems.
