This is a continuation of the bugfix and design discussion around git-credential-netrc's handling of symbolic port names. Brian Carlson responds to Maxim Cournoyer's earlier email about Emacs' auth-source behavior, advocating strongly for numeric port consistency throughout Git's credential system.

The key new technical point is Carlson's observation that the git-credential protocol documentation explicitly refers to "port number" (not symbolic port), suggesting the credential protocol should only deal with numeric ports. They note that libcurl doesn't support ports at all in netrc files, making the netrc credential helper somewhat unique in this regard.

Carlson proposes that any port resolution (symbolic to numeric) should happen before the credential protocol is involved - for example, `git send-email` should convert "submission" to "587" before making credential requests. While acknowledging Perl's `getservbyname` could implement this in the helper, they suggest the conversion belongs earlier in the call chain.

The email highlights an important documentation reference that wasn't previously discussed - the git-credential(1) man page's wording about port numbers. This strengthens the case for numeric-only in the protocol itself, though the question remains whether to resolve at helper level (as Cournoyer's Scheme example shows) or caller level (as Carlson prefers).

The discussion now has three concrete technical positions to consider:
1. Original regex validation (already approved)
2. Helper-side resolution (Cournoyer's Scheme example)
3. Caller-side resolution (Carlson's current proposal)

The thread remains focused on architectural question of where port resolution should occur, with Carlson now providing protocol documentation evidence supporting their position. The netrc format's underspecification and libcurl's behavior add weight to the argument that symbolic ports may be worth supporting at all.
