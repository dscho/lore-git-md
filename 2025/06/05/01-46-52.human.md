This is a discussion about the rejected `commit.signoff` configuration option, focusing on the philosophical objections to automated signoffs. Collin Funk weighs in to support Junio C Hamano's position against the feature.

Collin draws a parallel between the Git signoff process and the FSF's copyright assignment, arguing that both serve as important legal gatekeepers. He notes that while the signoff requirement might seem bureaucratic, it forces contributors to consciously consider whether they have the rights to contribute their work. His concern is that making signoffs automatic via configuration would remove this important moment of reflection, especially for exempt employees in the US who may not automatically own their work's copyright.

The email reinforces Junio's earlier veto of the feature, adding a new legal perspective to the maintainer's philosophical objection. While the technical implementation had been reviewed and approved by other contributors, this thread has now firmly settled on rejecting the feature based on principle rather than technical merit. The discussion appears to be concluding with consensus that Git should not lower the barrier to signing off on contributions.
