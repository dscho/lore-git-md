This is a discussion about Git's code style enforcement, specifically focusing on line length limits in the context of the ongoing clang-format integration work. Karthik Nayak responds to Junio Hamano's framing of the tension between documented 80-column guidelines and practical readability considerations.

Karthik provides statistical analysis of the current codebase showing that only 4.3% of lines exceed 80 characters, with the percentage dropping to 1.7% at 120 characters. He clarifies that the `.editorconfig` `max_line_length` setting forces hard wrapping only for lines exceed the specified limit, rather than packing shorter lines together. This supports Junio's suggestion that the limit should represent the point where a line becomes "unacceptably long" rather than a strict wrapping target.

The discussion remains focused on determining where to set this hard limit (80, 100, or 120 characters) while acknowledging there may be justified exceptions. Karthik notes that setting it at 80 would enforce wrapping at that point, which aligns with Git's guidelines but may be too restrictive for some logical units of code. The technical implementation details of the enforcement mechanism appear settled, with the remaining question being one of policy and philosophy around line length exceptions.
