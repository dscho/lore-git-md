This is an RFC patch series proposing an alternative approach to child process tracking in git-daemon, moving away from signal-based handling. The series represents a significant architectural shift from the ongoing discussion about SA_RESTART and EINTR handling in the daemon's signal processing.

The proposal introduces a self-pipe mechanism where child processes are tracked via pipe file descriptors rather than SIGCHLD signals. The first patch modifies `run-command.c` to add a pipe write end to child processes, while the second implements the tracking mechanism in `daemon.c` using these pipes in the poll() set. The author acknowledges this is a "poor man's pidfd" solution that avoids Linux-specific APIs but comes with some inefficiencies in fd array management.

Key technical aspects include:
- New pipe-based tracking infrastructure in run-command and daemon
- Potential portability benefits across *NIX systems
- Known limitations around accidental pipe closure by children
- Performance tradeoffs from fd array management overhead

The proposal emerges from the ongoing debate about signal handling robustness, offering a third path alongside the SA_RESTART toggling approach and Phillip Wood's documentation-only solution. The author positions this as a proof-of-concept rather than a final solution, noting it wouldn't work as a general-purpose mechanism but might suffice for git-daemon's controlled environment.

This RFC continues the thread's exploration of platform-specific signal handling challenges while introducing new considerations around file descriptor management and polling efficiency. The discussion now has three distinct architectural approaches to evaluate for the daemon's child process tracking.
