This is a performance analysis response in the ongoing thread about `git log --follow` slowness. Jeff King explains why the `--follow` option doesn't benefit from commit-graph optimizations, identifying two key performance bottlenecks: 1) the need to perform full tree diffs for each commit since path-specific pruning is disabled, and 2) inability to skip irrelevant side branches during traversal. He notes these limitations stem from how `--follow` was implemented as an afterthought to the traversal machinery.

The technical explanation reveals that proper optimization would require maintaining path-specific bloom filters throughout the history walk, adjusting them at rename points. While possible to hack bloom filters into the current implementation, Jeff suggests this would require significant special casing, implying the current architecture may not ideal for such optimizations. The response provides important context about fundamental limitations in Git's history traversal when following renames, setting up potential future discussion about architectural improvements versus incremental optimizations.
