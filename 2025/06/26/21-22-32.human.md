This is an RFC proposal for git-daemon that explores an alternative approach to child process management, moving away from signal-based handling entirely. Carlo Marcelo Arenas Belón presents a proof-of-concept that uses pipes instead of signals to track child processes, addressing the ongoing discussion about platform-specific signal handling issues in the daemon.

The proposal introduces two patches: one that modifies `run-command.c` to add pipe tracking for child processes, and another that implements the pipe-based approach in `daemon.c`. The author acknowledges this is a "poor man's pidfd" solution that trades some efficiency (due to FD array management overhead) for better portability across *NIX systems compared to Linux-specific pidfd APIs.

The key advantage is avoiding signal handling complexities entirely, though the solution has limitations: it's not general-purpose (relies on controlled pipe usage) and may double event handling in some cases. This represents a significant architectural shift from both the current SA_RESTART approach and Phillip Wood's proposed documentation-only solution discussed earlier in the thread.

The RFC comes as the third alternative in an ongoing debate about git-daemon's child process management, with the other approaches being signal-based (either with SA_RESTART toggling or documentation of platform behaviors). The patchset appears technically sound as a POC but would need performance evaluation and Windows compatibility assessment to determine if it's preferable to the signal-based approaches.
