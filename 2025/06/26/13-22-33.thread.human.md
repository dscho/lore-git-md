This thread addresses a bug in Git's handling of comment characters when `core.commentChar="auto"` is set, particularly during operations like rebase and cherry-pick that involve conflict markers. The issue could incorrectly treat conflict markers (like "<<<<<<<") as comment characters, potentially leaving them in final commit messages.

Ayush Chandekar, mentored by Ghanshyam Thakkar, has led the fix through five iterations. The final v5 series contains two patches: one that prevents scanning conflict markers when selecting comment characters, and another that standardizes the behavior by resetting to '#' when the auto-setting is used. Phillip Wood contributed the architectural approach for the second patch, which is now properly attributed with a "Based-on-a-patch-by" trailer as agreed with maintainer Junio Hamano.

The series has received reviews and approvals from multiple contributors including Wood, Hamano, Kristoffer Haugsbakk, and Christian Couder. With Wood's final confirmation that the technical work looks good, the only remaining question is whether Junio should merge this series directly or have Wood incorporate it into his parallel work deprecating `core.commentChar="auto"` entirely in Git 3.0. This fix serves as an interim solution until that larger deprecation is complete.
