Performance discussion on rerere GC refactoring. This is part of Patrick Steinhardt's series to split `git gc` functionality into standalone maintenance tasks, specifically implementing rerere garbage collection as a configurable maintenance operation.

Junio Hamano, the original author of the rerere code, raises a performance concern about the patch's approach of first collecting stale entries before pruning them. He questions whether the counting operation (which requires scanning all entries) might be as expensive as the actual pruning, making the new configurable threshold feature potentially not worth the overhead. He requests performance measurements to justify the design.

The patch itself refactors `rerere_gc()` into three phases (collect, prune, cleanup) and exposes the collection phase via a new `rerere_collect_stale_entries()` function. This enables the maintenance command to count stale entries before deciding whether to run the full GC. The implementation appears correct but may need performance validation for the new collection step.

This is a key discussion point in the final stages of a series that has otherwise received approval, touching on the practical tradeoffs of making maintenance operations more granular and configurable. The outcome could influence whether this approach is kept or modified before the series is merged.
