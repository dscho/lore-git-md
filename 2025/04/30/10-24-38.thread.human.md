This thread discusses the architectural placement of maintenance control in Scalar's repository setup commands (`scalar register` and `scalar clone`), specifically around the new `--no-maintenance` flag. The original implementation by Derrick Stolee modified `register_dir()` to handle maintenance preferences, but Junio Hamano has raised concerns about this approach, arguing that maintenance configuration should be handled at the command level (`cmd_register()`/`cmd_clone()`) for better separation of concerns.

Junio's latest email reinforces his position, emphasizing that `register_dir()` should focus solely on registration, while maintenance setup should be handled explicitly by the calling commands. He notes that since `toggle_maintenance()` must be called directly for the `--no-maintenance` case anyway, routing through `register_dir()` adds unnecessary indirection. While both sides agree on the flag's functionality and its proper handling of both `--maintenance` and `--no-maintenance` forms, the architectural disagreement remains unresolved.

The discussion highlights a tension between centralized configuration (Stolee's approach) and explicit command-level control (Junio's preference), with implications for how Scalar might handle future configuration options. The core functionality—preventing maintenance.lock conflicts via setup-time opt-out—is uncontested, but the implementation's structure awaits resolution in Stolee's anticipated v2 patch series.
