This is a discussion about AI-generated content in Git's development process, specifically regarding whether to expand anti-AI-spam guidelines beyond mentoring programs.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Christian Couder's earlier email about new AI guidelines for Git's GSoC/Outreachy programs, which were implemented due to an overwhelming flood of low-quality applications (71 out of 79 submissions being AI-generated spam). While acknowledging the problem in mentoring programs, Patrick suggests that project-wide AI guidelines aren't yet necessary for general Git development, as they haven't observed similar issues with AI-generated patches on the mailing list.

The email represents a cautious position - agreeing with the mentoring program measures but arguing against broader policy expansion until/unless AI spam becomes a demonstrable problem in mainline development. Patrick leaves the door open for reconsideration if the situation changes, inviting alternative viewpoints while maintaining that current evidence doesn't justify project-wide rules. The exchange highlights how the project is grappling with AI's impact at different levels of contribution (mentorship applications vs. technical patches).
