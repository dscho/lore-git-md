This is a technical discussion about Change-ID uniqueness properties in the ongoing standardization effort. Nico Williams responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about random Change-ID generation by arguing that perfect uniqueness may not be necessary for practical purposes.

The email makes several key points about collision handling: first noting that even cryptographic hashes (used in Git's content-addressed storage) aren't perfectly collision-proof, yet are still workable in practice. Williams suggests that for Change-IDs, occasional duplicates could be resolved contextually rather than requiring absolute uniqueness guarantees.

The response introduces two alternative approaches: using ticket/bug tracking system IDs (which have centralized uniqueness) as Change-IDs, and accepting that some duplication could be managed through social conventions (like rejecting obviously abusive cases where all Change-IDs are identical). This contrasts with both the random generation approach used by Jujutsu and the content-based method proposed by Hamano.

The discussion remains focused on implementation tradeoffs rather than challenging the fundamental need for Change-IDs. Williams' position appears pragmatic - accepting some theoretical weaknesses in exchange for simpler implementation and workflow integration. The email doesn't propose specific technical solutions but rather questions whether the uniqueness requirements are being discussed are truly necessary for the use case.
