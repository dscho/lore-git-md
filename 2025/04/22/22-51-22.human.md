This is a technical discussion about Change-ID generation methods in the ongoing standardization effort. Nico Williams responds to Junio C Hamano's concerns about collision risks in randomly-generated Change-IDs.

Nico argues that perfect uniqueness isn't strictly necessary for Change-IDs, noting that even content-based hashes (like Git's object names) aren't completely collision-proof. He suggests practical approaches could tolerate occasional duplicates, either by detecting them pre-push or resolving ambiguities contextually. The email also touches on using ticket/bug tracking system IDs as an alternative source of uniqueness.

The exchange highlights the philosophical divide in the thread: Junio emphasizes theoretical guarantees while Nico and others) advocate for practical solutions that work "well enough" in real-world use. Nico's perspective leans toward accepting some collision potential as an acceptable tradeoff for simplicity, though he doesn't directly address Junio's scaling concerns.
