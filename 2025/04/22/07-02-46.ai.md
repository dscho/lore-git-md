This is a discussion about expanding AI-spam guidelines from mentoring programs to the broader Git project. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Christian Couder's earlier email about implementing anti-spam measures for GSoC/Outreachy applications, where 90% of proposals were AI-generated spam this year.

The email notes that while the mentoring program guidelines (documented in GitHub PR #771) have been effective at reducing spam volume for those specific applications, Patrick argues against expanding them project-wide at this time. The key rationale is that the Git mailing list hasn't yet seen problematic levels of AI-generated patches, making broader policies premature. However, Patrick leaves the door open to reevaluation if the situation changes, inviting alternative perspectives from others on the list.

The exchange highlights a distinction between two spam vectors: the high-volume, low-quality mentoring program applications (where guidelines were implemented) versus the currently manageable flow of patches to the development list. Patrick's position suggests a preference for concrete evidence of problems before implementing project-wide restrictions, while acknowledging the mentoring program context required more immediate action. The thread remains open for others to weigh in on whether Git should proactively establish AI content policies or maintain its current reactive stance.
