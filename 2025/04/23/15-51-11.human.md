This is a continuation of the Change-ID standardization discussion, focusing on the uniqueness requirements for Change-IDs in commit messages. Junio C Hamano raises an interesting counterpoint to Martin von Zweigbergk's suggestion that forges could enforce unique Change-IDs on the main branch.

Hamano presents a scenario where duplicate code (from poor refactoring) leads to duplicate bugs that need fixing. In such cases, he argues the fixes should share the same Change-ID to show their relationship, even though they modify different parts of the codebase. This suggests Change-IDs might need to track logical relationships between changes rather than just serve as unique identifiers.

The exchange highlights an important tension in the Change-ID design: between enforcing uniqueness (to prevent confusion) versus allowing reuse (to show derivation). This comes as the thread has already expanded to consider GitLab's branch-based tracking as an alternative to Change-IDs, making the standardization question even more nuanced.

Hamano's example shows how real-world code maintenance scenarios might complicate what initially seemed like straightforward technical requirements. The discussion continues to explore how Change-IDs should behave across different workflow models and use cases.
