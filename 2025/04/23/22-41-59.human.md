This is a set of responses to Git's 20th anniversary community interview questions from Elijah Newren, focusing on technical insights and personal experiences with Git.

Newren highlights `git range-diff` as his favorite underutilized tool, advocating for its role in commit-based code review workflows. He shares a memorable disaster story involving accidentally dropping a production Gerrit database, emphasizing the value of blameless post-mortems and process improvements. 

The most significant design change he'd make is replacing Git's index data structure, citing both performance limitations (O(N) scaling) and cognitive complexity, though he acknowledges the index's utility in his own workflow. He credits Git's speed and improved merge/rebase capabilities (especially for renamed files) as transformative improvements over the years.

Looking ahead, Newren raises thoughtful concerns about Git's future scalability (particularly for monorepos), maintenance challenges (C codebase longevity), and competition from next-gen VCS tools like jj and Sapling. His responses blend technical depth with reflection on Git's ecosystem health, offering both praise for current capabilities and caution about potential future challenges. The answers demonstrate a maintainer's perspective, balancing appreciation for Git's strengths with clear-eyed assessment of its limitations.
