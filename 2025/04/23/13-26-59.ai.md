This is a naming discussion in the ongoing `git-blame-tree` feature thread. Marc Branchaud argues against the "blame-tree" name, suggesting it either be folded into `git blame` as a new mode (e.g. `git blame --latest`) or given a completely different name that avoids the "blame" terminology. The email cites Jeff King's original rationale for the name (combining traversal and diff concepts similar to blame) but contends this will increase Git's perceived obscurity by having multiple blame-related commands.

The thread context shows this is part of a mature RFC series (now 7 patches) that has implemented core functionality with multiple performance optimizations, making the naming discussion particularly timely as the feature nears finalization. The series has already incorporated feedback from multiple contributors including Jeff King, Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason, and Derrick Stolee, with GitHub's version also integrated.

Key technical details that may inform naming decisions include the command's ability to track modifications across directory trees (not just files), its three-layer performance optimization approach (Bloom filters, priority queues, revision walk bypass), and its dual recursive/non-recursive modes. The output format (commit-hash and path) differs from traditional blame's line-oriented output, which may factor into whether it logically belongs under the `blame` command.
