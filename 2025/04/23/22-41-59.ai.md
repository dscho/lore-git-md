Git 20th anniversary community interview response from Elijah Newren, providing detailed answers to several of the interview questions. The responses combine personal experience with technical analysis, particularly around Git's design and performance characteristics.

Newren highlights `git range-diff` as an underutilized but critical tool for code review workflows, advocating for commit-based review processes. He shares a Gerrit-related disaster story involving accidental database deletion on a production system, emphasizing the value of blameless post-mortems and process improvements.

The most substantial technical critique focuses on Git's index design, which Newren identifies as problematic for both large repositories (performance/scaling) and user experience (cognitive complexity). He notes how merge-ort's performance improvements came partly from avoiding index limitations, and suggests alternative approaches like tree-structured indexes or post-commit editing workflows (citing JJ as inspiration).

On Git's evolution, Newren credits speed (particularly instant branch switching) as the most impactful improvement and cites merge/rebasing performance with renames as a successfully solved historical pain point. Looking forward, he raises concerns about Git's long-term viability - questioning whether scaling challenges (monorepos, index design), development language (C), and competing tools (jj, sapling) might threaten Git's dominance despite its strengths. The response provides both technical depth and thoughtful reflection on Git's future trajectory.
