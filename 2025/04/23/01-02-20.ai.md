This email from Nico Williams continues the discussion about Change-ID semantics in the context of Git patch tracking. The thread has evolved to compare three approaches: GitLab's existing partial tracking, traditional ticket IDs, and the proposed Change-IDs.

Williams makes several technical points about uniqueness requirements:
1. Notes Git's existing object/ref uniqueness guarantees and how they differ from proposed Change-ID behavior
2. Explains why Change-IDs should *not* be required to be unique, since their purpose is to group related patch versions
3. Describes how cherry-picking between branches could reintroduce non-uniqueness even after review completion
4. Suggests servers could detect hash collisions at push time as a safety measure

The email reinforces the thread's emerging theme that Change-IDs serve a different purpose than Git's object model - they're meant to track evolution rather than guarantee uniqueness. This contributes to the ongoing evaluation of whether Change-IDs provide enough benefit over GitLab's existing tracking to justify their adoption, particularly given their semantic differences from Git's core concepts. The discussion remains focused on technical tradeoffs rather than workflow preferences at this point.
