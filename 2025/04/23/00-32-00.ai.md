This is a discussion about Change-ID semantics in the use of ticket IDs as change identifiers. Nico Williams responds to Remo Senekowitsch's concerns about mixing metadata in change IDs by pointing to long-standing industry practices.

Williams argues that using ticket IDs (like Bugzilla IDs) as change IDs has been a successful convention for decades, citing Sun Microsystems' practices since the early 1990s and continued use in Illumos and Oracle Solaris. He questions why this established practice would suddenly be considered problematic now, when it has effectively tracked change evolution for over 30 years in large-scale development environments.

The email reinforces the practical engineering perspective in the thread, countering concerns about semantic purity with real-world evidence of working systems. Williams positions ticket IDs as naturally fulfilling the change evolution tracking purpose that Change-IDs aim to address, suggesting the discussion may be overcomplicating a solved problem.

Key technical points include:
- Historical precedent of ticket IDs serving as effective change trackers
- Specific examples from Sun/Illumos/Oracle workflows
- The rebase workflow context where this has proven successful
- Implicit challenge to prove why existing practices are insufficient
