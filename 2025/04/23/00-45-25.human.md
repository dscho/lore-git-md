This is a discussion about change tracking in Git workflows, specifically examining GitLab's capabilities versus proposed Change-ID standards. Nico Williams responds to Remo Senekowitsch's analysis of GitLab's patch evolution tracking, acknowledging its limitations while maintaining that GitLab demonstrates such tracking is possible without Change-IDs.

The exchange highlights key pain points in current GitLab/GitHub review interfaces - particularly around navigating between versions and leaving contextual comments. Williams notes the tension between browser-based tools (cluttered but feature-rich) and terminal workflows (preferred by some developers but lacking integration). 

The discussion validates that GitLab can track patch evolution to some degree without Change-IDs, though imperfectly. Williams suggests Change-IDs may only be truly necessary for forward/back-porting scenarios, and that existing ticket-ID conventions might suffice. The thread continues to weigh semantic purity against practical, proven solutions while acknowledging room for improvement in all approaches.
