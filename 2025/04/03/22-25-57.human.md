This is a follow-up discussion about symlink handling in Git, specifically regarding the `core.symlinks` configuration. The thread was initiated by Andrej Zhilenkov asking about updating symlinks when toggling this setting, with brian m. carlson (the maintainer) clarifying this isn't a supported workflow.

In this response, brian expands on the discussion by drawing a comparison to a similar challenge in Git LFS, where there's no clean way to switch between pointer files and actual large objects. They suggest that if there's a compelling use case for symlink conversion, a more general solution for re-evaluating checked-out objects might be worth implementing - though they personally don't plan to work on this.

The maintainer remains curious about the original motivation for changing `core.symlinks`, indicating this could help determine whether such functionality would be valuable. The email keeps the conversation open while maintaining the position that runtime modification of `core.symlinks` isn't part of Git's design.

This appears to be a thoughtful response that both explains the current limitations and considers potential broader applications of the requested functionality, while still seeking to understand the specific need behind the original question.
