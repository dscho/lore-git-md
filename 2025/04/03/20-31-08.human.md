This is a discussion about the practical realities of change ID uniqueness in version control systems. 

Remo Senekowitsch responds to Theodore Ts'o and Elijah Newren's concerns about change IDs not always being unique in practice. While acknowledging that change IDs can sometimes be duplicated (either accidentally through copy-paste or intentionally during backporting), Remo argues that non-unique cases don't invalidate the usefulness of the system as a whole. 

The key point is that when change IDs do remain unique, they provide valuable tracking benefits for those specific patches, and problems with one change ID don't necessarily impact others. This maintains the thread's focus on balancing ideal design with real-world usage patterns, where some semantic drift in change IDs is inevitable but doesn't completely undermine the system's value.

The discussion continues to explore how change IDs function in practice across different tools and workflows, with participants working toward standards that can accommodate both clean theoretical models and the messy realities of distributed development.
