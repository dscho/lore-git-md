This is a technical discussion about the reliability and semantics of change IDs in Git workflows, particularly in the context of Gerrit usage.

Theodore Ts'o responds to Elijah Newren's concerns about change ID uniqueness by acknowledging that while change IDs are *intended* to be unique identifiers preserved across operations like rebase and cherry-pick, in practice they sometimes aren't. He shares real-world observations from Gerrit usage where change IDs can become non-unique or misleading due to user actions like copy-pasting commit descriptions or when backporting patches to LTS releases.

Ts'o notes that some Gerrit frontends deliberately regenerate change IDs during cherry-picks between branches, and warns that change IDs can't always be trusted as reliable indicators of commit semantics. This provides important practical context to the ongoing standardization effort, suggesting that any cross-tool change ID specification will need to account for these real-world inconsistencies.

The email serves as a cautionary note in the broader discussion about change ID standardization, highlighting gaps between the theoretical model (where change IDs uniquely identify logical changes) and actual usage patterns where IDs may not reliably track semantic meaning. This practical perspective may influence how the various proposed solutions for cherry-pick behavior are evaluated.
