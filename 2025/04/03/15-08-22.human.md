This is a technical discussion about authorship preservation during rebase operations, part of the ongoing rebase status reporting improvements series.

Phillip Wood responds to Johannes Schindelin's question about why `git commit` during a rebase loses authorship information, unlike during a cherry-pick. He explains that while `git cherry-pick` preserves authorship via CHERRY_PICK_HEAD, rebase intentionally removes this and instead stores author information in `.git/rebase-merge/author-script`. This difference dates back to 2011 and was designed to avoid confusing `git status` output. 

Phillip notes that with rebase's `--reset-author-date` option, using the cherry-pick mechanism wouldn't work. He argues that rather than trying to make `git commit` work during rebase (which has never properly preserved authorship), it's better to consistently direct users to use `git rebase --continue` for conflict resolution commits. He expresses concerns that making `git commit` work would be fragile and difficult to implement robustly.

The discussion reveals an architectural inconsistency between rebase and cherry-pick operations, but Phillip suggests this is a deliberate design choice rather than a bug. The exchange provides important context about the limitations of authorship preservation during rebase, though it doesn't directly impact the current patch series about status reporting improvements.
