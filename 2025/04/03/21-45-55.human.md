This is a discussion about change ID behavior during cherry-pick and rebase operations, part of the ongoing cross-tool standardization effort for change IDs.

Remo Senekowitsch responds to Nico Williams and Elijah Newren's earlier discussion about whether cherry-pick should preserve change IDs by default. The key disagreement centers on whether cherry-pick and rebase should be treated similarly (as both fundamentally being cherry-picks) or differently based on their typical use cases.

Remo argues that while rebase is technically implemented as a series of cherry-picks, their semantic purposes differ: rebase is often used to amend existing commits (where preserving change IDs makes sense), while cherry-pick is frequently used for backporting (where duplicate change IDs would degrade their tracking value). He notes Jujutsu's equivalent "duplicate" operation intentionally doesn't preserve change IDs for this reason.

The email highlights an important design tension between implementation consistency (treating rebase and cherry-pick the same way) and semantic appropriateness (different behaviors for different workflows). Remo pragmatically notes that even if Git chooses differently than Jujutsu, some standardization is better than none. This continues the thread's theme of balancing theoretical purity with practical tooling needs.
