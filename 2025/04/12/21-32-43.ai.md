Maintainer perspective on change tracking and commit reachability.

Junio Hamano responds to Phillip Wood's comparison of the Change-ID proposal to the earlier "git evolve" concept, specifically addressing concerns about commit rewriting. He questions whether the stated objection about requiring rewritten commits to be pushed is actually problematic, suggesting two technical approaches that could mitigate the issue: making predecessor links non-participatory in the reachability DAG, or using Git's existing `--filter` capability to exclude them.

This brief but pointed response from the Git maintainer brings architectural considerations to the discussion, highlighting that the technical constraints around commit rewriting may be more flexible than initially assumed. The email implies that the Change-ID proposal shouldn't be dismissed based on perceived limitations that could potentially be worked around through careful design.

Key technical points for future reference:
- Predecessor links could be made optional in reachability calculations
- `--filter` mechanism exists as a potential solution for hiding historical versions
- Maintainer appears open to solutions that don't strictly require pushing all rewritten commits
