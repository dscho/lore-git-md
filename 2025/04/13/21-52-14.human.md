Ben Knoble responds to Klaus Frank's clarification about the stateless CI environment by proposing an alternative approach where a local repository serves as the single source of truth, with manual or cron-controlled pushes to multiple remotes.

The key point here is Knoble's suggestion to bypass the automated sync complexity by maintaining control through a local authoritative copy, even in CI contexts. He acknowledges this trades off automation for control, but argues it may be simpler than building complex sync mechanisms. The proposal still doesn't fully address Frank's core constraint of truly stateless CI execution where no persistent local repo exists, though Knoble floats the idea of using local cron jobs as a potential middle ground.

This continues the thread's central tension between automated multi-remote synchronization versus controlled single-source publishing, now with clearer focus on the tradeoffs for CI workflows. The discussion remains unresolved on whether Git's existing push mechanisms can be adapted or if new features are needed to handle these stateless multi-remote sync scenarios.
