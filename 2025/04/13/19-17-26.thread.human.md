This thread has clarified the fundamental limitations and practical considerations around GPG signing in Git's email workflow. The discussion, initiated by Klaus Frank, has established a clear separation between two distinct needs: preserving commit signatures (which is impossible in patch workflows due to committer identity changes) and authenticating patch transport (which must happen at the email level).

Key participants including Matt Hunter, brian m. carlson, and Konstantin Ryabitsev contributed technical insights, with maintainer Junio Hamano providing multiple confirmations of the technical boundaries. The Linux kernel's patatt implementation emerged as a production-proven solution for transport authentication, while PGP signing was recognized as having practical key management challenges despite working in some trusted scenarios.

The current consensus is that transport authentication belongs at the email layer (MUA/MSA) rather than being Git's responsibility, though `git send-email` currently lacks this capability. Junio's latest response reinforces this by describing his personal workflow where verification happens at the mail client before patch application, noting the practical difficulties of maintaining contributor keys in the Web of Trust model.

With the technical constraints now well understood and validated by the maintainer, the discussion has effectively resolved the core questions. Any further work would focus on implementation possibilities for transport authentication in Git's email tooling, building on the established understanding that this is separate from Git's object signing capabilities.
