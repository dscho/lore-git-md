This is a technical discussion about GPG signing in Git's email workflow, specifically addressing the limitations of commit signatures for patch authentication during transfer.

Klaus Frank clarifies their original motivation in response to Matt Hunter's explanation of why commit signatures don't work with `git am`. While acknowledging the technical constraints around committer identity changes, Frank emphasizes their primary concern is ensuring patch integrity and authenticity during email transmission - verifying the patch hasn't been tampered with in transit and comes from the claimed author.

The email makes a key distinction between two scenarios: (1) preserving signatures through the entire workflow (which is fundamentally broken due to committer changes) versus (2) authenticating just the initial patch transfer (which remains technically possible but unimplemented). Frank notes that centralized forges like GitHub solve this through different mechanisms (TLS and account authentication), but the mailing list workflow lacks equivalent protections.

This response helps refine the problem space by separating the concerns of transport authentication from commit object preservation. It suggests the discussion might productively focus on transport-layer solutions rather than trying to make commit signatures work in a context they fundamentally can't. The email also implicitly raises the question of whether the Git community considers transport authentication important enough to warrant new features, given that current practice seems to accept the risks Frank identifies.
