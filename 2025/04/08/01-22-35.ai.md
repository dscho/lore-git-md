This is a response to Phillip Wood's RFC patch for preserving extra commit headers during rebase operations. Brian m. carlson raises significant concerns about propagating unvalidated headers, drawing from extensive experience with malformed commit metadata encountered in production environments.

The response outlines numerous real-world examples of corrupted commit headers including invalid timezones, malformed dates, non-compliant email addresses, and problematic encoding issues. These examples demonstrate how easily invalid data could be propagated through rebase operations without proper validation. The author expresses skepticism about the current approach, arguing that Git should not preserve headers it cannot validate, as this risks spreading corruption that may only be caught by future Git versions.

Key technical concerns center around the lack of validation mechanisms for these extra headers and the potential for propagating malformed data. The author suggests adding command-line and configuration options to control header preservation, giving users and forges the ability to opt out of this behavior. While not fundamentally opposed to the feature, they emphasize the need for safeguards given the prevalence of malformed commit metadata in real-world usage.

The response invites further discussion from Patrick Steinhardt (CC'd) and others, positioning this as a discussion-worthy tradeoff between functionality and data integrity. The detailed examples of real-world corruption patterns provide concrete grounding for the concerns raised, suggesting this may become a significant point of contention in the thread's development.
