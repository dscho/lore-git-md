This is a discussion about Change-ID standardization, focusing on semantic definitions and distributed workflow implications.

Theodore Ts'o responds to Nico Williams' proposal for multiple Change-IDs (code review ID and commit change ID) by suggesting a "patch series ID" concept. He argues this would be more broadly useful beyond Gerrit workflows, helping tools like `git format-patch` and Patchwork manage patch series relationships. Ts'o believes heuristic matching (commit ordering, content similarity) might suffice for many cases without needing explicit commit change IDs, based on observed differences between email and web-based review workflows.

The email highlights a key philosophical divide: Ts'o strongly advocates for well-defined semantics before standardizing any Change-ID header, drawing parallels to kernel-user interface problems. He warns against premature standardization with vague semantics, predicting tool fragmentation and maintenance headaches. This contrasts with Williams' more flexible view that project-specific interpretations could coexist.

The discussion now has two concrete technical directions in play: Nico's dual-ID system and Ts'o's patch-series-ID alternative, both attempting to address the distributed evolution challenges Martin von Zweigbergk highlighted earlier. The thread remains at an impasse between those wanting rigorous definitions and those prioritizing immediate workflow needs.
