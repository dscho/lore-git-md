This is a technical response in the ongoing Change-ID standardization discussion, specifically addressing commit evolution tracking in distributed workflows.

Martin von Zweigbergk responds to Junio Hamano's concerns about tracking commit evolution across distributed repositories using Change-IDs. He acknowledges that while Change-IDs can handle simple cases with a single remote (where the server can indicate successor relationships), they don't fully replace systems like Mercurial's Changeset Evolution for more complex distributed scenarios.

The key limitation demonstrated is when commits with the same Change-ID appear from different remotes - the client cannot automatically determine predecessor/successor relationships without additional metadata. This reinforces Junio's earlier concerns about the system's inability to track non-linear evolution paths. The example shows concrete cases where the Change-ID mechanism breaks down compared to Git's native reflog-based tracking.

The response introduces the concept of an "obsolescence graph" (borrowing Mercurial's terminology) but doesn't propose specific solutions for the distributed case. This leaves open the core challenges of evolution metadata storage, synchronization, and conflict resolution that were highlighted in the thread summary. The email effectively demonstrates that Change-IDs alone cannot solve the evolution tracking problem, supporting Junio's position that significant additional machinery would be needed.
