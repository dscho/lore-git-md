This is a discussion about Change-ID standardization and commit evolution tracking, specifically addressing the proposal for multiple change identifiers in distributed workflows.

Theodore Ts'o responds to Nico Williams' suggestion of having both a "code review ID" and "commit change ID" by proposing a "patch series ID" concept instead. He argues this would be more broadly useful beyond Gerrit workflows, enabling tools like `git format-patch` and Patchwork to group related commits and track series versions. Ts'o suggests heuristic matching could suffice for commit evolution tracking in many cases, reducing the need for a separate "commit change ID".

The email highlights a key philosophical divide in the thread: Ts'o strongly advocates for well-defined semantics before standardizing any Change-ID header, drawing parallels to problematic kernel-user interfaces. He warns against premature standardization of ill-defined fields that could lead to incompatible tooling interpretations. This directly challenges Nico's position that looser, project-defined semantics would be acceptable.

Technical points raised include:
- The proposed "patch series ID" would appear in email headers and be preserved by `git apply-message`
- Heuristic matching could use commit ordering, descriptions, and content similarity
- Different workflows (web-based vs email) tend to produce different commit granularity
- Gerrit could fall back to heuristics when Change-IDs are missing

The discussion continues the thread's tension between immutable tracking needs and Git's distributed nature, with Ts'o taking a conservative stance against standardizing weakly-defined metadata. His response adds weight to Junio's earlier concerns about distributed evolution tracking while offering a potentially simpler alternative to Nico's dual-ID proposal.
