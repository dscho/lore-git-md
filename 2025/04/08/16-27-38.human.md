This is a technical discussion about commit metadata approaches in the ongoing Change-ID standardization debate. Nico Williams responds to Phillip Wood's analysis of Junio's DAG-based approach, pushing back against the complexity of tracking full commit evolution history.

Nico argues that storing predecessor information (whether in the commit DAG or notes refs) creates too much internal history that would be distracting during code review, especially for frequent committers. He maintains that simpler Change-ID or Series-ID solutions would be sufficient, calling the DAG approach "ETOOMUCH" (too much work/overhead).

The exchange highlights a key tension in the discussion: between comprehensive commit evolution tracking (DAG/notes approach) versus lightweight identifiers (Change/Series IDs). Nico's position favors simplicity and reviewability over complete history preservation, suggesting the more complex solutions may not justify their implementation cost for most workflows.
