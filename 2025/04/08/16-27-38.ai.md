This is a technical discussion about commit metadata approaches in the ongoing Change-ID standardization thread. Nico Williams responds to Phillip Wood's analysis of Junio's DAG-based approach by arguing for simpler solutions.

Nico pushes back against the complexity of Phillip's notes-based predecessor tracking proposal (which itself was responding to Junio's DAG model). He makes two key points: First, that preserving extensive internal commit history would be distracting for reviewers and counter to Git's linear history philosophy. Second, that the implementation complexity of full predecessor tracking outweighs its benefits compared to simpler Change-ID or Series-ID approaches.

The response highlights a growing divide in the thread between comprehensive solutions (DAG/predecessor tracking) and lightweight approaches (Change-IDs). Nico positions himself firmly in the lightweight camp, arguing that Change-IDs provide sufficient tracking without the overhead of maintaining complete evolution histories. His "ETOOMUCH" comment underscores this preference for simplicity over completeness.

This email introduces new considerations about practical workflow impacts - particularly how different approaches would affect code review processes and the visibility of intermediate commit states. It also reinforces the implementation complexity spectrum that has emerged in the thread, with Nico advocating for the simpler end of that spectrum.

Key technical references:
- Rejects predecessor tracking via notes refs
- Argues against propagating intermediate rewritten commits
- Maintains support for Change-ID/Series-ID simplicity
- References Git's linear history philosophy as a design constraint

The discussion continues to balance comprehensive commit evolution tracking against implementation complexity and workflow impacts, with Nico's position favoring simpler solutions that don't preserve full internal history.
