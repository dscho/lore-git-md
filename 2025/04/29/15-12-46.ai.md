This is a follow-up discussion in the promisor-remote protocol enhancement series, focusing on backward compatibility and field handling strategies. The conversation between Christian Couder and Patrick Steinhardt explores edge cases around unknown field handling in the client-server interaction.

Key points raised:
- The series now strictly limits accepted fields to "partialCloneFilter" and "token" with documented semantics
- Backward compatibility requires clients to silently ignore unknown fields rather than failing
- Patrick raises concerns about future protocol evolution if unknown fields are ignored
- Christian argues this approach allows servers to serve both old and new clients simultaneously
- Both agree this avoids the worse alternative of clients failing on unknown fields
- The discussion touches on but dismisses full capability negotiation as overkill for current needs

The exchange confirms the v2 series' design choice of silent field filtering while acknowledging it may limit future non-backward-compatible changes. This maintains the thread's focus on constrained, security-conscious protocol evolution while leaving open questions about long-term extensibility strategies.
