This is a technical discussion email in the ongoing thread about patch series tracking in Git, specifically responding to Junio Hamano's proposal for predecessor-successor relationships as an alternative to Change-IDs.

Jacob Keller analyzes Hamano's proposal for tracking patch evolution through predecessor commit relationships rather than Change-IDs. He acknowledges this approach would be more powerful for representing complex evolution histories (splits, merges, parallel development) but notes it comes at the cost of implementation complexity compared to simpler Change-ID tracking. Keller suggests a potential middle ground where tooling could use root commit IDs as a simpler identifier in most cases, falling back to full predecessor tracking when needed for disambiguation.

Key technical points raised:
- Tradeoff between Hamano's powerful DAG-based evolution tracking and the simplicity of Change-IDs
- Suggestion to use root commit IDs as a lightweight identifier where possible
- Implementation complexity concerns for the predecessor-successor model
- Potential need for disambiguation tooling in complex cases

The email advances the discussion by providing a practical assessment of Hamano's proposal while maintaining the thread's focus on finding the right balance between tracking capability and implementation complexity. It doesn't resolve the fundamental debate but adds important considerations about real-world usability and tooling requirements.
