This is a continuation of the debate about adding a `--no-hooks` global option to disable Git hooks. Phillip Wood responds to Derrick Stolee's defense of the feature, maintaining his skepticism about its necessity.

Wood acknowledges the implementation is technically viable but questions the motivation, particularly for pre-commit hooks where `git commit --no-verify` already exists. He pushes back on the idea of accommodating third-party hook systems (like hypothetical "pre-command" hooks) that aren't part of core Git. The core of his argument is that without clearer demonstrated benefits, the change isn't justified - though he concedes it wouldn't be actively harmful given existing workarounds.

The exchange highlights the ongoing tension in the thread between those wanting to solve real-world pain points (like npm/husky workflows) and those who see the feature as solving problems that either have existing solutions or shouldn't be Git's responsibility. Wood's position remains that unless stronger use cases emerge, the change isn't worth the potential downsides.
