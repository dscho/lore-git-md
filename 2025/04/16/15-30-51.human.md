This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about disabling Git hooks globally. Derrick Stolee acknowledges Junio C Hamano's decisive rejection of the proposed `--no-hooks` global option, which had been met with skepticism from multiple contributors. The maintainer's position is clear: Git should solve such problems through internal refactoring rather than adding new user-facing options.

Stolee confirms he will proceed with a documentation-only change in v2 of his patch, focusing on documenting the existing workaround of setting `core.hooksPath=/dev/null` to disable hooks. This aligns with the thread's emerging consensus that while the hook bypass mechanism deserves documentation, the proposed new option doesn't meet Git's design standards for new features.

The exchange highlights Git's philosophical approach to problem-solving - favoring architectural solutions over superficial workarounds, even when addressing real workflow pain points like those encountered with npm/husky integrations. While the documentation update will proceed, the core debate appears settled against introducing new global hook-control mechanisms.
