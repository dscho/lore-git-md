Jacob Keller weighs in on the ongoing discussion about patch series tracking with Change-IDs versus Patch Set IDs. This is a thoughtful response to Theodore Ts'o's proposal, offering practical experience and workflow considerations.

Keller expresses a preference for Ts'o's Patch Set ID approach over Gerrit-style Change-IDs, citing his experience that individual Change-IDs for each patch in a series can discourage splitting patches during review. He appreciates how Ts'o's proposal would allow treating related commits as a single logical unit while still accommodating singleton changes.

The email highlights several key aspects of Ts'o's proposal that Keller finds compelling: the automatic generation of IDs during rebase/amend operations, the clean handling of both single commits and series, and the potential for tooling to retroactively group commits into series. He also notes the cleverness of the "reverse hex" encoding suggestion from earlier in the thread for making Change-IDs visually distinct from commit hashes.

Keller's contribution adds weight to the discussion by bringing real-world experience with Gerrit's limitations to bear on the design considerations. His perspective suggests that Ts'o's approach may better accommodate the natural evolution of patch series during review. The email doesn't raise new objections but rather reinforces the practical benefits of the proposed solution.
