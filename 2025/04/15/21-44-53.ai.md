This is a technical discussion about patch tracking solutions, specifically comparing Change-ID approaches with Theodore Ts'o's Patch Set ID proposal. Jacob Keller weighs in on the ongoing thread, offering perspective on the challenges of creating a unified solution that works across different Git tooling ecosystems.

Keller acknowledges the complexity of the problem space, noting that different projects (Gerrit, GitButler, Jujutsu) have similar-but-not-identical needs for tracking commit evolution. He suggests that while interdiffs and range-diffs are valuable for understanding changes between versions, they don't fully solve the underlying need for stable identifiers across patch series iterations.

The email reinforces the thread's emerging preference for Ts'o's Patch Set ID approach over Gerrit-style Change-IDs, while recognizing the difficulty of creating a solution that satisfies all use cases. Keller specifically calls out the challenge of web UI implementations for interdiffs, hinting at why simpler identifier-based solutions remain attractive despite their limitations.

Key technical points include the varying semantics between different tools' change tracking implementations, the complementary (but insufficient) role of diff visualization tools, and the inherent tension between general solutions and specific workflow needs. The discussion remains grounded in practical integration concerns rather than theoretical purity.
