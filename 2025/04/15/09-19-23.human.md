Part of Patrick Steinhardt's ongoing refactoring series to separate concerns in Git's object storage code. This email thread focuses on determining the proper home for index-related functions.

In this exchange, Jeff King provides historical context showing that while certain functions in object-file.c have "index_" prefixes and take index_state parameters, they're actually more about object creation than the index subsystem itself. He traces their evolution from early Git versions where they didn't even use an index, through later changes that added index awareness primarily for CRLF conversion and attribute checking.

Patrick acknowledges the point and agrees to keep these functions in their current location rather than moving them to read-cache.[ch] as originally proposed. This represents a course correction in the series based on deeper historical understanding of the code's purpose.

The discussion demonstrates the thoughtful consideration being given to function boundaries in this large-scale refactoring effort, with decisions being revisited when new historical context emerges. This particular change won't proceed, but the overall series continues to progress with other components already finalized.
