This is a patch series discussion about refactoring object-file utilities, specifically addressing Jeff King's feedback about function classification.

Patrick Steinhardt acknowledges Jeff King's historical analysis showing that the `index_*` functions in object-file.c are more about object creation than index operations, despite their naming. King traced these functions back to their origins, demonstrating they were originally index-agnostic and only later gained index awareness for CRLF conversion purposes. 

Steinhardt agrees with King's assessment that these functions don't logically belong with either the index subsystem (read-cache.[ch]) or object-file operations, but decides to leave them in their current location rather than splitting them out as originally proposed in the patch. This maintains the status quo while the architectural implications are better understood.

Key technical details:
- Functions in question: `index_path()` and related `index_*` functions
- Historical context: Originally index-agnostic, later modified for CRLF handling
- Current behavior: Can operate without index/repo in some cases
- Callers include: `hash-object`, `git-replace`, `diff`
- Decision: Functions remain in object-file.c for now

The exchange shows careful consideration of historical context and actual usage patterns when making architectural decisions, with Steinhardt demonstrating responsiveness to community feedback by withdrawing the proposed split. This leaves the broader refactoring effort (moving 200+ functions across 124 files) intact while avoiding premature reorganization of these borderline cases.
