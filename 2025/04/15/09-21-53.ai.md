This is a contributor housekeeping email announcing new AI guidelines for Git's mentoring programs (GSoC and Outreachy) to combat spammy AI-generated proposals. 

Christian Couder reports that 71 out of 79 GSoC 2025 proposals received were spammy/AI-generated, a significant increase from previous years. The guidelines were developed through discussions between mentors and org admins, documented on Git's General Application Information page (with a linked GitHub PR #771). The email notes similar issues affecting other organizations, with those having anti-AI-spam guidelines reporting reduced spam volume.

The guidelines appear to be a reactive measure to a growing problem, with the spike in low-quality AI submissions making manual review increasingly burdensome. The email invites feedback on whether these guidelines should be expanded to cover the entire Git project, suggesting this may be the beginning of broader policy discussions about AI use in Git contributions.

Key points for future thread context: the guidelines are currently specific to mentoring programs, were prompted by an overwhelming volume of AI-spam (71/79 proposals), and may be considered for project-wide adoption. The GitHub PR #771 contains the specific guideline language.
