This is a maintainer response in a concluded thread about POSIX-compliant printf usage in submodule tests. Junio Hamano weighs in on the final implementation that replaced loops with a single `printf` call using duplicated arguments from `test_seq | sed p`.

The maintainer questions whether the new approach offers any real benefits over the previously agreed "most natural" solution (variant #4 from earlier discussion). He notes that since `printf` is typically a shell builtin, there's no performance gain from reducing fork/exec calls. He also raises concerns about command-line length limits if the test were ever scaled up to more submodules, suggesting a while loop would eventually be needed anyway.

While not outright rejecting the change, Junio makes it clear he considers the loop-based approach more maintainable long-term, especially for potential test scaling. This comes as a gentle course correction after the thread had appeared settled, reminding contributors to consider practical maintainability over clever one-liners. The technical discussion appears complete, but the maintainer is signaling a preference for simpler, more scalable constructs even if they're less "cute".
