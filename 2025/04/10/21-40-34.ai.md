Contribution to the Change-ID standardization discussion, focusing on practical implementation differences between Git and Jujutsu (jj).

Martin von Zweigbergk clarifies the scope of the Change-ID proposal in response to Junio Hamano's concerns about tracking commit evolution. The key distinction is between:

1. Change-IDs as stable references to evolving commits (analogous to auto-updating branch names via `--update-refs`), versus
2. The more comprehensive "Git Evolve" proposal for tracking patch evolution history

The email highlights Jujutsu's implementation where Change-IDs:
- Enable convenient prefix-based commit referencing (e.g., `jj show xu`)
- Prevent divergent commits by always rewriting the same change
- Prioritize mutable (local) commits in prefix lookups

Notable technical differences from Git's model:
- Jujutsu's detached-HEAD workflow enables automatic descendant rewriting
- Change-IDs serve as primary commit identifiers in commands
- Built-in indexing of mutable commits

The response suggests Git's workflow constraints (preference for branch-based work vs detached HEAD) may limit some Change-ID benefits, and recommends experimenting with Jujutsu to understand the practical differences. The email maintains that Change-IDs provide value independent of full changeset evolution support, particularly for local workflow efficiency.
