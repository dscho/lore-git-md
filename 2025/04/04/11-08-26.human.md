This is a technical discussion in the ongoing GSoC 2025 proposal to consolidate ref-related functionality into `git-refs`. Karthik Nayak responds to Zheng Yuting's analysis of implementation approaches for the `list` subcommand, which would combine filtering and formatting capabilities from `git-for-each-ref` and `git-show-ref`.

Karthik suggests refining the categorization of options, noting that sorting should be considered distinct from filtering since it affects presentation rather than sample size. He questions whether all formatting options (like `--tcl`, `--shell`, `--perl`) are still necessary, implying the UX could be simplified. Performance benchmarks show that format choice significantly impacts speed, with `git-for-each-ref` potentially outperforming `git-show-ref` when using simpler formats.

Regarding the two proposed approaches - unified `list` vs. split `filter`/`show` subcommands - Karthik leans toward the unified approach (Approach 1) for better usability, though he acknowledges its complexity. He raises concerns that the split approach might force awkward piping between subcommands. As a middle ground, he suggests starting with a simpler standalone `git refs show` implementation first.

The email advances the technical design discussion by challenging assumptions about option categorization and performance, while maintaining focus on Patrick Steinhardt's established constraints (backward compatibility, `refs.c`-only changes).
