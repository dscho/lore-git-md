This is a discussion about change ID semantics in Git, specifically focusing on how they should behave during cherry-picks and backports. Elijah Newren presents a concrete counterpoint to earlier proposals by describing real-world Gerrit workflows where duplicate change IDs are actively used to track backports across LTS branches.

The key tension is between two models: Jujutsu's approach where change IDs are treated as unique review units (with automatic descendant rebasing to maintain uniqueness) versus Gerrit's model where duplicate IDs serve as lineage markers for tracking bugfixes across branches. Newren provides compelling examples from enterprise projects where maintaining identical change IDs across backports is critical for verifying fix propagation.

The email highlights practical problems with alternative approaches like cherry-pick trailers (dead ends, complex chains) and questions how commands like `git switch <change-id>` would work with non-unique IDs. He suggests treating ambiguous change IDs like abbreviated hashes (requiring disambiguation) as a potential compromise.

This represents a significant fork in the standardization discussion, forcing the thread to reconcile fundamentally different workflow needs - interactive development versus large-scale maintenance. The proposal to treat duplicate IDs as non-fatal (like hash collisions) could provide a path forward that accommodates both models.
