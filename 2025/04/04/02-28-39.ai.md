This is a continuation of the change ID standardization discussion, focusing on backporting workflows and the semantic meaning of change IDs. Elijah Newren provides concrete counterexamples from Gerrit-based projects where duplicate change IDs are actively used to track backports across LTS branches.

The email makes several key technical points:
1) Documents real-world Gerrit workflows where identical change IDs are intentionally maintained across cherry-picked backports to multiple LTS branches, used for verification that fixes are properly propagated
2) Highlights practical problems with cherry-pick trailers (dead ends, complex chains) compared to shared change IDs for tracking backports
3) Questions the feasibility of commands like `git switch <change-id>` in contexts where change IDs are deliberately non-unique
4) Contrasts Jujutsu's "unit of review" model with Gerrit's "tracking across branches" use case
5) Notes the current lack of Git functionality to automatically rebase descendants when amending commits

The discussion reveals a fundamental philosophical divide between:
- Change IDs as unique identifiers for interactive use (Jujutsu model)
- Change IDs as lineage markers for cross-branch tracking (Gerrit model)

Newren's contribution significantly expands the discussion's scope to include:
- Large-scale project maintenance requirements
- Practical backport verification needs
- The tension between uniqueness and traceability
- Real-world limitations of cherry-pick trailers
- Key open questions now include how to reconcile these competing models in a standardized approach that serves both interactive development and enterprise-scale maintenance workflows.
