This is a continuation of the change ID standardization thread, focusing on the practical implications of duplicate change IDs in backport workflows. Elijah Newren provides concrete examples from Gerrit-based projects where maintaining identical change IDs across branches is a deliberate design choice for tracking LTS backports, contrasting with Jujutsu's model where change IDs are treated as unique review units.

The email highlights several key technical tensions: Gerrit's established practice of using duplicate IDs for cross-branch tracking versus the command-line usability problems this creates (like ambiguous `git switch` operations), and the limitations of cherry-pick trailers as an alternative tracking mechanism. Newren describes real-world problems with cherry-pick trailers including dead ends from local/private commits and complex chains that complicate backport verification.

A notable proposal emerges: treating non-unique change IDs similarly to abbreviated hashes, where commands would error on ambiguity. This would preserve Gerrit's backport tracking while making the behavior explicit rather than magical. The email surfaces fundamental workflow differences - Gerrit's branch-aware change tracking versus Jujutsu's review-unit model - that any standardization effort must reconcile.

The discussion now hinges on whether to:
1) Standardize on one model (Gerrit's duplicate IDs or Jujutsu's unique IDs)
2) Support both with clear semantics
3) Implement disambiguation mechanisms like the hash-like proposal
4) Document recommended practices for different workflows

Technical constraints like Git's current inability to automatically rebase descendants remain relevant, as does the need to support enterprise-scale LTS maintenance scenarios where change ID tracking is already deeply embedded in workflows.
