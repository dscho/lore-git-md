This is a review response in the ongoing approxidate bugfix series, focusing on how "today" and "yesterday" interact with time specifications like "noon". Jeff King (Peff) examines the second patch which aims to fix cases where explicit day specifications weren't properly overriding time-based specials.

Peff confirms the core approach of resetting tm_mday makes sense but identifies a subtle edge case: when crossing month or year boundaries (like April 1st or January 1st), the current implementation could produce incorrect dates because it doesn't properly handle tm_mon and tm_year synchronization. He suggests a more direct approach of copying all date fields from "now" rather than relying on tm_mday resetting.

The review also notes that while adding "today" as a synonym for "now" is reasonable, it's slightly odd that "noon now" doesn't respect the time override like "noon today" does - though this is pre-existing behavior. For testing, Peff reiterates suggestions from the first patch about avoiding subshells and adding rather than replacing test cases.

This continues the careful technical review of the date parsing edge cases, with Peff both validating the core approach while surfacing additional boundary conditions that need consideration. The exchange shows the project's attention to detail in time/date handling, even for approximate parsing.
