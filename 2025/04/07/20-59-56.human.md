Junio Hamano weighs in on the Change-ID standardization discussion with a skeptical perspective on the practical utility of change IDs, particularly in complex development workflows. This is a thoughtful critique from Git's maintainer that challenges some fundamental assumptions of the proposal.

Hamano questions the claimed advantages of change IDs, noting that auto-generated hexadecimal strings don't provide meaningful usability benefits over commit hashes. He acknowledges the 80% case where single-commit topics work well with change IDs during rebasing, but argues that in these cases, merging might be preferable to rebasing anyway.

The more substantial critique focuses on the 20% of complex workflows where commits are split or combined during review. Hamano presents several scenarios that expose ambiguity in how change IDs should propagate through commit transformations - when splitting one commit into many or combining multiple commits into one. These cases reveal that change ID semantics may not be as clear-cut as proponents suggest.

The email suggests change IDs might be better suited as trailer metadata rather than core commit headers, since their meaning depends heavily on human judgment about "moral equivalence" between commit versions rather than mechanical guarantees. This challenges the thread's direction by questioning whether change IDs deserve the level of standardization being discussed.

Hamano's intervention represents a significant voice in the discussion, bringing the maintainer's perspective on real-world workflow complexities that the proposal needs to address. His skepticism about mechanical trustworthiness of change IDs may influence whether this feature gets integrated into core Git versus remaining a tool-specific convention.
