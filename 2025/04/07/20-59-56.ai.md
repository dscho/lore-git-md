Junio Hamano's response in the Change-ID standardization discussion raises fundamental concerns about the mechanical reliability and semantic clarity of Change-IDs. This is a critical evaluation of the proposal's core assumptions.

The email challenges the practical utility of Change-IDs in several key scenarios:
1. Questions the advantage of Change-IDs for commit identification when they're just auto-generated hashes
2. Highlights logical inconsistencies in rebasing workflows where Change-IDs imply moral equivalence between commits
3. Presents complex cases where commit splitting/combining breaks the 1:1 mapping assumption of IDs to changes
4. Argues the concept lacks clear enough semantics to warrant inclusion in commit headers

Key technical points raised:
- The rebase/merge workflow contradiction where Change-IDs suggest review preservation but rebasing implies changes
- The ambiguous handling of split/combined commits in multi-commit changesets
- Fundamental tension between Change-ID immutability and Git's mutable commit history
- Semantic ambiguity in what constitutes "the same change" across commit transformations

The response suggests Change-IDs may work for simple single-commit cases but break down in more complex scenarios, and questions whether they provide enough reliable value to merit first-class treatment in Git's object model. This moves the discussion toward deeper philosophical questions about change identity in version control systems.
