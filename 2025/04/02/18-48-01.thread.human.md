The thread on standardizing Change-IDs in Git has evolved into a nuanced discussion balancing technical needs with Git's design philosophy. Junio Hamano anchored the debate in Git's historical principles, drawing parallels to Linus Torvalds' rejection of explicit rename tracking and emphasizing that metadata should be derivable from content. This raised the bar for Change-ID proponents to demonstrate why they're essential.

Nico Williams has now responded with a detailed technical proposal addressing Theodore Ts'o's concerns about metadata semantics. The proposal specifies that Change-IDs should be preserved across operations like cherry-picks and rebases, introduces multiple header types (series ID, commit ID, derivation chain), and suggests optional indexing mechanism via refs. Nico acknowledges the complexity but argues that properly constrained Change-IDs enable workflows that pure content-addressability can't easily support.

The discussion now sits at an interesting crossroads: Nico's concrete specifications provide clear technical direction, but must still address Junio's philosophical concerns about metadata that isn't strictly necessary. Theodore Ts'o remains a key voice advocating for practical tooling needs, while Junio maintains Git's traditional skepticism of non-essential metadata. The path forward likely requires either demonstrating that Change-IDs meet Git's high bar for essential metadata, or finding alternative approaches that work within Git's design constraints.
