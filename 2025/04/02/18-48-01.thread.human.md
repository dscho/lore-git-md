This thread examines the challenges of tracking patch series versions, particularly focusing on edge cases in the `b4` tool's correlation logic and the role of Change-IDs in version tracking. The discussion was initiated by Theodore Ts'o, who identified scenarios where standalone fixes could be misidentified as new versions of a series. Konstantin Ryabitsev later provided technical details about `b4`'s current implementation, which prioritizes Change-IDs over subject-line matching but still faces limitations.

Junio Hamano has now weighed in, agreeing with Ts'o's observations about the practical difficulties encountered when applying large series via `b4 am`. The thread has developed a nuanced understanding that while Change-IDs offer automation benefits for tools like lore.kernel.org and patchwork, they require consistent contributor discipline to be effective. A key challenge is ensuring follow-up patches don't incorrectly inherit series IDs while still allowing proper correlation of legitimate version iterations.

The discussion has clarified that no perfect solution exists - subject-line matching remains necessary as a fallback, and tools need to better communicate their correlation confidence levels. The thread remains technical in nature, with participants focusing on real-world tool behaviors rather than theoretical ideals. While Change-IDs are recognized as helpful, the consensus acknowledges they don't solve all edge cases in patch tracking.
