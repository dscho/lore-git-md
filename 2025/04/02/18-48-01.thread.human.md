Junio Hamano, Git's maintainer, has weighed in with substantial technical objections to the Change-ID standardization proposal, shifting the debate's trajectory. The discussion had previously reached consensus on historical context, with Nico Williams accepting Eric Sunshine's clarification about Git's original design philosophy regarding metadata. 

Hamano's intervention raises fundamental concerns about whether Change-IDs can properly model Git's distributed nature, particularly their inability to distinguish between linear and parallel development or handle timing issues in distributed workflows. He argues that solutions with known architectural limitations shouldn't be baked into Git's core object model, even for workflow benefits. This presents a significant hurdle for proponents, who must now either redesign their approach to address these distributed systems concerns, demonstrate why the benefits outweigh the limitations, or find alternative implementations that don't require core changes.

The thread now balances between two perspectives: the workflow convenience arguments from Change-ID supporters, and the maintainer's concerns about preserving Git's distributed architecture. With the historical questions settled but major technical objections now on the table, the proposal's path forward appears more challenging than before Hamano's input.
