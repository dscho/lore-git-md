This is a discussion about the practical use of Change-IDs in Git workflows, particularly in relation to Gerrit usage. Theodore Ts'o responds to Remo Senekowitsch's points about Change-ID uniqueness with real-world experience from kernel development.

Ts'o acknowledges the utility of Change-IDs while cautioning against over-reliance on them for automation, noting they sometimes change between rebases or remain unchanged despite significant semantic changes to commits. He shares his personal workflow where he typically searches by human-readable commit subjects rather than Change-IDs, finding this more practical in day-to-day kernel work.

The email reinforces the thread's ongoing theme that Change-IDs are useful but imperfect identifiers, particularly highlighting the gap between theoretical benefits and practical usage patterns. Ts'o's perspective as a kernel maintainer adds weight to the discussion by grounding it in large-scale project experience, though he doesn't propose any technical solutions himself.

This contribution serves as a reality check in the broader standardization discussion, emphasizing that while Change-IDs have value, their limitations in real-world scenarios should inform any Git integration design.
