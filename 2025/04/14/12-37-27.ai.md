This is a response to the Git 20th anniversary community interview initiative, providing answers to three of the nine proposed questions. Luca Milanesio shares personal experiences and opinions:

1. **Git disaster recovery**: References a 2013 incident where he was "one of the worst offenders" (linking to an InfoQ article about a force-push disaster), but notes that using Gerrit Code Review with its replication plugin prevented permanent data loss by allowing recovery of the rewound refs.

2. **Design change wish**: States he would have implemented SHA-256 hashing from Git's inception (noting it was available when Git was created in 2005), rather than the current SHA-1 to SHA-256 migration path.

The response is brief and personal, offering concrete examples from the author's experience rather than general commentary. It contributes two specific data points to the anniversary project's historical/reflective questions, with one including a verifiable public reference to a past incident. The SHA-256 comment aligns with Git's ongoing hash algorithm work, suggesting this may prompt discussion about historical technical debt versus contemporary migration costs.
