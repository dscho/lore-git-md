This is a maintainer confirmation in the ongoing discussion about GPG signing in Git's email workflow. Junio C Hamano responds to brian m. carlson's point about signature verification, clarifying the separation between Git's responsibilities and email authentication.

The email reinforces several key points from the thread: Git itself doesn't handle email signature verification (that's the mail client's job), and maintaining a web of trust for patch contributors is impractical for most cases. Hamano notes that while some contributors still send PGP-signed patches, he no longer objects to the practice but acknowledges the verification burden falls entirely on the recipient's email setup.

The response subtly underscores the thread's consensus that transport-layer authentication (like PGP-signed emails) operates independently from Git's commit signing capabilities. Hamano's practical observation about only bothering with keys for prolific contributors highlights the real-world usability challenges of any email-based authentication system.

This email doesn't introduce new technical information but serves as a maintainer's validation of the discussion's direction, particularly the clear boundary between Git's domain (commit contents) and the email transport layer's authentication concerns. The tone suggests the technical debate has largely concluded, with implementation questions remaining open.
