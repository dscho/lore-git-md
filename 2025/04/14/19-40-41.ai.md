This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about multi-remote synchronization workflows, specifically addressing concerns about the local-source model's fragility. D. Ben Knoble responds to Klaus Frank's critique that keeping the authoritative copy on a local machine (like a notebook) creates single-point-of-failure risks.

The key points in this response are:
1. Knoble suggests that pushing to multiple remotes from the local source would effectively create redundant backups, mitigating the notebook fragility concern
2. He acknowledges Klaus Frank's consideration of abandoning hosted forges (GitLab) in favor of SSH-based workflows, noting this would make cron-based synchronization easier to implement
3. The exchange highlights the tension between local control (with backup requirements) and hosted solutions, though doesn't resolve the original stateless CI use case

The discussion continues to explore the tradeoffs between different architectural approaches without yet converging on a solution that addresses all constraints (particularly the stateless CI environment case). This email reinforces the local-source model's viability when combined with disciplined push-to-all-remotes practices, while recognizing some users may prefer simpler SSH-based alternatives to complex multi-remote synchronization.
