This is a community interview response for Git's 20th anniversary project, continuing the thread where contributors share their perspectives on Git's usage and evolution. Lucas Seiki Oshiro provides thoughtful answers to the 9 standard questions, with several notable highlights.

The response emphasizes Git's code archaeology tools (`git bisect`, `git log -S/-G`, etc.) as both personally valuable and underappreciated by newcomers. A practical disaster recovery story describes resolving an ambiguous `master` tag/branch collision through direct refspec manipulation. The most substantive technical critique suggests restructuring Git's CLI into more consistent subcommand hierarchies (e.g., `git branch add/delete/list`), drawing inspiration from Docker's command organization.

Oshiro identifies squash merges and submodules as commonly misunderstood features, while advocating for better utilization of commits as documentation resource. The response recommends several tools (Delta for diff formatting, Kworkflow for kernel contributions) and predicts Git's continued dominance as a platform for derivative version control systems, citing its adaptable design. The answers reflect a newcomer's perspective (unable to compare pre-Git workflows) combined with technical depth, particularly around debugging use cases. The tone is enthusiastic and community-oriented, concluding with a request to share the questions locally.
