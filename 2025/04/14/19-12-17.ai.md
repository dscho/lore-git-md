This is a maintainer confirmation in the ongoing discussion about GPG signing in Git's email workflow, specifically addressing transport-layer authentication versus commit signatures.

Junio Hamano responds to brian m. carlson's point about Git's inability to verify signatures by clarifying the separation of concerns between Git's role and email transport security. He notes that while some contributors send PGP-signed patches, verification happens at the mail client level rather than within Git itself. The maintainer describes his personal workflow where trusted keys would allow verification outside Git before applying patches with `git am`.

The email highlights the practical challenges of key management in the Web of Trust model, noting that maintaining and validating contributor keys only becomes worthwhile for highly active contributors. This reinforces the thread's earlier conclusions about the limitations of PGP signing for patch authentication while acknowledging it can work in specific trusted scenarios.

Key technical points include: the distinction between Git's patch application and email authentication, the role of mail clients in verification, and the practical limitations of key management. The response maintains the thread's consensus that transport-layer authentication belongs at the email level rather than being Git's responsibility, while also providing insight into real-world usage patterns of PGP-signed patches on the mailing list.
