Git 20th anniversary community interview initiative. The Git Rev News team is organizing a collaborative interview project to mark Git's 20th anniversary, collecting responses from the community on 9 reflective questions about Git's evolution, usage, and future. Questions range from practical tips ("favorite Git trick") to historical reflections ("worst Git disaster") and future predictions ("version control in 20 years"). Responses will be collected until approximately April 25th for inclusion in a special anniversary edition of Git Rev News. The effort is designed to be inclusive and generate diverse perspectives from both long-time contributors and users.

Luca Milanesio contributed responses to two questions: (1) Described a 2013 force-push disaster where he was involved (referencing an InfoQ article), noting Gerrit Code Review's replication plugin enabled recovery of rewound refs, demonstrating real-world disaster mitigation. (2) Expressed a design wish for SHA-256 hashing from Git's inception (available in 2005) rather than the current SHA-1 to SHA-256 migration path, connecting to ongoing hash algorithm work and technical debt considerations. The responses provide concrete examples from the contributor's experience, with one including a public reference to a verifiable incident. The SHA-256 comment may prompt discussion about historical decisions versus migration costs.
