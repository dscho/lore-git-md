This is a discussion email in the ongoing thread about change IDs and patch series tracking in Git. D. Ben Knoble raises several thoughtful questions about the proposed Change-ID semantics, particularly around the use of URIs and the problem statement itself.

Knoble questions whether using URIs in change IDs makes assumptions about code review tools, noting this might not be downstream-agnostic. He points out a chicken-and-egg problem with applying review URIs to local commits during initial authorship. The email also suggests the core problem being solved may be underspecified, proposing it might combine "tracking evolution" and "seeing related commits in review."

The message stands out two key concerns: first, that the current discussion may be solution-focused without enough clarity on the underlying problems, and second, that URI-based approaches may introduce tooling dependencies. Knoble's outsider perspective helpfully surfaces that the thread could benefit from re-grounding in clear use cases before finalizing technical solutions.

The PS note draws an interesting parallel to GitHub's UI limitations around interdiffs, suggesting better tooling for viewing commit evolution might reduce the need for change IDs altogether. This adds another dimension to the ongoing evaluation of whether native Git support is truly needed versus improving existing tools.
