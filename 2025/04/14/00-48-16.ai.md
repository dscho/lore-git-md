This is a technical discussion about GPG signing in Git's email workflow, focusing on transport authentication versus commit signature preservation. The email from brian m. carlson responds to Klaus Frank's proposal for implementing PGP signing in `git am`, offering counterpoints and alternative approaches.

The key technical points raised are:
1. **Implementation constraints**: Not all systems have GnuPG installed by default (notably macOS), which complicates mandating PGP support in `git am`.
2. **Standards clarification**: Confirms RFC 9580 supersedes RFC 4880 for clearsign, and notes PGP/MIME signs both content and headers of internal parts.
3. **Alternative approach**: Advocates for including commit metadata in email headers to reconstruct signatures, which has advantages for SSH signatures and avoids PGP-specific complexities.
4. **Command-line arguments**: Argues against baking specific GPG options (`--homedir`, `--keyring` etc.) into `git format-patch`, suggesting these should be handled by `gpg.program` scripts instead.

The email highlights tradeoffs between Frank's PGP-focused proposal and a more flexible approach that could accommodate SSH signatures and custom signing workflows. It emphasizes minimizing required functionality to maintain compatibility with diverse signing implementations. The discussion remains technical and constructive, with clear references to standards and practical deployment considerations.
