This thread investigates a concerning interaction between `git stash` and `cherry-pick --abort` that can lead to unexpected data loss. The discussion began with Markus Raab reporting that stashed before a failed cherry-pick could disappear permanently during the abort operation. Initial responses from brian m. carlson suggested this might be a misunderstanding, with stashed files remaining accessible through stash commands.

Markus has since provided a more precise reproduction showing the critical sequence: after stashing changes, performing a cherry-pick, popping the stash, and then aborting the cherry-pick, the stashed files become permanently unrecoverable through normal stash commands. This confirms actual data loss occurs in this specific workflow, contradicting earlier suggestions that the files were merely not auto-restored. Markus also demonstrated a manual recovery method using direct object store inspection.

The thread has now established that this is not just a documentation or expectation issue, but a genuine bug in how Git handles state during interrupted operations. The problem affects both SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories. Key open questions include whether this violates any documented guarantees about stash persistence, and whether the interaction between stash pop and cherry-pick abort needs code-level fixes. There's also discussion about whether the manual recovery method should be incorporated into Git's standard tooling.
