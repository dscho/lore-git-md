Here's the updated human-readable thread summary:

The thread examines an interaction between `git stash` and `cherry-pick --abort` that can lead to lost work. Markus Raab originally reported that files stashed during a cherry-pick operation became unrecoverable after aborting, though they remained in the object store. After initial skepticism from brian m. carlson, Markus provided a clear reproduction sequence showing the issue occurs across both SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories.

Maintainer Junio C Hamano has now weighed in, clarifying this is intentional behavior rather than a bug. He explains that `cherry-pick --abort` is designed to completely reset both the working tree and index to HEAD state, discarding any uncommitted changes - including those applied via `stash pop`. The key distinction is between `stash pop` (which applies and drops the stash) versus `stash apply` (which keeps the stash entry). Junio suggests the real issue is users choosing `pop` when they might need to revert, since `apply` preserves the stash for recovery via `reset --hard`.

The discussion has shifted from being about fixing a bug to considering better user education. While the behavior can lead to surprising data loss, it's working as designed. The remaining question is whether and how to document this interaction more clearly, rather than changing the implementation. No automated recovery mechanisms are currently being considered.
