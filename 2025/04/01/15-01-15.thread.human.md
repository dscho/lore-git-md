Patrick Steinhardt's series hardening Git's option parsing infrastructure has reached its third iteration (v3, 7 patches), with the latest patch fixing signedness mismatches that caused issues on big-endian platforms. The work systematically improves safety for integer, magnitude, and unsigned option handling across Git's codebase.

The series began with foundational refactoring to use designated initializers, then added precise overflow detection and type safety. The newest patch (6/7) introduces compile-time assertions to catch signedness mismatches between option declarations and their storage variables, fixing several real cases including the `-C` context option in `apply.c` and options in `backfill.c`, `column.c`, and `grep.c`. This completes the core safety improvements, with all prior review feedback from Phillip Wood, Junio Hamano, and René Scharfe addressed.

The final patch (7/7) remains an optional proof-of-concept for bounded integer validation. With comprehensive compile-time and runtime checks now in place for all numeric option types, the series appears technically complete and may be ready for maintainer consideration. The changes impact over 30 files while maintaining API compatibility, significantly hardening Git's option parsing against integer-related vulnerabilities.
