This thread began as a bug report about `git interpret-trailers` silently ignoring trailer keys containing periods (e.g., "My-6.11-Version"), which Junio Hamano confirmed was intentional behavior for reliability. The discussion evolved into Kristoffer Haugsbakk's comprehensive 11-patch documentation series, now fully merged to `master`, which overhauls the command's man page to clarify its strict parsing rules and proper usage.

The documentation improvements are thorough: they define valid key formats (ASCII alphanumeric plus hyphen only), introduce standardized terminology like "trailer block" and "metadata," remove outdated RFC 822 references, and provide clear examples of valid and invalid usage. Notably, the series clarifies that `git interpret-trailers` works on any text, not just commit messages, aligning the documentation with existing functionality like `git tag --trailer`. The series also explains comment handling via `core.commentString` and preserves the command's strict key format from its 2016-era design.

Kristoffer's fourth patch (the one just summarized) adds a concise preamble to the DESCRIPTION section, defining the minimal trailer format (key–value pairs separated by a colon) and introducing the terms "trailer" and "trailer block" early in the documentation. It also standardizes the term "blank line" (empty or whitespace-only) for consistent reuse throughout the man page. The series saw minor stylistic refinements, such as replacing "trailer lines" with "trailer metadata" and resolving a singular/plural debate in favor of "metadata" after feedback from Matt Hunter. The thread also revealed a minor workflow issue (a missing Message-ID in one patch) and a maintainer oversight in the "What's cooking" report, but these were resolved without affecting the series' technical content. The documentation is now technically settled and merged.
