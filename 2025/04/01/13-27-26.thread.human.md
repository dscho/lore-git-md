This thread documents the resolution of a long-standing documentation gap in `git-interpret-trailers`, where users encountered silent failures when using trailer keys containing periods (e.g., "My-6.11-Version"). The issue was confirmed as intentional behavior—Git enforces strict key formatting (ASCII alphanumeric + hyphen only)—but the lack of clear documentation made the behavior confusing. Kristoffer Haugsbakk addressed this with an 11-patch series that overhauled the man page, introducing standardized terminology ("trailer block," "metadata"), explicit key format definitions, and examples of valid/invalid usage. The series also clarified how comment lines (via `core.commentString`) are handled, filling another documentation gap.

The patches underwent four iterations, with Junio Hamano and D. Ben Knoble providing feedback on terminology and phrasing. The final version (v4) incorporated most of Junio’s copy-editing suggestions—such as simplifying "a blank line (specifically an empty line)" to "an empty line" and fixing typos—while Kristoffer defended one technical distinction (the two trailer block definition cases) as non-redundant based on 2016 implementation rationale. A minor stylistic debate emerged post-merge over singular ("trailer metadata") vs. plural ("trailers metadata") phrasing, with Kristoffer conceding to the singular form for readability, though no follow-up patch is planned unless a maintainer chooses to adopt the change.

The series was merged to `master` without further technical objections, though a procedural hiccup occurred when Junio’s "What’s cooking" draft incorrectly listed the topic as stalled. Junio later clarified the oversight and confirmed the series was already merged. The thread highlights Git’s emphasis on documentation precision, even in settled work, and serves as a case study in contributor accountability (e.g., Kristoffer’s explanation of a local workflow error that stripped a Message-ID header). No further action is expected beyond potential minor phrasing tweaks.
