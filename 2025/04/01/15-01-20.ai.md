This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt's series hardening integer handling in Git's parse-options infrastructure, specifically addressing signedness mismatches.

The patch introduces build-time assertions to catch signed/unsigned integer mismatches in parse-options usage, fixing a real-world issue where `--min-batch-size` in `git-backfill` failed on big-endian systems due to passing a `size_t` to `OPT_INTEGER()`. The solution adds `BARF_UNLESS_SIGNED()` and `BARF_UNLESS_UNSIGNED()` macros that trigger compile-time errors for type mismatches, then applies these to `OPT_INTEGER()`, `OPT_UNSIGNED()`, and `OPT_MAGNITUDE()` macros.

Key technical details include:
- New macros in git-compat-util.h using `__builtin_types_compatible_p` and signedness checks
- Fixes for several commands where unsigned values were incorrectly using `OPT_INTEGER`: `apply.c` (p_context), `backfill.c` (min_batch_size), `column.c` (colopts), and `grep.c` (pre/post_context)
- The changes maintain backward compatibility while preventing future misuse
- The patch follows the series' pattern of thorough safety improvements, with multiple reported-by tags indicating real-world impact
- Build-time checks rather than runtime validation ensures problems are caught early
- The wide impact across multiple commands (backfill, apply, grep, column) shows the systemic nature of the issue

This completes the series' goal of making integer option handling more robust throughout Git's codebase, particularly for edge cases like big-endian systems. The compile-time enforcement mechanism is a strong guarantee against similar issues recurring.
