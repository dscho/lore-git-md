Junio Hamano responds to Nico Williams in the ongoing Change-ID discussion, focusing on the philosophical and technical implications of recording metadata in commits. This is a philosophical rebuttal that uses file rename heuristics as an analogy to argue against recording Change-IDs.

Hamano clarifies Theodore Ts'o's earlier point about simplicity, emphasizing that Git's design intentionally avoids recording certain metadata (like renames/copies) not just for simplicity, but for correctness. He provides a concrete example where an IDE might incorrectly record a file relationship that a human would recognize as invalid. Git's approach of deriving relationships dynamically via heuristics allows for both better accuracy (since the interpretation can improve over time) and avoids baking incorrect assumptions into the commit history.

The response implicitly challenges the Change-ID proposal by suggesting that recording such metadata could similarly lead to incorrect assumptions being permanently recorded. Hamano's position maintains Git's content-addressable philosophy where relationships should be derived rather than recorded, though he doesn't directly address the specific technical mechanisms proposed for Change-IDs.

Key technical points raised:
- The distinction between recording vs. deriving metadata
- The risk of incorrect metadata being permanently recorded
- The advantage of heuristics that can improve over time
- The example of file copy/rename detection as precedent

This continues the thread's central tension between workflow utility (Change-IDs) and Git's design philosophy (content-addressability without baked-in metadata). Hamano's response suggests the burden remains on Change-ID proponents to demonstrate why their case differs from other metadata Git intentionally avoids recording.
