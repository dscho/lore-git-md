This is a workflow discussion about Git's integration timing between Junio Hamano (the maintainer) and Philippe Blain regarding how quickly patches move to the 'next' branch after being announced in the "What's cooking" report.

Philippe had expressed concern that his performance test fixes series was merged to 'next' just one day after being announced, leaving little time for part-time contributors to request delays or send new iterations. He suggests extending the window between announcement and merging.

Junio acknowledges the frustration but explains why a fixed waiting period wouldn't work well in practice, as different contributors have different response times. Instead, he suggests two concrete ways contributors can communicate their needs: (1) explicitly flagging uncertain aspects of patches to invite help, and (2) stating when they need more time before merging. This puts more responsibility on contributors to communicate their constraints while maintaining the project's velocity.

The exchange highlights the ongoing challenge of balancing rapid integration with inclusive participation, particularly for contributors with limited availability. Junio's response focuses on improving communication rather than changing fixed timelines, maintaining the project's flexible workflow while giving contributors tools to advocate for their needs.
