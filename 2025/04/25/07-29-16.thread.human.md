Patrick Steinhardt's series to complete the decomposition of `git gc` into granular maintenance tasks is nearing completion. The v4 patches implement the final two missing operations - worktree pruning and rerere garbage collection - as standalone tasks with configurable auto-execution thresholds. This represents the culmination of a multi-year effort to break up the monolithic `gc` command.

The design has evolved through review feedback, most notably Junio Hamano suggesting a simplification of the rerere GC auto-condition check from counting directory entries to a simpler existence check. The series now includes comprehensive test coverage and maintains backward compatibility while introducing new configuration options.

Reviewers Derrick Stolee and Eric Sunshine have been closely involved, with Eric identifying a memory leak in the worktree pruning path that was the last known issue. Patrick's latest email confirms this leak has been addressed (along with fixing a minor documentation typo), clearing the final blocker. Christian Couder has also contributed wording improvements to polish the commit messages.

With these fixes, the series appears technically complete and ready for merging. Once in, this will mark a significant milestone in Git's maintenance architecture, providing more control and transparency over repository upkeep operations that were previously bundled together in `git gc`. The changes maintain the same behavior while allowing finer-grained configuration and scheduling of these maintenance operations.
