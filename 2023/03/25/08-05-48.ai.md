This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about `git branch --contains` performance with replace refs. Oswald Buddenhagen responds to Jeff King's explanation of why commit-graph optimizations are disabled when replace refs exist and why there's no warning about it.

Oswald acknowledges the challenge of warning users about disabled optimizations without spamming them, particularly since commit graphs are now built automatically during GC and fetch operations. He suggests two potential solutions: allowing users to be manually squelch the warning (though this risks users missing future fixes), or making it an advisory message that can be explicitly disabled.

The exchange continues to explore the tradeoffs in Git's design around replace refs and commit-graph optimizations, though the core performance issue has already been resolved via the workaround of disabling replace refs. This follow-up focuses on improving the user experience around this edge case rather than introducing new technical solutions.
