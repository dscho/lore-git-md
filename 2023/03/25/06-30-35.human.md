This is a follow-up in the `git branch --contains` performance thread, where Jeff King (Peff) responds to Oswald Buddenhagen's confirmation that disabling replace refs via `-c core.useReplaceRefs=false` restored the expected commit-graph optimizations. The core performance issue is now resolved, but Peff addresses Oswald's two broader questions about Git's behavior in this case.

Peff explains that the decision to disable commit-graph optimizations when replace refs are present was initially the simplest safe approach, leaving room for future optimization work that nobody has yet pursued. He acknowledges Oswald's point about potentially warning users when the graph is ignored, noting that while this might be reasonable now that fast performance is expected, it could lead to frequent warnings in repos with replace refs since graphs are built automatically during gc/fetch.

The email also confirms that any additional optimizations Peff had previously suggested would provide negligible benefit compared to the massive speedup from properly enabling commit-graph optimizations. This effectively closes the immediate performance investigation while leaving open the design questions about how Git might better handle this edge case in the future. Peff hints that Oswald could be the one to drive such changes if motivated, but there's no urgency given the workaround's effectiveness.
