This is a follow-up in the `git branch --contains` performance thread, where Jeff King (Peff) responds to Oswald Buddenhagen's confirmation that disabling replace refs restored commit-graph optimizations. Peff addresses Oswald's two design questions about why the commit-graph isn't built with replace refs applied and why there's no warning when optimizations are disabled.

On the first question, Peff explains the historical context - the initial implementation took the conservative approach of disabling optimizations when replace refs are present, leaving the more complex solution (versioning graphs by replace ref state) as future work that no one has yet undertaken. He playfully suggests Oswald could be the one to implement it.

Regarding warnings, Peff acknowledges the merit in Oswald's suggestion but notes practical complications - since commit graphs are now built automatically during GC and fetch, warnings would become pervasive in repos with replace refs. He observes that while warnings were less critical when commit graphs were new, their absence may now be more noticeable given how much performance users expect from the optimizations.

Peff also confirms that any additional optimizations he had previously suggested would indeed be negligible compared to the massive speedup from properly enabling commit-graph, effectively closing the immediate performance investigation while leaving the door open for future improvements to Git's handling of this edge case. The exchange concludes the technical discussion while surfacing clear opportunities for design refinements.
