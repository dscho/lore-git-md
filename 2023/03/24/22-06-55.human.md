This is a follow-up in the performance investigation of `git branch --contains` when commit-graph optimizations are unexpectedly disabled. Oswald Buddenhagen confirms Jeff King's diagnosis that a replace ref in his Linux kernel repository was preventing commit-graph optimizations from being applied.

The key confirmation comes from testing with `-c core.useReplaceRefs=false`, which restored the expected dramatic performance improvement (from minutes to milliseconds). Oswald notes the speed difference is so significant that any additional optimizations from Peff's earlier patch would be negligible in comparison.

The email raises two architectural questions: why Git doesn't build commit-graphs with replace refs already applied (tagged for invalidation), and why there's no warning when the commit-graph is ignored due to replace refs. These suggest potential future improvements to make the optimization behavior more transparent and robust.

The thread has effectively resolved the immediate performance mystery while surfacing some design considerations about how replace refs interact with Git's performance optimizations. The workaround is clear, and the remaining questions point to possible enhancements rather than unresolved issues.
