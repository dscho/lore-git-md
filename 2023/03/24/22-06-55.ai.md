This is a follow-up in the `git branch --contains` performance investigation thread, where Oswald Buddenhagen confirms Jeff King's diagnosis about replace refs disabling commit-graph optimizations. The key new information is Oswald's confirmation that disabling replace refs via `-c core.useReplaceRefs=false` does indeed restore the expected performance improvement (from minutes to milliseconds). He also raises two important questions about the current behavior: why the commit-graph isn't built with replace refs applied (and versioned by their hash), and why there's no warning when the graph is ignored due to replace refs. These point to potential areas for future improvement in how Git handles this edge case.

The email also notes that any additional optimizations Jeff King proposed earlier would be negligible compared to the massive speedup from properly enabling the commit-graph optimization. This suggests the immediate performance issue is effectively resolved by the workaround, though the underlying design questions remain open for future consideration. The discussion has now conclusively identified both the root cause and a viable solution while surfacing opportunities to improve Git's handling of this case more generally.
